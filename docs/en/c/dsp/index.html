Digital Signal Processing Basics: key points

Once an AD converter has turned a signal into a sequence of numbers, processors and FPGAs filter it, analyze its frequency content and change its sampling rate. Digital signal processing runs inside almost every electronic product, from audio and communications to sensors, motor control and measuring instruments.

This topic starts with the normalized frequency and period of discrete-time signals, the unit impulse and step, and energy and power, and then deals with the properties of linear time-invariant systems (linearity, time invariance, causality and BIBO stability), impulse response and convolution, and computing with difference equations. It goes on to check DFT bin spacing and bin indices, the symmetry of the spectra of real signals, FFT workload, zero padding, spectral leakage and window functions, Parseval's theorem, transfer functions with poles and zeros via the z-transform, FIR and IIR filters, linear phase and group delay, moving averages and exponential smoothing, the bilinear transform, coefficient quantization, decimation and interpolation, and estimating multiply-accumulate counts and real-time processing budgets. The values in the calculation questions, including those of the wrong choices, have been checked by computing them in Python (NumPy). The sampling theorem, aliasing, quantization noise, oversampling and delta-sigma ADCs are covered in "A/D and D/A conversion", analog filters and op-amps in "Analog and electronic circuits", OFDM in "Wireless Communication Basics", convolution in CNNs in "Machine Learning Basics", and fixed-point representation in "Computer Arithmetic".
