Machine Learning Basics: key points

Machine learning is the technology of learning rules automatically from data in order to make predictions and classifications. There is supervised learning, which uses correct-answer labels; unsupervised learning, which finds structure in unlabeled data; and reinforcement learning, which learns actions from rewards. Whatever the method, what matters is how accurately the model predicts on data not used for training (generalization performance).

This topic covers, mainly through calculation questions, splitting data into training, validation and test sets and cross-validation, overfitting and the bias–variance trade-off, regularization and dropout, precision, recall and the F1 score computed from a confusion matrix, the ROC curve and the mean squared error. After representative models such as linear regression, logistic regression, decision trees, random forests, k-nearest neighbors, SVMs and naive Bayes, it goes on to the forward pass of neural networks, activation functions, loss functions, gradient descent and backpropagation, mini-batches and epochs, output sizes in CNNs, RNNs and the Transformer, transfer learning, generative models and the tokens of LLMs. It also helps with the AI sections of IT certification exams such as Japan's Fundamental and Applied Information Technology Engineer Examinations, and with the JDLA Deep Learning for GENERAL (G-kentei) certificate.
