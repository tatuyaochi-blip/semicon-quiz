Wireless Communication Basics: key points

Smartphones, wireless LANs and IoT devices put information onto radio waves and pick extremely weak signals out of noise. Their performance is set by the combination of communication schemes such as modulation and multiple access, RF circuits such as amplifiers, mixers and filters, and the semiconductor devices that make them possible.

This topic covers, with calculation questions, radio basics such as frequency and wavelength, link budgets in dB and dBm, free-space path loss, multipath fading and Doppler shift, and goes on to ASK, FSK, PSK and QAM, symbol rate and bit rate, Shannon's theorem, OFDM and the cyclic prefix, CDMA and OFDMA, and MIMO and beamforming. It also covers wireless LAN standards and the hidden node problem, WPA3, BLE, LPWA, NFC and GNSS, the concepts behind 4G LTE and 5G NR, the roles of LNAs, mixers, PAs and duplexers, noise figure and Friis's formula, receiver sensitivity, superheterodyne and direct-conversion receivers, and why compound semiconductors are used for PAs.
