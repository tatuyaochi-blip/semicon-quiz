Probability, Statistics and Queueing: key points

"What is the probability that it happens at least once?" "What is the probability that a part judged positive is really defective?" "How much longer does the wait get when server utilization rises from 80% to 90%?" Probability and statistics answer questions like these. They are the common tools for handling variation and uncertainty with numbers in many fields of engineering: semiconductor yield and reliability, bit errors in communication, computer performance evaluation, machine learning and more.

This topic covers counting cases with permutations and combinations, the inclusion-exclusion principle, complementary events and independence, conditional probability and Bayes' theorem, expected value and its linearity, and variance and standard deviation. It then checks the properties of the binomial, Poisson, uniform, normal (the 68-95-99.7 rule and standardization) and exponential (memorylessness) distributions through calculations. On the statistics side, it deals with the median, quartiles and box plots, the correlation coefficient and the slope of the regression line, the standard error and confidence intervals, and p-values and type I and type II errors in hypothesis testing. The last part calculates utilization, average waiting time and response time with the M/M/1 queueing model, covers Little's law and the effect of how queues are formed, and applies these ideas to the probabilities of bit errors, parity checks and sampling inspection. It helps with the applied mathematics (probability, statistics and queueing) of IT certification exams such as Japan's Fundamental and Applied Information Technology Engineer Examinations. Control charts and process capability indices are covered in "Yield and quality control", failure rates and the Weibull distribution in "Reliability", confusion matrices and machine learning models in "Machine Learning Basics", and availability calculations in "System Architecture and Reliability".

Which of the following correctly compares the average waiting time?
