Network Design and Operations: key points

Company and data center networks connect switches and routers redundantly, divide them with VLANs, choose the best of several paths, and are designed so that communication continues when something fails. On top of that, services such as DHCP, DNS, NAT and ACLs are combined, and the network is operated while traffic and faults are monitored.

As the next step after "Computer Networking Basics," this topic covers MAC address learning in L2 switches, IEEE 802.1Q tags, trunk ports and inter-VLAN routing, loop prevention with STP and RSTP, link aggregation, VLSM and route summarization calculations, longest prefix match, static routes and administrative distance, distance-vector and link-state protocols, OSPF cost calculations, BGP and ASes, and VRRP. It goes on, with calculation and tracing questions, to DHCP relay, recursive DNS queries and TTL, port forwarding, the order in which ACLs are evaluated, IPv6 SLAAC and EUI-64, tunneling, traceroute, IPv4 fragmentation, path MTU discovery, QoS and queuing, SNMP, syslog and NetFlow, and troubleshooting layer by layer. It also helps as an introduction to the network sections of IT certification exams such as Japan's Applied Information Technology Engineer and Network Specialist Examinations.
