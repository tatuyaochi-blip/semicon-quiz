Control Engineering Basics: key points

New to this topic? Start with: How Noise Cancelling Works: Using Sound to Erase Sound

The temperature of an air conditioner, the speed of a motor, the output voltage of a power supply, the position of a hard-disk head: many quantities around us are held constant by feedback control, which compares a sensor measurement with a setpoint and adjusts the manipulated variable. This topic covers classical control, the foundation for designing and analyzing such loops.

It starts with the differences between open-loop, feedback and feedforward control, block diagrams and closed-loop transfer functions, and how negative feedback suppresses variation, then covers the Laplace transform, transfer functions and the final value theorem. It continues by working through, with calculations, the time constant and step response of first-order systems, the damping ratio and natural angular frequency of second-order systems, overshoot and settling time, how pole positions relate to the response, and steady-state error and system type. For stability it covers the pole condition for stability, the Routh–Hurwitz stability criterion and the Nyquist stability criterion, and for frequency response it covers Bode plots and straight-line approximations, integrators, time delay, reading the gain margin and phase margin, and the relation between phase margin and overshoot. The second half examines what each action of P, PI and PID control does, the Ziegler–Nichols ultimate sensitivity method, integral windup and its countermeasures, derivative kick, discrete-time PI calculations on a microcontroller, the phase lag due to the zero-order hold and the correspondence between the s-plane and the z-plane, and touches on cascade control of motors, the control loop of a DC-DC converter and the basics of sequence control. It also serves as an introduction to automatic control as covered in the machinery subject of Japan's Class 3 Chief Electrical Engineer examination (Denken 3) and in the circuit theory and control engineering subject of Korea's Engineer Electricity examination (jeongi gisa). The values in the calculation questions, including those of the wrong choices, have been checked by computing them in Python (NumPy). The gain of negative feedback and the phase margin and compensation of op-amps are covered in "Analog and electronic circuits"; RC time constants in "Circuit and electromagnetism basics"; the z-transform, difference equations and the bilinear transform in "Digital Signal Processing Basics"; PWM and timer interrupts in "Embedded Systems & Firmware"; fixed point and saturating arithmetic in "Computer Arithmetic"; and process control in semiconductor fabs (APC) in "Yield and quality control".
