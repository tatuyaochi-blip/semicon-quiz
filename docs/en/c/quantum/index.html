Quantum Computing Hardware: key points

A quantum computer computes using superpositions of 0 and 1 and quantum entanglement. Its performance is not set by the number of qubits alone, but by gate error rates, how long quantum states can be kept (the coherence time), how the qubits are connected, and how many qubits can be devoted to quantum error correction.

After checking qubit states, measurement probabilities and the effect of quantum gates with small calculations, this topic covers T1, T2 and fidelity and the ideas of the surface code and its threshold. It then turns to the hardware challenges of scaling up: the strengths and weaknesses of superconducting qubits based on Josephson junctions, trapped ions, neutral atoms, silicon spin qubits that can draw on CMOS fabrication, and photonic qubits; dilution refrigerators that cool to about 10 mK; microwave control and dispersive readout; and cryo-CMOS and the heat load of wiring. Why Shor's algorithm threatens public-key cryptography, and post-quantum cryptography as the response, are also covered in relation to the scale of hardware they require.
