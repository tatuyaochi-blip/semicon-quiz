IT Service Management and Systems Audit: key points

A system is not finished once it has been built and switched on. It has to run stably every day at the quality agreed with its users; when a failure occurs, service must be restored quickly, the cause removed so that the same failure does not recur, and changes made only after their impact has been checked. A systems audit then checks, from an independent standpoint, whether these management mechanisms really work.

This topic covers SLAs and service level management, availability calculations that take service hours and planned maintenance into account, the difference between incident management and problem management, change, release and configuration management (CMDB), the service desk and escalation, capacity management and MTBF/MTTR calculations, business continuity plans (BCP), and facilities such as UPS units and fire suppression. The second half deals with the independence of systems auditors, how an audit proceeds from the preliminary survey and main audit to the audit report and follow-up, audit evidence and audit trails, audit techniques such as the test data and ITF methods, Japan's System Management Standards and System Audit Standards, the objectives and basic components of internal control, segregation of duties, and IT governance. It helps with the management sections (service management and systems audit) of IT certification exams such as Japan's Fundamental and Applied Information Technology Engineer Examinations. Project management is covered in "Software Development and Project Management", and backups, RPO and RTO in "System Architecture and Reliability".
