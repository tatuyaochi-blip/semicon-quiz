PN junctions and diodes: key points

When p-type and n-type semiconductors are joined, carriers diffuse and recombine near the boundary, forming a depletion layer with almost no carriers. A built-in potential develops across the depletion layer, and this is what allows current to flow in only one direction (rectification).

Under forward bias the barrier is lowered and current flows; under reverse bias the depletion layer widens and almost no current flows. If the reverse voltage is increased further, breakdown occurs, a phenomenon used in voltage-regulator (Zener) diodes.
