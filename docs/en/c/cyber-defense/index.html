Cyber Attacks and Defense in Practice: key points

A cyber attack does not end with exploiting a single weakness; it proceeds in stages: intrusion, establishing a foothold, expanding privileges, moving inside and acting on objectives. Defenders, too, are expected not only to think about stopping attacks at the entrance but also, on the assumption that intrusions will happen, to have organizational mechanisms for finding them quickly, stopping their spread, preserving evidence, removing the cause and learning for next time. This topic looks, from the defender's point of view, at how organizations are actually attacked and how they defend themselves.

It starts by organizing the flow of attacks with the Cyber Kill Chain and MITRE ATT&CK, covering initial access methods, clues to C2 traffic, privilege escalation, lateral movement and living-off-the-land attacks that abuse legitimate tools. It then turns to defensive mechanisms: the principles of zero trust and the policy decision and enforcement points of NIST SP 800-207, the difference between VPN and ZTNA, EDR, the roles of SIEM, SOAR and the SOC, IDS and IPS, signature-based and anomaly-based detection, WAFs and detection with decoys. For incident response it covers the phases of NIST SP 800-61, the order of volatility and chain of custody, IoCs and IoAs, CSIRTs and PSIRTs, and correcting clock offsets in logs. The second half covers prioritizing fixes by combining CVSS with exploitation status, penetration testing and red team exercises, coordinated vulnerability disclosure, SBOMs, and supply chain attacks such as dependency confusion and abuse of legitimate updates, and, for cloud and identity, public-access misconfiguration of storage, overly broad permissions, MFA fatigue attacks, why passkeys resist phishing, theft of session cookies, privileged access management and secrets committed to repositories. Finally, it checks by calculation the mean time to detect, the return on security investment (ROSI), the proportion of IDS alerts that are real attacks and the number of analysts needed. It helps with the security sections of IT certification exams such as Japan's Registered Information Security Specialist Examination and Applied Information Technology Engineer Examination. The values in the calculation questions, including those of the wrong choices, have been checked by computing them in Python. Standards and events given with a year or revision reflect the information as of that time. The three elements of information security, risk treatment, malware and phishing, web application vulnerabilities, the basics of CVE and CVSS and the first response to a ransomware infection are covered in "Information Security Basics"; cryptography and certificates in "Applied Cryptography"; defense in depth for hardware and secure boot in "Hardware Security"; the basics of firewalls and VPNs in "Computer Networking Basics"; the shared responsibility model in "Cloud and Container Basics"; and ITIL incident management in "IT Service Management and Systems Audit".

Which of the following is the correct combination of the mean time from occurrence to detection (MTTD) and the mean time from detection to containment (MTTC)?
