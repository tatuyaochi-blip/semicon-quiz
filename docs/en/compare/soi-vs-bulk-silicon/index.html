SOI vs Bulk Silicon: Differences for Chip Makers

Most chips are built directly in a plain silicon wafer, called bulk silicon. A silicon-on-insulator (SOI) wafer adds a thin layer of oxide buried under the thin silicon layer where the transistors sit. That insulating layer isolates the transistors from the substrate, which reduces parasitic capacitance and removes latch-up, so circuits can be faster or lower in power. The price is a more expensive wafer and poorer heat removal.

AspectBulk siliconSOI
StructureTransistors are made directly in the silicon waferA thin silicon layer sits on a buried oxide (BOX) on a silicon substrate
IsolationWells and shallow trench isolationComplete isolation from the substrate by the BOX, plus trench isolation sideways
Parasitic capacitanceLarger junction capacitanceSmaller, because the junctions sit on oxide
Latch-upPossible, because of parasitic thyristor structuresEliminated
Soft errorsMore sensitive to charge from radiationLess sensitive, because the charge-collection volume is small
Threshold tuningBody bias possible but limitedFully depleted SOI (FD-SOI) allows strong back-gate biasing through the BOX
Self-heatingHeat flows into the substrateWorse: the oxide layer conducts heat poorly
Wafer costLowSubstantially higher
Typical usesMost CMOS chips: memory, processors and general logicRF switches in smartphones, low-power FD-SOI chips for IoT and automotive, MEMS, high-voltage and radiation-tolerant ICs

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

Bulk
The default for mainstream chips, because the wafers are cheap and the ecosystem is mature.
SOI
When low power, RF performance, radiation tolerance or body biasing is worth the extra wafer cost.
