Thermal Diffusion vs Ion Implantation: Doping Methods Compared

Both methods put dopant atoms into silicon to control its electrical behavior. In thermal diffusion, dopants move in from a source at high temperature. In ion implantation, dopant ions are accelerated and shot into the wafer. Implantation gives far better control over how much dopant goes in and where, so it replaced diffusion for almost all doping in modern ICs, although it needs an annealing step to repair the crystal.

AspectThermal diffusionIon implantation
PrincipleDopant atoms diffuse into hot silicon (about 900 to 1,100 °C) from a gas, solid or film source, driven by the concentration gradientDopant ions are accelerated by an electric field (keV to MeV) and shot into the wafer
Dose controlHarder: depends on surface concentration, temperature and timePrecise: the dose is measured as ion current
Depth profileHighest concentration at the surface, falling with depthPeak below the surface at a depth set by the ion energy
DirectionIsotropic: also spreads sideways under the maskAlmost directional; a photoresist mask is enough
TemperatureHigh throughoutNear room temperature during implantation, but annealing at high temperature is needed afterwards
Crystal damageLittleDamages the lattice; annealing repairs it and activates the dopants
Shallow junctionsDifficult at small dimensionsWell suited: low energy places dopants near the surface
Equipment costLow: a simple furnaceHigh: an ion implanter is a large, expensive tool
Typical usesDrive-in of deep wells, solar-cell emitters, older processesSources and drains, wells, threshold-voltage adjustment in nearly all modern ICs

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

Thermal diffusion
When the junction is deep, the structure is simple and cost matters, as in some solar cells and power devices.
Ion implantation
When you need precise dose, depth and position, which is the case for transistors in logic and memory.
Together
Implantation puts the dopants in; a high-temperature step then repairs the crystal, activates the dopants and drives them in a little further by diffusion.
