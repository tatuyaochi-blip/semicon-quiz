OPC vs Phase-Shift Mask vs Off-Axis Illumination: What Each Changes, Effect on Resolution and DOF, and Cost

The smallest feature a lithography tool can print is given by the Rayleigh equation R = k1 × λ / NA. With the wavelength λ and numerical aperture NA fixed by the tool, printing finer patterns means lowering the factor k1, and the methods for doing so are called resolution enhancement techniques (RET). OPC corrects the shapes on the mask in anticipation of the distortion caused by diffraction. A phase-shift mask shifts the phase of light passing through parts of the mask by 180° and uses interference to raise image contrast. Off-axis illumination lights the mask from oblique angles so that more of the light diffracted by fine patterns enters the lens. Each changes something different, so they are not alternatives to choose between but techniques used together.

When to use which

OPC
Used on almost every layer with patterns near the resolution limit. The correction computation is also essential in EUV lithography.
Phase-shift mask
Used when image contrast is insufficient. In ArF lithography, the attenuated type has been standard. The alternating type is very effective, but its design and mask constraints are large, so it is used only in limited cases.
Off-axis illumination
Used to resolve fine-pitch periodic patterns. It works without changing the mask, but the illumination shape has to be chosen to match the orientation and pitch of the patterns.

FAQ

Which of the three improves resolution the most?

They play different roles, so they cannot be compared simply. Off-axis illumination and alternating phase-shift masks are what mainly make fine pitches resolvable, while OPC makes resolved patterns take the designed shape. In volume production, combining all three has brought k1 down to around 0.3.

What is the lower limit of k1?

The theoretical lower limit for printing a periodic line-and-space pattern in a single exposure is 0.25, expressed in terms of half-pitch. Finer patterns need multiple patterning or EUV lithography with its shorter wavelength.

What are SMO and ILT?

SMO (source-mask optimization) computes and optimizes the illumination shape and mask patterns together, treating off-axis illumination and OPC as one problem. ILT (inverse lithography technology) works backward from the desired image on the wafer to find the mask pattern, which can produce complex shapes that include curves. Both require heavy computation and have been adopted first on the most difficult layers.

What is the difference between rule-based and model-based OPC?

Rule-based OPC widens patterns or adds helper shapes according to tables set by line width and spacing. Model-based OPC simulates the exposure and the resist response and moves pattern edges repeatedly until the shape on the wafer approaches the design. Model-based OPC is the mainstay at finer generations.

Check your understanding

Which correctly describes OPC (optical proximity correction)?

  1. ACoating resist thicker to improve resolution
  2. BCleaning the mask to remove particles
  3. CCorrecting the mask pattern in advance to reduce shape distortion after transfer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Correcting the mask pattern in advance to reduce shape distortion after transfer

Near the resolution limit, diffraction and interference shorten line ends, round corners and make linewidth depend on surrounding pattern density. OPC uses simulation to anticipate these distortions, adding assist features such as hammerheads and serifs and finely adjusting dimensions on the mask. Computing OPC for advanced masks requires large-scale computing resources.

What is the purpose of a phase-shift mask?

  1. ATo change the phase of light passing through adjacent openings and use interference to improve resolution and contrast
  2. BTo make the mask lighter
  3. CTo double the exposure light
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. To change the phase of light passing through adjacent openings and use interference to improve resolution and contrast

A phase-shift mask shifts the phase of light through certain regions by 180°, so amplitudes from neighboring features cancel at their boundaries, darkening dark regions and sharpening edge contrast. Types include alternating (Levenson) masks, which invert the phase of adjacent openings, and attenuated (halftone) masks, whose absorber is a slightly transmissive phase-shifting film such as MoSi; the latter are widely used in ArF lithography.

Which technique illuminates the mask from oblique directions to improve the resolution of fine patterns?

  1. AOPC (optical proximity correction)
  2. BOff-axis illumination
  3. CPhase-shift mask
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Off-axis illumination

For fine periodic patterns, diffracted orders spread at angles too wide to enter the lens, and the zeroth order alone forms no image. Tilted (off-axis) illumination lets the zeroth order and one first order both enter the lens, theoretically resolving about half the pitch and deepening focus. Variants include annular, dipole and quadrupole illumination, and modern scanners use freeform pupils configurable pixel by pixel.

Key terms explained

OPC (optical proximity correction)
A technique that corrects mask patterns in advance, anticipating distortion of the printed pattern caused by diffraction of light and similar effects.
Phase-shift mask
A photomask that shifts the phase of light passing through neighboring openings, using interference to improve image contrast and resolution.
Depth of focus (DOF)
The range of focus over which a pattern prints with acceptable quality. Raising the numerical aperture improves resolution but reduces DOF, which makes wafer planarity important.
Numerical aperture (NA)
A value that expresses the range of angles of light a lens can collect. A larger NA gives higher resolution.
Photomask (reticle)
The master pattern used for exposure, typically a light-blocking pattern on a quartz substrate (reflective for EUV). For reduction scanners it is made four times larger than the pattern on the wafer.
Process window
The range of exposure dose and focus over which pattern dimensions stay within tolerance. The wider it is, the more robust manufacturing is to variation.
Multiple patterning
Forming patterns with a pitch too fine for a single exposure by splitting them across several exposures or process steps. Splitting into two is double patterning.
