Verilog vs VHDL: Differences Between the Two HDLs

Verilog and VHDL are the two main hardware description languages (HDLs) used to describe digital circuits for simulation and synthesis. Verilog has short, C-like syntax and is easier to start with. VHDL is verbose and strongly typed, which catches more mistakes early. Both can describe the same circuits, and the choice usually depends on the industry, the company and the existing code.

AspectVerilog (and SystemVerilog)VHDL
OriginCreated in the 1980s at Gateway Design Automation and standardized as IEEE 1364Developed for the US Department of Defense and standardized as IEEE 1076 in 1987
Syntax styleConcise and C-likeVerbose and Ada-like
TypingWeakly typed: implicit conversions are easy but can hide mistakesStrongly typed: explicit conversions are needed, so errors are caught at compile time
Case sensitivityCase-sensitiveNot case-sensitive
Learning curveEasier to get startedSteeper, but safer once learned
Verification featuresVerilog alone is limited; SystemVerilog adds classes, constrained-random tests, assertions and UVMVHDL-2008 plus libraries such as OSVVM and UVVM
Language abstractionsFewer: modules and parametersRicher: packages, generics, records and enumerated types
Tool supportSupported by all major simulators and synthesis toolsSupported by all major simulators and synthesis tools, and mixed-language simulation is possible
Typical usersASIC and SoC design, especially in the US and AsiaEurope, aerospace and defense, and many FPGA projects

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

Verilog / SystemVerilog
A good default for chip design and verification, because the industry tools and libraries are built around SystemVerilog.
VHDL
A good choice where strict typing and long-term maintainability matter, such as safety-critical and defense projects.
In practice
You often need to read both. The ideas (modules or entities, concurrent signals, clocked processes) are the same.
