CMOS vs CCD Image Sensors: Differences Explained

Both sensors turn light into electric charge in each pixel; they differ in how that charge is read out. A CCD moves the charge pixel by pixel to a single output amplifier. A CMOS sensor converts charge to voltage inside each pixel and reads many pixels in parallel. CMOS sensors are cheaper, faster and use less power, and they can integrate ADCs and image processing on the same chip, so they have replaced CCDs in almost all consumer products.

AspectCMOS image sensorCCD image sensor
ReadoutEach pixel has its own amplifier; signals are read out in parallel, usually through column ADCsCharge is shifted pixel by pixel through the array to one or a few output amplifiers
ManufacturingStandard CMOS-type process, built in common semiconductor fabsSpecial process designed for charge transfer
On-chip functionsADCs, timing and image processing can be integrated; stacked designs add logic and memoryMostly only the sensor; supporting circuits are separate chips
Power consumptionLowHigh, because charge transfer needs high-speed clocks and several supply voltages
Speed (frame rate)High, thanks to parallel readoutLower, limited by the serial transfer
Noise and uniformityOnce worse, now comparable or better thanks to pinned photodiodes and correlated double samplingTraditionally low noise and uniform pixels
ShutterRolling shutter is common (fast subjects can look skewed); global shutter versions need extra pixel circuitryGlobal shutter is natural in many designs
Blooming and smearLess proneMore prone, because excess charge can spill into neighboring pixels
CostLow, thanks to mass productionHigher
Typical usesSmartphones, digital cameras, automotive cameras, webcamsScientific and astronomical cameras, some industrial, medical and space imaging

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

CMOS
Almost everything today: phones, cameras, cars, security and industrial cameras. It is cheap, fast and flexible, with backside-illuminated and stacked versions improving sensitivity and speed.
CCD
Special cases that value its long track record of low noise and uniformity, such as some scientific, astronomy and space instruments.
