CVD vs PVD: Thin-Film Deposition Compared

Both methods deposit thin films on wafers. In chemical vapor deposition (CVD), gases react on the wafer surface to form the film. In physical vapor deposition (PVD), such as sputtering, atoms are knocked out of a solid target and land on the wafer without a chemical reaction. CVD is the usual choice for insulators and for coating deep structures evenly; PVD is the usual choice for metals.

AspectCVDPVD (sputtering)
PrincipleGaseous precursors react chemically on the heated or plasma-activated wafer surfaceAtoms are ejected physically from a solid target (for example by argon ions) and condense on the wafer
Typical materialsSilicon dioxide, silicon nitride, polysilicon, tungsten, low-k dielectricsMetals such as aluminum, titanium, titanium nitride, tantalum and copper seed layers
Step coverageGood, especially for LPCVD; ALD is even betterLine-of-sight deposition, so deep trenches and holes are covered poorly (improved by ionized PVD)
TemperatureHigher: LPCVD at about 600 to 800 °C, plasma CVD at about 200 to 400 °CLower: from room temperature to a few hundred degrees
Film purityCan include hydrogen, carbon or chlorine left over from the precursorsHigh, set by the purity of the target
EnvironmentAtmospheric to low pressure, with reactive gasesHigh vacuum with an inert gas plasma
HazardsToxic, flammable or corrosive gases (silane, WF6) need careful handling and abatementFewer hazardous chemicals, but high vacuum and plasma systems
Typical usesInterlayer dielectrics, spacers, polysilicon gates, tungsten plugsBarrier and seed layers for copper wiring, aluminum pads, contact metals

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

CVD
When you need an insulating film, or a film that must coat steep features evenly.
PVD
When you need a pure metal film at low temperature, for example the seed layer for copper plating.
Together
A chip uses both: CVD builds the insulating layers, and PVD deposits the thin metal liners inside the openings before they are filled.
