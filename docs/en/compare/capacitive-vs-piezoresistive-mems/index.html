Capacitive vs Piezoresistive MEMS Sensors: Key Differences

MEMS accelerometers and pressure sensors convert how much a proof mass or diaphragm moves or bends under force into an electrical signal. Capacitive sensors measure displacement from the change in capacitance between movable and fixed electrodes. Power consumption is low and temperature has little effect, but the change is tiny, so a dedicated high-sensitivity circuit is needed. Piezoresistive sensors use the effect that silicon's resistivity changes under stress and read it with a bridge circuit. The circuit is simple and the output is easy to handle, but current flows continuously, so power consumption is higher, and compensation for temperature-induced changes is essential.

When to use which

Capacitive
When you want low power consumption in battery-powered devices, stability in environments with large temperature changes, or high-resolution measurement of small changes.
Piezoresistive
When you want a simple circuit, value output linearity and ease of use, or need to measure high pressures or shocks.
When choosing
The performance of a real product depends heavily not only on the sensor structure but also on the accompanying circuit, temperature compensation and package. Compare power consumption, measurement range, accuracy and temperature range in the datasheets.

FAQ

Are piezoresistive and piezoelectric the same?

No. The piezoresistive effect is a change in resistivity caused by stress, measured by passing a current and detecting the resistance change. The piezoelectric effect is the generation of charge in a material under stress. Under a sustained constant force, the charge in a piezoelectric sensor leaks away, so it has difficulty measuring constant accelerations such as gravity, whereas a piezoresistive sensor can measure them.

Why are smartphone accelerometers capacitive?

Because they consume little power, are little affected by temperature and are easy to put in a small package together with the circuit chip. The capacitance change is tiny, but it is read with differential electrodes and a high-sensitivity circuit.

Why do piezoresistive sensors need temperature compensation?

Both the resistance of silicon and the size of the resistance change per unit stress (sensitivity) vary with temperature. Connecting four resistors in a bridge cancels part of the effect, and the rest is corrected with compensation circuits or calibration.

Check your understanding

What is the principle by which a capacitive MEMS accelerometer detects acceleration?

  1. AChange in resistance due to temperature change
  2. BChange in capacitance between electrodes caused by displacement of a movable mass
  3. CChange in optical interference fringes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Change in capacitance between electrodes caused by displacement of a movable mass

Under acceleration, a spring-suspended proof mass moves by inertia, changing its gap to comb-shaped fixed electrodes; the resulting capacitance change is measured to determine acceleration. Displacements are nanometers or less and capacitance changes below a femtofarad, read with differential electrodes and sensitive circuits. Simple, low-power and temperature-stable, capacitive accelerometers are ubiquitous in smartphones.

Which correctly describes the piezoresistive effect used in semiconductor pressure sensors?

  1. AThe electrical resistivity of a material changes when mechanical stress is applied
  2. BA voltage appears when light shines on it
  3. CThe direction of current is bent by a magnetic field
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The electrical resistivity of a material changes when mechanical stress is applied

Stress alters silicon's band structure, changing its resistivity tens of times more than a metal strain gauge (the piezoresistive effect). Four resistors diffused at the edges of a thin diaphragm that flexes under pressure form a Wheatstone bridge, converting pressure into an electrical signal. Such sensors measure engine and tire pressure, blood pressure and barometric pressure, and temperature compensation is key to their design.

What is the typical way a MEMS microphone converts sound into an electrical signal?

  1. ADetecting the temperature change of a resistor heated by sound
  2. BDetecting the light output of an element that glows with sound
  3. CDetecting the change in capacitance between a membrane vibrated by sound pressure and a fixed electrode
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Detecting the change in capacitance between a membrane vibrated by sound pressure and a fixed electrode

A typical MEMS microphone forms a capacitor from a diaphragm about 1 µm thick that vibrates with sound pressure and a perforated fixed backplate; an ASIC in the same package converts the capacitance change into an electrical signal. Smaller than electret condenser microphones, tolerant of solder reflow and highly uniform, they are used in multiples in smartphones for features such as noise cancellation.

Key terms explained

Capacitive sensing
A method that detects displacement, pressure and the like from changes in capacitance between movable and fixed electrodes. It is widely used in MEMS sensors.
Piezoresistive effect
The change in a material's electrical resistivity under mechanical stress. It is especially large in silicon and is used in semiconductor pressure sensors and similar devices.
Accelerometer
A sensor that detects acceleration from the movement of a small spring-supported mass. It is used for detecting smartphone orientation, in airbags and elsewhere.
MEMS
Micro-electro-mechanical systems. Devices that integrate tiny mechanical structures and electronic circuits using semiconductor microfabrication.
