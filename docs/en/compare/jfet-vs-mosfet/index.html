JFET vs MOSFET: Differences Explained

Both are field-effect transistors in which a gate voltage controls the current between source and drain. In a MOSFET the gate is insulated by an oxide layer. In a JFET the gate is a reverse-biased PN junction that squeezes the channel. This makes a JFET normally on and gives it very low noise, while the insulated gate of the MOSFET draws almost no current and makes it far easier to integrate by the billions.

AspectJFETMOSFET
Gate structureA PN junction, which must stay reverse-biasedA metal gate separated from the channel by a thin oxide
Default stateNormally on (depletion mode): the gate voltage pinches the channel offUsually normally off (enhancement mode); depletion types also exist
Gate currentSmall reverse leakage; it rises sharply if the gate is forward-biasedAlmost zero in steady state, because of the insulator
Input impedanceHighVery high
NoiseLow noise, especially at low frequencyHigher low-frequency (1/f) noise
ESD robustnessBetter, because there is no thin gate oxide to break downSensitive: the gate oxide can be destroyed by static discharge
IntegrationRarely used for large-scale integrationThe basis of CMOS: billions of transistors per chip
Typical usesLow-noise preamplifiers, op-amp input stages, audio and RF front ends, analog switchesDigital logic, memory, power switching and most modern ICs

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

JFET
When low noise and high input impedance matter in an analog front end, such as a microphone or sensor preamplifier.
MOSFET
For nearly everything else: digital circuits, power switching and anything that must be integrated at scale.

FAQ

Why is a JFET normally on?

Its channel exists with no gate voltage, and a reverse-biased gate junction squeezes it shut. This is depletion-mode behavior.

Which has lower noise, a JFET or a MOSFET?

A JFET, especially at low frequency, which is why it is used in low-noise preamplifier input stages.

Why is a MOSFET more sensitive to static electricity?

Its thin gate oxide can be destroyed by an electrostatic discharge. A JFET has no gate oxide to break down.

Why are MOSFETs used in ICs instead of JFETs?

The insulated gate draws almost no current, and MOSFETs are easy to shrink and to build in complementary pairs (CMOS), so billions fit on one chip.

Check your understanding

What is the condition for an enhancement-mode n-channel MOSFET to turn on (conduct)? (VGS: gate-source voltage, Vth: threshold voltage)

  1. AVGS is 0 V
  2. BVGS is greater than Vth
  3. CVGS is negative
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. VGS is greater than Vth

In an enhancement-mode nMOSFET, a positive gate voltage above Vth draws electrons to the surface of the p-type substrate, forming an inversion layer (channel) so current flows between source and drain. At VGS = 0 V there is no channel and the device is off (normally off), which suits logic and makes power circuits fail safe. Vth is typically 0.2–0.4 V in advanced logic and about 1–4 V in power devices.

Which is a characteristic of a depletion-mode MOSFET?

  1. AIt has no gate
  2. BA channel exists even at a gate voltage of 0 V, so current flows
  3. CNo current flows even when the gate voltage is above the threshold voltage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A channel exists even at a gate voltage of 0 V, so current flows

A depletion-mode MOSFET has a built-in channel and conducts even at 0 V gate voltage (normally on). For an n-channel device, a negative gate voltage removes the channel and stops the current. Early NMOS logic used them as load devices. Because they conduct when power is lost, they require safety considerations; GaN HEMTs are also inherently normally on, so special structures are used to make them normally off.

What circuit advantage results from almost no current flowing into the gate of a MOSFET?

  1. AA large DC current is needed to drive the gate
  2. BSwitching speed no longer depends on gate capacitance
  3. CThe input current is nearly zero, so almost no DC power is needed to hold a state
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The input current is nearly zero, so almost no DC power is needed to hold a state

Because a MOSFET's gate is insulated, essentially no DC input current is needed to hold its state. One output can therefore drive many gates, and CMOS circuits draw very little power when idle. This insulated gate is also what lets DRAM and flash retain charge. Charging and discharging gate capacitance still draws current at every transition, so drive power rises with frequency.
