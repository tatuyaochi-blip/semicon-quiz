Fan-In vs Fan-Out WLP: Key Differences Explained

Both are packages in which the redistribution layer (RDL) and solder balls are formed while still in wafer form, and then the wafer is diced. Fan-in WLP is built directly on the original wafer, so terminals can be placed only within the die area and the package is almost the same size as the die. Fan-out WLP places known-good dies cut from the wafer at intervals and embeds them in resin to make a reconstituted wafer, then extends the redistribution beyond the edges of each die. It allows many terminals and can combine multiple dies, but adds process steps, and warpage and die position shift become issues.

When to use which

Fan-in WLP
When you want to package a small die with few terminals as small and cheaply as possible. Power ICs, sensors and RF components for mobile devices.
Fan-out WLP
When there are many terminals relative to the die area, when you want a thin package, or when you want to combine multiple dies into one.
How to tell
If the terminals fit within the die area, use fan-in; if they do not fit and must be spread outward, use fan-out.

FAQ

What do "in" and "out" mean in fan-in and fan-out?

They refer to the area over which redistribution routes the terminals. Fan-in arranges terminals only within (in) the die area, while fan-out extends the wiring to the resin outside (out) the die and arranges terminals there.

What is a reconstituted wafer?

A wafer-like structure rebuilt for making fan-out packages by placing known-good dies at intervals on a carrier and embedding them in mold resin. Because a resin region surrounds each die, redistribution can be extended into it.

Why is warpage a problem in fan-out WLP?

Silicon, mold resin and the redistribution-layer dielectric have very different coefficients of thermal expansion. Each time the resin is cured or heated, the difference in expansion and contraction causes warpage, making exposure, handling and solder joining difficult.

Why aren't large dies made as fan-in WLP?

The package has no substrate, and the silicon is soldered directly to the printed circuit board, so the larger the die, the greater the strain on the joints from the thermal-expansion mismatch. Also, the terminals may no longer fit within the die area.

Check your understanding

Which correctly describes a feature of wafer-level packaging (WLP)?

  1. AChips are cut out one by one before the package is assembled
  2. BChips are shaped by melting them in resin
  3. CRedistribution and external terminals are formed while still in wafer form, and the wafer is then singulated
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Redistribution and external terminals are formed while still in wafer form, and the wafer is then singulated

In WLP, redistribution layers and solder balls are formed across the whole wafer before dicing, then the wafer is singulated into chips. In fan-in WLP the terminals fit within the die; in fan-out WLP the dies are embedded in resin to form a reconstituted wafer so wiring can extend beyond the die edge. Fan-out needs no substrate and is thin, so it is used even for smartphone processors.

Which correctly describes a feature of fan-out wafer-level packaging (FOWLP)?

  1. ARedistribution layers extend beyond the chip edges, so external terminals can be placed over an area larger than the chip
  2. BConnections are made only by wire bonding
  3. CThe package is always smaller than the chip
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Redistribution layers extend beyond the chip edges, so external terminals can be placed over an area larger than the chip

FOWLP embeds singulated dies in mold compound to form a reconstituted wafer, then builds redistribution layers that extend beyond the die edges. Terminals can spread over an area larger than the die, so even small dies get many terminals, and without a package substrate the result is thin. It is used for smartphone processors and similar chips. Connections use redistribution layers rather than wires, and the package is larger than the die.

In fan-out wafer-level packaging, what is the wafer-like form called that is rebuilt by placing diced chips with spacing on a support and embedding them in resin?

  1. ASOI wafer
  2. BEpitaxial wafer
  3. CReconstituted wafer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Reconstituted wafer

In fan-out packaging, tested known-good dies are placed with spacing on a carrier and surrounded with mold compound to rebuild a wafer shape, called a reconstituted wafer. Redistribution layers can extend over the mold compound beyond the die edges, so terminals can cover an area larger than the die, and using only good dies avoids wasting yield. SOI and epitaxial wafers are front-end substrates.

Key terms explained

WLP (wafer-level package)
A package in which redistribution wiring and external terminals are formed while the chips are still in wafer form, before dicing. The result is a small package about the size of the chip.
Redistribution layer (RDL)
A wiring layer formed on a chip or wafer surface to route pads out to the positions of the external terminals. It is used in fan-out packages and interposers.
CSP (chip-scale package)
A general term for packages shrunk to nearly the size of the chip. They are widely used in compact devices such as smartphones.
KGD (known good die)
A chip confirmed to be good before packaging. It matters when several chips go into one package, because a single bad chip ruins the whole module.
