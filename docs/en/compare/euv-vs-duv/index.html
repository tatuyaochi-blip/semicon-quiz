EUV vs DUV Lithography: What Is the Difference?

Lithography prints circuit patterns on the wafer with light. DUV (deep ultraviolet) lithography uses 193 nm light from an ArF laser, with lenses and a layer of water between the lens and wafer (immersion). EUV (extreme ultraviolet) lithography uses 13.5 nm light, so it can print far finer patterns in a single exposure, but the light is absorbed by almost everything, so the whole system must be built from mirrors in a vacuum. EUV is far more expensive and complex.

AspectDUV (ArF immersion)EUV
Wavelength193 nm13.5 nm
Light sourceArF excimer laserLaser-produced plasma: a laser hits tiny droplets of molten tin
OpticsLenses (refractive), with water between the last lens and the wafer in immersion systemsOnly mirrors (reflective multilayers), because almost every material absorbs EUV light
PhotomaskTransmissive mask made of quartzReflective mask with a multilayer coating
EnvironmentAir or nitrogen, plus the immersion waterVacuum
ResolutionSingle exposure resolves pitches down to about 80 nm; finer patterns need multiple patterningSingle exposure can print pitches around 30 nm or below; High-NA EUV goes further
Process complexityMultiple patterning (such as SADP, SAQP) multiplies the number of stepsFewer steps for the finest layers, but new challenges in resists and masks
Main defectsOverlay errors and defects from multiple patterningStochastic defects caused by the small number of photons, and mask defects
Tool costTens of millions of dollars per toolWell over a hundred million dollars per tool
SuppliersASML, Nikon and othersASML is effectively the only supplier
Typical useMost layers of most chips, including advanced onesThe most critical, finest layers at roughly the 7 nm class and below

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

DUV
Still prints the large majority of layers, even in leading-edge chips. It is mature, fast and much cheaper per layer.
EUV
Used for the finest critical layers, where DUV would need several patterning steps. It simplifies the process but the tools and operating costs are very high.
Next
High-NA EUV uses a larger numerical aperture to resolve even smaller features for the next nodes.
