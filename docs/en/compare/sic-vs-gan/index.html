SiC vs GaN Power Devices: Key Differences

Silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride (GaN) are both wide-bandgap semiconductors that beat silicon in power conversion: they withstand higher fields, switch faster and lose less energy. The practical split is voltage. SiC is the choice for high-voltage, high-power systems such as electric-vehicle inverters, while GaN excels at lower voltages and very high switching frequencies, such as compact fast chargers.

AspectSiCGaN
Band gapAbout 3.3 eV (4H-SiC), compared with 1.12 eV for siliconAbout 3.4 eV
Critical electric fieldAbout ten times that of siliconAbout ten times that of silicon
Thermal conductivityHigh, roughly three times silicon, which makes cooling easierSimilar to silicon, lower than SiC
Typical deviceVertical MOSFETs and Schottky diodesLateral HEMTs that use a two-dimensional electron gas as the channel
Typical voltage rangeAround 650 V to 3.3 kV, and higherMostly up to about 650 V
Switching frequencyHigh (tens to hundreds of kHz)Very high (hundreds of kHz up to MHz)
Substrate and wafersSiC wafers are expensive and difficult to growUsually grown on silicon wafers (GaN-on-Si), which is cheaper and works in silicon fabs; GaN-on-SiC is used for RF
Design challengesGate oxide reliability and the need for higher gate drive voltageNormally-on behavior must be avoided, and the gate tolerates only a small voltage swing
Typical usesEV traction inverters, on-board chargers, solar inverters, industrial motor drives, railwaysCompact fast chargers and adapters, data-center power supplies, 5G RF amplifiers

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

SiC
When voltage and power are high and efficiency and heat matter: vehicle inverters, solar and industrial power systems.
GaN
When voltage is moderate and you want small, light, very efficient converters at high switching frequency: chargers, adapters, server power.
Either
Both need care at high switching speed, because fast edges cause ringing and noise. Layout and gate driving matter as much as the device.
