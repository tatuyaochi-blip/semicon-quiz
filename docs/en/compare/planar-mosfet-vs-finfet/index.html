Planar MOSFET vs FinFET: Why the Industry Switched

A planar MOSFET has its gate on top of a flat channel, so the gate controls the channel from only one side. As gates became shorter than about 20 nm, the drain began to influence the channel too much, and leakage and short-channel effects grew. A FinFET raises the channel into a thin vertical fin and wraps the gate over three sides, which gives much tighter control. That is why logic moved from planar transistors to FinFETs starting at the 22 nm and 16/14 nm nodes.

AspectPlanar MOSFETFinFET
StructureThe gate lies on top of a flat channelThe gate wraps a thin vertical silicon fin on three sides
Gate controlFrom one side only: weaker at short gate lengthsFrom three sides: much stronger
Short-channel effects (such as DIBL)Severe at small dimensionsStrongly suppressed
Leakage currentHigher in scaled devicesLower
Subthreshold swingDegrades as the device shrinksClose to the ideal of about 60 mV per decade
Drive current per footprintLowerHigher: the channel width includes both fin sides and the top
Channel dopingHeavy, which causes random dopant fluctuationLight or undoped, so less variation
Width adjustmentContinuous: any width can be drawnIn steps: set by the number of fins
ManufacturingSimpler and cheaperMore complex: fin patterning and 3D gate formation
Where it is used todayMature nodes (28 nm and larger): analog, microcontrollers, RF, IoTAdvanced logic from 16/14 nm down to about 3 nm

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

Planar
When cost matters more than the last bit of speed, and the design does not need the smallest nodes: analog, mixed-signal and many microcontrollers.
FinFET
When performance and low leakage at small dimensions are essential: smartphones, PCs and data-center processors.
