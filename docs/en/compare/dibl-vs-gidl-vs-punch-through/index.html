DIBL vs GIDL vs Punch-Through: Where They Occur, Causes, Id-Vg Signatures and Countermeasures

DIBL, GIDL and punch-through all concern leakage current that flows when a transistor should be off, but they occur in different places and for different reasons. DIBL (drain-induced barrier lowering) happens when the gate is short enough for the drain field to reach the source side and lower the potential barrier between source and channel, so the threshold voltage drops as the drain voltage rises. Punch-through happens when the source and drain depletion regions merge and current flows deep in the substrate, out of the gate's reach; it can be seen as an extreme form of DIBL. GIDL (gate-induced drain leakage) is band-to-band tunneling current driven by the strong field where the gate overlaps the drain, and it depends more on the gate-to-drain voltage than on how short the gate is.

When to use which

DIBL
A metric for how much the threshold voltage and off current of a short-gate transistor change with drain voltage. Extract the threshold voltage at a low and a high drain voltage and divide the difference by the difference in drain voltage.
GIDL
Suspect it when driving the gate harder into the off state (a negative gate for nMOS) does not reduce leakage, or even increases it. It is especially important for DRAM data retention and for designs that bias the gate negative to cut standby leakage.
Punch-through
The extreme state of short-channel effects, where current flows with no gate control at all. Treat it as the limit that sets the minimum gate length and the maximum drain voltage of a transistor.

FAQ

What is the difference between DIBL and punch-through?

In both, the drain voltage lowers the barrier on the source side. In DIBL, the barrier near the channel surface drops and the threshold voltage falls, but the gate still controls the current. In punch-through, the source and drain depletion regions merge deep in the substrate and current flows along a path the gate cannot reach, so the transistor cannot be turned off. It helps to think of punch-through as DIBL taken to the extreme.

How can I tell DIBL from GIDL in an Id-Vg curve?

DIBL appears as a leftward shift of the subthreshold curve when the drain voltage is raised, and the current still falls as the gate voltage is lowered. GIDL appears as current that starts rising again when the gate voltage is pushed well below threshold. In short, DIBL shows up as a threshold-voltage shift, and GIDL as an upturn in current on the low gate-voltage side.

Why do halo (pocket) implants suppress short-channel effects?

Locally raising the doping near the source and drain edges with dopants of the same type as the channel keeps the drain depletion region from spreading into the channel. That suppresses DIBL, threshold-voltage roll-off and punch-through, with less mobility loss than raising the doping of the whole channel. Because doping near the junction goes up, junction leakage and junction capacitance can increase.

Do these effects disappear in FinFETs and GAA transistors?

They do not disappear, but DIBL and punch-through are greatly reduced. The gate surrounds a thin channel from several sides, so the drain field has less influence and there are fewer deep current paths out of the gate's reach. Bulk FinFETs, whose fins connect to the substrate, usually get a heavily doped punch-through stopper implanted at the base of the fin. GIDL is set by the field at the drain edge, so it still has to be managed through junction design and bias conditions even with new structures.

Which correctly describes DIBL (drain-induced barrier lowering)?

  1. AThe gate insulator breaks down over time
  2. BMetal atoms in wiring migrate due to current
  3. CThe higher the drain voltage, the lower the potential barrier at the source, lowering the threshold voltage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The higher the drain voltage, the lower the potential barrier at the source, lowering the threshold voltage

With a short gate, the drain's field reaches toward the source and lowers the source-channel potential barrier. Threshold voltage therefore falls as drain voltage rises, increasing off-state leakage. DIBL is quoted as threshold shift per volt of drain bias (mV/V), kept to a few tens of mV/V or less in advanced transistors. In amplifiers it also lowers output resistance.

Which phenomenon increases off-state leakage because band-to-band tunneling current flows due to the strong field where the gate overlaps the drain?

  1. ADIBL
  2. BNBTI
  3. CGIDL (gate-induced drain leakage)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. GIDL (gate-induced drain leakage)

Even when a transistor is off, a large gate-to-drain voltage bends the bands sharply near the drain edge of the gate, and band-to-band tunneling causes current to flow—GIDL, which in an nMOS notably grows as the gate is driven more negative. It is a problem in devices requiring ultra-low leakage such as DRAM cell transistors and is controlled by engineering the drain-edge doping and field. DIBL is threshold lowering by drain voltage, and NBTI is a pMOS degradation mechanism.

Which correctly describes punch-through in a MOSFET?

  1. AA drop in channel mobility with temperature
  2. BThe source and drain depletion layers merge and current flows that the gate cannot control
  3. CDielectric breakdown of the gate insulator
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The source and drain depletion layers merge and current flows that the gate cannot control

With a short channel and light substrate doping, the drain's depletion region can merge with the source's under drain bias, so current flows deep in the substrate independent of the gate and the device cannot turn off—punch-through. Remedies include heavily doped halo (pocket) implants beneath the source/drain, shallower junctions, and, as in FinFETs and GAA devices, wrapping the gate around the channel to eliminate uncontrolled paths.

Practice MOSFET questions

Key terms explained

DIBL
Short for drain-induced barrier lowering. In short-channel MOSFETs, a higher drain voltage lowers the potential barrier at the source, reducing the threshold voltage and increasing off-state leakage.
GIDL (gate-induced drain leakage)
Off-state leakage caused by band-to-band tunneling in the strong field where the gate overlaps the drain. It is a concern for data retention in DRAM, among others.
Punch-through
A condition in which the source and drain depletion regions merge and current flows that the gate cannot control. It occurs when the channel is too short or the substrate doping is too low.
Short-channel effect
Phenomena in which, as gate length shrinks, the drain's electric field lowers the threshold voltage and increases off-state leakage current.
Halo implant (pocket implant)
Angled implantation of dopant of the same type as the channel near the source and drain edges. It limits the spread of the drain depletion region and suppresses short-channel effects.
LDD structure
Short for lightly doped drain. The drain is made lightly doped only at its edge next to the gate, relaxing the electric field and suppressing hot-carrier degradation.
Threshold voltage
The gate voltage at which a channel (inversion layer) forms in a MOSFET and current starts to flow. Written as Vth.
FinFET
A 3D transistor whose channel stands up like a fin and is surrounded by the gate on three sides. It suppresses short-channel effects well.
