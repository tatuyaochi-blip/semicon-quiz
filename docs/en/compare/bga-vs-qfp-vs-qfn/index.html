BGA vs QFP vs QFN: IC Package Types Compared

These are three common surface-mount IC packages that differ in where the connections are. A QFP has gull-wing leads sticking out of all four sides. A QFN has flat pads on the underside edges with no leads. A BGA has a grid of solder balls across the whole underside. BGAs give the most connections in the smallest area, QFNs are compact with excellent heat dissipation, and QFPs are the easiest to inspect and solder by hand.

AspectBGAQFPQFN
TerminalsA grid of solder balls on the bottomGull-wing leads on all four sidesFlat pads on the bottom edges, plus an exposed thermal pad in the center

Typical pitchAbout 0.4 to 1.27 mmAbout 0.4 to 0.8 mmAbout 0.4 to 0.65 mm
SizeCompact for the pin countLargest: the leads extend beyond the bodySmall and thin
Electrical performanceShort paths, low inductanceLonger leads, higher inductanceShort paths, low inductance

Inspection and reworkJoints are hidden, so X-ray inspection is needed and rework is hardEasy: leads are visible and can be hand-solderedHarder: joints are partly hidden under the package
Narrow pitch problemNeeds fine vias and many board layersLeads can bend or bridge when the pitch is very narrowModerate
Typical usesCPUs, GPUs, FPGAs, SoCs and memoriesMicrocontrollers and older ASICsMicrocontrollers, RF, power management and sensors

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

BGA
When you need a lot of pins in a small area with good electrical performance, as in processors and memories.
QFP
When ease of assembly, inspection and prototyping matter more than size.
QFN
When you need a small, thin package with good heat dissipation and moderate pin count.
