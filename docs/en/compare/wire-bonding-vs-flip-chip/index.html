Wire Bonding vs Flip Chip: Chip Interconnect Compared

These are the two main ways to connect a chip to its package. In wire bonding, the chip sits face up and thin metal wires join its edge pads to the package. In flip chip, the chip is turned face down and solder or copper bumps on its surface connect directly to the substrate. Wire bonding is cheap and flexible; flip chip allows many more connections, shorter paths and better electrical performance.

AspectWire bondingFlip chip
ConnectionThin wires (gold, copper or aluminum, roughly 15 to 50 µm) welded from chip pads to the packageBumps of solder or copper on the chip surface joined directly to the substrate
Chip orientationFace upFace down
I/O location and countPads on the chip edge only; hundreds up to about a thousand connectionsBumps across the whole surface (area array); thousands of connections
Electrical performanceLong wires add inductance and resistance, limiting speed and power deliveryShort paths give low inductance and better signal and power integrity
Thermal pathHeat leaves mainly through the back of the chip into the die attachThe back of the chip can touch a heat spreader or heat sink directly
ProcessEach wire is bonded one after another, which is slowerAll bumps are joined at once by reflow, and underfill resin is added to relieve stress
Package sizeLarger: space is needed around the chip for the wiresSmaller
CostLow: mature, flexible and needs no bumping of the chipHigher: bumping, a finer substrate and underfill are needed
Typical usesMemories, microcontrollers, analog chips, LEDs and low-cost productsCPUs, GPUs, SoCs and other high-speed, high-pin-count chips

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

Wire bonding
When the pin count is modest and cost matters most: most microcontrollers, memories and analog parts.
Flip chip
When you need thousands of connections, high speed or high power: processors and high-performance chips.
Note
Flip-chip bumps are also the starting point of advanced packaging, where much smaller micro-bumps join stacked chips.

FAQ

Why does flip chip allow more connections?

Bumps can be placed across the whole chip surface (an area array), while wire bonding uses only pads at the chip edge, so flip chip can offer thousands of connections.

Is wire bonding obsolete?

No. It is cheap, flexible and mature, and it is still used for most microcontrollers, memories and analog chips.

What is underfill?

A resin filled between a flip chip and its substrate to relieve the stress on the bump joints caused by different thermal expansion.

Which has better electrical performance?

Flip chip, because its connections are short and have low inductance. Long bond wires limit speed and power delivery.

Check your understanding

Which correctly describes wire bonding?

  1. AA method that connects the chip's bond pads to a lead frame or substrate with fine metal wires
  2. BA method that flips the chip and connects it directly to the substrate with bumps
  3. CA method that encapsulates the chip in resin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A method that connects the chip's bond pads to a lead frame or substrate with fine metal wires

Wire bonding connects chip pads to lead-frame or substrate terminals with fine wires of gold, copper or aluminum. Metals are joined by applying pressure and ultrasound while heating to about 150–200 °C. Because the equipment is inexpensive and routing is flexible, it remains the most widely used interconnect by unit volume. Flipping the chip and connecting through bumps is flip-chip, and encasing in resin is molding (encapsulation).

Which correctly describes flip-chip mounting?

  1. AA method that ships chips without encapsulating them in resin
  2. BA method that places the chip circuit side down and connects it directly to the substrate with bumps
  3. CA method that mounts the chip standing vertically on the substrate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A method that places the chip circuit side down and connects it directly to the substrate with bumps

In flip-chip packaging, the chip is turned face down and connected to the substrate through solder or copper-pillar bumps formed on its circuit side. The short connections have low inductance, benefiting high-speed signals and power delivery, and bumps can cover the whole die, enabling thousands of terminals. It originated with IBM's C4 technology in the 1960s. It has nothing to do with shipping unencapsulated chips or mounting them vertically.

In flip-chip mounting, what is the main purpose of filling the gap between chip and substrate with underfill resin?

  1. ATo keep stress from thermal expansion mismatch between chip and substrate from concentrating on the bumps, improving connection reliability
  2. BTo make the chip emit light
  3. CTo block heat dissipation from the chip
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. To keep stress from thermal expansion mismatch between chip and substrate from concentrating on the bumps, improving connection reliability

Silicon expands at about 3 ppm/K while organic substrates expand at about 15–17 ppm/K, so each temperature cycle repeatedly strains the small bumps, risking fatigue cracks. Filling the chip–substrate gap with underfill bonds them together, spreading stress across all bumps and greatly extending joint life. Underfill is usually dispensed at the edge and drawn in by capillary action.

Key terms explained

Wire bonding
A method of connecting the chip's bond pads to a lead frame or substrate with thin wires of gold, copper or similar metals.
Flip chip
A mounting method in which the chip is placed face down and connected directly to the substrate with bumps. Short connections make it suitable for high pin counts.
BGA
Ball grid array. A high-pin-count package with solder balls arranged in a grid on its underside.
Microbump
A bump far smaller than an ordinary solder ball, used to connect TSV-stacked chips or chips to an interposer. The pitch is a few tens of micrometers.
