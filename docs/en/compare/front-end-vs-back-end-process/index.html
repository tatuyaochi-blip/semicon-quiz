Front-End vs Back-End Semiconductor Process: Steps, Equipment and Companies Compared

Semiconductor manufacturing is broadly divided into the front end and the back end. The front end builds transistors and wiring on a silicon wafer by repeating steps such as deposition, lithography, etching and ion implantation. The back end cuts the finished wafer into individual chips, assembles them into packages that connect to the outside world, and tests them for shipment. With advanced packaging such as chiplets, the steps that sit between the two are becoming more important.

When to use which

Front end
This is what determines a chip's performance, power consumption and density. It is the center of the race to scale down, and investment in equipment such as lithography tools is large.
Back end
This protects the chip, removes heat and connects it to the board. The package type and connection method affect product size, heat dissipation and signal speed.
Middle end
Steps that apply near-front-end techniques to packaging while still at the wafer stage, such as TSVs, fine redistribution layers and bump formation, are sometimes called the middle end. They are becoming important for chiplets and stacked memory.

FAQ

Where is the boundary between the front end and the back end?

Generally, everything up to finishing the circuit on the wafer is front end, and everything from cutting the wafer into chips and assembling packages onward is back end. Whether the per-chip electrical test (wafer test) belongs to one or the other differs between companies and sources.

Why does the front end take longer?

Because steps such as deposition, lithography and etching are repeated dozens of times, once for each transistor and wiring layer. Leading-edge logic chips go through more than a thousand steps, and it takes 3 months or more from wafer start to the end of the front end.

Why is the back end getting so much attention?

Because scaling alone can no longer keep raising performance, so methods that boost performance by placing or stacking multiple chips densely in one package, such as chiplets and 3D integration, are spreading. As with HBM, where memory is stacked right next to the processor, back-end technology increasingly decides product performance.

What is an OSAT?

OSAT stands for Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test: companies that take on back-end assembly and testing under contract. They receive finished wafers from fabless companies and foundries, then package and test them.

Check your understanding

Which correctly distinguishes the 'front end' and 'back end' of semiconductor manufacturing?

  1. AThe front end is design, and the back end is manufacturing
  2. BThe front end builds circuits on the wafer, and the back end cuts out chips and assembles and tests them
  3. CThe front end is testing, and the back end is wafer manufacturing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The front end builds circuits on the wafer, and the back end cuts out chips and assembles and tests them

The front end builds circuits on the wafer by repeating deposition, lithography, etching, ion implantation and other steps. The back end packages chips diced from the wafer, tests them and ships them. Design is a separate stage before manufacturing. As advanced packaging that performs redistribution or die stacking at the wafer level grows in importance, the boundary between the two is blurring.

For leading-edge logic chips, which is closest to the typical time from starting a wafer on the production line to completing the front-end process?

  1. AAbout one day
  2. BSeveral months (roughly three months or more)
  3. CAbout ten years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Several months (roughly three months or more)

Leading-edge logic involves over a thousand steps of lithography, deposition, etching and more, so the front end alone takes three months or longer. Back-end assembly and testing add time, and building a new fab takes two years or more, so supply cannot respond quickly to demand spikes, one reason for mismatches and shortages. One day is far too short to complete so many steps, and ten years is far too long.

Which is an appropriate reason the back end (packaging) has become more important in recent years?

  1. ABecause the back end is no longer needed
  2. BBecause package cost has fallen to almost zero
  3. CBecause chiplets and advanced packaging have become important means of raising system performance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Because chiplets and advanced packaging have become important means of raising system performance

As scaling alone struggles to raise performance, advanced packaging that densely interconnects multiple chips has become a way to boost performance and power efficiency. For AI chips, capacity for 2.5D packaging, which places HBM beside the processor and links them through a silicon interposer, now constrains product supply. The back end has neither become unnecessary nor free.

Key terms explained

Dicing
Cutting a wafer into individual chips (dies). Besides sawing with a rotating blade, there are methods such as stealth dicing, which forms a modified layer inside the wafer with a laser.
Backgrinding
Grinding the back of a wafer before dicing to thin it for packaging. Chips for stacking are thinned to a few tens of micrometers.
Wire bonding
A method of connecting the chip's bond pads to a lead frame or substrate with thin wires of gold, copper or similar metals.
Wafer test (probe test)
Checking the electrical characteristics of each chip on the wafer by touching probes to its pads before dicing. It finds bad chips before assembly so they are not packaged needlessly.
OSAT
A company that provides back-end services such as semiconductor assembly and testing under contract.
