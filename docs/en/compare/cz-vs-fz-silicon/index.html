CZ vs FZ Silicon: Czochralski and Float-Zone Crystal Growth

Almost all silicon wafers start as a single crystal grown by one of two methods. In the Czochralski (CZ) method, a seed crystal is dipped into molten silicon in a quartz crucible and slowly pulled up. In the float-zone (FZ) method, a polysilicon rod is melted in a narrow zone by a coil and the zone is moved along the rod, so no crucible touches the silicon. CZ is cheaper and makes large wafers; FZ gives purer silicon.

AspectCZ methodFZ method
How the crystal is grownA seed is dipped into silicon melted in a quartz crucible and pulled up while rotatingA radio-frequency coil melts a narrow zone of a polysilicon rod, and the zone is moved along it
Contact with a crucibleYes, so oxygen from the quartz dissolves into the meltNo, so there is very little oxygen
Oxygen contentHighVery low
Purity and resistivityLower purity; resistivity limited (the MCZ variant with a magnetic field helps)Very high purity and very high resistivity
DopingDopant is added to the melt; concentration varies along the ingotGas doping, or neutron transmutation doping (NTD) for uniform phosphorus
Wafer diameterUp to 300 mm in productionTypically up to about 200 mm; larger sizes are difficult
CostLower, and large ingots give good throughputHigher
Mechanical strength and getteringOxygen precipitates add strength and trap metal impurities (intrinsic gettering)Lacks these benefits, so wafers can be more fragile
Typical usesThe large majority of wafers: logic, memory and solar cellsHigh-voltage power devices (such as IGBTs), detectors, high-resistivity RF devices

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

CZ
The default for mainstream chips. It scales to the largest wafer sizes and is the most economical.
FZ
When high purity and very high resistivity matter more than cost or wafer size, mainly for power devices and sensors.
