BJT vs MOSFET: Differences Explained

A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is controlled by a base current, while a MOSFET is controlled by the voltage on an insulated gate. That one difference explains most of the rest: MOSFETs draw almost no steady input current and are easy to integrate by the billions, which is why they dominate digital chips, while BJTs offer high transconductance and low noise, which keeps them useful in analog and RF circuits.

AspectBJTMOSFET
ControlCurrent-controlled: base current sets collector currentVoltage-controlled: gate voltage sets drain current
Input currentA steady base current is needed (collector current divided by the gain)Almost no steady gate current, but the gate capacitance must be charged and discharged at each switching event
CarriersBoth electrons and holes take part (bipolar)Current is carried by one type of carrier in the channel (unipolar)
TransconductanceHigh for a given current (gm = IC / VT, about 38 mS at 1 mA)Lower for the same current (gm = 2 ID / (VGS - Vth))
Switching speedSlowed by minority-carrier storage when driven into saturationFast, because there is no minority-carrier storage
On-stateFixed saturation voltage VCE(sat) of about 0.1 to 0.3 VBehaves like a resistor RDS(on); the loss is I squared times R
Temperature behaviorCurrent rises with temperature, which can cause thermal runaway and makes paralleling harderOn-resistance rises with temperature, so current spreads out and paralleling is easy
NoiseLower low-frequency (1/f) noiseHigher 1/f noise
IntegrationLarger and draws bias current; used in analog, RF and high-speed circuits (SiGe HBT, BiCMOS)Small, nearly zero static power in CMOS; the basis of microprocessors and memories
Typical usesAnalog amplifiers, RF circuits, bandgap references, simple discrete switchesDigital logic, memory, power switching, most modern ICs

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

BJT
When you need high gain per unit current, low noise or a precise voltage reference: analog front ends, audio and RF amplifiers, bandgap references.
MOSFET
When you need a switch or a very large number of devices on one chip: digital logic, memory, power converters, motor drives.
IGBT
The IGBT combines a MOS gate with a bipolar output stage. It is used where voltage and current are high, such as motor drives and inverters.
