Wet vs Dry Etching: Key Differences in Semiconductor Processing

Etching removes material from the wafer to form patterns. Wet etching dissolves the material in a liquid chemical; it is simple, fast and highly selective, but it usually etches sideways as well as down (isotropic). Dry etching uses a plasma of reactive gases in a vacuum chamber, and ions can make it strongly directional (anisotropic), which is essential for cutting narrow, vertical features in modern chips.

AspectWet etchingDry etching
MediumLiquid chemicals, such as HF for oxide, hot phosphoric acid for nitride and KOH for siliconPlasma of reactive gases in a vacuum chamber, as in RIE
DirectionalityUsually isotropic: it also etches sideways and undercuts the mask (KOH on silicon is an exception that follows crystal planes)Can be highly anisotropic, giving near-vertical sidewalls through ion bombardment and sidewall protection
SelectivityUsually very high, because the chemistry is specificLower, and tuned by gas chemistry and plasma conditions
Smallest featuresLimited by undercut; used for larger features and film removalNeeded for nanometer-scale patterns
ThroughputHigh: many wafers can be processed together in a bathLower: usually one wafer at a time
Process controlDepends on bath temperature, concentration and timePrecise, with endpoint detection from the plasma emission
DamageNo plasma damagePossible plasma damage and charging (antenna effect)
Equipment and wasteSimple equipment, but liquid chemical wasteComplex vacuum equipment and exhaust gas treatment, so higher cost
Typical usesWafer cleaning, stripping films (oxide, nitride), bulk silicon etching for MEMSPatterning gates, contacts, trenches and vias in advanced ICs

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

Wet etching
When selectivity matters more than shape, or when you are removing a whole film or cleaning: stripping oxide, removing nitride, MEMS bulk etching.
Dry etching
When the pattern is fine and you need vertical walls: transistor gates, contacts, trenches and vias.
Together
A chip flow uses both. Dry etching defines the fine patterns, and wet steps clean and strip films afterwards.
