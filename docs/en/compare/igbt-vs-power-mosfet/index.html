IGBT vs Power MOSFET: Which to Use for What

Both are voltage-controlled power switches with an insulated gate, but they conduct differently. A power MOSFET carries current with one type of carrier and behaves like a resistor, so it is fast and efficient at low and medium voltage. An IGBT adds a bipolar output stage that floods the device with carriers, which keeps conduction loss low at high voltage and current, but makes turn-off slower. The usual split is MOSFETs below a few hundred volts or at high frequency, and IGBTs at high voltage and power.

AspectPower MOSFETIGBT
StructureUnipolar: majority carriers only, controlled by a MOS gateMOS gate driving a bipolar output stage, so both electrons and holes take part
Typical voltage rangeFrom a few volts up to about 900 V (silicon); SiC MOSFETs reach several kVAbout 600 V up to several kV
On-state lossResistive (RDS(on)); rises steeply as the voltage rating increasesNearly fixed saturation voltage (about 1.5 to 3 V); lower loss at high voltage and high current
Switching speedFast: tens or hundreds of kHz, and higherSlower: usually up to a few tens of kHz
Turn-off tail currentNoneYes: stored minority carriers keep current flowing briefly
Body diodeBuilt inNone: a separate antiparallel diode is added in the package
Parallel operationEasy: on-resistance rises with temperature, so current spreads outPossible, but needs more care
Gate driveVoltage-driven, with gate charge to supply at each switching eventVoltage-driven, about 15 V typically
Typical usesSwitching power supplies, DC-DC converters, chargers, low-voltage motor drivesElectric-vehicle inverters (silicon IGBTs), industrial motor drives, trains, welding, grid equipment

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

Power MOSFET
When switching frequency is high and the voltage is not too high: power supplies, converters, battery-powered motor drives.
IGBT
When voltage and current are high and frequency is moderate: traction inverters, industrial drives, heavy equipment.
Trend
SiC MOSFETs are taking over part of the IGBT range, offering high voltage with fast switching and low loss.
