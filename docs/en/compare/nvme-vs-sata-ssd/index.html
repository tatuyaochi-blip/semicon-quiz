NVMe vs SATA SSD: Speed, Interface and Which to Buy

Both are SSDs with flash memory inside, but they connect to the computer differently. A SATA SSD uses the SATA interface, designed for hard disks, which tops out at about 550 MB/s. An NVMe SSD talks over PCI Express with a protocol built for flash, reaching 3 to 14 GB/s or more with lower latency. For everyday use the difference is small, but for large file transfers and heavy workloads NVMe is far faster.

AspectSATA SSDNVMe SSD
InterfaceSATA III, 6 Gb/sPCI Express, usually x4 lanes
Sequential speedAbout 550 MB/s at mostAbout 3.5 GB/s (PCIe 3.0 x4), 7 GB/s (PCIe 4.0 x4) and 14 GB/s (PCIe 5.0 x4)
ProtocolAHCI, designed for hard disksNVMe, designed for flash memory
Command queuesOne queue of 32 commandsUp to 65,535 queues of up to 65,536 commands each
LatencyHigher (about 100 microseconds)Lower (tens of microseconds)
Form factors2.5-inch drive, and M.2 SATAM.2, U.2 or U.3, and add-in cards
Power and heatLow (a few watts)Higher at peak speed; fast drives often need a heatsink to avoid throttling
CompatibilityWorks in very old PCs and in 2.5-inch baysNeeds an M.2 or PCIe slot and firmware that can boot from NVMe
PriceCheap, and similar to entry-level NVMeSlightly more for the fastest drives; entry-level NVMe is now close to SATA prices
Best forUpgrading older PCs and laptops, bulk storageModern PCs, gaming, video editing, workstations and servers

Figures are typical orders of magnitude and vary by process generation and product.

When to use which

SATA SSD
When your computer has no NVMe slot, or when you just want a cheap, simple upgrade from a hard disk.
NVMe SSD
When you have an M.2 slot, move large files often, or need low latency: it is the default choice for new systems.
Check first
An M.2 slot can be SATA, NVMe or both. Check the manual before you buy, because an M.2 SATA drive and an M.2 NVMe drive look similar.
