Hybrid Bonding vs Microbumps: Key Differences

Both technologies connect chips to chips, or chips to wafers, vertically through many terminals. Microbumps join the chips by melting small amounts of solder, for example on the tips of copper pillars, so spacing is needed to keep solder from squeezing out and bridging to neighbors, and the pitch is typically tens of µm. Hybrid bonding uses no solder; it directly bonds polished flat surfaces of copper electrodes and dielectric, so the pitch can be below 10 µm and the interconnect density can be raised by orders of magnitude. In return, the bonding surfaces must be flat and clean at the nanometer level.

When to use which

Hybrid bonding
When you need a very large number of short, low-power connections between chips and a pitch below 10 µm: for example, stacking pixels on logic or memory on processors.
Microbumps
When a pitch of tens of µm is sufficient and you want to stack chips or mount them on an interposer with a proven process, without strict control of planarization and cleanliness.
Trend
Microbumps are being replaced by hybrid bonding, starting with applications that demand finer pitch and higher bandwidth, but for cost and yield reasons, both are used depending on the application.

FAQ

Why can hybrid bonding achieve a much finer pitch?

Because it uses no solder. Microbumps need spacing so that molten solder does not bridge to the neighboring bump, but hybrid bonding joins flat surfaces in which copper electrodes are embedded in dielectric, so that constraint does not apply. The number of connections is inversely proportional to the square of the pitch, so reducing the pitch to 1/10 increases density 100 times.

Why must the bonding surfaces be so flat and clean?

Because the surfaces are bonded directly to each other, any unevenness or particle lifts the area around it and leaves a cavity (void) that cannot bond. Even a single small particle can prevent bonding over a much larger area, so CMP, cleaning and control of a clean environment are critical.

What does "hybrid" combine?

It refers to bonding dielectric to dielectric and copper electrode to copper electrode simultaneously in the same process. The dielectric surfaces bond first, and during subsequent heating the copper expands, the copper surfaces come into close contact, and they interdiffuse into a single body.

Will microbumps stop being used?

Not soon. Microbumps are a proven process with looser flatness and cleanliness requirements, and many applications need only a pitch of tens of µm. The two are chosen according to the required pitch, bandwidth, cost and yield.

Check your understanding

Which correctly describes hybrid bonding?

  1. AA technology that enlarges solder bumps to improve connection reliability
  2. BA technology that connects using two wires at a time
  3. CA technology that directly bonds surfaces of copper electrodes and dielectric without bumps, enabling extremely dense connections
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A technology that directly bonds surfaces of copper electrodes and dielectric without bumps, enabling extremely dense connections

Hybrid bonding planarizes chip or wafer surfaces by CMP and directly bonds copper pads to copper pads and dielectric to dielectric simultaneously. Without solder bumps, connection pitch can shrink below 10 µm and down to a few micrometers, multiplying connections per square millimeter by orders of magnitude. First commercialized in stacked image sensors, it is spreading to processor–cache stacking and HBM.

What are the bumps called, far smaller than ordinary solder balls, used to connect chips stacked with TSVs and to connect chips to interposers?

  1. AMicrobumps
  2. BBGA solder balls
  3. CLeads
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Microbumps

Microbumps are tiny bumps 10 to several tens of micrometers in diameter at pitches of tens of micrometers, used for the many connections between dies. They join HBM DRAM dies in stacks and chips to interposers, often by thermocompression bonding. BGA solder balls, by contrast, have pitches of about 0.4–1 mm. When even finer pitch is needed, hybrid bonding is used.

Which correctly describes the conditions required of the surfaces to be joined in hybrid bonding?

  1. AThe surfaces should be roughened
  2. BVery high flatness and cleanliness at the nanometer level
  3. CA thick oil film on the surface
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Very high flatness and cleanliness at the nanometer level

Hybrid bonding joins copper pads and dielectric surfaces directly without bumps, so the bonding surfaces must have sub-nanometer roughness and be free of particles and contamination. A single particle of about 100 nm can leave an unbonded void millimeters across. CMP, cleaning and a very clean environment are therefore crucial. Rough surfaces or oil films prevent bonding.

Key terms explained

Hybrid bonding
A technique that directly bonds copper electrodes and dielectric surfaces without bumps, providing huge numbers of connections at extremely fine pitch.
Microbump
A bump far smaller than an ordinary solder ball, used to connect TSV-stacked chips or chips to an interposer. The pitch is a few tens of micrometers.
TSV (through-silicon via)
An electrode that passes through the silicon substrate. It connects stacked chips vertically over a short distance.
CMP
Chemical mechanical polishing. Planarizes the wafer surface by combining the chemical action of a slurry with mechanical polishing by a pad.
HBM
High Bandwidth Memory. Multiple DRAM dies stacked with TSVs, achieving very high bandwidth through an extremely wide bus.
