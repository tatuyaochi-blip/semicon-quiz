FSI vs BSI vs Stacked Image Sensors: Key Differences

CMOS image sensors fall into three structures depending on which side light enters from and where the circuits are placed. Front-side illumination (FSI) brings light in through the front surface, where the wiring layers are, so part of the light is blocked by the wiring. Back-side illumination (BSI) flips the wafer, thins it to a few µm and brings light in through the back, which has no wiring, so even small pixels keep their sensitivity. A stacked sensor bonds a BSI pixel chip to a logic chip that carries the readout and image-processing circuits, making the chip smaller and the readout faster.

When to use which

Front-side illumination (FSI)
When pixels are large and light loss from the wiring is small, or when cost must be kept as low as possible.
Back-side illumination (BSI)
When you need to keep sensitivity even as pixels shrink to around 1 µm. It is the standard structure for smartphone cameras.
Stacked
When you want a smaller chip, or want to put high-speed readout and image-processing functions in the sensor.
How they relate
The three structures evolved in sequence. BSI needs a support wafer for bonding, and replacing that support wafer with a logic chip containing circuits gives the stacked sensor.

FAQ

Why is back-side illumination more sensitive?

In front-side illumination, light passes between several wiring layers before reaching the photodiode, so part of it is blocked or reflected. Back-side illumination brings light in through the back, which has no wiring, so almost the entire pixel area receives light and oblique light is less likely to be lost.

Is a stacked sensor also back-side illuminated?

Yes. The pixel chip of a stacked sensor has a back-side illuminated structure, and a logic chip with readout and image-processing circuits is bonded underneath it. Back-side illumination describes how light enters, while stacked describes where the circuits are placed.

Is front-side illumination no longer used?

It is still used. With large pixels, light loss from the wiring is small, and the process is simple and cheap, so front-side illumination is still sometimes chosen for large-pixel and low-cost sensors.

What is the main advantage of a backside-illuminated (BSI) CMOS image sensor?

  1. AIt can reduce the number of pixels
  2. BLight reaches the photodiode without passing through the wiring layers, making it easier to increase sensitivity
  3. CIt no longer needs color filters
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Light reaches the photodiode without passing through the wiring layers, making it easier to increase sensitivity

In front-side illumination, light must pass through gaps in several metal layers above the photodiode, where it is blocked or reflected. Back-side illumination thins the wafer to a few µm and admits light from the side opposite the wiring, collecting it efficiently even at oblique angles. Commercialized by Sony in 2009, it preserves sensitivity in pixels around 1 µm and became standard in smartphone cameras.

Which correctly describes a stacked CMOS image sensor?

  1. AA structure with many lenses stacked
  2. BA structure that does not use photodiodes
  3. CA structure in which the pixel section and the signal-processing circuits are made on separate chips and bonded together
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A structure in which the pixel section and the signal-processing circuits are made on separate chips and bonded together

A stacked sensor builds the pixel chip and the logic chip (ADCs, image processing) in separately optimized processes and bonds them, for example with direct copper-to-copper bonding. Introduced by Sony in 2012, it shrinks die area while enabling fast readout, HDR and high frame-rate video. Three-layer products with a memory die in between also exist.

What does the "fill factor" of an image sensor pixel mean?

  1. AThe size of the lens aperture
  2. BThe ratio of effective pixels to total pixels
  3. CThe fraction of the pixel area that receives light and converts it to charge
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The fraction of the pixel area that receives light and converts it to charge

Fill factor is the fraction of a pixel's area that is light-sensitive photodiode. More transistors and wiring lower it and reduce sensitivity; early CMOS sensors had fill factors of only about 30%, a disadvantage versus CCDs. Microlenses raise the effective fill factor by focusing light, and back-side illumination removes the wiring from the light path, allowing nearly 100% effective collection.

Key terms explained

Backside illumination (BSI)
An image sensor structure in which light enters from the back of the substrate, where there is no wiring. Light is not blocked by wiring, so sensitivity stays high even with small pixels.
Stacked CMOS image sensor
An image sensor whose pixel array and signal-processing circuitry are made on separate chips and bonded together. Each can use the most suitable process, making the chip smaller and processing faster.
Microlens
A tiny lens placed over each pixel of an image sensor. It gathers light and directs it efficiently onto the photodiode, raising sensitivity.
CMOS image sensor
An image sensor made with a CMOS process that has an amplifier in each pixel. It is low-power, can integrate peripheral circuits, and dominates smartphone cameras and more.
Hybrid bonding
A technique that directly bonds copper electrodes and dielectric surfaces without bumps, providing huge numbers of connections at extremely fine pitch.
