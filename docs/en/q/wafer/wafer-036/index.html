Silicon wafers

What causes "slip", a problem when large-diameter wafers are heat-treated at high temperature?

  1. AStress from temperature differences within the wafer or its own weight shifts crystal planes and introduces dislocations
  2. BParticles adhere to the wafer surface
  3. CThe impurity concentration decreases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Stress from temperature differences within the wafer or its own weight shifts crystal planes and introduces dislocations

At high temperature silicon weakens greatly, so stress from temperature gradients during rapid heating or cooling, or from the wafer's own weight at support points, can make crystal planes ({111}) slip, creating dislocations (slip). It often starts near edge support pins and causes leakage, yield loss and lithography overlay errors. It is prevented by limiting ramp rates and optimizing support methods and geometry.

  • A. Correct. Stress shifts crystal planes and introduces dislocations.
  • B. Particle adhesion is a contamination problem, not slip.
  • C. A change in impurity concentration is not slip.
