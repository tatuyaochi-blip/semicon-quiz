Silicon wafers

Along which crystal planes does single-crystal silicon cleave most easily?

  1. A{100} planes
  2. B{111} planes
  3. C{110} planes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. {111} planes

Breaking bonds across {111} planes takes the least energy in silicon, so it cleaves most readily along them. (100) wafers tend to crack along {110} planes perpendicular to the surface, producing straight fractures at right angles, whose directions reveal the wafer's crystal orientation. This behavior is exploited when cleaving chips for cross-section inspection and when scribing wafers by hand.

  • A. {100} is the surface plane of a (100) wafer, not the easiest cleavage plane.
  • B. Correct. Silicon cleaves most easily along {111}.
  • C. Silicon also cleaves along {110}, but {111} is the easiest.
