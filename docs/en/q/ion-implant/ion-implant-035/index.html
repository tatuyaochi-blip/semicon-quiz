Ion implantation

What is the main reason the beam path in an ion implanter is kept at high vacuum?

  1. ATo make the equipment lighter
  2. BTo cool the wafer
  3. CTo prevent ions from colliding with gas molecules and being scattered or neutralized
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. To prevent ions from colliding with gas molecules and being scattered or neutralized

If gas remains in the beam path, ions collide with gas molecules and change direction or lose their charge and become neutral. They would then not be implanted at the intended location, energy or dose, so the beamline is kept at high vacuum.

  • A. It has nothing to do with equipment weight.
  • B. The vacuum is not for cooling the wafer.
  • C. Correct. It prevents scattering and neutralization of ions.
