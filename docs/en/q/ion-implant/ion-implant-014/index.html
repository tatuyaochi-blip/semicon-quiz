Ion implantation

Why is BF2⁺ sometimes implanted instead of boron alone to form shallow p-type layers?

  1. ABecause BF2⁺ acts as an n-type dopant
  2. BBecause BF2⁺ makes the equipment cheaper
  3. CBecause the molecular ion is heavy, so boron goes in shallower at the same acceleration energy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Because the molecular ion is heavy, so boron goes in shallower at the same acceleration energy

Boron, at mass 11, is light and penetrates deeply even at low energy. Implanted as BF2⁺ (mass 49), the molecule breaks up on impact and the boron atom receives only 11/49 (about 22%) of the energy, so it stops shallower. Very low-energy beams spread from space charge and deliver little current, which is why this method was used. Fluorine also aids amorphization, though it can cause side effects such as bubbles.
