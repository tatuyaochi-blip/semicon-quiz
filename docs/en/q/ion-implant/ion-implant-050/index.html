Ion implantation

What problem tends to arise when removing photoresist that has received a high-dose ion implant?

  1. AThe resist becomes transparent
  2. BThe surface becomes a carbonized, hardened crust, and rapid heating during normal ashing tends to make it burst from gas inside (popping)
  3. CThe resist dissolves away in water
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The surface becomes a carbonized, hardened crust, and rapid heating during normal ashing tends to make it burst from gas inside (popping)

Resist exposed to high-dose implants loses hydrogen at its surface and carbonizes into a hard crust tens of nm thick. Ashing it hot right away lets gas from the soft resist underneath burst the crust (popping), leaving residue and particles. Processes therefore start at low temperature and ramp up or remove the crust first with hydrogen-based plasmas; post-implant strip, residue removal included, is one of the trickier steps.

  • A. Becoming transparent is not the problem.
  • B. Correct. The hardened crust and popping are the problem.
  • C. Implanted resist actually becomes harder to remove.
