Ion implantation

Which correctly pairs dopants implanted into silicon with the conductivity type they produce?

  1. APhosphorus: p-type; arsenic: p-type
  2. BBoron: n-type; phosphorus: p-type
  3. CBoron: p-type; phosphorus and arsenic: n-type
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Boron: p-type; phosphorus and arsenic: n-type

Group-III boron acts as an acceptor to make p-type silicon, and group-V phosphorus and arsenic act as donors to make n-type; antimony (n-type) and indium or gallium (p-type) are also sometimes used. Implantation places these elements exactly where needed in the required amounts, building nMOS devices with n-type source/drains in p-wells and pMOS devices the other way around to form CMOS.

  • A. Both phosphorus and arsenic produce n-type.
  • B. The types for boron and phosphorus are reversed.
  • C. Correct. Boron gives p-type; phosphorus and arsenic give n-type.
