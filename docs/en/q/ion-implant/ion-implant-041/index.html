Ion implantation

Why is the wafer cooled during high-current ion implantation?

  1. ATo make the implant deeper
  2. BTo prevent the beam energy from heating the wafer, which can degrade the resist mask and change implant conditions
  3. CTo change the mass of the ions
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. To prevent the beam energy from heating the wafer, which can degrade the resist mask and change implant conditions

High-current implants can deposit hundreds of watts to nearly a kilowatt (tens of keV × tens of mA) into the wafer, heating it above 100 °C without cooling. Deformed or altered resist changes mask dimensions and becomes hard to strip, and temperature also affects damage recovery (how readily amorphization occurs), so wafers are cooled on electrostatic chucks. Some processes deliberately implant at cryogenic temperatures to control damage.

  • A. Cooling does not make the implant deeper.
  • B. Correct. It prevents resist degradation and similar effects from heating.
  • C. Cooling does not change the ions' mass.
