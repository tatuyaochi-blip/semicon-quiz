Ion implantation

When implanting aluminum or similar into SiC, why is the wafer often heated to several hundred degrees Celsius during implantation (hot implantation)?

  1. ATo melt the wafer
  2. BTo recover damage during implantation and prevent hard-to-recover damage such as amorphization
  3. CTo make the ions heavier
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. To recover damage during implantation and prevent hard-to-recover damage such as amorphization

High-dose room-temperature implants in SiC leave hard-to-repair damage such as amorphization, and because dopants barely diffuse in SiC, the implanted profile is used as is. Implanting with the wafer heated to about 300–600 °C repairs part of the damage in situ, and subsequent activation anneals at 1,600–1,800 °C yield good crystallinity and high activation. Heat-resistant masks such as SiO2 are used instead of resist.

  • A. SiC does not melt at several hundred degrees.
  • B. Correct. It recovers damage during implantation.
  • C. Heating does not change the ions' mass.
