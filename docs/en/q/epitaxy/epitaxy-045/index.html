Epitaxial growth

When silicon is grown epitaxially on a substrate with patterns such as buried layers, what is the phenomenon called in which the steps appearing on the surface are offset from the underlying pattern or distorted in shape?

  1. APattern shift
  2. BLoading effect
  3. CMicroloading
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Pattern shift

In epitaxy, growth rates differ with crystal orientation, so steps shift sideways as growth proceeds (pattern shift) or lose their shape (pattern distortion). This causes errors when later lithography steps align to the underlying layer, so it is suppressed through growth conditions and choice of orientation.

  • A. Correct. It is the shifting of step positions.
  • B. The loading effect is a change in etch or deposition rate depending on the area being processed.
  • C. Microloading is a local change in etch rate depending on pattern density and similar.
