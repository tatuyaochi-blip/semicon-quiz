Epitaxial growth

When a polar compound semiconductor such as GaAs is grown on a non-polar silicon substrate, what is the boundary called between regions in which the Ga and As positions are inverted?

  1. AAntiphase boundary (APB)
  2. BTwin boundary
  3. CGrain boundary
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Antiphase boundary (APB)

Growing a polar compound such as GaAs on nonpolar silicon lets domains with Ga and As positions swapped form on either side of single-atomic-layer surface steps, with antiphase boundaries (APBs) between them. APBs contain wrong bonds (Ga-Ga, As-As) that act as electrical defects. Remedies include annealing off-cut substrates to form double-atomic-layer steps and growing in trenches to trap APBs.

  • A. Correct. It separates regions with inverted atomic arrangement.
  • B. A twin boundary separates crystals related by mirror symmetry.
  • C. A grain boundary separates grains with different crystal orientations.
