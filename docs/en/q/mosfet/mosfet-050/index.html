MOSFET

Which is the common condition for "strong inversion" used to define the threshold voltage of a MOSFET? (φF: Fermi potential)

  1. AThe surface potential becomes −φF
  2. BThe surface potential becomes 2φF (the surface minority carrier density equals the bulk majority carrier density)
  3. CThe surface potential becomes zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The surface potential becomes 2φF (the surface minority carrier density equals the bulk majority carrier density)

Strong inversion is conventionally defined as beginning when the surface potential reaches 2φF—that is, when the surface electron density equals the bulk hole density. The gate voltage at that point is the threshold voltage, Vth = VFB + 2φF + √(2εqNA·2φF)/Cox. In measurements, Vth is extracted by the constant-current method or by extrapolating the linear part of the ID-VG curve, so values differ slightly with the definition.

  • A. A negative surface potential puts the surface of a p-type substrate into accumulation.
  • B. Correct. A surface potential of 2φF marks strong inversion.
  • C. Zero surface potential is the flat-band condition.
