MOSFET

In a MOSFET with a very thin gate insulator, what is the effect of the electron distribution in the inversion layer peaking slightly away from the interface?

  1. AThe threshold voltage becomes zero
  2. BThe gate insulator appears effectively thicker, reducing gate capacitance (and drive current)
  3. CThe gate insulator appears effectively thinner and the gate capacitance increases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The gate insulator appears effectively thicker, reducing gate capacitance (and drive current)

Because of quantum mechanical effects, inversion-layer electrons peak not right at the interface but slightly inside (about 1 nm). The charge centroid therefore sits away from the interface, which acts like a thicker insulator and reduces gate capacitance and drive current. Together with polysilicon gate depletion, it increases the effective insulator thickness.

  • A. This effect does not make the threshold voltage zero.
  • B. Correct. The effective insulator thickness increases and the capacitance decreases.
  • C. The charge centroid moves away from the interface, so the insulator appears thicker.
