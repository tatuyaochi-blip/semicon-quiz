MOSFET

If the gate insulator of an nMOSFET is made thicker with all else unchanged, how does the magnitude of the threshold voltage generally change?

  1. AIt becomes larger
  2. BIt becomes smaller
  3. CIt becomes zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It becomes larger

A thicker gate insulator reduces the gate capacitance Cox, so a larger gate voltage is needed to support the same depletion charge. The magnitude of the threshold voltage therefore increases. High-voltage transistors with thick oxide, used in I/O circuits, also tend to have higher threshold voltages.

  • A. Correct. Cox decreases, so the required gate voltage rises.
  • B. A thinner insulator tends to reduce the threshold voltage.
  • C. Oxide thickness alone does not make the threshold voltage zero.
