MOSFET

When a MOSFET is used as a switch, which statement correctly describes its on-resistance?

  1. AThe source-drain resistance when the device is off
  2. BThe resistance of the gate electrode itself
  3. CThe source-drain resistance when the device is on; the smaller it is, the lower the loss
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The source-drain resistance when the device is on; the smaller it is, the lower the loss

On-resistance RDS(on) is the drain-source resistance when the MOSFET conducts, and conduction loss is I²R—10 A through 10 mΩ dissipates 1 W. It decreases as gate voltage rises (more channel carriers) and increases with temperature (about 1.5× at 100 °C as a rule of thumb). In power MOSFETs, on-resistance rises steeply with voltage rating, so superjunction structures and SiC are used to break that trade-off.

  • A. The resistance when off is very large and is not called on-resistance.
  • B. The resistance of the gate electrode is called gate resistance, a different thing.
  • C. Correct. It is the drain-source resistance while conducting.
