Automotive Semiconductors and Functional Safety

Which correctly describes "load dump," a problem in automotive power supplies?

  1. AA phenomenon in which a large current flows into the starter motor at engine start, causing the battery voltage to drop sharply
  2. BA phenomenon in which the battery is disconnected while the alternator (generator) is charging, producing a large surge voltage on the supply line
  3. CA phenomenon in which lightning strikes the vehicle body and a large current flows into the supply line, destroying components
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A phenomenon in which the battery is disconnected while the alternator (generator) is charging, producing a large surge voltage on the supply line

Load dump is a phenomenon in which the battery is disconnected, for example by a terminal coming loose, while the generator is charging at high current, and the energy with nowhere to go makes the supply line voltage spike. Even in a 12 V system, without suppression circuitry it can reach roughly several tens of V to about 100 V and last for tens to hundreds of ms. For this reason, many ICs connected directly to the battery have a voltage rating of about 40 V or more, and they are protected with TVS diodes and similar devices. The voltage drop at engine start is a different phenomenon called cranking.
