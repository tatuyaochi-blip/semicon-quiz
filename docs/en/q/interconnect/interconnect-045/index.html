Interconnect processes

In electromigration testing, wires shorter than a certain length show greatly extended lifetimes, with almost no degradation. What is this phenomenon called?

  1. AThe Blech effect (short-length effect)
  2. BThe Miller effect
  3. CThe Early effect
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The Blech effect (short-length effect)

When electromigration carries atoms in one direction, a stress gradient builds up toward the wire end and drives a counter-flow of atoms. In short wires this back-flow balances the current-driven flow, so degradation hardly proceeds. This critical product of length and current density is called the Blech effect and is used in design rules for current density.

  • A. Correct. It is the suppression of degradation in short wires.
  • B. The Miller effect makes feedback capacitance appear multiplied by the gain.
  • C. The Early effect concerns the slope of transistor output characteristics.
