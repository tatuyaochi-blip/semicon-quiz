Interconnect processes

In scaled multilevel wiring, what is the main reason via resistance has become a problem?

  1. AThe via cross-section shrinks, and the high-resistance barrier metal takes up a larger share of it
  2. BVias have become longer
  3. CCurrent no longer flows through vias
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The via cross-section shrinks, and the high-resistance barrier metal takes up a larger share of it

As vias shrink, their cross-sectional area falls and resistance rises, while the resistive barrier metal needed to block copper diffusion (2–3 nm thick) cannot thin much, occupying a large share of a 20 nm via. The barrier at the via bottom also sits directly in series with the current path, magnifying its impact. Thinner ALD barriers, bottom-barrier removal and barrier-free metals are therefore being pursued.

  • A. Correct. A shrinking cross-section and a growing barrier share are the causes.
  • B. Scaling does not make vias longer.
  • C. Current does flow through vias.
