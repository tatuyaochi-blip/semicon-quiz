Interconnect processes

What is the effect of increasing a wire's thickness (height) without changing its width?

  1. ANeither resistance nor capacitance changes
  2. BBoth resistance and capacitance drop
  3. CResistance drops, but the area facing adjacent wires grows, raising inter-wire capacitance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Resistance drops, but the area facing adjacent wires grows, raising inter-wire capacitance

A thicker wire has a larger cross-section, so its resistance drops. But the sidewall area facing adjacent wires increases, raising inter-wire capacitance. Wire thickness (aspect ratio) is therefore chosen by balancing delay, power and crosstalk.

  • A. Both the cross-section and the facing area change, so both quantities change.
  • B. Capacitance rises because the facing area grows.
  • C. Correct. Resistance drops and capacitance rises.
