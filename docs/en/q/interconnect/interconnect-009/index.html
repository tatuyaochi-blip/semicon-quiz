Interconnect processes

When copper wires become very narrow, what is the main reason their effective resistivity rises?

  1. ABecause the wire temperature drops
  2. BBecause surface and grain-boundary scattering of electrons increase, and the high-resistance barrier layer takes up a larger share
  3. CBecause copper turns into a semiconductor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Because surface and grain-boundary scattering of electrons increase, and the high-resistance barrier layer takes up a larger share

When wire width approaches the electron mean free path (tens of nm in copper), scattering at the wire surfaces and grain boundaries increases and resistivity rises. In addition, the barrier layer, which needs a certain thickness, occupies a larger share of the cross-section. New wiring materials such as cobalt and ruthenium are therefore being considered.
