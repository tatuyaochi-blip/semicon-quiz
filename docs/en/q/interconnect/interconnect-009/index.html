Interconnect processes

When copper wires become very narrow, what is the main reason their effective resistivity rises?

  1. ABecause the wire temperature drops
  2. BBecause surface and grain-boundary scattering of electrons increase, and the high-resistance barrier layer takes up a larger share
  3. CBecause copper turns into a semiconductor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Because surface and grain-boundary scattering of electrons increase, and the high-resistance barrier layer takes up a larger share

When linewidths approach or fall below the electron mean free path (about 40 nm in copper), scattering at surfaces and grain boundaries rises and resistivity can reach several times the bulk value; narrow lines also have smaller grains, adding grain-boundary scattering. Meanwhile, barrier layers of fixed thickness occupy a growing share of the cross-section. Metals with shorter mean free paths—cobalt, ruthenium, molybdenum—are therefore being evaluated and introduced.
