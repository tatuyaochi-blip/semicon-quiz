Interconnect processes

Why has an Al-Cu alloy with about 0.5% copper been used for aluminum wiring?

  1. ATo improve resistance to electromigration
  2. BTo greatly lower resistivity
  3. CTo change the color of the wiring
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. To improve resistance to electromigration

Pure aluminum wiring readily formed voids and hillocks through electromigration, the leading cause of IC failure in the late 1960s. Adding about 0.5–4% copper, which segregates to grain boundaries and blocks aluminum transport along them, extends lifetime by an order of magnitude or more. Resistivity rises slightly, but the reliability gain made Al-Cu the standard before copper interconnects.

  • A. Correct. It improves electromigration resistance.
  • B. Adding a little copper actually raises resistivity slightly.
  • C. It is not for changing color.
