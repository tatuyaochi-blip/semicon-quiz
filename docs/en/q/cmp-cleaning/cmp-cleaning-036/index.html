CMP and cleaning

In Cu interconnect CMP, why is polishing generally divided into stages?

  1. ABecause Cu and the barrier metal beneath it (such as TaN) need different polishing conditions, so most of the Cu is removed first and then the barrier
  2. BTo give the polishing tool a rest
  3. CTo make the Cu thicker
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Because Cu and the barrier metal beneath it (such as TaN) need different polishing conditions, so most of the Cu is removed first and then the barrier

Copper CMP sequentially removes the thick overburden copper (hundreds of nm to about 1 µm), the few-nm barrier metal (e.g., Ta/TaN) and a little of the dielectric beneath. Because optimal rates and selectivities differ by material, the process is split into a fast bulk-copper step, a gentler copper clearing step and a final barrier step that removes barrier and slight dielectric to planarize, each with its own slurry.

  • A. Correct. Polishing proceeds in stages with conditions changed for each material.
  • B. It is not to rest the tool.
  • C. CMP removes Cu, not adds it.
