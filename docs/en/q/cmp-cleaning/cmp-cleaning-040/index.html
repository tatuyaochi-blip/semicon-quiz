CMP and cleaning

When removing particles from a wafer surface by cleaning, how does particle size generally relate to ease of removal?

  1. AThe smaller the particle, the stronger its adhesion relative to its weight and the forces from flow, so it is harder to remove
  2. BParticle size does not matter
  3. CSmaller particles are easier to remove
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The smaller the particle, the stronger its adhesion relative to its weight and the forces from flow, so it is harder to remove

Adhesion forces holding particles to a surface, such as van der Waals forces, scale roughly with particle diameter, while hydrodynamic drag scales with its square and gravity with its cube. Smaller particles are therefore relatively more strongly held and harder to remove—10 nm particles far more so than 100 nm ones. As killer-defect size shrinks with scaling, cleaning becomes progressively harder.

  • A. Correct. Adhesion becomes relatively stronger for smaller particles.
  • B. Particle size strongly affects ease of removal.
  • C. Smaller particles are harder to remove.
