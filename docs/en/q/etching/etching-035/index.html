Etching

In plasma tools such as capacitively coupled ones, what is the phenomenon called in which the wafer-side electrode naturally acquires a negative DC potential that accelerates ions toward the wafer?

  1. AThe Hall effect
  2. BThe Seebeck effect
  3. CSelf-bias
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Self-bias

On an RF-driven electrode, light, mobile electrons flow in more readily than ions, so a capacitively coupled electrode settles at a negative average DC potential—the self-bias, tens to hundreds of volts. This potential accelerates positive ions from the plasma nearly vertically into the wafer, enabling anisotropic etching. Changing RF power changes the self-bias and thus ion energy.

  • A. The Hall effect is a voltage perpendicular to current in a magnetic field.
  • B. The Seebeck effect is a voltage produced by a temperature difference.
  • C. Correct. A negative potential arises from the difference in electron flow.
