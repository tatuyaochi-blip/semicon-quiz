Etching

In gate electrode etching, what is commonly done to preserve the thin gate insulator underneath?

  1. AEtching with the same aggressive conditions from start to finish
  2. BEtching most of the way with fast conditions, then switching to conditions with high selectivity to the underlayer as it is approached
  3. CContinuing with aggressive conditions for a while after the underlayer appears
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Etching most of the way with fast conditions, then switching to conditions with high selectivity to the underlayer as it is approached

Gate etching first removes most of the material with a fast main-etch step that sets the profile, then, after endpoint, switches to a high-selectivity overetch that clears residual film at steps while barely touching the few-nm dielectric below. Splitting the etch into stages achieves both vertical profiles and underlayer protection. Punching through the gate dielectric would gouge the silicon and ruin the transistor.

  • A. Aggressive conditions throughout would also etch the thin underlayer.
  • B. Correct. Conditions are switched in stages to preserve the underlayer.
  • C. Continuing aggressive etching would punch through the underlayer.
