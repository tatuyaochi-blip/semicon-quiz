Power semiconductors

When one switch of an inverter turns on, which loss increases due to the reverse recovery of the diode in the opposite arm?

  1. AThe turn-on loss of the switch that turns on (the reverse recovery current is added to it)
  2. BOnly the loss in the gate drive circuit
  3. CThe loss in the heat sink
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The turn-on loss of the switch that turns on (the reverse recovery current is added to it)

When a freewheeling diode switches to reverse bias, stored carriers produce reverse-recovery current. This current flows through the switch that is turning on, raising its peak current and turn-on loss, and the diode itself also dissipates recovery loss. The effect grows with voltage and current, so replacing the diode with a SiC Schottky diode, which has negligible recovery, greatly reduces the switch's turn-on loss.

  • A. Correct. The reverse recovery current adds to the switch's turn-on loss.
  • B. Gate drive loss is not the main part of the increase due to reverse recovery.
  • C. A heat sink does not generate losses.
