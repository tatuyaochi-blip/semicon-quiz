Power semiconductors

Why is a negative gate voltage (for example, −3 to −5 V) sometimes applied to SiC MOSFETs or IGBTs in the off state?

  1. ATo raise the switching frequency
  2. BTo prevent the gate voltage from being pushed up by voltage changes from the opposite switch, which could turn the device on unintentionally
  3. CTo lower the on-resistance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. To prevent the gate voltage from being pushed up by voltage changes from the opposite switch, which could turn the device on unintentionally

In bridge circuits, the steep voltage change (dv/dt) when the opposite switch turns on can drive current through the Miller capacitance into the off device's gate, raising its voltage. If it exceeds the threshold, the device turns on falsely and shoots through. SiC MOSFETs, with low thresholds of 2–3 V and high dv/dt, are especially susceptible, so negative gate bias or Miller-clamp circuits provide margin.

  • A. It is not meant to raise the switching frequency.
  • B. Correct. It prevents false (parasitic) turn-on.
  • C. On-resistance is set by the positive gate voltage and is not lowered by negative bias.
