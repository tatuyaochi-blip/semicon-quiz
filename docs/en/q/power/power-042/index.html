Power semiconductors

When a power MOSFET's gate is charged with a constant current, why does the gate voltage stay flat for a while (the Miller plateau)?

  1. ABecause the gate current stops
  2. BBecause the gate insulator temporarily breaks down
  3. CBecause while the drain voltage is changing, the gate current is used to charge and discharge the gate-drain capacitance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Because while the drain voltage is changing, the gate current is used to charge and discharge the gate-drain capacitance

While the drain voltage swings widely during switching, the current fed into the gate goes into charging or discharging the gate-drain capacitance (Miller capacitance), so the gate voltage stays nearly constant. This flat region is the Miller plateau, and its length strongly affects switching speed and loss.

  • A. The gate current keeps flowing.
  • B. It is not a breakdown of the insulator.
  • C. Correct. The current goes into charging the Miller capacitance.
