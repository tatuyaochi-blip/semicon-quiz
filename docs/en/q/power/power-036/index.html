Power semiconductors

Why is a switching power supply more efficient than a linear power supply, which operates its transistor linearly?

  1. ABecause the transistor is used fully on or fully off, minimizing the time it sees voltage and current at once
  2. BBecause it uses no transistors
  3. CBecause its output voltage is always higher than its input
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Because the transistor is used fully on or fully off, minimizing the time it sees voltage and current at once

A transistor's loss is the product of voltage and current. In a linear supply, the transistor carries current while dropping the excess voltage, so losses are large. A switching supply alternates between on (almost zero voltage) and off (almost zero current), so losses are small and efficiency is high.

  • A. Correct. It minimizes the time voltage and current are present at once.
  • B. Switching supplies also use transistors, as switches.
  • C. Switching supplies include both step-down and step-up types.
