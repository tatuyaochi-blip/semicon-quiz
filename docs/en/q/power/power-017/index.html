Power semiconductors

If the current in a wire with a parasitic inductance of 10 nH changes by 10 A in 10 ns, approximately what voltage is induced?

  1. A1 V
  2. B10 V
  3. C100 V
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 10 V

The voltage across an inductance is V = L·di/dt: 10 nH × (10 A ÷ 10 ns) = 10 V. Ten nH corresponds to only about 1 cm of wiring. Switching 100 A in tens of ns with SiC can generate surges of tens to over 100 V, threatening the device's voltage rating. Power modules therefore emphasize low-inductance layouts that place opposing current paths close together to cancel magnetic fields, and capacitors right next to the devices.
