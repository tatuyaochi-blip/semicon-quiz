Power semiconductors

What is a typical failure seen in power cycling tests, which repeatedly heat and cool power modules?

  1. ABond wire lift-off and solder layer cracking caused by differences in thermal expansion coefficients
  2. BA change in chip color
  3. COnly gate oxide breakdown
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Bond wire lift-off and solder layer cracking caused by differences in thermal expansion coefficients

Power modules combine materials with different thermal expansion coefficients, such as silicon or SiC chips, aluminum wires, solder and substrates. Repeated temperature changes from current flow stress the interfaces, causing wire bonds to lift off and solder layers to crack. This is one of the main factors determining lifetime.

  • A. Correct. Wire lift-off and solder cracking from thermal expansion mismatch.
  • B. Color change is not a failure criterion.
  • C. Oxide breakdown is not a typical power cycling failure.
