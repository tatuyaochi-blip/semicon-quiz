Power semiconductors

In SiC power modules operating at high temperatures, which material is sometimes used instead of solder to bond the chips?

  1. ASintered silver (a bonding material made by sintering silver nanoparticles and the like)
  2. BEpoxy resin
  3. CRubber
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sintered silver (a bonding material made by sintering silver nanoparticles and the like)

Conventional solders melt in the 200 °C range and fatigue under repeated hot cycling, preventing full use of SiC's high-temperature capability. Sintered silver, made by heating and pressing silver nanoparticles, bonds at around 250 °C yet the resulting silver melts at about 960 °C and conducts heat several times better than solder, so it is used in high-temperature, high-reliability modules such as those for EVs. Its high cost is a drawback.
