Reliability

In TDDB lifetime evaluation, why are tests run at voltages higher than the actual operating voltage?

  1. ATo accelerate failures and estimate lifetime in a short time
  2. BTo confirm that no failures occur at operating voltage
  3. CTo avoid damaging the test equipment
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. To accelerate failures and estimate lifetime in a short time

At actual operating voltage, TDDB failures take years to occur. Elevated voltage and temperature make them occur within hours to weeks, and these results are extrapolated with a voltage-acceleration model to estimate life under use conditions. Because the extrapolation spans a large range, the choice of model can change predictions by orders of magnitude, so selecting an appropriate model is critical. Directly confirming no failure at use voltage would take impractically long.
