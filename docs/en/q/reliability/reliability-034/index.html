Reliability

Based on the rule of thumb often used for chemical reactions and degradation, that "reaction speed roughly doubles for every 10 °C rise", how does lifetime change when the operating temperature is lowered by 10 °C?

  1. AIt roughly halves
  2. BIt roughly doubles
  3. CIt does not change
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It roughly doubles

If degradation doubles in speed every 10 °C, lowering temperature by 10 °C halves the rate and roughly doubles lifetime. This rule of thumb holds approximately near room temperature for an activation energy around 0.5 eV; mechanisms with larger Ea are even more temperature-sensitive. Precise estimates use the Arrhenius model. The rule illustrates why cooling electronics extends their life.

  • A. Lifetime halves when the temperature is raised by 10 °C.
  • B. Correct. Reactions slow and lifetime roughly doubles.
  • C. Temperature strongly affects the rate of degradation.
