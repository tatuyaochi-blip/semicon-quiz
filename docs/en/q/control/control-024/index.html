Control Engineering Basics

Which of the following correctly describes the frequency characteristics of a time-delay element e^(−Ls) with delay time L, and its effect on a control system whose loop transfer function contains it?

  1. AIt only delays the appearance of the response and does not change the frequency characteristics, so it does not affect the stability of the control system
  2. BIts gain falls at −20 dB/dec and its phase is a constant −90°, so like an integrator it brings the steady-state error to zero
  3. CIts gain is 1 (0 dB) at every frequency, but its phase lags by ωL [rad], growing in proportion to frequency, so it reduces the phase margin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Its gain is 1 (0 dB) at every frequency, but its phase lags by ωL [rad], growing in proportion to frequency, so it reduces the phase margin

A time delay is an element whose output is the input delayed by a time L; e^(−jωL) always has a magnitude of 1 and a phase of −ωL [rad]. The phase lag grows without limit as the frequency rises, so it reduces the phase margin at the gain crossover frequency. For example, with L = 10 ms the phase lags by as much as about 57° at 100 rad/s. Transport delay through piping and the computation and output delays of digital control are examples, and with a large time delay the gain often has to be lowered at the cost of a slower response.

  • A. The phase of the frequency characteristics changes, which strongly affects stability.
  • B. This describes an integrator. The gain of a time delay is constant regardless of frequency.
  • C. Correct. Only the phase lags, in proportion to frequency.
