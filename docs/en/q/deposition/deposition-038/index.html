Deposition (CVD and PVD)

What is the purpose of placing a plate with many holes (a collimator) between the target and wafer in a sputtering system?

  1. ATo block atoms flying at oblique angles and pass only near-vertical ones, making it easier to coat the bottoms of holes
  2. BTo increase the deposition rate
  3. CTo cool the wafer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. To block atoms flying at oblique angles and pass only near-vertical ones, making it easier to coat the bottoms of holes

A collimator is a honeycomb plate (hole length-to-diameter ratio of about 1–2) that blocks obliquely traveling sputtered atoms and passes only those heading nearly perpendicular to the wafer, improving bottom coverage in deep holes. Film also accumulates on the collimator, however, lowering deposition rate and flaking off as particles, so ionized PVD has largely replaced it.

  • A. Correct. It passes only near-vertical atoms.
  • B. It blocks some atoms, so the rate actually drops.
  • C. Cooling the wafer is not the purpose.
