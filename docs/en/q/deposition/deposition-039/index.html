Deposition (CVD and PVD)

In PVD such as sputtering and evaporation, why is it hard to coat the sidewalls and bottoms of deep trenches?

  1. ABecause atoms move to avoid trenches in PVD
  2. BBecause atoms travel almost in straight lines, they have difficulty reaching areas not in line of sight from the opening
  3. CBecause the film becomes liquid in PVD
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Because atoms travel almost in straight lines, they have difficulty reaching areas not in line of sight from the opening

In PVD, atoms leaving the target or evaporation source travel in nearly straight lines and barely migrate after landing. Top surfaces and trench entrances therefore receive film readily, while sidewalls and bottoms not in line of sight from the opening receive little (shadowing). CVD and ALD, whose precursor gases penetrate deep into features and react at the surface, achieve much better step coverage.

  • A. Atoms do not move to avoid trenches.
  • B. Correct. Straight-line travel makes it hard to reach areas out of sight.
  • C. PVD films do not become liquid.
