Silicon photonics

Which correctly describes wavelength division multiplexing (WDM)?

  1. ALight of a single wavelength is split into multiple fibers to send the same signal over many fibers simultaneously
  2. BSeparate signals are placed on multiple lights of different wavelengths and sent together over a single fiber
  3. CMultiple signals are sent in turn over a single fiber by dividing time into fine slots
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Separate signals are placed on multiple lights of different wavelengths and sent together over a single fiber

WDM (Wavelength Division Multiplexing) exploits the fact that light of different wavelengths travels through the same fiber with almost no mutual interference, placing a separate signal on each wavelength. For example, sending 100 Gb/s signals on four wavelengths delivers 400 Gb/s over a single fiber. A multiplexer is used on the transmit side and a demultiplexer on the receive side; in silicon photonics they are implemented with ring resonators, arrayed waveguide gratings (AWGs), Mach-Zehnder filters and the like. Sending signals in divided time slots is called time-division multiplexing.
