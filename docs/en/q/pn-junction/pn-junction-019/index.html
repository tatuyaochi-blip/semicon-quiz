PN junctions and diodes

In the diode equation I = Is {exp(qV / nkT) − 1}, what current flows when a large reverse voltage is applied (within the range where no breakdown occurs)?

  1. AIt becomes infinite
  2. BIt keeps increasing in proportion to exp(qV / nkT)
  3. CIt stays nearly constant at about −Is (the reverse saturation current)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It stays nearly constant at about −Is (the reverse saturation current)

With a large reverse voltage (V strongly negative), exp(qV/nkT) is nearly zero and I ≈ −Is. The current reaches this nearly constant value once the reverse voltage exceeds a few kT/q (about 0.1 V), hence the name "saturation" current. For a small-signal silicon diode, Is is tiny—below a picoampere. In real diodes, generation current in the depletion region makes the reverse current creep up with voltage, and at higher voltage breakdown occurs.
