PN junctions and diodes

How is a photodiode generally used to detect light?

  1. AA large forward current is passed to make it emit light
  2. BReverse bias is applied, and carriers generated by light in the depletion layer are collected as current
  3. CAC voltage is applied to make it resonate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Reverse bias is applied, and carriers generated by light in the depletion layer are collected as current

Reverse-biasing a photodiode widens the depletion region, where light-generated electrons and holes are quickly separated by the field, producing a photocurrent proportional to light intensity. Reverse bias also reduces junction capacitance, speeding the response. Because the photocurrent tracks light level over many decades, photodiodes are used in light meters, optical-communication receivers and CD/DVD pickups. Zero-bias (photovoltaic) mode has lower dark current and suits precise measurement of weak light.
