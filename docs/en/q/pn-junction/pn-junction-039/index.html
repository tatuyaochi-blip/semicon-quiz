PN junctions and diodes

For an ideal diode (ideality factor n = 1) at room temperature, roughly how much must the forward voltage increase to raise the forward current tenfold?

  1. AAbout 6 mV
  2. BAbout 60 mV
  3. CAbout 600 mV
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. About 60 mV

Forward current is proportional to exp(qV/kT), so increasing it tenfold requires raising V by kT/q × ln 10 ≈ 26 mV × 2.3 ≈ 60 mV—hence the "about 60 mV per decade" rule. With an ideality factor n this becomes n × 60 mV, so n can be extracted from the slope of a semilog I-V plot. The theoretical limit of a MOSFET's subthreshold swing has the same value.

  • A. About 6 mV only raises the current by a factor of about 1.3.
  • B. Correct. kT/q × ln 10 ≈ 60 mV.
  • C. About 600 mV would raise the current by a factor of about 10¹⁰.
