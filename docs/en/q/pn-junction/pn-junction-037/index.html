PN junctions and diodes

What is the main purpose of placing a capacitor in parallel with the output of a rectifier?

  1. ATo lower the forward voltage of the diode
  2. BTo raise the frequency of the AC
  3. CTo reduce the ripple of the output voltage and make it closer to DC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. To reduce the ripple of the output voltage and make it closer to DC

A rectifier's raw output is a pulsating voltage. A parallel capacitor charges when the voltage is high and discharges into the load when it falls, reducing the ripple. Ripple voltage is roughly load current ÷ (ripple frequency × capacitance), so larger capacitance and smaller load current reduce it. A larger capacitor also increases inrush current when charging, which practical supplies must address.

  • A. A capacitor does not change the forward voltage of the diode.
  • B. A capacitor does not change the frequency.
  • C. Correct. The capacitor's charging and discharging flattens the ripple.
