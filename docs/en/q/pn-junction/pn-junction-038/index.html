PN junctions and diodes

How does the forward voltage of a silicon diode carrying a constant current change as the temperature rises?

  1. AIt falls at about 2 mV/°C
  2. BIt rises at about 2 mV/°C
  3. CIt does not change
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It falls at about 2 mV/°C

The forward voltage of a silicon diode at constant current drops by about 2 mV for every 1 °C rise. The intrinsic carrier density increases with temperature, so less voltage is needed to pass the same current. This property is also used in temperature sensors.

  • A. Correct. Its temperature coefficient is about −2 mV/°C.
  • B. The forward voltage falls, not rises, as temperature increases.
  • C. The forward voltage has a clear temperature dependence.
