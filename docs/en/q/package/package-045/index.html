Packaging and assembly

What is the defect called in which bonding wires are pushed aside and deformed by the resin flow during molding?

  1. AWire sweep
  2. BElectromigration
  3. CBlooming
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Wire sweep

If the inflowing mold compound pushes wires sideways, they can touch neighbors and short, or break. This is called wire sweep, and long, thin wires are most susceptible. It is prevented by adjusting wire length and layout, compound viscosity, injection speed and gate location, and is checked by X-ray. Copper wire, being stiffer than gold, has the advantage of resisting deformation.

  • A. Correct. It is wire deformation caused by resin flow.
  • B. Electromigration is current-driven atomic migration.
  • C. Blooming is charge overflow in image sensors.
