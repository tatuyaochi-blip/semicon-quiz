Environment, health and safety

Which correctly describes the occupational exposure limit of a hazardous chemical?

  1. AThe concentration at which almost no one is judged to suffer adverse health effects even with repeated exposure of about eight hours a day
  2. BThe concentration above which fire always occurs
  3. CThe maximum concentration at which the substance dissolves in water
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The concentration at which almost no one is judged to suffer adverse health effects even with repeated exposure of about eight hours a day

Occupational exposure limits (Japan Society for Occupational Health) and TLVs (ACGIH in the US) are guideline concentrations below which nearly all workers exposed about 8 hours a day, 40 hours a week, show no adverse health effects. Arsine's, for example, is set very low at about 0.005 ppm. They are used to manage the work environment and decide whether local exhaust or protective equipment is needed. Combustion relates to LEL, and how much dissolves is solubility.

  • A. Correct. It is a concentration benchmark for preventing health effects.
  • B. Values such as the lower explosive limit relate to combustion.
  • C. How readily something dissolves in water is its solubility.
