DRAM

Why is a boosted voltage (VPP), higher than the supply voltage, used to drive DRAM word lines?

  1. ATo make refresh unnecessary
  2. BTo avoid losing a threshold voltage across the cell transistor so the capacitor is written with a full voltage
  3. CTo lower the bit-line voltage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. To avoid losing a threshold voltage across the cell transistor so the capacitor is written with a full voltage

The cell transistor is an nMOS, so if its gate were at the supply voltage, the capacitor could only charge to the supply minus Vth. Driving the word line at a boosted VPP (e.g., about 2.5–3 V), more than Vth above the supply, writes a full "1" and preserves read margin. VPP is generated on-chip by a charge pump, and the gate dielectric is designed thick enough to withstand it.

  • A. Refresh is still required even with boosting.
  • B. Correct. It avoids the threshold voltage drop.
  • C. It is not meant to lower the bit-line voltage.
