A/D and D/A conversion

For ADCs with the same input range, what happens when the resolution (number of bits) is increased?

  1. AThe voltage per LSB becomes larger, so small changes can no longer be distinguished
  2. BThe sampling frequency automatically increases
  3. CThe voltage per LSB becomes smaller, so finer voltage differences can be distinguished
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The voltage per LSB becomes smaller, so finer voltage differences can be distinguished

An N-bit converter divides its input range into 2ᴺ steps, and each additional bit halves the LSB. Whether finer differences can actually be resolved also depends on noise: once the LSB is smaller than the noise, the lower bits merely fluctuate. For example, 1 LSB at 24 bits over 5 V is about 0.3 µV, demanding very low-noise design to be useful. Resolution (bit count) and accuracy (error size) are separate specifications.

  • A. More bits make 1 LSB smaller.
  • B. Resolution and sampling frequency are separate specifications.
  • C. Correct. 1 LSB becomes smaller, allowing finer distinctions.
