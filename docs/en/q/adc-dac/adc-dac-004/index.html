AD/DA conversion

Which correctly describes the operation of a successive-approximation (SAR) AD converter?

  1. AIt integrates the input voltage for a fixed time and measures the discharge time
  2. BStarting from the most significant bit, it compares the input voltage with the output of an internal DAC and decides one bit at a time
  3. CIt achieves high resolution through oversampling and noise shaping
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Starting from the most significant bit, it compares the input voltage with the output of an internal DAC and decides one bit at a time

A successive-approximation converter asks, starting from the most significant bit, whether the input is larger than the internal DAC output, deciding one bit at a time like a binary search. An N-bit conversion needs N comparisons. Its good balance of speed, accuracy and power makes it widely used, for example in microcontroller ADCs. Measuring by integration is the dual-slope type, and oversampling is used by the delta-sigma type.
