Probability, Statistics and Queueing

A sample of size 36 is drawn at random from a population with a standard deviation of 12. Which of the following is the standard error of the sample mean (the standard deviation that describes the spread of the sample mean)?

  1. A2
  2. B4
  3. C12
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2

The standard error of the sample mean is σ/√n, the population standard deviation σ divided by the square root of the sample size n: 12 ÷ √36 = 12 ÷ 6 = 2. With a sample four times as large, 144, the standard error halves to 1, so doubling the precision of the estimate requires four times as many samples.

  • A. Correct. 12 ÷ √36 = 2.
  • B. σ² ÷ n = 144 ÷ 36 = 4 is the variance of the sample mean, without taking the square root.
  • C. 12 is the standard deviation of the individual values in the population; the spread of the sample mean is smaller than this.
