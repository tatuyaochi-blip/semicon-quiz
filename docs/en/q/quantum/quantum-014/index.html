Quantum Computing Hardware

A circuit uses a two-qubit gate with a fidelity of 99.5% per operation (an error rate of 0.5%) 100 times in a row. Assuming errors occur independently, which is closest to the probability that no error occurs at all?

  1. AAbout 37%
  2. BAbout 61%
  3. CAbout 99.5%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. About 61%

The probability of no error in each operation is 0.995, so the probability of no error in 100 operations in a row is 0.995 to the power of 100, about 0.61, or about 61%. Even with an error rate as small as 0.5%, the success rate drops quickly as the circuit gets deeper: for 1,000 operations it is about 0.7%. This is why the depth of circuit that a device without error correction can run is roughly set by its gate error rate. Real errors are not necessarily independent, and measurement and idle errors add to them, so this is a rough estimate.

  • A. This is the value for an error rate of 1% (0.99 to the power of 100 ≈ 0.37).
  • B. Correct. 0.995 to the power of 100 ≈ 0.61.
  • C. This takes the fidelity of a single operation as the success rate of the whole circuit.
