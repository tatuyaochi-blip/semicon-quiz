Advanced packaging (3D and chiplets)

When a TSV wafer is thinned to tens of micrometers and processed, which technique temporarily attaches it to a support wafer (carrier) to prevent cracking and warpage?

  1. AWire bonding
  2. BTemporary bonding and debonding
  3. CReflow
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Temporary bonding and debonding

Wafers thinned to tens of micrometers crack easily and warp badly on their own. They are temporarily bonded with adhesive to glass or silicon carrier wafers, backside grinding and wiring are performed, and then they are released by laser, heat or mechanical means (debonding). With a glass carrier, a laser can pass through to decompose only the adhesive layer. It is a key technology for handling thin wafers safely.

  • A. Wire bonding connects a chip to terminals with fine metal wires.
  • B. Correct. The wafer is handled while temporarily attached to a support wafer.
  • C. Reflow is the step of melting solder to make joints.
