Advanced packaging (3D and chiplets)

Which correctly explains how copper electrodes are joined to each other in copper-dielectric hybrid bonding?

  1. ASolder is melted to connect the copper
  2. BThe copper is stuck together with adhesive
  3. CThe dielectric surfaces are bonded first, then heating expands the copper so the copper pads press together and join by diffusion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The dielectric surfaces are bonded first, then heating expands the copper so the copper pads press together and join by diffusion

In hybrid bonding, planarized and activated dielectric (oxide or similar) surfaces are bonded near room temperature, and a subsequent anneal strengthens the dielectric bond. The copper pads are slightly recessed; thermal expansion during heating presses them together into close contact, and atomic diffusion merges them.

  • A. Not using solder is a defining feature of hybrid bonding.
  • B. No adhesive is used.
  • C. Correct. It combines dielectric bonding with copper expansion from heating.
