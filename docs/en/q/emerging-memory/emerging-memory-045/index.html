Emerging memories

Which memory changes resistance by having metal ions such as silver or copper move through a solid electrolyte to form or break a conductive path?

  1. ACBRAM (conductive-bridge memory)
  2. BFeRAM
  3. CSTT-MRAM
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. CBRAM (conductive-bridge memory)

In CBRAM, metal ions from an active electrode such as silver or copper dissolve into a solid electrolyte, migrate under voltage to the opposite electrode and build a metallic conductive bridge; reversing the voltage dissolves and breaks it, switching between low and high resistance. It operates at low voltage with low write energy. Considered a type of ReRAM alongside oxygen-vacancy oxide devices, it has been commercialized as low-power embedded memory.

  • A. Correct. It uses a conductive bridge formed by metal ions.
  • B. FeRAM stores data as ferroelectric polarization.
  • C. STT-MRAM stores data as magnetization direction.
