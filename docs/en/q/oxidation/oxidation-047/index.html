Oxidation and diffusion

Why does the compressive stress in thermally grown oxide become smaller when oxidation is done above about 950 °C?

  1. ABecause at high temperature the oxide can flow viscously and relax the stress
  2. BBecause the silicon melts
  3. CBecause the oxide crystallizes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Because at high temperature the oxide can flow viscously and relax the stress

Converting silicon to SiO2 expands its volume about 2.2×, placing the oxide in compression. Above about 950 °C, amorphous SiO2 can flow slowly (viscous flow), relieving the stress. At lower temperatures stress persists, retarding oxidation at convex corners such as STI trench edges, leaving sharp corners that concentrate electric fields and degrade devices—so high-temperature oxidation or ISSG is used to round them.

  • A. Correct. Viscous flow relaxes the stress.
  • B. Silicon does not melt at this temperature.
  • C. Thermal oxide normally remains amorphous.
