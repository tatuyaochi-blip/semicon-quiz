Oxidation and diffusion

Why can SiO2 serve as a diffusion mask against boron and phosphorus but works poorly as a mask against gallium and aluminum?

  1. ABecause SiO2 reacts with gallium and aluminum to become a conductor
  2. BBecause gallium and aluminum diffuse very fast in SiO2
  3. CBecause gallium and aluminum are electrically inactive in silicon
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Because gallium and aluminum diffuse very fast in SiO2

Boron and phosphorus diffuse far more slowly in SiO2 than in silicon, so oxide masks them effectively. Gallium and aluminum, however, diffuse rapidly through SiO2, making oxide useless as a mask for selective diffusion. Aluminum, which also diffuses quickly to great depths, is sometimes used for deep junctions in power devices. This is one reason boron is the main p-type dopant in silicon.

  • A. They do not react to form a conductor.
  • B. Correct. They diffuse fast in SiO2, so it cannot block them.
  • C. Gallium and aluminum also act as acceptors in silicon.
