Oxidation and diffusion

Once the oxide has become sufficiently thick in thermal oxidation, how does its thickness relate to oxidation time?

  1. AThickness is roughly proportional to the square root of time (parabolic law)
  2. BThickness is proportional to the square of time
  3. CThickness is constant regardless of time
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Thickness is roughly proportional to the square root of time (parabolic law)

According to the Deal-Grove model, while the oxide is thin the interface reaction limits growth and thickness is roughly proportional to time (linear law). As the film thickens, diffusion of the oxidant through it becomes limiting, and thickness becomes roughly proportional to the square root of time (parabolic law). The thicker the oxide, the slower it grows.
