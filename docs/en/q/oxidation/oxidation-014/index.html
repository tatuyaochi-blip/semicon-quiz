Oxidation and diffusion

When boron-doped silicon is thermally oxidized, how does the boron concentration on the silicon side near the interface tend to change?

  1. AIt decreases, because boron is readily taken into the oxide
  2. BIt increases, because boron is pushed out to the interface
  3. CIt does not change
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It decreases, because boron is readily taken into the oxide

Each dopant has a fixed concentration ratio across the Si/SiO2 interface—the segregation coefficient m (in silicon / in oxide). Boron, with m ≈ 0.3, is drawn into the oxide, depleting the silicon surface (pile-down). Phosphorus and arsenic, with m ≈ 10, are rejected by the oxide and accumulate in the silicon near the interface (pile-up). These effects are considered when designing threshold voltage and channel doping.
