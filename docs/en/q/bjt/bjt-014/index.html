Bipolar transistors

Which correctly describes thermal runaway in a bipolar transistor?

  1. AOperation stops because the temperature drops too much
  2. BThe current suddenly becomes zero
  3. CRising temperature increases the collector current, which generates more heat, and this positive feedback leads to destruction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Rising temperature increases the collector current, which generates more heat, and this positive feedback leads to destruction

For a fixed VBE, a bipolar transistor's collector current rises with temperature (VBE drifts about −2 mV/°C). More current means more heat and higher temperature; if this positive feedback takes hold, current grows without limit until the device fails—thermal runaway. It is prevented by emitter-resistor degeneration, adequate heat sinking, and staying within the safe operating area (SOA). Paralleled devices also get individual emitter resistors to share current evenly.
