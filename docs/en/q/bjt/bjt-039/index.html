Bipolar transistors

In a common-emitter amplifier with transconductance gm = 40 mS and collector resistor RC = 5 kΩ, roughly what is the magnitude of the voltage gain? (Ignore the load.)

  1. AAbout 2,000
  2. BAbout 200
  3. CAbout 8
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. About 200

The small-signal voltage gain of a common-emitter amplifier is Av ≈ −gm × RC, here 40 mS × 5 kΩ = 200. Since gm = IC/VT, Av = IC·RC/VT, so gain scales with the DC voltage drop across RC; a 5.2 V drop gives about 200. In practice the load and the transistor's output resistance appear in parallel and reduce the gain, with an upper limit of gm × ro (thousands).

  • A. About 2,000 is an order of magnitude too large.
  • B. Correct. gm × RC = 0.04 S × 5,000 Ω = 200.
  • C. About 8 comes from converting the units of gm and RC incorrectly.
