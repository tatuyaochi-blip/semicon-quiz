Bipolar transistors

What does the transition frequency fT of a bipolar transistor mean?

  1. AThe frequency of the power supply
  2. BThe frequency at which the common-emitter current gain falls to 1
  3. CThe frequency at which a circuit oscillates
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The frequency at which the common-emitter current gain falls to 1

Current gain falls at high frequency, mainly because of base transit time and charging of junction capacitances. The frequency at which common-emitter current gain drops to 1 is the transition frequency fT, roughly fT ≈ gm/(2π(Cπ + Cμ)). It is a key high-frequency metric: a few hundred MHz for general-purpose small-signal transistors and above 300 GHz for some SiGe HBTs. The maximum oscillation frequency fmax, where power gain reaches 1, is also used.
