Bipolar transistors

What is the state of the junctions when a bipolar transistor is in the saturation region?

  1. ABoth the base-emitter and base-collector junctions are forward biased
  2. BBoth junctions are reverse biased
  3. CThe base-emitter junction is reverse biased and the base-collector junction is forward biased
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Both the base-emitter and base-collector junctions are forward biased

In saturation the base-collector junction joins the base-emitter junction in forward bias. Collector current is limited by the external circuit (e.g., VCC/RC) and barely increases with more base current. IC/IB is then below hFE; this ratio, the "forced beta," indicates how deep the saturation is. It is the on state of a switch with VCE only about 0.1–0.2 V, but stored charge delays turn-off.

  • A. Correct. Saturation is when both junctions are forward biased.
  • B. Both reverse biased is the cutoff region.
  • C. This combination is reverse-active operation, where the roles of emitter and collector are swapped.
