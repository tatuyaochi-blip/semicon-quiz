Bipolar transistors

How does the phase of the output voltage of a common-emitter amplifier compare with the input voltage?

  1. AIn phase
  2. BInverted by 180°
  3. CLeading by 90°
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Inverted by 180°

In a common-emitter amplifier, raising the input (base) voltage increases collector current and the voltage drop across the collector resistor, so the output (collector) voltage falls. The output is therefore inverted by 180°, and the voltage gain is written with a negative sign, −gm·RC. It offers both high voltage and current gain among the three configurations and is the most widely used. Common-collector and common-base stages are non-inverting.
