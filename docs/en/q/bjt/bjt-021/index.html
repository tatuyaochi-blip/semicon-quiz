Bipolar transistors

What is the main reason for using a current-feedback bias circuit with an emitter resistor in a transistor amplifier?

  1. ATo maximize the voltage gain
  2. BTo stabilize the operating point against temperature changes and hFE variations
  3. CTo make the circuit oscillate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. To stabilize the operating point against temperature changes and hFE variations

An emitter resistor creates negative feedback: if collector current rises, the emitter voltage rises, VBE falls and the increase is checked. Fixing the base with a resistor divider and dropping a voltage across the emitter resistor much larger than VBE variations (e.g., over 1 V) makes the bias insensitive to temperature and hFE spread. A bypass capacitor across the emitter resistor is often added to keep the AC gain from dropping.
