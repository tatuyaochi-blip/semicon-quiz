Image sensors and optical devices

Why do images taken in dark places look grainy (with conspicuous noise)?

  1. ABecause the image sensor's pixel count drops in the dark
  2. BBecause light gets longer wavelengths in the dark
  3. CBecause few photons reach the pixels, so noise is relatively large compared with the signal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Because few photons reach the pixels, so noise is relatively large compared with the signal

In dim light, few photons reach each pixel, so the signal is small while photon fluctuations (shot noise, √N) and readout noise remain, lowering the signal-to-noise ratio and making images grainy. Raising ISO merely amplifies signal and noise together. SNR is improved by gathering more light with larger sensors and brighter lenses, or by stacking and averaging multiple frames (night modes).

  • A. The pixel count does not change with brightness.
  • B. Darkness does not change the wavelength of light.
  • C. Correct. The signal shrinks and the SNR drops.
