Logic circuits

What is a typical use of a linear feedback shift register (LFSR), which feeds the XOR of some outputs of a shift register back to its input?

  1. AMeasuring precise time
  2. BAmplifying analog signals
  3. CGenerating pseudo-random patterns, such as test patterns, and computing CRCs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Generating pseudo-random patterns, such as test patterns, and computing CRCs

With suitable feedback taps, an n-bit LFSR cycles through 2ⁿ − 1 states, producing a seemingly random sequence. Because it needs very little logic, it is used for pattern generation in built-in self-test (BIST), CRC calculation, scramblers and similar purposes.

  • A. It is not used for measuring precise time.
  • B. An LFSR is a digital circuit and does not amplify analog signals.
  • C. Correct. It is used for pseudo-random and test patterns and CRCs.
