Yield and quality control

With the same defect density, what generally happens to yield when chip area is increased?

  1. AIt decreases
  2. BIt increases
  3. CIt does not change
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It decreases

In the Poisson model, yield is Y = exp(−D×A), where D is defect density and A is die area. With randomly landing defects, a larger die is more likely to contain at least one, so yield falls exponentially with area. This is one reason chiplets, which split a large die into several small ones, attract attention: small dies can be tested and only good ones combined.
