Yield and quality control

When analyzing yield data, which example correctly illustrates that "a correlation between two quantities does not mean one causes the other"?

  1. AConclusions can be drawn without looking at data
  2. BEven if lots processed on a certain tool have low yield, another factor may affect both, such as that tool happening to receive many difficult products
  3. CCorrelation always implies causation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Even if lots processed on a certain tool have low yield, another factor may affect both, such as that tool happening to receive many difficult products

Correlation only shows that two quantities tend to change together; it does not prove causation. A common hidden factor (a confounder) may influence both. It is important to identify the true cause through stratification and confirmation experiments.

  • A. It is important to verify conclusions with data.
  • B. Correct. Another factor may be affecting both.
  • C. Correlation does not necessarily imply causation.
