Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA)

How did the subthreshold swing (SS) generally change with the move from planar transistors to FinFETs?

  1. AIt fell below the theoretical limit of about 60 mV/dec
  2. BIt approached (decreased toward) the theoretical limit of about 60 mV/dec
  3. CIt increased, making the device harder to turn off
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It approached (decreased toward) the theoretical limit of about 60 mV/dec

Subthreshold swing is the gate voltage needed to change the drain current by one decade below threshold, with a theoretical limit of about 60 mV/dec at room temperature. In a FinFET the gate controls the channel from three sides, strengthening its control, so SS approaches the 60 mV/dec limit. However, conventional MOSFET physics cannot go below 60 mV/dec.

  • A. Conventional MOSFETs cannot go below 60 mV/dec because of the thermal limit.
  • B. Correct. Stronger gate control brings it closer to the limit.
  • C. SS improves (decreases) in FinFETs.
