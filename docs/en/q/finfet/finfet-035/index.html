Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA)

What does the "k" in "high-k gate dielectric" represent?

  1. ACrystal hardness
  2. BThermal conductivity
  3. CRelative permittivity (dielectric constant)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Relative permittivity (dielectric constant)

The "k" in high-k is the relative permittivity; materials above SiO2's (about 3.9) are called high-k. Representative examples are HfO2 (about 20–25) and HfSiON, which provide large gate capacitance with physically thicker films. Higher-k materials tend to have smaller bandgaps, so selection balances leakage. Between interconnects, by contrast, low-k materials are used to reduce capacitance.

  • A. It does not stand for hardness.
  • B. It does not stand for thermal conductivity.
  • C. Correct. k represents the relative permittivity.
