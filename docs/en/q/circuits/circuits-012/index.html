Circuit and electromagnetism basics

When a direct current flows in a long straight wire, which correctly describes the shape and direction of the magnetic field around it?

  1. AConcentric circles around the wire, in the direction a right-handed screw turns when advancing in the direction of the current
  2. BParallel to the wire, in the same direction as the current
  3. CRadially outward from the wire
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Concentric circles around the wire, in the direction a right-handed screw turns when advancing in the direction of the current

The magnetic field around a straight current forms concentric circles centered on the wire, circulating in the direction a right-handed screw turns as it advances along the current (the right-hand screw rule). Equivalently, point your right thumb along the current and your fingers curl in the field direction. The flux density is B = μ0I/(2πr), inversely proportional to the distance r from the wire. A radial pattern is the shape of the electric field around a point charge.

  • A. Correct. The field forms concentric circles given by the right-hand screw rule.
  • B. The field around a straight current is not parallel to the current.
  • C. A radial pattern describes the electric field of a point charge, not the magnetic field of a current.
