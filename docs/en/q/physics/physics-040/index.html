Semiconductor physics

How does the band gap of silicon change as the temperature rises?

  1. AIt becomes slightly smaller
  2. BIt becomes slightly larger
  3. CIt stays constant regardless of temperature
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It becomes slightly smaller

As temperature rises, thermal expansion and electron-phonon interaction make the band gap slightly smaller. In silicon it shrinks by about 0.3 meV/K near room temperature, from about 1.17 eV at 0 K to about 1.12 eV at 300 K (described by the Varshni equation). A diode's forward voltage falls by about 2 mV/°C mainly because ni increases, and the shrinking gap adds to this. The effect is used in on-chip temperature sensors.

  • A. Correct. The band gap shrinks slightly as temperature rises.
  • B. In most semiconductors, the band gap decreases as temperature rises.
  • C. The band gap does change slightly with temperature.
