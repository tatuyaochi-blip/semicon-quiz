Semiconductor physics

What happens to the drift velocity of electrons in silicon when a strong electric field is applied?

  1. AIt keeps increasing in proportion to the field
  2. BIt approaches a certain value (about 1×10⁷ cm/s) and hardly increases further
  3. CIt decreases as the field becomes stronger
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It approaches a certain value (about 1×10⁷ cm/s) and hardly increases further

At low fields drift velocity is proportional to field, but at high fields carriers lose energy by emitting optical phonons, and electron velocity in silicon levels off at about 1×10⁷ cm/s (velocity saturation). As a result, short-channel MOSFET current becomes roughly linear in gate voltage rather than quadratic. In GaAs, electrons at high field transfer to a heavier valley and slow down, an effect used for Gunn-diode oscillators.

  • A. The velocity is proportional to the field only at low fields.
  • B. Correct. Velocity saturation occurs at high fields.
  • C. A velocity drop at high fields occurs in some materials such as GaAs; in silicon the velocity saturates.
