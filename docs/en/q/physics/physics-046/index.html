Semiconductor physics

Which value is closest to the electron mobility in lightly doped silicon at room temperature?

  1. AAbout 14 cm²/(V·s)
  2. BAbout 140,000 cm²/(V·s)
  3. CAbout 1,400 cm²/(V·s)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. About 1,400 cm²/(V·s)

In lightly doped silicon at room temperature, electron mobility is about 1,400 cm²/(V·s) and hole mobility about 450. In a MOSFET channel, surface roughness and interface-charge scattering cut this to about half or less. Some compound semiconductors are much faster—about 8,500 for electrons in GaAs and about 77,000 cm²/(V·s) in InSb—and are used for high-frequency applications. In every material, mobility drops as doping increases.

  • A. About 14 cm²/(V·s) is two orders of magnitude too small.
  • B. About 140,000 cm²/(V·s) is two orders of magnitude too large; even high-mobility compound semiconductors do not reach this at room temperature.
  • C. Correct. The electron mobility is about 1,400 cm²/(V·s).
