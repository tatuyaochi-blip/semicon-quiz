Semiconductor physics

Which light can silicon absorb?

  1. AIt absorbs all wavelengths equally
  2. BLight with energy greater than the band gap (wavelengths shorter than about 1.1 µm)
  3. COnly light with energy smaller than the band gap
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Light with energy greater than the band gap (wavelengths shorter than about 1.1 µm)

To excite an electron from the valence band to the conduction band, light must have at least the band gap energy. Silicon's ~1.12 eV corresponds to about 1.1 µm, so shorter wavelengths are absorbed and longer infrared light mostly passes through. Absorption depth varies strongly with wavelength: blue light is absorbed within about 0.1–0.5 µm of the surface, while red light reaches several µm. Image sensor and solar cell designs take this depth difference into account.
