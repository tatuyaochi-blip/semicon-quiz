Semiconductor physics

In an indirect-gap semiconductor such as silicon, why is the probability of electron-hole recombination with light emission low?

  1. ABecause the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum have different momenta, so the transition requires a phonon
  2. BBecause the hole mobility is lower than the electron mobility
  3. CBecause the band gap is too large
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Because the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum have different momenta, so the transition requires a phonon

In an indirect-gap semiconductor, the conduction band minimum and the valence band maximum are at different positions in momentum (wave-vector) space. A photon carries almost no momentum, so the transition requires the simultaneous involvement of a lattice vibration (phonon), which makes it unlikely. This is why silicon is not suited to light-emitting devices.

  • A. Correct. A phonon is needed to conserve momentum.
  • B. The difference in mobility does not determine the probability of light emission.
  • C. Silicon's band gap (about 1.12 eV) is not especially large and is not the reason it emits light poorly.
