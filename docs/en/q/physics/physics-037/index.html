Semiconductor physics

With electron density n, hole density p, electron mobility μn, hole mobility μp and elementary charge q, which expression gives the conductivity σ of a semiconductor?

  1. Aσ = q(nμn − pμp)
  2. Bσ = (nμn + pμp) / q
  3. Cσ = q(nμn + pμp)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. σ = q(nμn + pμp)

Electrons and holes move in opposite directions but also have opposite charges, so their currents add and the conductivity is the sum σ = q(nμn + pμp); resistivity is its inverse. In doped material the majority-carrier term alone is a good approximation, e.g. σ ≈ qnμn for n-type. In intrinsic silicon n = p = ni, so σ = q·ni(μn + μp), giving a high resistivity of about 2×10⁵ Ω·cm.

  • A. Holes move opposite to electrons but also have the opposite charge, so their currents flow in the same direction and add rather than subtract.
  • B. Conductivity is proportional to q, so dividing by q is wrong.
  • C. Correct. The electron and hole contributions are added.
