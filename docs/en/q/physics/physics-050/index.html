Semiconductor physics

Why is the intrinsic Fermi level of silicon slightly offset from the exact center of the band gap?

  1. ABecause the band gap disappears as the temperature rises
  2. BBecause the effective densities of states (effective masses) of the conduction and valence bands differ
  3. CBecause silicon crystals always contain impurities
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Because the effective densities of states (effective masses) of the conduction and valence bands differ

In an intrinsic semiconductor the Fermi level sits where electron and hole densities are equal. Because effective masses differ, the effective densities of states of the conduction and valence bands are unequal (Nc > Nv), giving Ei = (Ec+Ev)/2 + (kT/2)·ln(Nv/Nc); in silicon the level lies about 0.01 eV below midgap. This is small compared with the 1.12 eV gap, so calculations usually treat it as midgap.

  • A. The band gap does not disappear when temperature rises.
  • B. Correct. The difference in effective densities of states causes a small shift.
  • C. The intrinsic Fermi level is defined for the ideal case without impurities.
