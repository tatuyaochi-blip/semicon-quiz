Memory Hierarchy

Roughly how many times longer is a main-memory DRAM access than an L1 cache hit (for typical PC or server CPUs)?

  1. A2 to 3 times
  2. BTens to about a hundred times
  3. CMore than 100,000 times
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Tens to about a hundred times

An L1 hit takes a few cycles (around 1 ns), while a DRAM access takes tens to about 100 ns, a difference of tens to a hundred times. For a CPU running at several GHz that means waiting hundreds of cycles, which is why cache hierarchies and prefetching are essential to performance. If the gap were only 2 to 3 times, caches would matter far less. More than 100,000 times is closer to the gap with SSDs or hard disks, not DRAM.

  • A. The gap is far larger than 2 to 3 times.
  • B. Correct. Tens to a hundred times, equal to hundreds of CPU cycles.
  • C. More than 100,000 times is closer to the gap with storage.
