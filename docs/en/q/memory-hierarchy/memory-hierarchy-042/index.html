Memory Hierarchy

Each miss fetches a 64-byte line and memory latency is 100 ns. With only one miss outstanding at a time, read bandwidth is about 0.64 GB/s. If 10 misses can be outstanding at once, what is the bandwidth approximately (assuming no other limits)?

  1. A0.064 GB/s
  2. B0.64 GB/s
  3. C6.4 GB/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 6.4 GB/s

Bandwidth equals the amount of data in flight divided by latency (Little's law). With one miss, 64 B ÷ 100 ns = 0.64 GB/s; with 10 outstanding, 10 × 64 B ÷ 100 ns = 6.4 GB/s. Saturating memory bandwidth requires not just lower latency but memory-level parallelism, servicing many misses at once. 0.64 GB/s is the single-miss value unchanged, and 0.064 GB/s divides by 10 instead of multiplying.

  • A. 0.064 GB/s divides by 10 instead of multiplying.
  • B. 0.64 GB/s is the value with only one outstanding miss.
  • C. Correct. 10 × 64 B ÷ 100 ns = 6.4 GB/s.
