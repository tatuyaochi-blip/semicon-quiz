Power Electronics Circuits

Which of the following correctly describes continuous conduction mode (CCM) of a DC-DC converter?

  1. AOperation in which a DC current of constant magnitude flows from the input supply at all times, regardless of the state of the switch
  2. BOperation in which the switch stays on all the time and current keeps flowing
  3. COperation in which the inductor current keeps flowing and never falls to zero during a period
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Operation in which the inductor current keeps flowing and never falls to zero during a period

In continuous conduction mode the inductor current ripples but never falls to zero within a period. When the load current decreases, the valleys of the current reach zero and stay there for a while, and the converter moves into discontinuous conduction mode (DCM). Formulas such as Vout = D × Vin for the buck converter and Vout = Vin/(1 − D) for the boost converter hold in continuous conduction mode. In discontinuous conduction mode the output voltage also depends on the load current.

  • A. In a buck converter the input current is pulsating, flowing only while the switch is on. The modes are distinguished by the inductor current.
  • B. The switch turns on and off periodically. A switch that stays on cannot convert the voltage.
  • C. Correct. The condition for continuous conduction mode is that the inductor current never falls to zero.
