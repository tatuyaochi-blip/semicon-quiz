Power Management

Which describes the idea behind adaptive voltage scaling (AVS)?

  1. ARunning every chip at a high voltage set for the slowest chip
  2. BChanging voltage randomly every time
  3. CMeasuring each chip's speed and temperature on chip and approaching the minimum voltage at which it works, trimming wasted voltage margin
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Measuring each chip's speed and temperature on chip and approaching the minimum voltage at which it works, trimming wasted voltage margin

Manufacturing variation makes some chips of the same product fast and others slow. Running every chip at a voltage that works even for the slowest wastes power on fast chips, whose voltage is higher than necessary. With AVS, on-chip ring oscillators or delay-measuring circuits check actual speed, and voltage is adjusted to just what that chip needs, accounting for temperature and aging.

  • A. That is the traditional, margin-heavy approach.
  • B. Voltage is not changed randomly.
  • C. Correct. It exploits chip-to-chip variation.
