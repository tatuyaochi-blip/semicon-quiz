Timing analysis and CDC

To pass a level signal reliably from a 400 MHz domain to a 100 MHz domain through a synchronizer, the signal width should be at least 1.5 times the receiving clock period. What is the minimum width in sending-clock cycles?

  1. A4 cycles
  2. B6 cycles
  3. C15 cycles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 6 cycles

The receiving period is 1 ÷ 100 MHz = 10 ns, and 1.5 times that is 15 ns. The sending period is 1 ÷ 400 MHz = 2.5 ns, so a width of at least 15 ÷ 2.5 = 6 cycles is needed. 4 cycles (10 ns) is exactly one receiving period; if both the start and end of the signal fall right at edges, both captures can fail and the signal can be missed. With 1.5 times (three receiving edges' worth), as long as setup/hold times and clock jitter are well below a quarter of the period, there is always at least one edge at least a quarter period away from both ends of the signal, so a stable value is captured. 15 cycles confuses ns with cycles.
