SRAM and flash memory

Which is an example of a "write assist" technique to prevent SRAM write failures at low voltage?

  1. APulling the bit line on the 0 side slightly below ground during a write
  2. BRaising the cell supply voltage during a write
  3. CLowering the word-line voltage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Pulling the bit line on the 0 side slightly below ground during a write

At low voltage, the access transistor may lack the drive to overpower the cell's pull-up, causing write failures. The negative bit-line technique pulls the bit line on the 0 side below ground (about −100 to −200 mV), increasing the access transistor's gate-source voltage and easing the write. Lowering the cell supply during writes to weaken the pull-up is also used; such assist circuits are essential in advanced-node SRAM.

  • A. Correct. A negative bit line assists the write.
  • B. Lowering, not raising, the cell supply during a write makes the cell easier to flip.
  • C. Lowering the word line weakens the access transistor and makes writing harder.
