SRAM and flash memory

In SRAM, which correctly describes the "data retention voltage" that sets the lower limit when the supply is lowered during standby to reduce leakage?

  1. AThe lowest supply voltage at which the cell keeps its contents without being read or written
  2. BThe maximum voltage needed for reading
  3. CThe voltage needed for writing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The lowest supply voltage at which the cell keeps its contents without being read or written

In standby, when it is neither read nor written, an SRAM cell can retain data at voltages below its normal operating voltage. The lowest voltage at which it does not lose data is the data retention voltage (DRV), and lowering the supply close to it in standby greatly reduces leakage.

  • A. Correct. It is the lowest supply voltage that preserves the contents.
  • B. The data retention voltage concerns the lowest voltage.
  • C. Writing requires a higher voltage than retention.
