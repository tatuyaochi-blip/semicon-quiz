Analog and electronic circuits

How does the magnitude of a capacitor's impedance (capacitive reactance) change as frequency increases?

  1. AIt increases
  2. BIt decreases
  3. CIt does not change
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It decreases

A capacitor's impedance is 1/(2πfC), shrinking as frequency rises: it blocks DC and passes high frequencies more easily. For example, 0.1 µF is about 1.6 kΩ at 1 kHz and about 0.16 Ω at 10 MHz. This makes capacitors ideal as bypass capacitors placed near IC supply pins to shunt high-frequency noise to ground, and in filters. Real capacitors have parasitic inductance, though, so their impedance rises again at very high frequencies.

  • A. An inductor's impedance is the one that increases with frequency.
  • B. Correct. Since it is 1/(2πfC), it decreases at higher frequencies.
  • C. A capacitor's impedance depends strongly on frequency.
