Analog and electronic circuits

Which noise, particularly noticeable in MOSFETs, grows larger at lower frequencies?

  1. A1/f noise (flicker noise)
  2. BShot noise
  3. CThermal noise
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1/f noise (flicker noise)

1/f noise has a power spectral density inversely proportional to frequency, so it grows at low frequencies. In MOSFETs it is attributed mainly to traps at the gate dielectric and interface capturing and releasing carriers, and since it scales inversely with gate area, larger transistors reduce it. The corner frequency where it equals thermal noise can range from kHz to MHz. Precision near-DC amplifiers avoid it by chopper stabilization, which moves the signal to higher frequency.

  • A. Correct. 1/f noise is larger at lower frequencies.
  • B. Shot noise is white noise, independent of frequency.
  • C. Thermal noise is white noise, constant across frequency.
