Analog and electronic circuits

When a constant current keeps flowing into a capacitor, how does its voltage change?

  1. AIt approaches zero exponentially
  2. BIt stays constant
  3. CIt rises linearly in proportion to time
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It rises linearly in proportion to time

A capacitor's charge is Q = CV, and a constant current I adds charge in proportion to time, so the voltage rises linearly: V = It/C. For example, 1 mA into 1 µF raises the voltage by 1 V every millisecond. Charging through a resistor is exponential only because the current falls as the voltage rises. This linearity is used to generate sawtooth waves, in integrating ADCs, and to create the triangle wave for pulse-width modulation.

  • A. Exponential change is the behavior when charging or discharging through a resistor.
  • B. Charge keeps accumulating, so the voltage keeps rising.
  • C. Correct. It rises linearly as V = It/C.
