Analog and electronic circuits

Which correctly describes a Butterworth low-pass filter?

  1. AIts passband gain is as flat as possible (maximally flat)
  2. BIt has ripple in the passband and a steep cutoff
  3. CIt has constant group delay and the least waveform distortion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Its passband gain is as flat as possible (maximally flat)

A Butterworth filter is designed for the flattest possible passband gain, hence "maximally flat." It is −3 dB at cutoff regardless of order and then rolls off at −20n dB/decade for order n. Chebyshev filters accept passband ripple for a steeper cutoff, and Bessel filters prioritize flat group delay (waveform fidelity); the choice depends on whether flatness, steepness or waveform matters most. Butterworth filters are common as anti-aliasing filters before ADCs.

  • A. Correct. Its passband is maximally flat.
  • B. Passband ripple characterizes the Chebyshev response.
  • C. Emphasis on flat group delay characterizes the Bessel response.
