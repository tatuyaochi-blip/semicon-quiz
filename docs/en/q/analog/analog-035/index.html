Analog and electronic circuits

How does the magnitude of an inductor's impedance change as frequency increases?

  1. AIt does not change
  2. BIt decreases
  3. CIt increases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It increases

An inductor's impedance 2πfL rises in proportion to frequency, passing DC easily but impeding high frequencies. For example, 10 µH is about 0.06 Ω at 1 kHz and about 630 Ω at 10 MHz. Inductors serve as chokes that block noise on power lines, as energy-storage elements in switching regulators, and in LC filters. Note that abruptly changing their current produces a high voltage, V = L·di/dt.

  • A. An inductor's impedance changes in proportion to frequency.
  • B. A capacitor's impedance is the one that decreases with frequency.
  • C. Correct. Since it is 2πfL, it increases with frequency.
