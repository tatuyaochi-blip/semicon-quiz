Analog and electronic circuits

What is the circuit called in which the input resistor of an op-amp inverting amplifier is replaced by a capacitor?

  1. ADifferentiator
  2. BIntegrator
  3. CComparator
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Differentiator

With a capacitor at the input and a resistor in feedback, the output is Vout = −RC·dVin/dt, proportional to the input's rate of change (its derivative). Gain rises with frequency, so it amplifies high-frequency noise strongly and, combined with the op-amp's limited bandwidth, tends to oscillate. Practical circuits add a resistor in series with the input capacitor to cap high-frequency gain. Differentiators are used for edge detection and the derivative term of PID control.
