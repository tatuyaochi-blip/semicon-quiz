Analog and electronic circuits

What is the effect of applying negative feedback to an amplifier?

  1. AGain is reduced, but gain stability improves, bandwidth widens and distortion decreases
  2. BGain becomes infinite
  3. CIt always starts to oscillate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gain is reduced, but gain stability improves, bandwidth widens and distortion decreases

Negative feedback subtracts part of the output from the input, reducing gain. In return, closed-loop gain becomes about 1/β (the inverse of the feedback fraction), largely independent of device variation and temperature; bandwidth widens; and distortion and output impedance shrink by a factor of (1 + loop gain). Op-amp circuits are built on this. At frequencies where the phase shifts enough to make feedback positive, however, the loop oscillates, so phase margin must be secured.
