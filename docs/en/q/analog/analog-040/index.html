Analog and electronic circuits

Which correctly states Thevenin's theorem?

  1. AA linear circuit of sources and resistors, seen from two terminals, can be replaced by a single voltage source in series with a single resistor
  2. BThe current is the same everywhere in a circuit
  3. CRearranging all the resistors in parallel does not change the current
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A linear circuit of sources and resistors, seen from two terminals, can be replaced by a single voltage source in series with a single resistor

Thevenin's theorem says a linear circuit, seen from two terminals, is equivalent to a voltage source equal to the open-circuit voltage Vth in series with the internal resistance Rth (measured with sources removed). The load current is then simply Vth/(Rth + RL), and it follows that power to the load is maximized when RL = Rth (the maximum power transfer theorem). Replacing it with a current source and parallel resistor is Norton's theorem.

  • A. Correct. It can be replaced with a voltage source and series resistor.
  • B. The current is the same everywhere only in a series circuit.
  • C. Changing how resistors are connected generally changes the current.
