Analog and electronic circuits

Which correctly describes the idea of dominant-pole compensation used in op-amps?

  1. AGather all poles at the same frequency
  2. BMake the gain 0 dB at all frequencies
  3. CPlace one pole at a sufficiently low frequency so the gain falls below unity before reaching the other poles, ensuring stability
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Place one pole at a sufficiently low frequency so the gain falls below unity before reaching the other poles, ensuring stability

Dominant-pole compensation adds a capacitor to move one pole to a very low frequency (a few to tens of Hz). Gain then rolls off at −20 dB/decade and drops below 0 dB before other poles add much phase shift, so even unity gain (a follower) has ample phase margin. Op-amps exploit the Miller effect to create this low-frequency pole with a small capacitor. The cost is limited bandwidth and slew rate.

  • A. Gathering poles at the same frequency makes the phase turn sharply and worsens stability.
  • B. Making the gain 0 dB at all frequencies would make the amplifier useless.
  • C. Correct. A dominant pole reduces the gain early to ensure stability.
