Compound semiconductors

What is the empirical rule called that says the lattice constant of an alloy semiconductor such as AlGaAs varies almost linearly with composition?

  1. AMoore's law
  2. BVegard's law
  3. COhm's law
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Vegard's law

Vegard's law is the empirical rule that an alloy's lattice constant interpolates linearly between those of its constituents by composition. For InxGa1-xAs, a straight line between GaAs (0.565 nm) and InAs (0.606 nm) gives x ≈ 0.53 to match InP (0.587 nm). Bandgaps usually sag below the straight line (bowing), so a quadratic correction term is added.

  • A. Moore's law concerns increases in integration density.
  • B. Correct. It states that an alloy's lattice constant varies in proportion to composition.
  • C. Ohm's law relates voltage, current and resistance.
