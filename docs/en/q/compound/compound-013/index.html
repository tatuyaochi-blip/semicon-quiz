Compound semiconductors

Which correctly describes the quantum well structure used in the light-emitting layers of semiconductor lasers and LEDs?

  1. AA very thin layer with a small band gap sandwiched between layers with a larger band gap, confining carriers to increase light-emission efficiency
  2. BA structure with thick metal layers stacked
  3. CA structure without a light-emitting layer
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A very thin layer with a small band gap sandwiched between layers with a larger band gap, confining carriers to increase light-emission efficiency

In a quantum well, carriers are confined in an emitting layer only a few nm thick, so electrons and holes meet readily and recombine efficiently. When the layer is comparable to the electron wavelength, energy levels become discrete, and thickness tunes the emission wavelength. In lasers this sharply lowers the threshold current, and multiple quantum wells (MQWs) are standard in LEDs and lasers.
