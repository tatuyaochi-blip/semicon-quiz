Compound semiconductors

When silicon is added to GaAs to make it n-type, which site does the silicon mainly occupy?

  1. AThe arsenic (As) site
  2. BGaps between atoms (interstitial sites)
  3. CThe gallium (Ga) site
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The gallium (Ga) site

When group-IV silicon occupies a group-III gallium site, it has an extra valence electron and acts as a donor, making GaAs n-type; on a group-V arsenic site it lacks one and acts as an acceptor. Dopants whose role depends on the site are called amphoteric. Silicon normally favors Ga sites and is the standard n-type dopant in GaAs, but some growth conditions push it to p-type, so conditions must be controlled.
