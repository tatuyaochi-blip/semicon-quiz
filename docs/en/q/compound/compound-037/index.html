Compound semiconductors

In LEDs with an InGaN emitting layer, how does the emission color change when the indium (In) fraction is increased?

  1. AIt does not change
  2. BToward ultraviolet (shorter wavelength)
  3. CToward longer wavelengths (from blue to green)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Toward longer wavelengths (from blue to green)

InN (about 0.7 eV) has a much smaller band gap than GaN (about 3.4 eV), so raising the indium fraction in InGaN narrows the gap and lengthens the emission wavelength: about 15–20% indium gives blue and around 30% gives green. Because indium and gallium atoms differ in size, higher indium causes compositional fluctuation, strain and strong polarization fields that reduce efficiency—the root of the "green gap."

  • A. The band gap changes with composition, so the color changes too.
  • B. Moving toward UV requires widening the band gap, for example by adding Al.
  • C. Correct. The band gap shrinks and the wavelength lengthens.
