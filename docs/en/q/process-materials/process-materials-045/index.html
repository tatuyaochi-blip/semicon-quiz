Process materials (gases and chemicals)

How are sources that are liquid at room temperature (such as TEOS) supplied as gas to CVD tools?

  1. AA carrier gas is bubbled through the liquid to carry its vapor (bubbling), or the liquid is vaporized in a vaporizer before supply
  2. BThe liquid is solidified and fed in as powder
  3. CThe liquid is poured directly into the reactor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A carrier gas is bubbled through the liquid to carry its vapor (bubbling), or the liquid is vaporized in a vaporizer before supply

Liquid precursors are delivered as gas either by bubbling, in which carrier gas passes through a temperature-controlled container and carries vapor, or by metering the liquid and flash-vaporizing it. Bubbling supply fluctuates with liquid temperature, while vaporizer systems give tighter control. TEOS is a liquid boiling at about 168 °C, so lines are heated to prevent recondensation. Pouring liquid into the reactor would not yield uniform films.

  • A. Correct. It is vaporized by bubbling or a vaporizer before supply.
  • B. Feeding powder is not a common method.
  • C. Pouring in liquid would not give uniform deposition.
