What is HARA (hazard analysis and risk assessment)?

The ISO 26262 analysis that identifies hazardous events at the vehicle level and rates severity, exposure and controllability to determine ASILs and safety goals. It is done at the very start of development.

Japanese name: HARA(ハザード分析とリスクアセスメント)

Where HARA sits in ISO 26262

HARA is carried out in the concept phase of ISO 26262, after the item, the vehicle-level function under development such as electronic throttle control, has been defined and before any architecture exists. It considers malfunctioning behaviors of the item, for example unintended acceleration or loss of braking, and combines each with operational situations such as highway driving, parking or a wet road to form hazardous events. The analysis looks at effects at the vehicle level, not at how hardware or software might fail.

Rating severity, exposure and controllability

Each hazardous event is classified by severity S (S0 to S3, from no injuries to life-threatening or fatal injuries), exposure E (E0 to E4, how often or how long the vehicle is in the relevant situation) and controllability C (C0 to C3, how likely the driver or others can avoid harm). A table combines the three into QM (quality management, no ASIL-specific requirements) or ASIL A to D, with D the most demanding. Sudden loss of braking at high speed, severe and hard to control, lands at a high ASIL.

From hazardous events to safety goals

Hazardous events rated ASIL A to D are covered by safety goals, top-level requirements such as "avoid unintended sudden acceleration", each inheriting the highest ASIL of the events it addresses. Safety goals flow down into the functional safety concept, technical safety requirements and finally hardware and software requirements. Quantities such as failure rates, diagnostic coverage and the fault-tolerant time interval (FTTI) come later, when checking whether a design meets those requirements, not when determining the ASIL.

HARA vs. TARA

HARA addresses hazards caused by random hardware failures and systematic faults such as design errors. Intentional attacks are handled by ISO/SAE 21434 through threat analysis and risk assessment (TARA), which identifies assets, threat scenarios and attack feasibility. The two activities are coordinated, because an attack that disables a safety mechanism becomes a safety problem. Hazards arising from performance limits of an intended function, such as sensor misperception without any fault, fall under the separate SOTIF standard, ISO 21448.

Answer: A. Severity (S), exposure (E), controllability (C)

The ASIL is determined for each hazardous event by evaluating three factors: severity S, representing how serious the injuries are; exposure E, representing the frequency or duration of exposure to driving situations in which the hazard can occur; and controllability C, representing how likely the driver or others are to avoid the hazard. For example, sudden loss of braking at high speed has high severity and is hard to avoid, so it receives a high ASIL. Failure rate, diagnostic coverage and FTTI are quantities used after the ASIL is determined, to evaluate whether a design meets its requirements.

FAQ

What are S, E and C in ISO 26262?

Severity of potential harm, exposure to the driving situation in which the hazard can occur, and controllability by the driver or others. Together they determine the ASIL of a hazardous event.

What does QM mean in ASIL classification?

QM, quality management, means the risk is low enough that normal quality processes suffice and no ASIL-specific safety requirements apply.

When is HARA performed?

At the very start of development, in the concept phase, once the item is defined. Its safety goals and ASILs drive all later safety requirements and design work.
