What is Effective mass?

An apparent mass used to treat electrons and holes in a crystal as if they were free particles responding to external forces. It is set by the curvature of the band; a smaller effective mass tends to give higher mobility.

Japanese name: 有効質量

Which correctly compares electron and hole mobility in silicon?

  1. AElectron mobility is higher
  2. BHole mobility is higher
  3. CThey are always equal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Electron mobility is higher

In silicon at room temperature and low doping, electron mobility is about 1400 cm²/V·s and hole mobility about 450 cm²/V·s, so electrons are about three times faster, mainly because holes have a larger effective mass. An nMOS of the same size therefore carries more current, and in CMOS the pMOS is typically made 2–3 times wider than the nMOS to balance drive strength. Advanced processes boost pMOS mobility with compressive strain from SiGe.

What is the mass introduced so that the motion of an electron in a crystal can be treated like that of a free electron in response to external forces?

  1. ARest mass
  2. BAtomic mass
  3. CEffective mass
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Effective mass

Electrons in a crystal feel the periodic potential of the atoms, so they respond to forces differently from electrons in vacuum. The effective mass lumps this effect into a different mass and is set by the band curvature (sharper curvature means lighter). A smaller effective mass gives higher mobility, which is why electrons in GaAs (about 0.067 times the free-electron mass) are faster than in silicon. Holes have lower mobility because their effective mass is larger.
