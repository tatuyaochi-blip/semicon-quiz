What is Dilute hydrofluoric acid (DHF)?

Hydrofluoric acid diluted with water. It removes native oxide from silicon and leaves a hydrophobic, hydrogen-terminated surface.

Japanese name: 希フッ酸(DHF)

When etched in the same dilute hydrofluoric acid, how do the etch rates of thermal oxide and CVD oxide generally compare?

  1. AThermal oxide etches more slowly (it is denser)
  2. BThermal oxide etches faster
  3. CThey always etch at the same rate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Thermal oxide etches more slowly (it is denser)

Thermal oxide is dense and nearly defect-free, so it etches slowly in dilute HF (for example, 1%)—only a few nm per minute. CVD oxides, depending on deposition conditions, may be less dense or contain hydrogen and moisture, so they usually etch faster. The wet etch rate ratio relative to thermal oxide (WERR) is used as an index of film quality, and annealing densifies CVD oxide toward thermal-oxide behavior.

What is the main purpose of cleaning with dilute hydrofluoric acid (DHF)?

  1. ARemoving organics
  2. BDepositing metal films
  3. CRemoving native oxide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Removing native oxide

HF dissolves SiO2, so tens of seconds in 0.5–1% dilute HF strips the roughly 1 nm native oxide from silicon. The cleaned surface is hydrogen-terminated and hydrophobic—water beading confirms removal. The treatment is often performed just before gate oxidation, epitaxy or silicide formation. SC-1 removes organics and particles, and SC-2 removes metals.
