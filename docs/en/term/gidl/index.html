What is GIDL (gate-induced drain leakage)?

Off-state leakage caused by band-to-band tunneling in the strong field where the gate overlaps the drain. It is a concern for data retention in DRAM, among others.

Japanese name: GIDL(ゲート誘起ドレインリーク)

GIDL (gate-induced drain leakage) appears in 3 practice questions across 2 topics, including MOSFET, DRAM.

Field: MOSFET

Where the tunneling happens

Consider an off nMOS with its gate at 0 V or below and its drain at a high voltage. In the region where the gate overlaps the heavily doped drain, the large gate-to-drain voltage creates a strong vertical field that depletes the drain surface and bends the energy bands so steeply that the valence band on one side lines up with the conduction band a few nanometres away. Electrons then tunnel band to band. They are collected by the drain, while the generated holes flow to the body, so GIDL (gate-induced drain leakage) appears as drain-to-body current.

How to recognize GIDL

On a log-scale drain current versus gate voltage plot, ordinary subthreshold leakage keeps falling as the gate is driven further off. GIDL does the opposite: making the gate more negative for an nMOS, or raising the drain voltage, increases the gate-to-drain field, so the off-current turns upward again. The total leakage therefore has a minimum at some intermediate gate voltage. GIDL is also distinct from DIBL (drain-induced barrier lowering), which lowers the threshold voltage and increases subthreshold current rather than adding a tunneling path.

Why DRAM cares about GIDL

A DRAM cell must hold its charge for tens of milliseconds between refreshes, so its access transistor must leak extremely little. Charge escapes mainly through subthreshold leakage and junction leakage at the storage node, which includes GIDL. Driving the word line slightly negative when the cell is off suppresses subthreshold current but raises the gate-to-drain voltage and therefore GIDL, so the two must be balanced. Because a small tail of the leakiest cells sets the refresh period, designers ease the fields with buried-gate cell transistors and carefully shaped junction doping.

Controlling GIDL

GIDL grows with the electric field at the gate edge, so the remedies reduce that field: lighter or graded doping where the drain meets the gate edge, reduced gate-to-drain overlap, a thicker dielectric at the gate corner, and limits on how far the gate is driven negative. Thin gate dielectrics make the field and the GIDL stronger. The same mechanism can also be useful; some 3D NAND designs deliberately generate holes by GIDL to raise the channel potential during erase.

Answer: C. GIDL (gate-induced drain leakage)

Even when a transistor is off, a large gate-to-drain voltage bends the bands sharply near the drain edge of the gate, and band-to-band tunneling causes current to flow—GIDL, which in an nMOS notably grows as the gate is driven more negative. It is a problem in devices requiring ultra-low leakage such as DRAM cell transistors and is controlled by engineering the drain-edge doping and field. DIBL is threshold lowering by drain voltage, and NBTI is a pMOS degradation mechanism.

Which combination lists the main components of leakage current in a MOSFET in the off state?

  1. ASubthreshold leakage, gate leakage and junction leakage
  2. BOnly photocurrent
  3. CNo leakage current flows at all
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Subthreshold leakage, gate leakage and junction leakage

Even an off MOSFET leaks: subthreshold leakage below threshold, gate leakage through the dielectric, and junction leakage at the source/drain junctions (including GIDL). Each device leaks less than a nanoampere, but billions of them add up to watts in standby. Countermeasures combine high-k dielectrics, multiple threshold voltages, power gating of idle blocks, and body-bias control.

FAQ

What is the difference between GIDL and DIBL?

GIDL is a leakage current from band-to-band tunneling in the gate-drain overlap region, and it grows as the gate is driven further off. DIBL is drain voltage lowering the source barrier, which reduces the threshold voltage and increases subthreshold current.

Why does GIDL increase with negative gate voltage?

For an nMOS, a more negative gate raises the gate-to-drain voltage, increasing the vertical field and band bending at the drain edge. Stronger band bending makes band-to-band tunneling more likely, so leakage rises.

Is GIDL part of subthreshold leakage?

No. Subthreshold leakage is carrier diffusion over the source barrier, while GIDL is a tunneling current at the drain, usually grouped with junction leakage. They respond to gate voltage in opposite directions.
