What is O-band?

The optical fiber communication band around 1260 to 1360 nm. It includes the region near 1.31 µm where chromatic dispersion in standard single-mode fiber is nearly zero, and is widely used for short links such as those inside data centers.

Japanese name: Oバンド

O-band appears in 3 practice questions across 1 topic, including Silicon photonics.

Field: Silicon photonics

Where it sits in the spectrum

Fiber-optic bands are labeled by letter: O (original, about 1260–1360 nm), E (extended, 1360–1460 nm), S (short, 1460–1530 nm), C (conventional, 1530–1565 nm), L (long, 1565–1625 nm) and U (ultra-long, above that). The O-band was the first band used for single-mode fiber systems, hence its name. It contains the zero-dispersion wavelength of standard single-mode fiber near 1310 nm, where pulses spread the least as they travel.

Why data centers favor it

Links inside and between nearby data center buildings run from a few hundred meters to roughly 10 km. At these distances fiber loss matters little, even though O-band loss, about 0.3–0.35 dB/km, is higher than the roughly 0.2 dB/km near 1550 nm. Near-zero chromatic dispersion matters more: fast intensity-modulated signals travel several kilometers without dispersion compensation, keeping transceivers simple and low in power. Coarse wavelength grids with 20 nm spacing are commonly placed in this band to carry several lanes on one fiber.

O-band vs. C-band

The C-band around 1550 nm wins for long-haul links thanks to the lowest loss and direct optical amplification by erbium-doped fiber amplifiers (EDFAs). In return, standard single-mode fiber has about 17 ps/(nm·km) of dispersion there, which long links must compensate, today usually in the digital signal processor of a coherent receiver. The O-band lacks an equally mature and widespread amplifier, which together with its higher loss confines it mostly to shorter reaches.

O-band in silicon photonics

At 1310 nm the photon energy is about 0.95 eV, below silicon's bandgap of about 1.12 eV, so silicon waveguides are transparent while germanium photodetectors still absorb the light. Many silicon photonic transceivers for data centers therefore operate in the O-band. Light reaches the chip through edge couplers, which give low loss over a broad range spanning the O- and C-bands, or grating couplers, which are more wavelength-selective but can be placed anywhere on the surface and tested on the wafer.

Answer: B. Chromatic dispersion in standard single-mode fiber is nearly zero, so high-speed signal waveforms are less distorted

Chromatic dispersion is the spreading of optical pulses caused by slight differences in propagation speed through the fiber at different wavelengths. In standard single-mode fiber, dispersion is nearly zero around 1.31 µm, so high-speed signals can easily be sent a few km to about 10 km without dispersion compensation, and many data center optical transceivers use this band. Loss is lowest around 1.55 µm (about 0.2 dB/km); at 1.31 µm it is somewhat higher, about 0.3–0.35 dB/km. The photon energy at 1.31 µm is about 0.95 eV, which silicon does not absorb.

FAQ

What wavelength range is the O-band?

Approximately 1260 to 1360 nm, centered around the 1310 nm zero-dispersion region of standard single-mode fiber.

Why is 1310 nm used for short-reach optical links?

Chromatic dispersion in standard single-mode fiber is nearly zero there, so high-speed signals keep their shape over several kilometers without compensation. The somewhat higher fiber loss is unimportant at those distances.

What does the O in O-band stand for?

Original: it was the first wavelength band used for single-mode fiber communication.
