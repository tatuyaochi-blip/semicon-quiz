What is PECVD (plasma-enhanced CVD)?

CVD that decomposes source gases with plasma to deposit films. It can deposit at lower temperatures than thermal CVD and is used for insulating films in interconnect steps and the like.

Japanese name: PECVD(プラズマCVD)

How plasma replaces heat

In plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD), RF power drives a glow discharge between a showerhead electrode and the heated wafer stage. Energetic plasma electrons break precursor molecules into reactive radicals and ions while the gas and wafer stay comparatively cool, so films grow at about 300–400 °C instead of the 600–800 °C typical of thermal CVD. That makes PECVD usable after aluminum or copper wiring is in place, when high temperatures are forbidden. The showerhead, whose many small holes spread gas evenly over the wafer, doubles as the upper electrode in most single-wafer PECVD chambers.

Typical films and chemistries

Silicon oxide is commonly deposited from silane with nitrous oxide (N2O) as a gentle oxidant, or from TEOS with oxygen. Silicon nitride comes from silane and ammonia, and adjusting the gas mix yields oxynitride (SiON), whose tunable refractive index suits antireflective layers for lithography. Carbon-doped oxide (SiOC) low-k dielectrics, with relative permittivity around 2.7–3.0 versus about 3.9 for SiO2, are mostly made by PECVD. PECVD nitride also serves as the final chip passivation and as a cap and diffusion barrier over copper lines.

PECVD vs. thermal CVD film quality

Low temperature has a cost. Precursors react incompletely, so PECVD films keep hydrogen as Si-H and N-H bonds: PECVD nitride may contain about 10–30% hydrogen, versus a few percent for low-pressure CVD (LPCVD) nitride, and is non-stoichiometric, often written SiNx. Such films are less dense and etch faster in wet chemicals. Conformality in narrow gaps is also generally poorer than LPCVD. Engineers therefore use thermal CVD where the thermal budget allows and reserve PECVD for steps where temperature, not film perfection, is the binding constraint.

Tuning stress with dual-frequency RF

Many PECVD tools apply a high frequency (13.56 MHz) and a low frequency (hundreds of kHz). Heavy ions can follow the slow field and bombard the growing film, densifying it and pushing it toward compressive stress. Changing the high-to-low power ratio, gas composition and temperature tunes nitride stress roughly from −3 GPa (compressive) to +1.5 GPa (tensile). This control is used for stress-liner films that strain the channel to boost transistor mobility, and for keeping wafer bow within limits in thick film stacks.

Answer: C. It can deposit films at lower temperatures than thermal CVD

Plasma-enhanced CVD uses energetic plasma electrons to decompose and activate precursors, so it deposits at about 300–400 °C rather than the 600–800 °C typical of thermal CVD. It is used for interlayer dielectrics and passivation after aluminum or copper wiring, when high temperatures are not allowed. Films tend to contain hydrogen from the precursors, and their quality is generally below that of thermal CVD films.

FAQ

Why can PECVD deposit at lower temperatures than thermal CVD?

Energetic plasma electrons, rather than wafer heat, supply the energy to break precursor bonds. The wafer can therefore stay around 300–400 °C, which is safe for finished metal interconnects.

What is the difference between PECVD and LPCVD silicon nitride?

LPCVD nitride is deposited hot and is dense and near-stoichiometric with little hydrogen. PECVD nitride is deposited at low temperature, contains roughly 10–30% hydrogen, etches faster, and is used where the thermal budget is limited, such as passivation.

How is film stress controlled in PECVD?

Mainly through the ratio of low-frequency to high-frequency RF power, which sets how strongly ions bombard the film, along with gas mix and temperature. More ion bombardment generally makes the film more compressive.
