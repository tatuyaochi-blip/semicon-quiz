What is Ceria (cerium oxide)?

An abrasive used in CMP slurries. It reacts chemically with oxide and gives high selectivity to nitride, so it is used for STI CMP.

Japanese name: セリア(酸化セリウム)

Field: CMP and cleaning

How ceria removes oxide

Ceria, cerium dioxide (CeO2), polishes silicon dioxide by a combined chemical and mechanical action. Its surface reacts with the oxide to form Ce-O-Si bonds, an activity commonly attributed in part to Ce3+ sites, so when the particle moves it tears away silica units rather than simply scratching. Because the removal is so chemically assisted, ceria slurries can polish oxide quickly at abrasive concentrations much lower than typical silica slurries. Ceria was used to polish optical glass long before it was adopted in semiconductor chemical mechanical planarization (CMP).

Selectivity in STI CMP

In shallow trench isolation (STI), trenches etched into silicon are overfilled with oxide, and CMP must remove the excess down to a silicon nitride layer that protects the active areas. Ceria slurries contain additives, such as amino acids or other surfactants, that adsorb on the nitride and suppress its removal while oxide continues to polish, giving oxide-to-nitride selectivity of tens to over 100. The nitride then works as a reliable stop. Afterwards the nitride is stripped, typically in hot phosphoric acid, leaving oxide-filled isolation trenches.

Ceria versus silica and alumina

Colloidal or fumed silica is the general-purpose abrasive, used for oxide interlayer dielectric CMP and, with oxidizers, for tungsten and copper. Alumina is harder and has been used in some metal slurries. Ceria is chosen when high oxide removal and selectivity to nitride matter most. With additives that adsorb on the surface, ceria slurries also tend to self-stop on patterns: once the surface is nearly flat, the local pressure drops and removal slows sharply, which helps reduce dishing compared with simple Preston-like behavior.

Defects and cleaning

The strong chemical affinity that makes ceria effective also makes particles cling to oxide surfaces after polishing, so post-CMP cleaning must remove residual ceria thoroughly, typically with dedicated chemistries and brush scrubbing. Large or agglomerated particles cause micro-scratches, so particle size distribution and filtration are tightly controlled. Fixed-abrasive pads embed ceria in the pad and supply only particle-free chemistry, giving strong planarization and less slurry waste, though pad-induced defects must be managed.

Answer: A. A polishing liquid with abrasive particles such as silica or ceria dispersed in a chemical solution

Slurry disperses abrasive particles tens to over a hundred nm in size—silica (SiO2), ceria (CeO2), alumina—in chemistry containing oxidizers, pH adjusters, corrosion inhibitors and dispersants. Particles and chemistry are chosen per film: silica or ceria for oxide, oxidizer-containing silica slurries for tungsten and copper. Slurry is a major CMP consumable cost, and Japanese companies are strong suppliers.

  1. ACeria (cerium oxide)
  2. BIron powder
  3. CCoarse diamond grit

Answer: A. Ceria (cerium oxide)

Ceria (cerium oxide) bonds chemically with oxide and removes it efficiently, while additives such as amino-acid surfactants adsorb on nitride and suppress its polishing, giving oxide-to-nitride selectivity of tens to over 100. It is used in STI CMP, where nitride serves as the stop, to combine planarity with a reliable stop. Ceria also polishes quickly at low particle concentrations.

FAQ

Why is ceria used instead of silica for STI CMP?

Ceria reacts chemically with oxide for fast removal, and with additives it barely polishes silicon nitride, so it can stop reliably on the nitride layer. Silica slurries give much lower oxide-to-nitride selectivity.

What is the oxide to nitride selectivity of ceria slurry?

With selectivity-enhancing additives, ceria slurries typically reach oxide-to-nitride selectivity of tens to over 100.

Is cerium oxide the same as ceria?

Yes. Ceria is the common name for cerium dioxide, CeO2, the cerium oxide used as an abrasive in CMP and glass polishing.
