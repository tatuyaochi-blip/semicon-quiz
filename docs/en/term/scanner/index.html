What is Scanner?

An exposure tool that moves the mask and wafer in sync under a slit of light. It is the mainstream production lithography tool and generally projects the mask pattern at 1/4 size.

Japanese name: スキャナー

Try it: questions about Scanner

Which is a characteristic of scanner-type exposure tools?

  1. AThey expose the whole wafer at once
  2. BThey write directly without a mask
  3. CThey expose while moving the mask and wafer synchronously relative to a slit of light
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. They expose while moving the mask and wafer synchronously relative to a slit of light

A scanner illuminates only a slit a few mm wide and moves the mask and wafer synchronously in opposite directions at a 4:1 speed ratio to expose each 26 × 33 mm field. It uses only the low-aberration center of the lens, exposes a long field in the scan direction and averages variations across the field. The stages accelerate at several m/s² while holding nanometer precision. A stepper, by contrast, exposes each field statically in one flash.

In current scanner exposure tools, what is the typical maximum area on the wafer that can be exposed in one exposure (one shot)?

  1. AThe whole wafer (300 mm diameter)
  2. BAbout 1 mm × 1 mm
  3. CAbout 26 mm × 33 mm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. About 26 mm × 33 mm

A typical scanner exposes about 26 mm × 33 mm (roughly 858 mm²) per shot and steps across the wafer to cover it. This sets the practical maximum die size (the reticle limit), which large GPUs approach. Larger systems are built from chiplets connected in the package. High-NA EUV fields are half this size.
