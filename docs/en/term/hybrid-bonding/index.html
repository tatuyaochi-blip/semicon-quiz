What is Hybrid bonding?

A technique that directly bonds copper electrodes and dielectric surfaces without bumps, providing huge numbers of connections at extremely fine pitch.

Japanese name: ハイブリッドボンディング

How hybrid bonding works

Each surface is planarized by chemical mechanical polishing (CMP) so that copper pads sit a few nanometres below the surrounding dielectric. The surfaces are cleaned and activated with plasma, aligned and pressed together, and the dielectrics bond near room temperature. A subsequent anneal strengthens that bond, while thermal expansion pushes the recessed copper pads into contact, where atomic diffusion joins them. The result is simultaneous dielectric-to-dielectric and copper-to-copper bonding with no solder, no bumps and no underfill, which is why it is called hybrid.

Hybrid bonding vs. microbumps

Solder microbumps, bonded for example by thermocompression, need space so molten solder does not bridge neighbours, which keeps their pitch at tens of micrometres, around 20 µm at best. Hybrid bonding goes below 10 µm and down to a few micrometres. Because connection count scales inversely with the square of pitch, shrinking from 40 µm to 4 µm gives 100 times more connections per area. The joints are also very short, cutting parasitic capacitance and inductance, which raises bandwidth and lowers energy per bit transferred between dies.

Wafer-to-wafer vs. chip-to-wafer

Wafer-to-wafer (W2W) bonding joins whole wafers at once, giving high throughput and the best alignment, but it pairs good dies with bad ones and requires identical die sizes, so stacked yield is the product of both wafers' yields. Chip-to-wafer (C2W) bonding places tested, singulated known-good dies onto a target wafer, improving stacked yield and allowing different die sizes, at the cost of slower placement. Both demand sub-nanometre roughness and extreme cleanliness: a single particle of about 100 nm can leave an unbonded void millimetres across.

Where you will meet it

Stacked CMOS image sensors, which bond a pixel wafer to a logic wafer, were the first major commercial use. Processors now stack a cache SRAM die directly on the compute die, adding several times the cache capacity without enlarging the processor, and hybrid bonding is being extended to HBM memory stacks. It also connects photonic and electronic chips with minimal parasitics. These are 3D packages, stacking dies vertically, as opposed to 2.5D packages that place dies side by side on an interposer; the two are often combined in one product.

Answer: B. Chip-to-wafer (C2W) bonding

Placing dies one at a time, however, makes it slower than W2W.

FAQ

What is the difference between hybrid bonding and microbumps?

Microbumps join dies with small solder bumps at pitches of tens of micrometres. Hybrid bonding directly fuses copper pads and surrounding dielectric without solder, reaching pitches below 10 µm and far higher connection density.

What pitch can hybrid bonding achieve?

Production hybrid bonding works below 10 µm pitch, down to a few micrometres, compared with roughly 20 µm or more for solder microbumps.

What is the difference between wafer-to-wafer and chip-to-wafer bonding?

Wafer-to-wafer bonds entire wafers at once for speed and alignment accuracy but cannot exclude bad dies. Chip-to-wafer places only known-good dies, improving yield and allowing different die sizes, but is slower.
