What is Subthreshold swing?

The gate voltage needed to change drain current tenfold below threshold. Its theoretical lower limit at room temperature is about 60 mV per decade; the smaller it is, the sharper the on/off switching.

Japanese name: サブスレッショルドスイング

Which value is closest to the theoretical lower limit of a MOSFET's subthreshold swing at room temperature (the gate voltage needed to change the drain current by one decade below threshold)?

  1. AAbout 600 mV/dec
  2. BAbout 6 mV/dec
  3. CAbout 60 mV/dec
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. About 60 mV/dec

Below threshold, drain current still rises exponentially with gate voltage. The gate voltage needed to change it by a decade, the subthreshold swing S = (kT/q)·ln10·(1 + Cd/Cox), is about 60 mV/dec at room temperature even in the ideal case (Cd = 0). Every 60 mV of Vth reduction thus raises off-current tenfold, a wall that blocks further supply-voltage scaling. Devices such as tunnel FETs are being researched to beat this limit.

Which devices are being researched with the aim of breaking the 60 mV/dec subthreshold swing barrier?

  1. ADiodes
  2. BThyristors
  3. CTunnel FETs and negative-capacitance FETs
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Tunnel FETs and negative-capacitance FETs

Conventional MOSFETs rely on carriers thermally surmounting a barrier, so at room temperature they cannot switch more steeply than 60 mV/dec, a barrier to lowering supply voltage. Tunnel FETs conduct by band-to-band tunneling between source and channel, escaping the thermal distribution, while negative-capacitance FETs use a ferroelectric's negative capacitance to amplify gate voltage internally—both aiming below the limit. Low on-current is among the remaining challenges.
