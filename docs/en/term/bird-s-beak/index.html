What is Bird's beak?

In LOCOS, the beak-shaped encroachment of oxide under the edge of the nitride mask. Because it wastes active area, it led to the switch to STI at finer geometries.

Japanese name: バーズビーク

Bird's beak appears in 1 practice question across 1 topic, including Oxidation and diffusion.

Field: Oxidation and diffusion

How the bird's beak forms

In LOCOS (local oxidation of silicon), a thin pad oxide and a silicon nitride film are stacked on the silicon, the nitride is patterned to cover the areas where devices will be built, and the wafer is oxidized, typically in high-temperature steam. Because nitride blocks oxygen and water, a thick field oxide grows only where the silicon is uncovered. Oxidant also diffuses sideways through the pad oxide, however, so oxidation proceeds under the edge of the nitride as well. The oxide therefore encroaches under the mask as a tapering wedge and lifts the nitride edge. Its cross-section resembles a bird's beak, hence the name.

Effects on devices

The bird's beak can extend about half the field oxide thickness on each side. With a 500 nm field oxide, for example, as much as about 250 nm is lost on each side, or about 500 nm in total, from the device area. Because devices cannot be built to the mask dimensions, isolation width and device width could not be shrunk, which limited integration density. As transistors get narrower, the bird's beak and the channel-stop dopant beneath it also matter relatively more, causing the narrow-channel effect in which threshold voltage rises.

Ways to reduce the encroachment

The bird's beak tends to grow longer with a thicker pad oxide, a thinner nitride and a thicker grown oxide. A thinner pad oxide and thicker nitride reduce the encroachment, but there is a limit, because the nitride's stress then acts on the silicon and readily creates crystal defects. Many refined versions were developed, such as poly-buffered LOCOS, which inserts polysilicon between the pad oxide and the nitride, and schemes that also cover the sidewalls with nitride, but none could eliminate the bird's beak entirely.

Replacement by STI

Because of the bird's beak and the surface step it leaves, device isolation moved from LOCOS to STI (shallow trench isolation) from around the 0.25 µm generation. STI etches trenches about 200–300 nm deep into the silicon, fills them with CVD oxide and planarizes them by CMP, giving narrow, flat isolation with no lateral encroachment. STI has the opposite concern: the gate field concentrates at the trench corners, so narrower transistors show lower threshold voltage, the inverse narrow-width effect. Because the process is simple, LOCOS is still used in older processes with larger dimensions.

Answer: C. Bird's beak

In LOCOS, nitride-covered areas resist oxidation, but oxidant diffuses laterally through the pad oxide beneath the nitride edge, so oxide encroaches in a bird's-beak shape. The encroachment can reach about half the oxide thickness on each side, eating into active area, so STI replaced LOCOS around the 0.25 µm generation. The pad oxide is needed to protect silicon from the nitride's stress.

FAQ

Why does the bird's beak form?

During LOCOS oxidation, oxidant diffuses sideways through the pad oxide under the nitride and oxidizes the silicon under the mask edge as well. The encroaching oxide tapers into a wedge, giving a cross-section shaped like a bird's beak.

Would removing the pad oxide prevent the bird's beak?

It would reduce the encroachment, but it is not practical. With nitride directly on silicon, the nitride's large stress readily creates dislocations and other crystal defects in the silicon. The pad oxide serves to relieve this stress.

What is the difference between the bird's beak and the Kooi effect?

Both occur at the nitride edge in LOCOS. The bird's beak is oxide encroaching under the nitride, which narrows the active area. The Kooi effect (white ribbon) is nitridation of the silicon surface under the nitride edge by ammonia formed during oxidation, which later thins the gate oxide locally; it is removed with a sacrificial oxidation.
