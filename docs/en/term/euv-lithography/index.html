What is EUV lithography?

Lithography using extreme ultraviolet light with a wavelength of 13.5 nm. It uses reflective optics and a vacuum environment.

Japanese name: EUV露光

Try it: questions about EUV lithography

What wavelength of light is used in EUV lithography?

  1. A193 nm
  2. B248 nm
  3. C13.5 nm
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 13.5 nm

EUV (extreme ultraviolet) lithography uses 13.5 nm light—about 1/14 of ArF's wavelength—to print fine patterns in a single exposure. Since nearly all materials, including air and glass, absorb it, EUV uses reflective optics of Mo/Si multilayer mirrors instead of lenses and operates in vacuum. Each mirror reflects only about 70%, so after nearly ten mirrors most light is lost and a very powerful source is needed.

Which correctly describes masks used in EUV lithography?

  1. AReflective masks with an absorber pattern on a multilayer mirror that reflects light
  2. BTransmissive masks through which light passes glass
  3. CNo mask is used
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Reflective masks with an absorber pattern on a multilayer mirror that reflects light

EUV light is absorbed by almost everything, so transmissive masks cannot be used. An EUV mask is reflective: a low-thermal-expansion glass substrate carries about 40 Mo/Si pairs with a period of about 7 nm forming a multilayer mirror (about 65% reflectivity), topped by a patterned absorber such as a tantalum-based film. Light strikes it at about 6° and reflects into the projection optics. Reducing multilayer defects is a major manufacturing challenge.
