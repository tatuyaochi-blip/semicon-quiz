What is Cutoff frequency?

The frequency at which the output of a filter or amplifier falls 3 dB (to about 0.707 times) below the passband. For an RC circuit it is 1/(2πRC).

Japanese name: カットオフ周波数

Field: Analog and electronic circuits

Deriving fc for an RC low-pass filter

The transfer function of an RC low-pass filter is H = 1/(1 + jωRC). Its magnitude equals 1/√2 when ωRC = 1, so the cutoff frequency is fc = 1/(2πRC), where the phase lag is exactly 45°. With R = 1 kΩ and C = 1 µF, RC = 1 ms and fc ≈ 159 Hz. Swapping the positions of R and C makes a high-pass filter with the same fc; an RL filter has fc = R/(2πL).

Why the −3 dB point

The cutoff is defined where the output voltage is 1/√2 ≈ 0.707 of the passband value. In decibels this is 20·log10(0.707) ≈ −3.01 dB, and because power goes with voltage squared it is also the half-power point. Do not confuse it with −6 dB, which is half the voltage. Some filter families, such as Chebyshev designs, are often specified at the edge of their passband ripple rather than at −3 dB, so check the definition used.

Slope beyond the cutoff and filter order

Well above fc a first-order filter rolls off at 20 dB per decade, or about 6 dB per octave, which is gentle: at ten times fc the signal is only reduced to one tenth. Each additional pole adds another 20 dB per decade, so an nth-order filter falls at 20n dB per decade. Sharper cutoffs, as needed for anti-aliasing in front of an ADC, are built as second- or higher-order active filters using op-amps.

Cutoff frequency as amplifier bandwidth

For amplifiers the upper −3 dB frequency defines the bandwidth, and coupling capacitors create a lower cutoff as well. For a voltage-feedback op-amp the closed-loop bandwidth is roughly the gain-bandwidth product divided by the noise gain, so higher gain means less bandwidth. A single-pole system also links bandwidth to step response: the 10–90% rise time is about 0.35 divided by the −3 dB frequency, a handy conversion between time and frequency domains.

FAQ

What is the formula for the cutoff frequency of an RC filter?

fc = 1/(2πRC). For example, 1 kΩ and 1 µF give about 159 Hz, where the output is 3 dB down with a 45° phase shift.

Why is the cutoff frequency defined at −3 dB?

At −3 dB the output voltage is 1/√2 of the passband value, so the delivered power is halved; this half-power point also falls exactly where the RC term equals one, giving a simple formula.

Is the cutoff frequency the same as bandwidth?

For a low-pass filter or amplifier, the bandwidth is usually quoted as the −3 dB cutoff frequency. For a band-pass filter it is the difference between the upper and lower −3 dB frequencies.
