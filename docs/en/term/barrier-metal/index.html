What is Barrier metal?

A thin metal film around copper wiring that prevents copper from diffusing into insulating films or silicon, such as Ta or TaN.

Japanese name: バリアメタル

In Cu interconnect CMP, why is polishing generally divided into stages?

  1. ABecause Cu and the barrier metal beneath it (such as TaN) need different polishing conditions, so most of the Cu is removed first and then the barrier
  2. BTo give the polishing tool a rest
  3. CTo make the Cu thicker
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Because Cu and the barrier metal beneath it (such as TaN) need different polishing conditions, so most of the Cu is removed first and then the barrier

Copper CMP sequentially removes the thick overburden copper (hundreds of nm to about 1 µm), the few-nm barrier metal (e.g., Ta/TaN) and a little of the dielectric beneath. Because optimal rates and selectivities differ by material, the process is split into a fast bulk-copper step, a gentler copper clearing step and a final barrier step that removes barrier and slight dielectric to planarize, each with its own slurry.

What is the main purpose of barrier metals such as Ta and TaN in copper wiring?

  1. ATo reduce wire resistance
  2. BTo prevent copper from diffusing into insulators and silicon
  3. CTo oxidize the copper surface
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. To prevent copper from diffusing into insulators and silicon

Copper diffuses readily into dielectrics and silicon; in silicon it forms deep levels that cause transistor leakage and degradation, and in dielectrics it compromises insulation between wires. Copper lines are therefore lined on sides and bottom with a few nm of barrier metal such as Ta/TaN and capped on top with a dielectric film. The barrier also improves adhesion to the dielectric and helps texture the copper grains.
