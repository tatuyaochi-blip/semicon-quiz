What is Ion implantation?

A technique that ionizes impurities, accelerates them and implants them into the wafer. The dose and depth can be controlled precisely.

Japanese name: イオン注入

Ion implantation appears in 22 practice questions across 6 topics, including Ion implantation, Epitaxial growth, Oxidation and diffusion.

Field: Ion implantation

How an implanter works

Dopant ions are extracted from a plasma in the ion source, an analyzer magnet selects only the desired mass, an accelerator brings them to the required energy, and the beam is scanned across the wafer, or the wafer moved through it, for uniform coverage. Faraday cups measure beam current, which is integrated to give dose with roughly ±1 percent accuracy and in-wafer variation down to about 0.5 percent. Because positive ions charge isolated gates and resist, a plasma flood gun supplies low-energy electrons to neutralize them. Reaching several MeV needs multistage RF linear accelerators or tandem accelerators.

Energy sets depth, dose sets concentration

The projected range Rp, the average stopping depth, grows with energy: boron in silicon stops around 30 to 40 nm at 10 keV and about 0.3 µm at 100 keV, and lighter ions go deeper at equal energy. Dose, counted in ions per square centimetre, spans roughly 10¹¹ to 10¹⁶ cm⁻² and is set independently of depth. Thermal diffusion, by contrast, ties surface concentration to depth and needs high temperatures. Since implantation runs near room temperature, photoresist can serve as the mask, provided it is several times thicker than the ion range.

Channeling, damage and annealing

Ions travelling along crystal rows can slip through open channels and penetrate far deeper than intended. Tilting the wafer about 7° with a twist offset, implanting through a thin screen oxide or pre-amorphizing the surface suppresses this channeling tail. Every implant also displaces lattice atoms, and most implanted dopants land in interstitial sites where they are electrically inactive. A post-implant anneal repairs the damage and activates the dopants; seconds-long rapid thermal anneals and millisecond flash or laser anneals limit the diffusion that would otherwise deepen shallow junctions.

Where you will meet it

CMOS processes use many implants: wells and retrograde wells, threshold adjustment, source/drain extensions and contacts. MeV implants form deep photodiodes and pixel isolation in image sensors and superjunction pillars in power devices. Hydrogen implantation splits wafers in the Smart Cut process for SOI. Alternatives appear where beamline implantation struggles: plasma doping for high doses at very low energy on 3D fins, and in-situ doped epitaxy for source/drain regions near 10²¹ cm⁻³. In SiC, where dopants barely diffuse, implantation is often done hot and the implanted profile is used as is.

  1. AAcceleration energy
  2. BDose
  3. CPost-implant cleaning time

Answer: A. Acceleration energy

Implant depth is set mainly by acceleration energy—the higher the energy, the deeper the ions go. As a guide, boron implanted into silicon at 10 keV stops around 30–40 nm deep, and at 100 keV about 0.3 µm. At equal energy lighter ions penetrate deeper, so ion species also matters. Dose—ions per unit area—sets concentration independently of depth, a key strength of ion implantation.

What is the main purpose of annealing (heat treatment) after ion implantation?

  1. ATo evaporate and remove the implanted dopants
  2. BTo repair crystal damage and place dopants on lattice sites to activate them electrically
  3. CTo grow a thick oxide on the wafer surface
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. To repair crystal damage and place dopants on lattice sites to activate them electrically

Implanted ions disrupt the silicon lattice and mostly sit at interstitial sites where they do not act as donors or acceptors, so they are largely electrically inactive right after implantation. Annealing repairs the damage and moves dopants onto lattice sites, activating them. To avoid excessive diffusion, lamp-based RTA lasting seconds or flash-lamp and laser anneals lasting milliseconds or less are used.

FAQ

What determines the depth of ion implantation?

Mainly the acceleration energy: higher energy gives a deeper projected range. Ion mass also matters, since lighter ions penetrate further at the same energy, and channeling can add an unwanted deep tail.

What is the difference between ion implantation and thermal diffusion?

Implantation shoots accelerated ions into the wafer near room temperature, controlling dose and depth independently and allowing resist masks. Diffusion drives dopants in from the surface at high temperature, coupling surface concentration and depth, and needs oxide masks.

Why is annealing needed after ion implantation?

Implantation damages the crystal and leaves most dopants on interstitial sites where they are inactive. Annealing repairs the lattice and moves dopants onto substitutional sites, activating them.
