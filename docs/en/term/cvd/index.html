What is CVD?

Chemical vapor deposition. A method of depositing thin films on a wafer through chemical reactions of source gases.

Field: Deposition (CVD and PVD)

How CVD works

In chemical vapor deposition, precursor gases flow over a heated wafer, adsorb on the surface, react and leave a solid film while volatile by-products are pumped away. For example, tungsten is deposited by reducing WF6 with hydrogen, releasing HF. This distinguishes CVD from physical vapor deposition (PVD) such as sputtering, where atoms are knocked off a solid target and fly in straight lines without chemical change. Because the film grows by reaction on whatever surface the gas reaches, CVD can coat the sidewalls and bottoms of features far better than PVD.

Reaction-limited vs. transport-limited growth

At relatively low temperature the surface reaction is the slow step, so growth rate follows the Arrhenius law and depends exponentially on temperature; thickness uniformity then depends on temperature uniformity. At high temperature the reaction is fast and the supply of gas limits growth, so gas-flow uniformity becomes critical. Reaction-limited conditions, favored by lower temperature and lower pressure, give the best conformality because gas penetrates deep into trenches before reacting. Under supply-limited conditions, film builds up at openings and can pinch them off, trapping voids.

Main CVD variants

Low-pressure CVD (LPCVD) at tens to hundreds of pascals coats more than 100 wafers uniformly at about 600 to 800 °C, depositing polysilicon, silicon nitride from dichlorosilane and ammonia, and TEOS oxide. Plasma-enhanced CVD (PECVD) uses plasma electrons to break precursors at about 300 to 400 °C, so it can be used after metallization, at the cost of hydrogen in the film. High-density plasma CVD sputters corners while depositing for gap fill, and epitaxial CVD grows single-crystal layers, at about 1,500 to 1,650 °C for SiC. Atomic layer deposition (ALD) is a self-limiting cousin with near-100 percent step coverage.

Where you will meet it

CVD fills shallow trench isolation, deposits interlayer dielectrics, spacers and passivation, forms tungsten contact plugs and 3D NAND word lines, and grows epitaxial device layers. Film quality is a recurring concern: CVD oxide is usually less dense than thermal oxide and etches faster in dilute HF, so the wet etch rate ratio (WERR) to thermal oxide serves as a quality index, and annealing densifies the film. Choosing between CVD variants is mostly a balance of temperature limits, conformality, throughput and film purity.

Try it: questions about CVD

In epitaxial growth by CVD, the growth rate depends strongly on temperature in the relatively low-temperature regime. Why?

  1. ABecause the growth rate is determined only by the source gas supply
  2. BBecause the rate of the chemical reaction at the surface determines the growth rate (surface-reaction limited)
  3. CBecause the growth rate does not depend on temperature
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Because the rate of the chemical reaction at the surface determines the growth rate (surface-reaction limited)

At low temperature, surface reactions are slow and limit the growth rate (reaction-limited). Reaction rates follow the Arrhenius law, depending exponentially on temperature, so growth rate does too, and temperature uniformity across the wafer translates directly into thickness uniformity. At high temperature reactions are fast and the arrival rate of precursor gas limits growth (mass-transport-limited), making gas-flow uniformity critical.

Which is closest to the typical growth temperature for SiC epitaxy (CVD)?

  1. AAbout 600 °C
  2. BAbout 1,000 °C
  3. CAbout 1,500 to 1,650 °C
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. About 1,500 to 1,650 °C

SiC's strong bonds keep surface atoms immobile, so high-quality epitaxy requires about 1,500–1,650 °C, roughly 500 °C hotter than silicon. Precursors are silane or trichlorosilane with a hydrocarbon such as propane, and the C/Si ratio controls dopant incorporation. To withstand these temperatures, reactor parts are graphite coated with SiC or TaC.

FAQ

What is the difference between CVD and PVD?

CVD forms films through chemical reactions of precursor gases on the wafer surface, while PVD, such as sputtering, physically transfers atoms from a solid source without a reaction. CVD generally gives much better step coverage in deep features.

Why is PECVD done at lower temperature than LPCVD?

In PECVD, energetic plasma electrons supply the energy to decompose precursors, so the wafer needs only about 300 to 400 °C instead of the 600 to 800 °C of thermal LPCVD. This makes it usable after aluminum or copper wiring is in place.

What is the difference between CVD and ALD?

CVD supplies precursors together and grows film continuously, while ALD alternates precursor pulses so each cycle adds about one self-limited atomic layer. ALD is slower but gives nearly perfect conformality and atomic-scale thickness control.
