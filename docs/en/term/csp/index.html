What is CSP (chip-scale package)?

A general term for packages shrunk to nearly the size of the chip. They are widely used in compact devices such as smartphones.

Japanese name: CSP(チップサイズパッケージ)

What qualifies as chip-scale

CSP is a family name rather than a single structure. A package counts as chip-scale when its footprint is only slightly larger than the die, commonly no more than about 1.2 times the die area. Several constructions meet that definition: a die mounted on a small substrate with an array of solder balls underneath, lead-frame-based packages with terminals on the bottom, and wafer-level CSP (WLCSP), where redistribution wiring and solder balls are formed directly on the wafer so the finished package is exactly the size of the die.

CSP vs. older packages

Traditional packages such as the DIP (dual in-line package) and QFP (quad flat package) bring leads out from the body edges, so pin count is tied to perimeter and the body is much larger than the die. CSPs use an area array of terminals on the bottom, like a BGA (ball grid array) but scaled down to the die footprint. Short connections between die and board give lower parasitic inductance and capacitance. The trade-off is that the terminal pitch must be fine, commonly about 0.4–0.5 mm, which demands precise board design and assembly.

Board-level reliability

Silicon expands with temperature far less than an FR-4 circuit board, roughly 3 ppm/K versus around 15 ppm/K. In a WLCSP the die is soldered almost directly to the board, so thermal cycling strains the solder joints, especially the outermost balls. Large-die WLCSPs are therefore limited in size or reinforced with underfill, an epoxy flowed beneath the package to share the stress. Mechanical drop shock is another key qualification test for CSPs in handheld products.

Fan-in limits and where you will meet it

A fan-in WLCSP can only place balls within the die outline, so dies with many I/O but a small area run out of room; fan-out packages extend the redistribution layer beyond the die edge to solve this. CSPs are everywhere that board area and height are scarce: smartphones, wearables, earbuds and camera modules, typically for power management chips, sensors, RF parts and memory. Large processors with very high pin counts generally use larger flip-chip BGA packages instead.

Answer: B. A package shrunk to nearly the same size as the chip

CSP is the general term for packages shrunk to nearly the size of the chip, generally no more than about 1.2 times its area. Most use BGA-style array terminals on the bottom and are common in smartphones and wearables, where board area is precious. Fan-in WLP, which forms terminals while still on the wafer, is a type of CSP that matches the die size exactly. CSPs are unrelated to large multi-chip packages or QFPs.

FAQ

What is the difference between CSP and BGA?

BGA describes the terminal style, an array of solder balls under the package, while CSP describes the size, a footprint close to the die. Many CSPs use BGA-style balls, but a BGA package is often much larger than its die.

Is WLCSP a type of CSP?

Yes. A wafer-level CSP forms its wiring and solder balls while still on the wafer, so after dicing the package is exactly the size of the die.

How big is a chip-scale package compared with the die?

It is commonly defined as no larger than about 1.2 times the die area, and a wafer-level CSP is the same size as the die.
