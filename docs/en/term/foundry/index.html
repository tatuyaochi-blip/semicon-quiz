What is Foundry?

A company or business model that specializes in manufacturing semiconductors designed by other companies.

Japanese name: ファウンドリ

Foundry appears in 5 practice questions across 2 topics, including Semiconductor industry and market, IC design flow and EDA.

Field: Semiconductor industry and market

How the foundry model works

A foundry manufactures wafers from circuit layouts supplied by its customers, who own the designs and sell the finished chips. The customer submits layout data, photomasks are made from it, and the foundry runs the wafers through front-end and back-end-of-line processing, measures test structures in a wafer acceptance test (WAT) to confirm parameters such as threshold voltage and sheet resistance, and ships the wafers. Probe testing, dicing and packaging may be done by the foundry or by an outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) company. A pure-play foundry sells no chips of its own, so it never competes with its customers.

The PDK: what designers receive

Designers cannot target a process without knowing its rules, so the foundry supplies a process design kit (PDK). It contains the design rules, SPICE models for transistors and passive devices including variation models, rule decks for design rule checking (DRC) and layout versus schematic (LVS), parasitic extraction data, and layouts of basic devices. Standard-cell libraries, memory compilers and interface IP may come from the foundry or third parties. The PDK is the contract: a design that passes its checks is expected to be manufacturable and to behave as the models predict.

Shared costs and MPW shuttles

A leading-edge fab costs so much that few companies can fill one with their own products, and a foundry spreads that cost across many customers. The same idea applies to prototyping. A multi-project wafer (MPW), or shuttle, places designs from several customers on one mask set and wafer run, so they share mask and processing costs, which for advanced-node mask sets alone can reach tens of millions of dollars. Each participant receives only a small number of chips, enough for evaluation but not production, which makes MPWs popular with universities and startups.

Foundry, fabless, IDM and OSAT

These four business models divide the industry's work. A fabless company designs and sells chips but owns no fab and relies on foundries. An integrated device manufacturer (IDM) both designs and manufactures its own products, and some IDMs also offer foundry services. A foundry manufactures for others, and an OSAT packages and tests. The pure-play foundry model was established in 1987, and today leading-edge logic manufacturing is heavily concentrated in Taiwan, which is why governments elsewhere treat foundry capacity as a supply-chain risk.

Answer: A. A company specializing in contract manufacturing of semiconductors designed by other companies

A foundry is a company (or business) that manufactures wafers under contract from circuit data designed by its customers. TSMC, founded in 1987, established the pure-play foundry model. Sharing costly leading-edge fabs among many customers spread the division of labor with fabless companies. Companies that own no fabs and focus on design and sales are fabless, and those selling equipment parts belong to the tool supply chain.

FAQ

What is the difference between a foundry and a fabless company?

A fabless company designs and sells chips without owning manufacturing plants, while a foundry owns fabs and manufactures chips designed by other companies. The two models depend on each other.

What is a pure-play foundry?

A pure-play foundry only manufactures chips for customers and sells no chip products of its own. This avoids competing with the companies whose designs it builds, which helps earn their trust.

What does a foundry provide to chip designers?

Mainly a process design kit (PDK) with design rules, device models, verification rule decks and basic device layouts, often together with cell libraries and IP. Designers use it to make layouts the foundry can manufacture.
