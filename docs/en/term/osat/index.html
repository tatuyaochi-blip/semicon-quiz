What is OSAT?

A company that provides back-end services such as semiconductor assembly and testing under contract.

Japanese name: OSAT

Field: Semiconductor industry and market

What an OSAT does

An OSAT (outsourced semiconductor assembly and test) company performs the back-end steps that turn finished wafers into tested, shippable chips. Typical services include wafer probing, backgrinding to thin the wafer, dicing, die attach, wire bonding or flip-chip bumping, molding, marking, and final electrical test. The front end, building transistors and interconnect on the wafer, is done by an integrated device manufacturer (IDM) or a foundry; the OSAT receives the processed wafers and returns packaged, tested parts to the chip company.

Place in the industry's division of labor

The modern supply chain splits work among fabless design companies, foundries for wafer fabrication, and OSATs for packaging and test. A fabless company may never own a factory: it sends designs to a foundry and has the resulting wafers assembled and tested by an OSAT. IDMs often keep some back-end work in house but outsource part of it for capacity or cost. Back-end work is more labor- and floor-space-intensive than wafer fabrication, and major OSAT sites are concentrated in Taiwan, China and Southeast Asia.

Advanced packaging blurs the boundaries

As scaling slows, more performance comes from packaging: flip-chip bumps, copper pillars, fan-out wafer-level packaging, silicon interposers and chiplets stacked or placed side by side. These steps use wafer-level processes such as lithography, plating and redistribution layers, so OSATs have invested in fab-like equipment, while foundries have started offering their own advanced packaging. The simple split of front end at the foundry and back end at the OSAT therefore no longer holds for the most advanced products.

Answer: A. Companies that take on back-end processes such as semiconductor assembly and test on contract

An OSAT (outsourced semiconductor assembly and test) company provides back-end services, assembling (packaging) and testing chips. Major OSATs are concentrated in Taiwan, China and Southeast Asia, with strengths in labor and geographically dispersed sites. Together with foundries for front-end work and fabless design houses, they form the industry's division of labor. As advanced packaging grows in importance, foundries themselves increasingly do back-end work.

Answer: A. MPW (multi-project wafer)

An MPW (multi-project wafer) combines several designs on one mask set and wafer run, sharing mask and fabrication costs among participants; it is also called a shuttle, like a shared bus. Since advanced-node mask sets can cost tens of millions of dollars, universities and startups use MPWs for low-cost prototyping. Each participant gets only a few chips, so it serves prototyping and evaluation, not volume production. OSAT and IDM are business models.

FAQ

What does OSAT stand for?

OSAT stands for outsourced semiconductor assembly and test. It refers to companies that package and test chips under contract for other semiconductor companies.

What is the difference between an OSAT and a foundry?

A foundry manufactures the wafers, building transistors and wiring in the front-end process. An OSAT takes finished wafers and does the back-end work of cutting, packaging and testing the chips.

Do fabless companies need OSATs?

Usually yes. A fabless company owns no fabrication or assembly lines, so it relies on a foundry for wafers and on an OSAT, or a foundry offering packaging, for assembly and test.
