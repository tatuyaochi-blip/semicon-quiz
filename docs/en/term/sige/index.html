What is SiGe (silicon-germanium)?

An alloy of silicon and germanium with a larger lattice constant than silicon. Grown epitaxially, it is used to strain channels, as the base of HBTs and as the sacrificial layer in GAA transistors.

Japanese name: SiGe(シリコンゲルマニウム)

Field: Epitaxial growth

Material properties of SiGe

Silicon and germanium are both group IV elements with the diamond structure and mix in any ratio, so SiGe is a continuous alloy rather than a compound. Adding germanium enlarges the lattice constant from about 0.543 nm for silicon toward 0.566 nm for germanium, roughly 4% larger, and lowers the band gap from 1.12 eV toward 0.66 eV. SiGe also has higher hole mobility than silicon. It is grown by chemical vapor deposition using germane (GeH4) with silane or dichlorosilane, and the gas flow ratio sets the germanium content, which high-resolution X-ray diffraction can then measure together with strain.

Strain engineering in CMOS

SiGe grown on silicon is compressively strained because its larger lattice is squeezed to match the substrate, and this strain stays coherent only up to a critical thickness, roughly tens of nanometers at 20% germanium. Selective epitaxy embeds boron-doped SiGe in pMOS source and drain regions, which squeezes the channel and raises hole mobility; advanced processes also use SiGe as the pMOS channel itself. The reverse trick serves nMOS: a thin silicon layer grown on relaxed SiGe, reached through a graded buffer several micrometers thick, is stretched into tensile strain that raises electron mobility.

SiGe heterojunction bipolar transistors

In a SiGe heterojunction bipolar transistor (HBT), the base is an epitaxial SiGe layer tens of nanometers thick, boron-doped during growth. Its narrower band gap blocks holes from flowing back into the emitter, so the base can be thin and heavily doped without losing injection efficiency. Grading the germanium from near 0% at the emitter side to 20 to 30% at the collector side builds in an electric field that speeds electrons across. A little carbon limits boron diffusion. Transition frequencies of hundreds of gigahertz suit SiGe BiCMOS to RF front ends, optical links and automotive radar.

Sacrificial layers in GAA nanosheets

Nanosheet gate-all-around (GAA) transistors start from an epitaxial stack of alternating silicon and SiGe layers, with about 25 to 30% germanium. The SiGe ends are first recessed and filled with dielectric inner spacers. After the dummy gate is removed, the remaining SiGe is etched away with high selectivity, often by remote-plasma or chemical etching, leaving suspended silicon sheets that high-k dielectric and metal gate then wrap on all sides. Because high temperature would relax strain or intermix the layers, later steps are held to a thermal budget of about 600 to 700 °C or less.

Answer: B. Thanks to a heterojunction with a SiGe base, it can operate up to high frequencies

A SiGe HBT is a heterojunction bipolar transistor with a narrow-bandgap SiGe base. The band gap difference between emitter and base suppresses hole back-injection, so the base can be heavily doped and thin without losing injection efficiency, and grading the Ge content creates a field in the base that accelerates electrons. With fT reaching hundreds of GHz on a silicon process, it is used in smartphone RF circuits, optical communications and automotive radar.

  2. BAn alloy semiconductor of two group IV elements
  3. CA II-VI compound semiconductor

Answer: B. An alloy semiconductor of two group IV elements

SiGe is an alloy of group-IV silicon and germanium mixed in any proportion; both have the diamond structure and are fully miscible, and more germanium means a smaller band gap and larger lattice constant. Compatible with silicon processing, it is used in HBT bases, in pMOS source/drain regions that apply compressive strain, and as sacrificial layers when forming GAA transistors.

FAQ

Why is SiGe used in pMOS transistors?

SiGe has a larger lattice constant than silicon, so SiGe embedded in the source and drain compresses the silicon channel, and compressive strain raises hole mobility. SiGe also has higher intrinsic hole mobility, so it is used as a pMOS channel material as well.

What is a SiGe HBT?

It is a bipolar transistor whose base is made of SiGe, whose narrower band gap than the silicon emitter forms a heterojunction. This allows a thin, heavily doped base and, with graded germanium, a built-in accelerating field, giving transition frequencies of hundreds of gigahertz.

Is SiGe a compound semiconductor?

No, it is an alloy of two group IV elements that mix in any proportion, not a compound of elements from different groups like GaAs. Its properties vary continuously with germanium content, and it is processed with silicon-compatible equipment.
