What is Transconductance?

The ratio of the change in drain current to the change in gate voltage, written gm. It expresses a transistor's amplifying ability; amplifier gain is roughly gm times the load resistance.

Japanese name: 相互コンダクタンス

What is the approximate transconductance gm of a bipolar transistor operating at a collector current of 1 mA? (Room temperature, thermal voltage VT ≈ 26 mV)

  1. AAbout 0.38 mS
  2. BAbout 38 mS
  3. CAbout 3.8 S
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. About 38 mS

A bipolar transistor's transconductance is gm = IC/VT, so 1 mA ÷ 26 mV ≈ 38 mS. It depends only on current, not on device size or process variation. A MOSFET's gm = 2ID/(VGS − Vth), and since VGS − Vth is usually over 0.1 V, a bipolar gives 2–4 times more gm at the same current. This high gm is one reason bipolars are used in low-noise, high-gain analog circuits.

In a common-emitter amplifier with transconductance gm = 40 mS and collector resistor RC = 5 kΩ, roughly what is the magnitude of the voltage gain? (Ignore the load.)

  1. AAbout 2,000
  2. BAbout 200
  3. CAbout 8
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. About 200

The small-signal voltage gain of a common-emitter amplifier is Av ≈ −gm × RC, here 40 mS × 5 kΩ = 200. Since gm = IC/VT, Av = IC·RC/VT, so gain scales with the DC voltage drop across RC; a 5.2 V drop gives about 200. In practice the load and the transistor's output resistance appear in parallel and reduce the gain, with an upper limit of gm × ro (thousands).
