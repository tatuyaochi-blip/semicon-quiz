What is Gettering?

A technique that collects and traps metal impurities in a wafer at locations away from the surface where devices are built, using oxygen precipitates and the like.

Japanese name: ゲッタリング

Gettering appears in 9 practice questions across 4 topics, including Silicon wafers, Epitaxial growth, Oxidation and diffusion.

Field: Silicon wafers

Why metals must be trapped

Transition metals such as iron, copper and nickel form deep energy levels near midgap in silicon. These act as recombination and generation centers, shortening carrier lifetime, raising junction leakage and dark current in image sensors, and degrading gate oxides; surface concentrations around 10^10 atoms/cm² can already matter. Many of these metals diffuse quickly at process temperatures and become less soluble on cooling, so they tend to precipitate wherever they find a sink. Gettering provides deliberate sinks away from the device region so the metals end up there instead of near transistors.

Intrinsic gettering and the denuded zone

Czochralski (CZ) silicon absorbs oxygen from its quartz crucible, typically about 10^17 to 10^18 cm⁻³, mostly at interstitial sites and measured by infrared absorption near 1107 cm⁻¹. A high-temperature anneal above about 1100 °C drives oxygen out of the surface, creating a precipitate-free denuded zone (DZ) tens of micrometers deep. Lower-temperature anneals then nucleate and grow oxygen precipitates, called bulk microdefects (BMDs), at around 10^8 to 10^10 cm⁻³ in the interior. Their strain fields and associated dislocations capture metals, while devices sit in the clean surface layer.

Extrinsic gettering

Extrinsic gettering adds sinks from outside the crystal. Mechanical damage on the wafer back or a deposited polysilicon backside layer provides grain boundaries and defects that collect metals. Heavily doped regions also attract certain metals, which is one benefit of p/p+ epitaxial wafers, where a lightly doped epitaxial layer sits on a heavily doped p+ substrate. Historically, phosphosilicate glass (PSG) gettering of sodium ions was crucial in stabilizing early MOS threshold voltages. Trace nitrogen doping of CZ crystals, around 10^13 to 10^15 cm⁻³, strengthens precipitation for intrinsic gettering.

Gettering vs. cleaning

Gettering is a second line of defense, not a substitute for contamination control. Cleaning keeps surface metals below roughly 10^9 to 10^10 atoms/cm², and chemicals and ultrapure water are controlled at parts-per-trillion levels. Gettering only works if metals can diffuse to the sinks during the thermal steps the wafer actually sees, and if precipitate density is high enough without intruding into the active layer. Oxygen content therefore has to be specified carefully: too little gives weak gettering, too much creates near-surface defects and thermal donors.

Answer: B. A technique that captures harmful metal impurities at locations away from the device region

Gettering traps metallic impurities such as iron, copper and nickel—which degrade devices—at sites away from the surface device region, inside the wafer or at the back. Metals raise leakage current and degrade oxides, and contamination as low as about 10¹⁰ atoms/cm² can matter. Intrinsic gettering uses oxygen precipitates in the wafer bulk; extrinsic gettering uses backside damage or polysilicon films.

FAQ

What is the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic gettering?

Intrinsic gettering uses oxygen precipitates grown inside the wafer bulk as sinks, while extrinsic gettering adds sinks from outside, such as backside damage, a polysilicon backside film or heavily doped layers.

What is a denuded zone in a silicon wafer?

A near-surface layer, typically tens of micrometers deep, from which oxygen has been out-diffused by high-temperature annealing so that no oxygen precipitates form where devices are built.

Which metals does gettering target?

Mainly fast-diffusing transition metals such as iron, copper and nickel, which create deep levels that increase leakage and reduce carrier lifetime.
