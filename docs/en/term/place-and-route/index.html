What is Place and route?

The design step that arranges cells on the chip (placement) from the netlist produced by logic synthesis and determines the wiring between them (routing).

Japanese name: 配置配線

Place and route appears in 4 practice questions across 2 topics, including IC design flow and EDA, Timing analysis and CDC.

Field: IC design flow and EDA

Where place and route sits in the flow

Logic synthesis turns RTL into a gate-level netlist, but that netlist has no geometry. Place and route (P&R) converts it into a physical layout. It starts with floorplanning, which sets the die size, the positions of large blocks such as memories, the I/O locations and the backbone of the power grid. Standard cells are then placed in rows, the clock tree is synthesized (clock tree synthesis, CTS) to deliver the clock with controlled skew, and finally the signal nets are routed on the metal layers. The output goes on to extraction, timing sign-off and physical verification.

What placement optimizes

Placement positions thousands to millions of cells to minimize total wire length while avoiding congestion, regions where more wires are needed than routing tracks exist. It is timing-driven: cells on critical paths are pulled together, and tools insert buffers or resize gates as they go. A final legalization step snaps every cell onto the standard-cell rows without overlap. Decisions made at floorplanning, such as placing blocks that exchange heavy traffic next to each other, set the limits of what placement can achieve and are expensive to change later.

How routing is done

Routing usually proceeds in two stages. Global routing divides the chip into a coarse grid and assigns each net a path through regions, estimating congestion. Detailed routing then assigns real tracks and vias on each metal layer while obeying the design rules for width and spacing. Layers typically alternate preferred directions, horizontal on one and vertical on the next, to keep routing regular. Lower layers are thin and dense for local connections, while thick upper layers carry long global signals, clocks and power with lower resistance.

Extraction and timing closure

After routing, parasitic extraction computes each wire's resistance and capacitance from its geometry and its overlap and spacing with neighbors. These values feed static timing analysis, power analysis and crosstalk checks. In scaled technologies wire delay is a large share of path delay, so a design that met timing after synthesis can fail after routing. Fixes such as buffer insertion, cell resizing and rerouting are iterated until timing closes, and design rule checking (DRC) and layout-versus-schematic (LVS) verification confirm the layout before tape-out.

These values enable accurate static timing, power and crosstalk analysis. Scaling has made wire delay large relative to cell delay, increasing its importance. Answer: A. Floorplanning

Floorplanning determines die size, the positions of large blocks such as memories, I/O placement and the backbone of the power grid. Placing blocks that exchange data near each other shortens wires and eases congestion. Decisions here strongly affect congestion, timing and power and are hard to change later, making it a critical step. CTS and DRC come later in the flow.

FAQ

What is the difference between placement and routing?

Placement decides where each cell sits on the die, while routing creates the metal wires and vias that connect the placed cells according to the netlist. Placement quality largely determines how easy routing will be.

What comes before and after place and route?

Logic synthesis comes before it and supplies the gate-level netlist. After it come parasitic extraction, static timing analysis and physical verification such as DRC and LVS before the layout is released for manufacturing.

Why is floorplanning so important in place and route?

Floorplanning fixes the die size, block positions, I/O placement and power grid, which constrain everything that follows. A poor floorplan causes congestion and long wires that later steps cannot fully repair.
