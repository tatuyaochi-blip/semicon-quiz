What is Rectifier circuit?

A circuit that converts AC into current flowing in one direction (DC). Types include the half-wave rectifier, which removes the negative half cycles with a diode, the full-wave rectifier, which turns both half cycles the same way with a bridge of four diodes, and the three-phase full-wave rectifier, which rectifies three-phase AC with six diodes. The output contains ripple, so a smoothing capacitor or an inductor brings it closer to DC. Using thyristors instead of diodes lets the firing angle adjust the output voltage.

Japanese name: 整流回路

Average value and ripple

The average of a full-wave rectified sine wave of RMS value V is (2√2/π) × V ≈ 0.90V; for half-wave rectification it is about 0.45V. The fundamental ripple frequency equals the supply frequency for half-wave, twice it for full-wave and six times it for three-phase full-wave rectification, and the higher the frequency, the easier the smoothing. The average output of a three-phase full-wave rectifier is about 1.35 times the RMS line voltage, so 200 V three-phase AC gives about 270 V DC.

Input current with capacitor smoothing

With a large smoothing capacitor after the diodes, the output stays near the peak voltage, but the diodes conduct only for a short time near the peaks, when the input voltage exceeds the capacitor voltage. The input current therefore takes the form of narrow pulses, rich in harmonics, and the power factor falls sharply. Power factor correction (PFC) circuits are used to counter this.

Choosing the devices

General-purpose silicon rectifier diodes are used at mains frequency. High-frequency rectification in switching power supplies uses fast-recovery diodes with quick reverse recovery or Schottky barrier diodes with a low forward voltage, and synchronous rectification with MOSFETs where losses must be cut further. In a bridge the current always passes through two diodes, so at high currents the forward-voltage loss and the thermal design become important.

  1. AConverts DC to AC
  2. BAmplifies a signal
  3. CFull-wave rectifies AC into a current flowing in one direction (pulsating DC)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Full-wave rectifies AC into a current flowing in one direction (pulsating DC)

In a bridge rectifier, a different pair of diodes conducts on the positive and negative half-cycles, so current flows through the load in the same direction either way (full-wave rectification). Current always passes through two diodes, costing about 1.4 V. The output still pulses and is smoothed with a capacitor for DC use. The ripple frequency is twice the input (100 Hz for 50 Hz mains), making it easier to smooth than half-wave rectification.

When a sinusoidal AC signal is applied to a half-wave rectifier made of one diode and a resistor, what is the output?

  1. AThe AC signal passes through unchanged
  2. BThe negative half-cycles are also flipped and output as positive
  3. COnly one half-cycle, either positive or negative, is output
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Only one half-cycle, either positive or negative, is output

In a half-wave rectifier, current flows only during the half-cycle when the diode is forward-biased; the other half is blocked, so only one half-cycle appears at the output. The output peak is lower than the input peak by the diode drop (about 0.7 V). It needs just one diode, but it uses only half the input and its ripple has the same frequency as the input, making it hard to smooth, so power supplies mainly use full-wave rectification.

FAQ

Which is better, full-wave or half-wave rectification?

Almost all power supplies use full-wave rectification. Using both half cycles doubles the average value and the ripple frequency, which makes smoothing easier, and no DC component flows in the transformer to magnetize its core in one direction. Half-wave rectification, which needs fewer parts, is used only for very small currents.

What does a DC voltmeter read on a rectified voltage?

A typical DC voltmeter, which indicates the average, reads about 90 V on an unsmoothed full-wave rectified output from a 100 V RMS input. With a smoothing capacitor and a light load, the output approaches the peak value of about 141 V.
