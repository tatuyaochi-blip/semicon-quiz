What is Instrumentation amplifier?

A differential amplifier, typically built from three op-amps, with high input impedance and high CMRR. It amplifies small differential signals from sensors with high accuracy.

Japanese name: 計装アンプ(インスツルメンテーションアンプ)

Answer: B. Instrumentation amplifier

An instrumentation amplifier uses two input op-amps for high input impedance and gain and a third differential stage to reject common-mode signals, with CMRR that can exceed 100 dB. It precisely amplifies tiny differential signals riding on large common-mode voltages, such as strain-gauge bridges or ECG electrodes. Gain is set with a single external resistor, while resistor matching is handled on-chip.
