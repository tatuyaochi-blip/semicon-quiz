What is Reactive power?

In an AC circuit, power that only shuttles back and forth between source and load and is not consumed on average. With a phase difference φ between voltage and current it is Q = VI sin φ [var], related to the active power P = VI cos φ [W] and the apparent power S = VI [VA] by S² = P² + Q² (the power triangle). The complex power is S = V × (conjugate of I) = P + jQ; for example, P = 800 W and Q = 600 var give S = 1,000 VA and a power factor of 0.8. Because it increases the current in the wires and causes losses, it is cancelled with capacitors in power-factor correction.

Japanese name: 無効電力

Try it: questions about Reactive power

In an AC circuit, with active power P [W] and apparent power S [VA], which expression gives the power factor?

  1. AP / S
  2. BS / P
  3. CP × S
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. P / S

The power factor is the ratio P/S of active power P to apparent power S (RMS voltage × RMS current). For sinusoids it can be written cos φ, where φ is the phase difference between voltage and current. For example, S = 2000 VA and P = 1800 W give a power factor of 0.9. A low power factor means more current is needed to deliver the same active power, increasing losses in wiring and transformers.

An AC load has an active power of 800 W and an apparent power of 1000 VA. What is its reactive power? (Sinusoidal voltage and current.)

  1. A600 var
  2. B200 var
  3. C1800 var
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 600 var

Apparent power S, active power P and reactive power Q satisfy S² = P² + Q² (the power triangle). Q = √(1000² − 800²) = √360000 = 600 var, and the power factor is P/S = 0.8. Reactive power only shuttles back and forth between source and load and is not consumed on average, but it increases the current and therefore the losses in wiring and transformers.
