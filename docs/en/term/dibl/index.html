What is DIBL?

Short for drain-induced barrier lowering. In short-channel MOSFETs, a higher drain voltage lowers the potential barrier at the source, reducing the threshold voltage and increasing off-state leakage.

Japanese name: DIBL

DIBL appears in 3 practice questions across 2 topics, including Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA), MOSFET.

Field: MOSFET

How drain voltage lowers the barrier

In an off-state nMOSFET, electrons in the source are held back by a potential barrier at the source end of the channel, and the gate controls its height. In a long-channel device the drain is too far away to influence that barrier. When the gate is short, the drain's depletion region and electric field reach toward the source through the silicon, a two-dimensional electrostatic effect, and pull the barrier down. More electrons then spill over it, so the transistor turns on at a lower gate voltage: the threshold voltage falls as drain voltage rises.

How DIBL is measured

DIBL is quoted as the threshold shift per volt of drain bias, in mV/V: the threshold is extracted at a small drain voltage, such as 50 mV, and again at the full supply voltage, and the difference is divided by the change in drain voltage. Advanced transistors keep it to a few tens of mV/V or less. Because subthreshold current rises exponentially as threshold drops, DIBL multiplies off-state leakage: as an illustration, 80 mV/V at a 1 V supply with a subthreshold swing of 80 mV/decade raises leakage about tenfold compared with a device without DIBL.

DIBL vs. other short-channel effects

DIBL is one of several short-channel effects. Threshold roll-off is the drop in threshold with shorter gate length even at low drain bias; DIBL is the additional dependence on drain voltage. Punch-through is the extreme case where source and drain depletion regions merge and current flows deep below the gate control. GIDL (gate-induced drain leakage) is different again: band-to-band tunneling at the drain edge under a strong gate-to-drain field, which grows as the gate is driven more negative. In analog circuits DIBL also lowers output resistance, alongside channel-length modulation, reducing intrinsic gain.

How designers suppress it

Every remedy aims to strengthen the gate's control of the channel relative to the drain's. Thinner equivalent gate dielectrics, made possible by high-k materials, couple the gate more tightly. Shallow source/drain extensions and halo or pocket implants, which raise doping near the junction edges, limit how far drain fields penetrate. The most effective step is changing the geometry: thin-body SOI (silicon on insulator), FinFETs and gate-all-around nanosheets surround a thin channel with gate on several sides, so there is no deep silicon path through which the drain field can reach the source.

FAQ

What is the unit of DIBL?

Millivolts per volt (mV/V): the threshold voltage shift divided by the change in drain voltage used to measure it.

What is the difference between DIBL and channel length modulation?

DIBL lowers the threshold voltage as drain voltage rises, mainly affecting off-state leakage and threshold; channel-length modulation shortens the effective channel in saturation, raising on-current gradually. Both reduce output resistance in short devices.

Why do FinFETs have lower DIBL than planar MOSFETs?

The gate wraps around a thin fin on three sides, so it controls the channel potential much more strongly and the drain field cannot easily reach the source through deep silicon.
