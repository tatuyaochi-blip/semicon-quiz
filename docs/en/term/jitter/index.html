What is Jitter?

Small fluctuations in the timing of clock or signal edges from their ideal positions. When sampling high-frequency signals, even slight jitter causes large voltage errors.

Japanese name: ジッタ

Jitter appears in 12 practice questions across 8 topics, including A/D and D/A conversion, Timing analysis and CDC, IC design flow and EDA.

Field: A/D and D/A conversion

Kinds of jitter

Jitter can be measured several ways: period jitter is the variation of each cycle's length, cycle-to-cycle jitter is the change between adjacent periods, and time interval error is each edge's deviation from an ideal clock. By origin, random jitter comes from thermal and other noise, is roughly Gaussian and unbounded, and is quoted as an rms value. Deterministic jitter is bounded and includes data-dependent jitter from intersymbol interference, periodic jitter from supply or crosstalk coupling, and duty-cycle distortion. In the frequency domain, the same phenomenon appears as phase noise, which can be integrated over a band to give rms jitter.

Jitter in data converters

If the sampling instant shifts by Δt, the signal's change during Δt becomes error. A sine wave's slope grows with frequency, so jitter caps SNR at −20·log₁₀(2π·f·tj) regardless of resolution. Holding 70 dB on a 100 MHz input requires jitter below about 0.5 ps. That is why measured ENOB of a high-speed ADC drops at high input frequencies even when quantization noise, set by 6.02N + 1.76 dB, would allow more. In undersampling receivers, the jitter budget is set by the input frequency, not the lower sample rate, making it especially strict.

Jitter vs. skew in digital timing

Skew is the difference in clock arrival time between flip-flops at different locations, a mostly static, spatial effect set by the clock tree. Jitter is the variation of a clock edge over time at one point. Static timing analysis covers jitter with clock uncertainty, a margin subtracted from the available time; before clock tree synthesis it also includes expected skew. Period jitter matters for setup, where launch and capture are one cycle apart, but largely cancels in hold checks, where both sides use the same edge.

Jitter in serial links

On a serial link, jitter narrows the horizontal opening of the eye diagram, leaving less time margin for the receiver's sampling point. Because random jitter is unbounded, total jitter is specified at a target bit error rate; at 10⁻¹² it is commonly estimated as the deterministic part plus about 14 times the rms random jitter. Clock and data recovery circuits track slow jitter but not fast jitter, so standards specify jitter tolerance masks. In a different role, jitter is a resource: on-chip true random number generators harvest ring-oscillator jitter as entropy.

In timing constraints (SDC), which setting specifies margin subtracted from the clock period to allow for expected clock jitter, skew and the like?

  1. AClock uncertainty
  2. BDose
  3. CAspect ratio
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Clock uncertainty

Clock uncertainty is a setting that subtracts margin in timing analysis for clock jitter and, before place-and-route, expected skew. With a 1 ns period and 50 ps of uncertainty, data must arrive within 0.95 ns. The value is adjusted by design stage; after CTS, skew is calculated from the actual clock tree and only the jitter allowance remains. Dose and aspect ratio are manufacturing terms.

FAQ

What is the difference between jitter and skew?

Skew is a difference in clock arrival time between two locations, largely fixed by the clock tree. Jitter is the cycle-by-cycle variation of edge timing at a single point, caused mainly by noise.

How does clock jitter limit ADC performance?

Jitter turns timing error into voltage error proportional to the signal's slope, limiting SNR to about −20·log₁₀(2π·f·tj). For a 100 MHz input, about 0.5 ps rms jitter already caps SNR near 70 dB.

What is the difference between random and deterministic jitter?

Random jitter comes from noise, follows a roughly Gaussian distribution and is unbounded, so it is specified as an rms value. Deterministic jitter is bounded and has identifiable causes such as intersymbol interference, crosstalk or duty-cycle distortion.
