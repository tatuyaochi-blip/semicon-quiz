What is Systematic defect?

A defect that occurs repeatedly at particular patterns or locations because of interactions between design and process. It is distinguished from random defects caused by chance particles.

Japanese name: システマティック不良

For a chip that failed scan testing, what is the process called that logically narrows down candidate fault locations from which outputs mismatched on which patterns?

  1. AFault diagnosis
  2. BGettering
  3. CLithography
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Fault diagnosis

Fault diagnosis matches a failing chip's test results against simulations of hypothesized faults to narrow down candidate nets or cells. It pinpoints where to cross-section in physical failure analysis, greatly shortening analysis time. Volume diagnosis statistically aggregates results from many failing chips to reveal systematic defects concentrated in particular layout patterns or cells, helping improve yield.

Among the causes of yield loss, what are defects called that recur in specific patterns due to, for example, interactions between design and manufacturing processes?

  1. ASystematic defects
  2. BRandom defects
  3. CSoft errors
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Systematic defects

Yield loss divides into random defects, which occur probabilistically from particles and the like, and systematic defects, which recur reproducibly with particular layouts or process conditions. Systematic failures appear repeatedly at specific locations, such as pattern shapes prone to thinning during lithography. Their share grows with scaling, making design for manufacturability (DFM) essential. A soft error is a transient upset caused by radiation.
