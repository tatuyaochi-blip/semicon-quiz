What is Chromatic dispersion?

The broadening of light pulses in an optical fiber because light of different wavelengths travels at slightly different speeds. In standard single-mode fiber it is nearly zero near 1.31 µm and about 17 ps/(nm·km) at 1.55 µm.

Japanese name: 波長分散

Field: Silicon photonics

Where it comes from

Any real optical signal occupies a range of wavelengths, from the laser's own linewidth and from the modulation itself. In fiber, group velocity depends on wavelength through two effects: material dispersion, because silica's refractive index varies with wavelength, and waveguide dispersion, because how tightly the mode is confined in the core changes with wavelength. Different spectral components therefore arrive at slightly different times, broadening each pulse. In standard single-mode fiber the two effects cancel near 1310 nm, the zero-dispersion wavelength.

Units and typical numbers

The dispersion parameter D is quoted in ps/(nm·km): picoseconds of delay spread per nanometer of spectral width per kilometer. Standard single-mode fiber has about 17 ps/(nm·km) at 1550 nm. Pulse spreading is roughly D × spectral width × length, so a signal 0.1 nm wide sent 100 km at 1550 nm spreads by about 170 ps, more than one full symbol at 10 Gbaud. Because spectral width also grows with symbol rate, dispersion-limited reach falls roughly with the square of the rate.

Dispersion vs. loss when choosing a band

Fiber loss is lowest near 1550 nm, about 0.2 dB/km, while dispersion is nearly zero near 1310 nm. Short links of a few kilometers to about 10 km, typical in data centers, favor the O-band so that simple intensity-modulation direct-detection (IM-DD) transceivers work without compensation. Long-haul systems use the C-band for its low loss and erbium-doped amplifiers and deal with the dispersion. Fiber that shifts the zero to 1550 nm exists, but it worsens nonlinear mixing between closely spaced WDM channels.

How it is compensated

Older long-haul systems inserted spools of dispersion-compensating fiber with opposite-sign dispersion at amplifier sites. Coherent systems instead recover the full optical field, amplitude and phase on two polarizations, by mixing the signal with a local oscillator laser, so the receiver's digital signal processor (DSP) can undo chromatic dispersion as a linear filter. IM-DD receivers detect only optical power and lose the phase, so they cannot fully reverse dispersion electronically, which limits their reach at high rates.

  2. BIt is the band where silicon absorbs light most strongly, making photodetectors easy to build
  3. CIt is the band where chromatic dispersion is zero, so pulses do not spread at all

Answer: A. It is the band where loss in silica optical fiber is lowest

Loss in silica (SiO2) fiber increases from Rayleigh scattering at short wavelengths and from infrared absorption at long wavelengths, so it is lowest in between, around 1.55 µm (about 0.2 dB/km). Even after 100 km the loss is only about 20 dB, and the ability to amplify the light directly in the optical domain with erbium-doped fiber amplifiers (EDFAs) is another reason the C-band is used for long-haul communication. In standard single-mode fiber, chromatic dispersion is nearly zero around 1.31 µm (O-band); dispersion remains at 1.55 µm. Silicon barely absorbs this wavelength.

  2. BChromatic dispersion in standard single-mode fiber is nearly zero, so high-speed signal waveforms are less distorted
  3. CThe photon energy equals silicon's band gap, so silicon can be used as-is for the photodetector
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Chromatic dispersion in standard single-mode fiber is nearly zero, so high-speed signal waveforms are less distorted

Chromatic dispersion is the spreading of optical pulses caused by slight differences in propagation speed through the fiber at different wavelengths. In standard single-mode fiber, dispersion is nearly zero around 1.31 µm, so high-speed signals can easily be sent a few km to about 10 km without dispersion compensation, and many data center optical transceivers use this band. Loss is lowest around 1.55 µm (about 0.2 dB/km); at 1.31 µm it is somewhat higher, about 0.3–0.35 dB/km. The photon energy at 1.31 µm is about 0.95 eV, which silicon does not absorb.

FAQ

What is the chromatic dispersion of single-mode fiber at 1550 nm?

About 17 ps/(nm·km) for standard single-mode fiber, compared with nearly zero around 1310 nm.

What is the difference between chromatic dispersion and modal dispersion?

Modal dispersion occurs in multimode fiber because different modes travel different effective paths. Single-mode fiber eliminates it, leaving chromatic dispersion, which comes from different wavelengths traveling at different speeds.

Why can coherent receivers compensate dispersion digitally?

They measure both amplitude and phase of the optical field, so dispersion appears as a known linear phase distortion that a DSP filter can reverse. Direct-detection receivers lose the phase and cannot undo it fully.
