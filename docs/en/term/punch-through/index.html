What is Punch-through?

A condition in which the source and drain depletion regions merge and current flows that the gate cannot control. It occurs when the channel is too short or the substrate doping is too low.

Japanese name: パンチスルー

Punch-through appears in 5 practice questions across 4 topics, including Ion implantation, MOSFET, Bipolar transistors.

Field: MOSFET

How punch-through develops in a MOSFET

When drain voltage is applied, the drain-to-body junction is reverse biased and its depletion region spreads toward the source. Depletion width grows roughly with the square root of voltage divided by doping, so a short channel and lightly doped substrate let it reach the source-side depletion region. Once the two merge below the surface, the drain itself pulls down the potential barrier that normally holds back source carriers. Current then flows deep in the substrate, where the gate field is weak, so the transistor cannot be switched off and drain current climbs steeply with drain voltage.

Punch-through vs. DIBL and avalanche breakdown

Punch-through is the extreme case of drain-induced barrier lowering (DIBL). With moderate DIBL, the drain only partly lowers the source barrier, so threshold voltage falls as drain voltage rises and the subthreshold swing worsens. In punch-through the barrier in the subsurface region effectively disappears and gate control is lost. It is a purely electrostatic effect and needs no impact ionization, unlike avalanche breakdown, where carriers gain enough energy in a high field to generate new electron-hole pairs.

How process and structure suppress it

The usual cure is more doping exactly where the depletion regions would meet, without raising doping at the channel surface, which would increase threshold voltage and cut mobility. Halo (pocket) implants, tilted about 20–45°, place channel-type dopant under the source/drain edges. Retrograde wells use implants of hundreds of keV to MeV to put peak doping deep below the surface. Bulk FinFETs add a heavily doped punch-through stopper at the fin base, which the gate cannot control. SOI substrates and gate-all-around (GAA) structures remove the uncontrolled path altogether.

Punch-through in bipolar transistors

Bipolar transistors suffer a similar failure. A thin base reduces recombination and raises current gain and speed, but at high collector-base reverse voltage the collector depletion region can spread across the whole base and reach the emitter depletion region. The base potential barrier then collapses and collector current rises uncontrollably, which caps the usable breakdown voltage. Base width and base doping are therefore chosen together, trading current gain and high-frequency performance against punch-through voltage.

Answer: B. The source and drain depletion layers merge and current flows that the gate cannot control

With a short channel and light substrate doping, the drain's depletion region can merge with the source's under drain bias, so current flows deep in the substrate independent of the gate and the device cannot turn off—punch-through. Remedies include heavily doped halo (pocket) implants beneath the source/drain, shallower junctions, and, as in FinFETs and GAA devices, wrapping the gate around the channel to eliminate uncontrolled paths.

FAQ

What is the difference between punch-through and avalanche breakdown?

Punch-through happens when the drain and source depletion regions merge and the source barrier collapses, which is purely electrostatic. Avalanche breakdown happens when carriers in a high field create new electron-hole pairs by impact ionization, multiplying the current.

How do halo implants prevent punch-through?

Halo implants add extra dopant of the substrate type locally under the edges of the source and drain. This narrows the drain depletion region near the channel ends, so it cannot reach the source, while leaving the rest of the channel lightly doped.

Why do bulk FinFETs need a punch-through stopper?

The gate wraps only the upper part of the fin, so the lower part near the substrate offers a leakage path the gate cannot shut off. A heavily doped layer of the opposite type at the fin base blocks this path.
