What is Auger recombination?

Recombination in which the released energy is given to another carrier instead of being emitted as light. It becomes significant at high carrier densities and affects lifetime in heavily doped layers and efficiency loss in LEDs.

Japanese name: オージェ再結合

Try it: questions about Auger recombination

Which recombination process becomes significant at high carrier concentrations, where the released energy is given to another carrier instead of being emitted as light?

  1. ARadiative recombination
  2. BAuger recombination
  3. CSurface recombination
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Auger recombination

In Auger recombination, the energy released is handed to a third carrier and is eventually lost as heat. Because three carriers are involved, the rate scales roughly with the cube of carrier density and grows sharply at high concentration. It therefore limits heavily doped emitters and solar-cell efficiency, and is considered one cause of "droop," the drop in LED efficiency at high current. It differs from radiative recombination, which emits light.

What is the phenomenon called in which the efficiency of an InGaN LED actually decreases as the current is increased?

  1. AEfficiency droop
  2. BBlooming
  3. CElectromigration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Efficiency droop

InGaN LED efficiency peaks at relatively low current densities of a few A/cm² and declines as current increases—efficiency droop. Auger recombination, which scales with the cube of carrier density, and electron leakage over the active region are thought to be the main causes. High-power lighting lowers current density by using larger chips or many chips; laser-based lighting, with less droop, is also being explored.
