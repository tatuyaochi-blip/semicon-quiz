What is FIB (focused ion beam)?

An instrument that mills or deposits material with a finely focused ion beam. It is used for cross-sectioning tiny areas, preparing thin TEM samples and editing circuits.

Japanese name: FIB(集束イオンビーム)

Field: Metrology and analysis

How an FIB mills and deposits

Most FIB systems use gallium ions from a liquid metal ion source, accelerated to around 30 kV and focused to a spot of a few nanometers. Where the beam strikes, atoms are sputtered away, so scanning it mills a chosen shape. If a precursor gas is introduced near the surface, the beam decomposes it locally to deposit platinum, tungsten or an insulating film, and other gases can speed up or make etching more selective. The secondary electrons the beam knocks out also form an image of the surface.

Preparing TEM lamellae

A transmission electron microscope (TEM) needs samples about 100 nm thick or less. In the lift-out method, a protective metal strip is deposited over the region of interest, trenches are milled on both sides, the slice is cut free underneath, picked up with a micromanipulator needle, welded to a grid and thinned. A final pass at low beam energy reduces the damaged surface layer. FIB is also used to shape needle specimens for atom probe tomography.

Circuit edit and failure analysis

On prototype chips, FIB can cut a metal line and deposit a new conductive connection, letting engineers test a design fix on real silicon before paying for new masks. In failure analysis it exposes a cross-section exactly through a defective via or transistor. Dual-beam tools combine the ion column with a scanning electron microscope (SEM), so a cross-section can be imaged with electrons while it is being milled.

Limitations: damage and speed

FIB is destructive and local: the analyzed chip or wafer area cannot be used as product. Gallium ions implant into the sample and amorphize a surface layer, which can distort analysis, and sputtered material can redeposit nearby. Removing large volumes is slow, so plasma FIB tools using heavier ions such as xenon are used when tens to hundreds of micrometers must be removed, trading some precision for much higher milling rate.

Answer: A. Machining tiny areas, preparing cross-section samples and modifying circuits

An FIB focuses ions such as gallium onto the sample to mill chosen spots with nanometer precision, or to deposit platinum or tungsten when combined with precursor gases. It is used to prepare TEM lamellae, cross-section failure sites and edit circuits on prototype chips by cutting and reconnecting wires. Dual-beam tools combined with an SEM allow imaging while milling. It does not measure full-wafer film thickness or clean wafers.

FAQ

What is the difference between FIB and SEM?

An SEM scans electrons to image a surface and causes little damage, while an FIB uses heavier ions that sputter material, so it can mill and deposit as well as image. Dual-beam systems combine both.

Why does FIB use gallium?

Gallium is liquid near room temperature and forms a stable liquid metal ion source that can be focused to a very small spot, making it practical for precise milling.

Can FIB be used to repair a chip?

Yes, for prototypes: FIB circuit edit cuts and reconnects wires to verify a fix, but it is not used to repair chips in production.
