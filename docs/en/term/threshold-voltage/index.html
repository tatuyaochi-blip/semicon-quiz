What is Threshold voltage?

The gate voltage at which a channel (inversion layer) forms in a MOSFET and current starts to flow. Written as Vth.

Read the beginner explainer: What Is a Transistor? How a Transistor Works as a Switch

Japanese name: しきい値電圧

Threshold voltage appears in 43 practice questions across 14 topics, including MOSFET, Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA), Reliability.

Field: MOSFET

What sets the threshold voltage

Threshold voltage (Vth) is conventionally the gate voltage at which strong inversion begins, when the surface potential reaches twice the Fermi potential φF and the surface minority-carrier density equals the bulk majority density. For an nMOSFET, Vth = VFB + 2φF + Qd/Cox. The flat-band voltage VFB depends on the gate-to-silicon work-function difference and on charge in the oxide; Qd is the depletion charge. Raising channel doping or thickening the gate oxide therefore raises Vth. In high-k/metal-gate FinFETs and gate-all-around devices, channels are nearly undoped and Vth is set mainly by the gate metal's work function.

Signs and typical values

An enhancement-mode nMOSFET conducts when VGS exceeds a positive Vth; an enhancement-mode pMOSFET has a negative Vth, around −0.4 V, and turns on when its gate is more than |Vth| below its source, so documents often quote magnitudes. Advanced logic uses about 0.2 to 0.4 V, power devices about 1 to 4 V. Above threshold, a long-channel device in saturation follows ID = ½μCox(W/L)(VGS − Vth)², so the overdrive voltage VGS − Vth sets drive current. Short channels deviate from this square law because of velocity saturation.

The speed versus leakage trade-off

Lowering Vth increases overdrive and on-current at a given supply, but below threshold the current falls only exponentially, so off-state leakage grows roughly tenfold for every 60 to 80 mV of reduction. Processes therefore offer low-, standard- and high-Vth transistors, and designers place low-Vth cells only on critical paths. Operating conditions move Vth too: reverse body bias raises it (the body effect), higher drain voltage lowers it through drain-induced barrier lowering (DIBL), and rising temperature lowers its magnitude by about 1 to 2 mV/°C.

Variation and drift

Vth is never exactly the same twice. In small transistors, random dopant fluctuation and gate-metal grain effects scatter Vth by tens of millivolts, with the spread scaling roughly as one over the square root of gate area (Pelgrom's law); SRAM cells feel this most. In fins and nanosheets a few nanometers thick, quantum confinement turns thickness variation into Vth variation. Over a product's life, negative and positive bias temperature instability (NBTI, PBTI) and hot carrier injection shift Vth upward and slow circuits, so timing must include aging margin.

Try it: questions about Threshold voltage

What is the condition for an enhancement-mode n-channel MOSFET to turn on (conduct)? (VGS: gate-source voltage, Vth: threshold voltage)

  1. AVGS is 0 V
  2. BVGS is greater than Vth
  3. CVGS is negative
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. VGS is greater than Vth

In an enhancement-mode nMOSFET, a positive gate voltage above Vth draws electrons to the surface of the p-type substrate, forming an inversion layer (channel) so current flows between source and drain. At VGS = 0 V there is no channel and the device is off (normally off), which suits logic and makes power circuits fail safe. Vth is typically 0.2–0.4 V in advanced logic and about 1–4 V in power devices.

When a long-channel MOSFET operates in the saturation region, the drain current ID is roughly proportional to what? (VGS: gate-source voltage, Vth: threshold voltage)

  1. AThe square of (VGS − Vth)
  2. BVGS − Vth
  3. CThe reciprocal of VGS
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The square of (VGS − Vth)

In saturation, a long-channel MOSFET follows ID = (1/2)μCox(W/L)(VGS − Vth)², so drain current is proportional to the square of (VGS − Vth) (the square law). VGS − Vth, the overdrive voltage, is a basic analog design parameter. In short-channel devices velocity saturation pushes the dependence toward linear, and mobility degradation and parasitic resistance add further deviations, so real designs use detailed models such as BSIM.

FAQ

What is a typical threshold voltage for a MOSFET?

Transistors in advanced logic processes typically have threshold voltages of about 0.2 to 0.4 V in magnitude, with several options offered per process. Power MOSFETs are usually around 1 to 4 V so they stay reliably off with noise on the gate.

Why is the threshold voltage of a pMOS transistor negative?

A pMOS channel is made of holes, which are attracted to the surface of the n-type body when the gate is driven below the source. Turning it on therefore requires a negative gate-to-source voltage, so its threshold is negative.

How is threshold voltage measured?

It is usually extracted from the measured ID-VG curve, either as the gate voltage where drain current reaches a fixed value scaled by W/L (constant-current method) or by extrapolating the linear part of the curve to zero current. The two methods give slightly different numbers, so the definition should always be stated.
