What is ISO 26262?

The international standard for automotive functional safety, aimed at reducing risks from failures of electrical and electronic systems to an acceptable level. It adapts IEC 61508 to road vehicles and covers the whole lifecycle from concept through development and production to decommissioning.

What functional safety means

ISO 26262 defines functional safety as the absence of unreasonable risk due to hazards caused by malfunctioning behavior of electrical and electronic (E/E) systems. The aim is not zero failures, which is impossible, but making sure a failure does not turn into a hazardous situation. A typical pattern is detect and react: if electric power steering detects an anomaly in its motor control, it stops assist, lights a warning lamp and leaves the driver able to steer manually. Crash protection and general quality management are separate topics, the latter covered by IATF 16949.

From hazard analysis to requirements

Work starts with hazard analysis and risk assessment (HARA). Each hazardous event is rated for severity (S1–S3), exposure (E1–E4) and controllability (C1–C3), which together give an ASIL (Automotive Safety Integrity Level) from A to D, or QM when ordinary quality management is enough. A useful shortcut is that S + E + C of 10 gives D, 9 gives C, 8 gives B, 7 gives A. Each hazard becomes a safety goal, such as no unintended sudden acceleration, which inherits the ASIL and is refined into functional, technical, hardware and software safety requirements.

Random and systematic failures

The standard treats two failure types differently. Random hardware failures, from wear-out or radiation-induced bit flips, are handled quantitatively with failure rates in FIT and metrics such as the single-point fault metric (SPFM), latent fault metric (LFM) and probabilistic metric for random hardware failures (PMHF). Systematic failures, from specification errors, design mistakes or software bugs, recur whenever the same conditions occur and are prevented through process rigor scaled to the ASIL. Safety mechanisms must detect a fault and reach a safe state within the fault tolerant time interval (FTTI), and latent faults, such as a broken ECC corrector, must be uncovered before a second fault arrives.

How chip suppliers fit in

A general-purpose automotive microcontroller is designed before its final vehicle application is known, so it is developed as a Safety Element out of Context (SEooC): the supplier assumes safety requirements and usage conditions and documents them in a safety manual, and the system integrator checks those assumptions. ASIL decomposition lets a requirement be split across independent redundant elements, for example ASIL D into B(D) plus B(D), provided dependent failures are analyzed. Neighboring standards cover other risks: ISO 21448 (SOTIF) addresses performance limits of a non-failed system, and ISO/SAE 21434 addresses cyberattacks.

ISO 26262 is a functional safety standard for reducing to an acceptable level the risk that a vehicle behaves hazardously due to failures of electrical and electronic systems. It adapts IEC 61508, the basic functional safety standard for electrical/electronic/programmable electronic systems, to automotive use and covers the entire lifecycle from concept through development, production and decommissioning. Answer: A. Severity (S), exposure (E), controllability (C)

The ASIL is determined for each hazardous event by evaluating three factors: severity S, representing how serious the injuries are; exposure E, representing the frequency or duration of exposure to driving situations in which the hazard can occur; and controllability C, representing how likely the driver or others are to avoid the hazard. For example, sudden loss of braking at high speed has high severity and is hard to avoid, so it receives a high ASIL. Failure rate, diagnostic coverage and FTTI are quantities used after the ASIL is determined, to evaluate whether a design meets its requirements.

FAQ

What is the difference between ISO 26262 and IEC 61508?

IEC 61508 is the generic functional safety standard for electrical, electronic and programmable electronic systems. ISO 26262 adapts it to series-production road vehicles, replacing its concepts with automotive ones such as the ASIL and covering the vehicle lifecycle from concept to decommissioning.

Does ISO 26262 certification mean a car cannot have an accident?

No. It only aims to reduce the risk from E/E malfunctions to an acceptable level, typically by detecting faults and moving to a safe state. Hazards from sensor limitations or attacks are handled by SOTIF and ISO/SAE 21434 respectively.

Is AEC-Q100 the same as ISO 26262 compliance?

No. AEC-Q100 is a stress-test qualification of an IC's reliability, while ISO 26262 governs the safety lifecycle, safety mechanisms and failure-rate metrics. Automotive chips are usually expected to satisfy both.
