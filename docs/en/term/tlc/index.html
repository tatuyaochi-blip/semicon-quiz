What is TLC (triple-level cell)?

A flash format that stores three bits per cell, distinguishing eight threshold-voltage states. It lowers cost per bit but reduces endurance. Four bits per cell is QLC.

Japanese name: TLC(トリプルレベルセル)

Field: SRAM and flash memory

Eight states in one cell

A flash cell stores data as the threshold voltage of its transistor, set by how much charge sits in the floating gate or charge-trap layer. Triple-level cell (TLC) flash programs each cell to one of eight threshold-voltage windows, encoding three bits, and reading a cell requires comparing it against seven reference voltages. The three bits are mapped to separate logical pages on the same word line, often called lower, middle and upper pages. Adjacent states are assigned Gray-coded patterns that differ in only one bit, so the common error of reading a neighboring state corrupts just one bit.

Why more bits cost endurance and speed

Squeezing eight states into the same voltage window leaves narrow gaps between them. Programming must therefore proceed in small incremental pulses, each followed by a verify read, which makes TLC writes slower than single-level cell (SLC) writes. Each program and erase also drives electrons through the tunnel oxide at high field, gradually trapping charge and broadening distributions until neighbors overlap. Typical endurance is about 100,000 cycles for SLC, several thousand to 10,000 for MLC, a few thousand for TLC and about 1,000 for QLC; data retention also suffers as gaps narrow.

How SSDs make TLC work

Controllers hide TLC's weaknesses. An SLC cache runs part of the free space in one-bit mode so incoming writes land quickly, then migrates data to TLC blocks when the drive is idle; sustained writes that overflow the cache drop to the slower native speed. Strong error-correcting codes recover from bit errors that grow with wear, wear leveling spreads erases evenly across blocks, and spare area gives garbage collection room to work. Drive endurance is rated as total bytes written (TBW) or drive writes per day (DWPD) rather than raw cell cycles.

TLC vs. SLC, MLC and QLC

Bits per cell trade density against robustness: SLC stores one bit, MLC two, TLC three and quad-level cell (QLC) four. For the same number of cells, TLC holds three times the data of SLC, which is why it has become the mainstream choice for SSDs, usually built in 3D NAND with cells stacked in many layers. QLC pushes cost per bit lower for read-heavy, high-capacity storage, while SLC and SLC-mode operation remain in industrial and automotive parts that need maximum endurance and temperature tolerance.

In TLC and QLC SSDs, what is the main purpose of using part of the space as SLC to absorb writes temporarily?

  1. ATo increase capacity
  2. BTo encrypt data
  3. CTo make writes faster

Answer: C. To make writes faster

TLC and QLC take time to program because states must be placed precisely. SSDs therefore run part of their free space in SLC mode, writing there quickly first and migrating data to TLC/QLC areas when idle (an SLC cache). If sustained writes overflow the cache, speed drops sharply to the native rate, which is why large file copies can slow down midway.

Which orders SLC, MLC and TLC from the most rewrite cycles to the fewest?

  1. ATLC > MLC > SLC
  2. BSLC > MLC > TLC
  3. CMLC > SLC > TLC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. SLC > MLC > TLC

Fewer bits per cell mean wider gaps between threshold states, so cells still read correctly as wear broadens the distributions, giving higher endurance. Generally SLC > MLC > TLC > QLC, roughly 100,000 cycles for SLC, several thousand to 10,000 for MLC, a few thousand for TLC and about 1,000 for QLC. SLC mode is used in industrial products needing high endurance and in SSD cache areas.

FAQ

How many voltage states does TLC flash use?

Eight threshold-voltage states, which encode three bits per cell and require seven reference levels to read.

How many write cycles can TLC NAND endure?

Typically a few thousand program/erase cycles, compared with roughly 100,000 for SLC and around 1,000 for QLC.

Why does a TLC SSD slow down during long file copies?

Writes first go to a fast SLC-mode cache; once a long transfer fills it, the drive must write directly in TLC mode, which is much slower.
