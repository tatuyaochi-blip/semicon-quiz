What is Oxynitride (SiON)?

Gate oxide with nitrogen incorporated. It blocks boron penetration from the gate electrode and has a slightly higher permittivity, so it was used as the gate insulator before high-k films.

Japanese name: 酸窒化膜(SiON)

Field: Oxidation and diffusion

Why nitrogen was added to gate oxides

pMOS transistors with p+ polysilicon gates are doped with boron, and during the dopant activation anneal boron can diffuse through a gate oxide only about 2 nm thick into the channel, shifting the threshold voltage and degrading reliability. Nitrogen in the film strongly blocks boron diffusion. It also raises the relative permittivity from 3.9 for SiO2 to roughly 5 to 6, so for the same equivalent oxide thickness (EOT) the physical film can be thicker, and direct-tunneling leakage, which falls steeply with thickness, is reduced. Resistance to hot-carrier damage also improves.

Thermal vs. plasma nitridation

Annealing an oxide in NO or N2O incorporates nitrogen that piles up at the Si/SiO2 interface. Plasma nitridation instead exposes the oxide to active nitrogen from a plasma at a few hundred °C, placing roughly 10 to 20% nitrogen near the top surface. That location blocks boron arriving from the gate and raises permittivity while leaving the channel interface close to pure oxide. Too much nitrogen near the channel lowers carrier mobility and can worsen negative-bias temperature instability (NBTI) in pMOS devices, so the nitrogen profile is tuned carefully.

From SiON to high-k

SiON was the standard gate dielectric in the generations just before high-k. As EOT approached about 1 nm, even nitrided films leaked too much gate current, and the industry moved to hafnium-based oxides with permittivity around 20 to 25 combined with metal gates, which also removed the boron-penetration problem of polysilicon. A thin silicon oxide-based interfacial layer still sits beneath the high-k film to keep a good interface with the channel. For comparison, silicon nitride (Si3N4) has a permittivity of about 7.

SiON beyond the gate

Silicon oxynitride is also deposited by plasma-enhanced CVD (PECVD) from silane with nitrous oxide, sometimes with ammonia, at about 300 to 400 °C. Adjusting the gas ratio tunes the refractive index anywhere between oxide (about 1.46) and nitride (about 2.0), so SiON serves as a dielectric antireflective coating under photoresist, as well as a hard mask and passivation layer. These deposited films are much thicker and differ in composition from nitrided gate oxides.

What is the main purpose of introducing nitrogen into the gate oxide to make an oxynitride (SiON)?

  1. ATo suppress boron penetration from the gate electrode and improve reliability
  2. BTo make the film conductive
  3. CTo increase the etch rate

Answer: A. To suppress boron penetration from the gate electrode and improve reliability

Boron from p+ polysilicon gates can penetrate gate oxides about 2 nm thick during annealing, reaching the channel and shifting threshold voltage. Incorporating nitrogen blocks boron diffusion and raises permittivity from 3.9 to about 5–6, allowing a physically thicker film at the same EOT with less leakage. It also improves reliability such as hot-carrier resistance, and was standard in the generations before high-k.

Which gas is sometimes used as the oxidant with silane to deposit silicon oxide or oxynitride films by CVD?

  1. AArgon (Ar)
  2. BHelium (He)
  3. CNitrous oxide (N2O)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Nitrous oxide (N2O)

Nitrous oxide (N2O) serves as the oxidant reacting with silane to form silicon oxide in PECVD, allowing deposition at around 300–400 °C. It reacts more gently than oxygen, reducing gas-phase particle formation, and under some conditions it incorporates nitrogen to form oxynitride (SiON). Because SiON's refractive index can be tuned, it is also used as an antireflective layer for lithography. Argon and helium are inert.

FAQ

Why is nitrogen added to gate oxide?

Nitrogen blocks boron diffusion from p+ polysilicon gates into the channel and raises the dielectric constant, allowing a physically thicker film with lower leakage at the same EOT. It also improves hot-carrier reliability.

What is the dielectric constant of SiON?

It depends on nitrogen content, but gate-quality SiON is typically about 5 to 6, between SiO2 at about 3.9 and silicon nitride at about 7.

Why was SiON replaced by high-k dielectrics?

At an EOT near 1 nm, SiON films became so thin that tunneling leakage was too high. Hafnium-based high-k oxides, with permittivity around 20 to 25, give the same EOT with a much thicker physical film.
