What is IDM (integrated device manufacturer)?

A semiconductor company that does everything in-house, from design through front-end and back-end manufacturing to sales.

Japanese name: IDM(垂直統合型デバイスメーカー)

What an IDM does in-house

An IDM (integrated device manufacturer) owns the whole chain: circuit design, front-end wafer fabrication in its own fabs, back-end assembly and test, and selling the finished devices under its own name. This contrasts with the disaggregated model, in which a fabless company designs chips, a foundry fabricates wafers for many customers, and an OSAT (outsourced semiconductor assembly and test) company packages and tests them. Many companies sit between the extremes, so the label describes a business structure rather than a product category.

Why some products favor the IDM model

When manufacturing technology itself differentiates the product, keeping design and process teams under one roof pays off. Memory, power devices and analog chips are typical examples: cell structures, high-voltage device layouts or precision passive components are co-optimized with process steps that the company develops itself. Short feedback loops between yield analysis and design changes also speed up learning. The cost is capital: building and continuously upgrading fabs requires enormous investment, and fabs must stay highly utilized through demand cycles to remain profitable.

IDM vs. fabless, foundry and fab-lite

A fabless company owns no fabs and buys wafers from foundries, so it avoids fab capital but depends on the foundry's process and capacity. A foundry manufactures to customer designs and does not sell its own chips. A fab-lite company is a partial IDM: it keeps fabs for mature processes and products where it has unique process strengths, and outsources leading-edge products that would need the largest investments to foundries. Fab-lite is common among microcontroller, analog and power semiconductor makers seeking to limit capital burden while keeping manufacturing know-how.

How prototyping differs

An IDM can run engineering lots on its own lines and adjust the process as needed. Companies without fabs, and universities or startups, often prototype through an MPW (multi-project wafer) run, in which a foundry places designs from several customers on one mask set and shares the mask and wafer costs among them. Each participant receives only a small number of chips, which is enough for evaluation but not for volume production. These options illustrate how ownership of manufacturing shapes a company's development cycle.

Answer: B. Because it has no fabs, its tangible fixed assets and fixed costs are relatively small, and the cost of outsourcing manufacturing to foundries is mainly a cost that varies with production volume

A fabless company specializes in design and sales and outsources manufacturing to foundries, so it does not bear huge fab investments or their depreciation itself. Its share of fixed costs is therefore low and its break-even point tends to be relatively low, so its profit tends to fall less than an IDM's when demand drops. On the other hand, because it cannot secure manufacturing capacity in-house, securing foundry capacity when demand suddenly rises becomes a challenge, and it sometimes reserves production capacity through long-term contracts.

FAQ

What does IDM stand for in semiconductors?

IDM stands for integrated device manufacturer, a company that designs, fabricates, assembles, tests and sells its own chips rather than relying on foundries and outsourced assembly.

What is the difference between an IDM and a fabless company?

An IDM owns and runs its own wafer fabs, while a fabless company only designs chips and pays a foundry to manufacture them.

What is a fab-lite company?

A fab-lite company is a partial IDM that keeps some fabs, usually for mature or specialized processes, while outsourcing products that need leading-edge processes to foundries.
