What is DRC (design rule check)?

Verification that checks whether a layout follows the geometric rules required for manufacturing, such as line widths and spacings.

Japanese name: DRC(デザインルールチェック)

DRC (design rule check) appears in 9 practice questions across 2 topics, including IC design flow and EDA, Testing and inspection.

Field: IC design flow and EDA

What design rules check

A DRC (design rule check) tool scans the polygons of every layout layer and flags geometry that the fab cannot reliably build. Typical rules cover minimum width and spacing, enclosure of a via or contact by the metal above and below, extension of one layer past another, minimum area, and minimum and maximum pattern density so that CMP polishes evenly. Antenna rules, which limit how much metal can connect to a gate during processing, are usually run in the same flow. Each rule traces back to a limit of lithography, etching, CMP or reliability.

Why rule decks keep growing

At mature nodes a few hundred simple width and spacing rules were enough. Advanced processes have thousands, many of them context dependent: required spacing can depend on the width of neighbouring lines and how far they run in parallel, line ends need extra clearance, and multiple patterning adds coloring rules that govern which shapes may share a mask. The rules are delivered by the foundry as a rule deck within the PDK (process design kit) and executed by physical verification tools, because manual checking of billions of shapes is impossible.

DRC vs. LVS vs. ERC

These three sign-off checks are easily confused. DRC looks only at geometry and does not care what the circuit does. LVS (layout versus schematic) extracts devices and connectivity from the layout and compares them with the schematic netlist, catching opens, shorts and wrong devices. ERC (electrical rule check) looks for electrical mistakes such as floating gate inputs, shorted supplies or unconnected wells. Synthesis and place-and-route tools also report design rule violations of a different kind, such as maximum transition or capacitance limits, which are electrical constraints rather than layout geometry.

DRC in the tape-out flow

DRC runs repeatedly during physical design, on individual blocks and then on the full chip after everything is assembled, since violations often appear at block boundaries. Routers fix many errors automatically, and the rest are fixed by hand. A clean DRC and LVS result is required before mask data is released to the fab. Occasionally a violation is accepted through a formal waiver agreed with the foundry. Many processes also supply recommended rules for manufacturability, which are not mandatory but improve yield when followed.

Answer: B. Verifying that the layout satisfies manufacturing rules such as minimum width and spacing

DRC checks whether layout shapes violate design rules derived from manufacturing, such as minimum width, spacing, overlap and density. Advanced processes have thousands of rules, verified automatically by tools. Checking that layout connectivity matches the schematic is LVS (layout versus schematic), and checking logic function is simulation. DRC and LVS are always run before mask data is released.

FAQ

What is the difference between DRC and LVS?

DRC checks that layout shapes obey manufacturing rules such as minimum width and spacing. LVS checks that the circuit extracted from the layout matches the intended schematic, so a layout can pass DRC and still fail LVS.

What is a DRC rule deck?

A rule deck is the foundry-supplied file that encodes a process's design rules in the language of a physical verification tool. It is part of the process design kit and is updated as the process matures.

What happens if a chip is taped out with DRC violations?

The foundry normally requires DRC-clean data or approved waivers. Unwaived violations risk shapes that print incorrectly, causing shorts, opens or reliability failures that reduce yield.
