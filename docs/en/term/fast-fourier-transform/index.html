What is Fast Fourier transform (FFT)?

A family of algorithms that compute the discrete Fourier transform (DFT) exactly with few operations. The best-known approach (the Cooley-Tukey algorithm) repeatedly splits an N-point DFT into DFTs of half the size and shares the common calculations, cutting the number of operations from about N² for the definition to about N log₂N. For N = 1024 that is about 1/100. The algorithm is simplest when the number of points is a power of two, but methods exist for other sizes. Besides spectrum analysis, it is used for OFDM modulation and demodulation and for fast computation of long convolutions.

Why it is fast

Splitting an N-point DFT into two N/2-point DFTs of the even-numbered and odd-numbered samples lets the original result be assembled just by multiplying by twiddle factors e^(−j2πk/N) and adding and subtracting. Repeating this gives log₂N stages of about N operations each, so the total is on the order of N log₂N. Because of its shape, this add-and-subtract pair is called a butterfly.

How much faster

The ratio is N/log₂N: 1024 ÷ 10 = 102.4 for N = 1024 and 65,536 ÷ 16 = 4096 for N = 65,536. The larger the number of points, the bigger the gain, which makes the FFT indispensable for real-time spectrum displays and for uses such as communication receivers that compute many DFTs in a row.

Convolution with the FFT

The convolution of two sequences becomes a multiplication on the frequency side. Transforming both with the FFT, multiplying them and transforming back with the inverse FFT therefore computes the convolution of a long FIR filter with fewer operations than direct computation. Multiplying DFTs, however, corresponds to circular convolution, so the lengths must be matched by zero padding to prevent wrap-around at the ends.

Answer: B. About 1/100

FAQ

Must the number of FFT points be a power of two?

No. A power of two is often the simplest and fastest case, but mixed-radix FFTs that factor the size into 3, 5 and so on, and methods for prime sizes also exist, and common libraries compute any size. When a power of two is preferred, the number of points may be increased by zero padding.

Are FFT results the same as the DFT?

They compute the same values, so they agree apart from rounding errors. The FFT is not an approximation but an algorithm that reorganizes the calculation to obtain the same result faster.
