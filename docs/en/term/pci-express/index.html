What is PCI Express?

A high-speed serial expansion interface connecting the CPU with GPUs, SSDs, network cards and more, also written PCIe. Lanes are bundled for bandwidth, and speed roughly doubles each generation.

Japanese name: PCI Express

PCI Express appears in 29 practice questions across 3 topics, including I/O Interfaces & Buses, GPU Programming, SRAM and flash memory.

Field: I/O Interfaces & Buses

Lanes, links and topology

A PCI Express lane is one differential pair for transmitting plus one for receiving, four wires that carry data in both directions at once. Lanes are bundled into links of x1, x4, x8 or x16, with data striped across them so bandwidth scales with lane count. Unlike the old parallel PCI bus, where devices shared one bus and contended for it, PCIe is a tree of point-to-point links: the root complex beside the CPU and memory connects through switches to endpoints, and each link has its bandwidth to itself.

How to calculate PCIe bandwidth

Bandwidth is the per-lane signaling rate times the coding efficiency times the lane count. Generations 1 and 2 used 8b/10b encoding, so a 5 GT/s Gen 2 lane carries 4 Gb/s, or 500 MB/s per direction. From Gen 3 on, 128b/130b encoding with scrambling raised efficiency to about 98.5 percent, so an 8 GT/s Gen 3 x16 link gives about 15.8 GB/s per direction. Signaling rates have roughly doubled each generation, from 8 to 16, 32 and 64 GT/s for Gen 3 through Gen 6. Packet headers make real throughput somewhat lower.

The physical layer

Each lane is driven by a SerDes (serializer/deserializer) that turns wide on-chip buses into a single fast bit stream, while the receiver recovers the clock from the data itself, so transitions must occur often enough. To reach 64 GT/s without raising the symbol rate, Gen 6 switched from two-level NRZ to four-level PAM4, sending two bits per symbol. Squeezing four levels into the same swing leaves about one third of the spacing, roughly 9.5 dB less noise margin, so Gen 6 adds lightweight forward error correction (FEC). At start-up, link training settles on the widest lane count and fastest generation both ends support.

Where you will meet it

Graphics cards typically use x16 slots, NVMe SSDs x4 and network cards x1 to x8. NVMe, designed for flash over PCIe with many parallel command queues, reaches about 7 GB/s on Gen 4 x4, far beyond SATA's roughly 600 MB/s. For discrete GPUs, data must be copied across PCIe into GPU memory before computing, and PCIe bandwidth is more than ten times lower than the GPU's own memory bandwidth, so offloading pays only when much computation is done per transferred byte or data stays on the GPU.

Try it: questions about PCI Express

Which interface standard, built on PCI Express, is widely used to connect high-speed SSDs to the host?

  1. AIDE
  2. BUSB 1.1
  3. CNVMe
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. NVMe

NVMe (NVM Express) is designed to drive flash efficiently over PCI Express and supports up to 64K command queues in parallel. Compared with SATA (AHCI: one queue of up to 32 commands, about 600 MB/s maximum) built for HDDs, it offers lower latency and higher bandwidth—about 7 GB/s with PCIe 4.0 x4 and over 10 GB/s with PCIe 5.0. It is widely used in compact M.2 SSDs.

Which is closest to the efficiency of the 128b/130b encoding adopted from PCI Express 3.0 onward?

  1. AAbout 80%
  2. BAbout 50%
  3. CAbout 98.5%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. About 98.5%

128b/130b encoding sends 128 data bits with a 2-bit header as 130 bits, so efficiency is 128/130 ≈ 98.5%, a big reduction in waste compared with 8b/10b's 80%. Transitions are ensured by scrambling the data (XOR with a fixed pseudo-random sequence) to mix 0s and 1s. This is why PCI Express 3.0 nearly doubled effective throughput while raising the signaling rate only from 5 to 8 GT/s.

FAQ

Can an x4 PCIe card work in an x16 slot?

Yes. Link training negotiates the largest lane count and speed both sides support, so an x4 card in an x16 slot simply runs with four lanes. The same negotiation lets newer and older generations interoperate at the lower common speed.

What is the difference between GT/s and GB/s in PCIe?

GT/s is the raw signaling rate per lane, counting encoding overhead, while GB/s is usable data bandwidth. Convert by multiplying by the coding efficiency (0.8 for 8b/10b, about 0.985 for 128b/130b) and the lane count, then dividing by 8.

Why did PCIe 6.0 switch to PAM4?

Doubling the bit rate with NRZ would require doubling the symbol rate, and channel loss grows sharply with frequency. PAM4 sends two bits per symbol at the same symbol rate as Gen 5, at the cost of smaller noise margin, which FEC compensates for.
