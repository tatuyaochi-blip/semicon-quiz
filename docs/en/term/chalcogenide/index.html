What is Chalcogenide?

A compound containing group 16 elements such as sulfur, selenium or tellurium. It is used as the recording material of phase-change memory (such as GST) and in selectors (OTS) that switch on abruptly above a threshold voltage.

Japanese name: カルコゲナイド

What counts as a chalcogenide

The chalcogens are the group 16 elements: oxygen, sulfur, selenium and tellurium. Although oxygen belongs to the group, oxides are normally treated as their own class, so in electronics a chalcogenide usually means a compound built on sulfur, selenium or especially tellurium, combined with elements such as germanium, antimony or arsenic. Many of these compounds readily form glasses, meaning amorphous solids that remain stable at room temperature, and some can be switched back and forth between amorphous and crystalline phases with heat. That combination is what makes them useful in memory devices.

How GST stores a bit

In phase-change memory (PCM) the cell is a small volume of a chalcogenide such as Ge2Sb2Te5 (GST) heated by the cell current itself. A short, high-current RESET pulse melts the region and the rapid cool-down freezes it as high-resistance amorphous material. A longer, more moderate SET pulse holds it above the crystallization temperature but below melting, so it recrystallizes within tens to hundreds of nanoseconds into a low-resistance state. Reading uses a small voltage that does not disturb the phase. Because the two states differ in resistance by two to three orders of magnitude, intermediate levels can hold more than one bit.

Threshold switching and OTS selectors

Amorphous chalcogenides show threshold switching: above a threshold voltage they snap into a conducting state within nanoseconds, and they return to high resistance once the current falls below a holding value. In PCM this effect is what lets enough current flow through an amorphous cell to heat and crystallize it. An ovonic threshold switch (OTS) uses a composition that switches this way but does not crystallize, so it stores nothing and acts purely as a selector. Placed in series with each storage element in a cross-point array, its very low off-current suppresses sneak paths through unselected cells, which makes stacked arrays practical.

Engineering trade-offs

GST crystallizes at roughly 150 °C, so data held in the amorphous state can be lost at high temperature; Ge-rich compositions raise that limit for automotive and other hot environments, where stored data must survive long periods at elevated temperature. The RESET operation needs a relatively large current to melt the material, which constrains the size of the access device and the power budget. The resistance of the amorphous phase also drifts slowly upward after programming, which complicates multi-level storage because the gaps between levels shift over time.

Answer: A. OTS (ovonic threshold switch)

An OTS (ovonic threshold switch) is a chalcogenide device that snaps to low resistance within nanoseconds once its threshold voltage is exceeded and returns to high resistance when the current drops. Unlike PCM it does not crystallize or store a state, so it works as a selector. Its strong nonlinearity—tiny off-current, large on-current—suppresses sneak currents through unselected cells, enabling stacked crosspoint memories.

FAQ

Is oxygen a chalcogen?

Yes, oxygen is a group 16 element and therefore a chalcogen, but oxides are conventionally treated separately. In device engineering, chalcogenide almost always refers to sulfides, selenides and tellurides.

What is GST in phase change memory?

GST is a germanium-antimony-tellurium chalcogenide, typically Ge2Sb2Te5. It switches between a low-resistance crystalline phase and a high-resistance amorphous phase when heated by current pulses, and that resistance difference stores the data.

What is the difference between an OTS and a phase-change cell?

Both are chalcogenides that threshold-switch, but a phase-change cell crystallizes and keeps its new state, while an OTS stays amorphous and returns to high resistance once the current is removed. The OTS is therefore used as a selector, not as storage.
