What is CD-SEM?

A scanning electron microscope for measuring pattern line widths (critical dimensions). Used for dimensional control after lithography and etching.

Japanese name: CD-SEM

CD-SEM appears in 3 practice questions across 2 topics, including Metrology and analysis, Lithography.

Field: Metrology and analysis

How a CD-SEM measures a line

A critical-dimension scanning electron microscope (CD-SEM) scans a finely focused, low-energy electron beam, typically below about 1 keV, across the pattern from above and collects the secondary electrons that are emitted. Edges and sidewalls emit more secondary electrons than flat surfaces, so a line appears with bright edges in the intensity profile, or linescan. An algorithm locates each edge in the profile, for example by a threshold or maximum-slope criterion, and reports the distance between them as the critical dimension (CD). Low beam energy limits charging and damage to resist and dielectric films.

What it is used to control

CD-SEMs measure line and space widths, contact hole diameters, line ends and gaps after lithography and again after etching, the difference revealing the etch bias. Repeating measurements across many fields and wafers yields CD uniformity (CDU), usually stated as 3σ, which at advanced nodes must stay around a nanometer on features tens of nanometers wide. The same images give line edge roughness (LER) and line width roughness (LWR). The results feed back to lithography, for example by correcting exposure dose within each field or adjusting focus.

Measurement pitfalls

ArF photoresists are sensitive to electrons: the beam breaks bonds and the resist shrinks during measurement, so repeated scans read progressively smaller. Engineers minimize dose and characterize the shrinkage so it can be corrected. Insulating films can charge, distorting images, and stage or beam drift shifts readings over time. Because the reported CD depends on the edge-detection algorithm and where on a sloped sidewall the edge is defined, CD-SEMs excel at precision and matching between tools rather than absolute accuracy, and they see the profile from above only.

CD-SEM vs. OCD, AFM and hybrid metrology

Optical critical dimension metrology (OCD), or scatterometry, fits measured spectra from a periodic target to a model, giving fast, nondestructive estimates of width, height and sidewall angle averaged over the target, but it depends on the model and needs dedicated gratings. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) probes the profile physically but is slow and limited by tip shape. Cross-section transmission electron microscopy (TEM) is accurate but destructive. Hybrid metrology combines such methods, for example fixing film thickness from one technique so another can extract the remaining parameters more reliably.

Answer: A. Shrinkage (resist shrinkage due to the electron beam)

ArF resists are sensitive to electrons: beam irradiation during measurement breaks down molecules and reduces volume, so patterns shrink. Because values change with each measurement, dose is minimized or the shrinkage is characterized in advance and corrected. Charging is charge buildup on insulators that distorts images, and drift is gradual shift of the image or readings over time; neither is shrinkage.

What is the approach called that combines data from multiple measurement methods (optical, CD-SEM, AFM and so on) to determine dimensions and shapes more accurately than any single method?

  1. ABlind metrology
  2. BHybrid metrology
  3. CSingle shot

Answer: B. Hybrid metrology

For advanced nanoscale structures, any single metrology technique is limited in what it can measure and how accurately. Hybrid metrology combines techniques to cover each other's weaknesses, for example feeding film thickness measured precisely by XRR into an OCD model as a fixed value so the remaining shape parameters are extracted more stably. The key is to identify what each technique measures best and combine them.

FAQ

What does CD-SEM stand for?

Critical-dimension scanning electron microscope: an SEM optimized to measure pattern widths such as gate lengths, lines and contact holes on wafers.

Why does resist shrink during CD-SEM measurement?

Electron irradiation breaks chemical bonds in sensitive resists such as ArF resists, causing volume loss, so the pattern gets narrower with each scan. Low dose and correction for known shrinkage limit the error.

What is the difference between CD-SEM and scatterometry?

CD-SEM images individual features from above with an electron beam, while scatterometry (OCD) fits optical spectra from a periodic target to a model, giving averaged dimensions and profile information quickly and nondestructively.
