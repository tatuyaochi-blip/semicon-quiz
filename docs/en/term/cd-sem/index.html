What is CD-SEM?

A scanning electron microscope for measuring pattern line widths (critical dimensions). Used for dimensional control after lithography and etching.

Japanese name: CD-SEM

CD-SEM appears in 3 practice questions across 2 topics, including Metrology and analysis, Lithography.

Try it: questions about CD-SEM

When ArF resist patterns are measured with a CD-SEM, what is the phenomenon called in which electron-beam irradiation makes the patterns shrink?

  1. AShrinkage (resist shrinkage due to the electron beam)
  2. BCharge-up
  3. CDrift
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Shrinkage (resist shrinkage due to the electron beam)

ArF resists are sensitive to electrons: beam irradiation during measurement breaks down molecules and reduces volume, so patterns shrink. Because values change with each measurement, dose is minimized or the shrinkage is characterized in advance and corrected. Charging is charge buildup on insulators that distorts images, and drift is gradual shift of the image or readings over time; neither is shrinkage.

What is the approach called that combines data from multiple measurement methods (optical, CD-SEM, AFM and so on) to determine dimensions and shapes more accurately than any single method?

  1. ABlind metrology
  2. BHybrid metrology
  3. CSingle shot
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Hybrid metrology

For advanced nanoscale structures, any single metrology technique is limited in what it can measure and how accurately. Hybrid metrology combines techniques to cover each other's weaknesses, for example feeding film thickness measured precisely by XRR into an OCD model as a fixed value so the remaining shape parameters are extracted more stably. The key is to identify what each technique measures best and combine them.
