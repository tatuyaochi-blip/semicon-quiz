What is Derating?

Using parts with margin below their ratings for voltage, current, temperature and so on, rather than at the full rating, to improve reliability. Typical guidelines are voltage at no more than 70 to 80% of rating, or junction temperature at no more than 125 °C against a 150 °C maximum.

Field: How to read datasheets

Why parts are run below their ratings

Most failure mechanisms accelerate with stress: chemical degradation and diffusion speed up exponentially with temperature, dielectrics wear out faster at higher field, and electromigration worsens with current density and temperature. Running parts with margin therefore lowers failure rates and also absorbs real-world effects that the nominal operating point hides, such as component tolerances, transients, ambient extremes and aging. Typical guidelines keep applied voltage at about 70 to 80% of the rating and junction temperature at about 125 °C against a 150 °C maximum, with exact numbers set by company or industry rules.

Voltage margin in power devices

In power electronics, switching a current through stray inductance produces voltage spikes of L·di/dt above the DC bus, and sustained high voltage makes devices vulnerable to single-event burnout triggered by cosmic-ray neutrons, a risk that rises steeply with voltage and with altitude. Designers therefore pick devices rated around 1.5 times the working voltage, such as 650 V parts on a 400 V bus. Ceramic and electrolytic capacitors are derated too, since capacitance, leakage and lifetime all depend on applied voltage and temperature.

Thermal derating and power limits

Datasheets often show a power derating curve: full rated dissipation up to a reference temperature, then a straight-line decrease to zero at the maximum junction temperature. It follows from Tj = Ta + P·θJA, where θJA is junction-to-ambient thermal resistance. With Tj(max) = 125 °C, θJA = 50 °C/W and Ta = 85 °C, allowable power is (125 − 85)/50 = 0.8 W. Because θJA depends strongly on board layout and airflow, designs keep further margin below this calculated value.

Derating in timing analysis

In chip design the word has a related meaning. On-chip variation (OCV) derating in static timing analysis multiplies path delays by factors to cover local differences in transistors, wires, voltage and temperature. For setup checks the launch clock and data paths are made slower and the capture clock faster; for hold checks the factors are reversed. Common path pessimism removal (CRPR) then credits back the impossible pessimism on clock buffers shared by both paths. Advanced OCV variants scale the factors with path depth or distance.

  1. ATo ensure reliability against switching surge voltages and failures caused by cosmic rays
  2. BTo lower the component price
  3. CTo lower the on-resistance

Answer: A. To ensure reliability against switching surge voltages and failures caused by cosmic rays

Real circuits add surge voltages from parasitic inductance during switching, and at sustained high voltage, cosmic-ray neutrons can generate charge inside the device and cause sudden failure (single-event burnout), whose probability rises steeply with voltage. Designers therefore choose devices rated about 1.5× the operating voltage—650 V parts for a 400 V bus, for example—with even more margin at high altitude.

For OCV derating in setup analysis, the launch clock path and data path delays are multiplied by 1.1 and the capture clock path delay by 0.9. With a launch clock path of 1.0 ns, a data path (including clock-to-output) of 1.5 ns, a capture clock path of 1.0 ns (no common portion), a period of 3.0 ns and a setup time of 0.1 ns, what is the setup slack?

  1. A1.05 ns
  2. B1.40 ns
  3. C1.25 ns
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1.05 ns

OCV derating accounts for delay varying with location even within the same chip, and for setup assumes the most pessimistic combination (slow launch side, early capture clock). The arrival time is 1.1 × (1.0 + 1.5) = 2.75 ns and the required time is 3.0 + 0.9 × 1.0 − 0.1 = 3.8 ns, so the slack is 1.05 ns. 1.40 ns is the value without derating, and 1.25 ns applies the factor to the data path only. In hold analysis the factors are applied the other way around.

FAQ

What does derating mean in electronics?

It means operating a component below its maximum ratings for voltage, current, power or temperature to increase reliability and absorb transients and tolerances. A common rule is 70 to 80% of rated voltage and a junction temperature well below the absolute maximum.

How do you read a power derating curve?

The curve gives allowable dissipation versus ambient or case temperature: flat up to a reference point, then falling linearly to zero at the maximum junction temperature. Its slope is the inverse of the thermal resistance.

What is OCV derating in static timing analysis?

It applies multiplying factors to cell and wire delays so that launch and capture paths are evaluated at pessimistic, different speeds, covering location-dependent variation within one chip.
