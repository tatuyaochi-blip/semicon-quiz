What is Target row refresh (TRR)?

One of the countermeasures against Rowhammer. Frequently activated rows are tracked and the rows next to them are refreshed earlier than usual to prevent bit flips. It is built into the DRAM or the memory controller. In-DRAM implementations often can track only a limited number of rows, and research has shown cases in which they were bypassed by activating many rows in turn.

Japanese name: TRR(ターゲットロウリフレッシュ)

Answer: C. The number of rows it can track is limited, so activating many rows leaves some unprotected, and flips could also occur in rows two rows away

Because of circuit-area limits, many in-DRAM TRR implementations recorded only a few frequently activated rows and gave the rows next to them extra refreshes. TRRespass, published in 2020, showed that activating many rows in turn overwhelmed the records, so bits flipped in unprotected rows. Half-Double, published in 2021, showed that flips occur even in rows two rows away, and that the extra refreshes (activations) meant to protect the adjacent rows can themselves add to the interference on rows further out. These results led to calls for mechanisms that count activations of every row exactly and for layered countermeasures across the whole system.
