What is CFET (complementary FET)?

A structure that stacks nMOS and pMOS transistors vertically. It is under development because it could greatly shrink the area of CMOS circuits.

Japanese name: CFET(相補型FET)

Field: Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA)

How a CFET stacks the CMOS pair

In conventional CMOS, every logic gate places an nMOS and a pMOS transistor side by side, so a standard cell must hold both footprints plus the spacing between them. A complementary FET (CFET) builds one device directly on top of the other, typically an nMOS nanosheet stack above or below a pMOS nanosheet stack, with a shared gate running vertically through both. Because the two transistors of an inverter share the same input and output, folding them into one vertical stack can implement the same function in close to half the area.

Why CFET is hard to manufacture

The upper and lower tiers need different gate work-function metals and different source/drain epitaxy, such as boron-doped SiGe for the p device and phosphorus-doped silicon for the n device, all formed inside one tall structure. Two broad routes are studied: monolithic integration, where both tiers are patterned in one continuous sequence, and sequential integration, where the top tier is built on a layer bonded above the finished bottom tier. In both cases thermal budget, alignment between tiers and isolating the two tiers electrically are major difficulties.

GAA vs. forksheet vs. CFET

A gate-all-around (GAA) nanosheet transistor wraps the gate fully around stacked horizontal sheets, but n and p devices still sit side by side, typically tens of nanometers apart. A forksheet FET inserts a dielectric wall roughly ten-odd nanometers thick between n and p sheets so they can sit much closer, at the cost of the gate wrapping each sheet on only three sides. CFET removes the lateral n-to-p spacing altogether by moving one device above the other, which is why it is usually described as the step after GAA and forksheet.

Wiring becomes the bottleneck

Once transistors are stacked, the hard part is reaching them. Contacts to the lower device must pass beside the upper one without shorting to it, and both tiers need power and signal connections in a cell that has shrunk. This is why CFET is often discussed together with backside power delivery, which can feed the bottom tier from below while front-side wiring handles signals. Development targets practical use in the 2030s, and the routing scheme matters as much to that outcome as the transistor itself.

FAQ

What does CFET stand for?

CFET stands for complementary field-effect transistor. It refers to a structure in which the nMOS and pMOS transistors of a CMOS circuit are stacked vertically instead of being placed side by side.

What is the difference between CFET and GAA?

A GAA nanosheet transistor describes how the gate surrounds the channel, while n and p devices still sit next to each other. A CFET keeps nanosheet-style channels but stacks the n and p devices on top of each other to shrink the cell footprint.

Why can a CFET nearly halve cell area?

In a CMOS gate the nMOS and pMOS share inputs and outputs, so most of the area goes to placing two devices and the gap between them side by side. Stacking one over the other removes that lateral duplication, leaving roughly one device footprint per n-p pair.
