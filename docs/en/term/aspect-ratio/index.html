What is Aspect ratio?

The ratio of a hole's or trench's depth to its width. Higher ratios make etching and deposition harder; memory holes in 3D NAND reach several tens or more.

Japanese name: アスペクト比

Aspect ratio appears in 6 practice questions across 5 topics, including Etching, DRAM, CMP and cleaning.

Field: Etching

Calculating aspect ratio

Aspect ratio is depth divided by width. A hole 100 nm across and 1 µm deep has an aspect ratio of 10. Memory holes in 3D NAND, about 100 nm wide and several µm deep, reach 50 to 100, and DRAM capacitor holes are also extremely tall and narrow. The same idea applies to lines: a copper interconnect's thickness-to-width ratio is often around 2. Aspect ratio should not be confused with selectivity, which is the ratio of etch rates of two materials, or with the overall shape of a die.

Why deep, narrow features are hard to etch

As aspect ratio rises, fewer reactive radicals reach the bottom because they react with or stick to the walls along the way, ions are deflected by charge built up on insulating sidewalls, and reaction products escape slowly. The etch rate therefore falls with depth, an effect called aspect-ratio-dependent etching (ARDE) or microloading, so narrow holes end up shallower than wide ones etched at the same time. Deep etches also suffer bowing, twisting and tapered bottoms, which is why high-aspect-ratio etching is one of the hardest steps in memory fabrication.

Deposition and gap fill

Physical vapor deposition (PVD) atoms travel in nearly straight lines, so sidewalls and bottoms of deep features are shadowed and receive little film. CVD under surface-reaction-limited conditions coats much more uniformly, and ALD can coat features with aspect ratios of several tens because each step is self-limiting. Filling a gap completely is harder than lining it: film builds up at the opening and pinches off, trapping a void. High-density plasma CVD, which sputters back the corners, and flowable CVD, which fills from the bottom, were developed for this.

Collapse, wiring and memory scaling

Tall, thin structures can be pulled together during drying by the surface tension of liquid between them, and the force grows with aspect ratio and with narrower gaps. Low-surface-tension rinses such as IPA, hydrophobic surface treatments and supercritical CO2 drying reduce pattern collapse. In interconnects, raising aspect ratio lowers resistance but increases sidewall capacitance to neighbors, so it is balanced per metal level. In DRAM, capacitor aspect ratio is approaching practical limits, one motivation for stacking cells in 3D DRAM.

FAQ

How is aspect ratio calculated in semiconductor etching?

Divide the depth of the hole or trench by its width or opening diameter. For example, a trench 50 nm wide and 500 nm deep has an aspect ratio of 10.

What is ARDE in plasma etching?

Aspect-ratio-dependent etching (ARDE) is the slowing of etch rate as features become narrower and deeper, because fewer radicals and ions reach the bottom and products escape more slowly. It makes narrow features shallower than wide ones etched at the same time.

Why do high-aspect-ratio patterns collapse?

When rinse liquid dries between tall, narrow structures, capillary forces from surface tension pull neighboring structures together. Higher aspect ratio and narrower spacing increase the risk.
