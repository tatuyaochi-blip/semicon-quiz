What is Wafer test (probe test)?

Checking the electrical characteristics of each chip on the wafer by touching probes to its pads before dicing. It finds bad chips before assembly so they are not packaged needlessly.

Japanese name: ウエハテスト(プローブテスト)

Field: Testing and inspection

How probing works

A prober loads the wafer onto a chuck, aligns it optically and steps it so that the needles of a probe card land on each die's pads, which are tens of micrometres square. After first touch, the needles are pushed a little further, called overdrive, so the tips scrub through the aluminium oxide and make stable contact. Automatic test equipment (ATE) with hundreds to thousands of channels then supplies power, drives patterns and measures responses. The chuck can be heated or cooled, roughly −40 to 150 °C, and many probe cards contact several dies at once to cut test time.

What gets tested

Typical wafer-level tests check continuity, opens and shorts, supply and leakage currents, and logic function through scan patterns or built-in self-test, plus analog and memory blocks. Memory testing also finds failing cells whose addresses are programmed into fuses or antifuses, redirecting accesses to spare rows and columns (redundancy repair). Analog references are often trimmed the same way. Some products also write the lot number, wafer number and die coordinates into on-chip fuses, so a field return can be traced back to its manufacturing and test history.

Wafer test vs. final test

The usual sequence is wafer test, assembly, final test and shipment, sometimes with burn-in or system-level test added. Wafer test screens out bad dies before money is spent packaging them, since defects cost more the later they are found. Final test checks the packaged part and confirms nothing was damaged in assembly; some high-speed measurements are also easier through a socket than through probe needles. In multi-die packages, wafer test must deliver known good die, because one bad die can scrap the whole assembly. Stage yields multiply: 95% process yield times 90% test yield gives about 85.5%.

Using the test data

Results are stored as electronic wafer maps rather than the ink dots once used to mark bad dies; die bonders read the map and pick only good, correctly graded dies. The maps are also a diagnostic tool. Failures clustered at the wafer edge point to deposition or etch uniformity, failures repeating at the same position in every exposure shot point to a reticle defect, and streaks suggest scratches. Adaptive test changes limits based on upstream data, and part average testing rejects in-spec dies that are statistical outliers, a common practice for automotive parts.

Answer: C. To identify the lot, wafer and position of a returned failed chip and cross-reference it with manufacturing and test records

Once a chip is sealed in a package, you cannot tell from outside which wafer it came from or where on the wafer it was. If identification data is written into the chip, it can be read out when a failed part is returned from the field, tracing back to manufacturing conditions and even wafer test measurements. It also helps narrow down which other products processed on the same equipment might be affected. This is chip-level traceability. Encryption keys generally use a separate dedicated mechanism that is hard to read out.

Answer: A. Before dicing, probes contact each chip on the wafer to test its electrical characteristics

In wafer test, a prober moves the wafer so that probe-card needles touch each chip's pads, and a tester checks electrical characteristics and function. Catching bad chips at this stage avoids wasting package materials and assembly effort, since defects cost more the later they are found. Testing after encapsulation is final test, and running products long-term to measure failure rates is reliability testing.

FAQ

What is the difference between wafer test and final test?

Wafer test probes each die on the uncut wafer to screen out bad dies before packaging, while final test checks the finished, packaged part before shipment. Both use automatic test equipment, paired with a prober and a handler respectively.

What is a probe card?

A probe card is the interface between the tester and the wafer, carrying many fine needles or MEMS contacts laid out to match the pad positions of one or more dies. It is custom-made for each product.

Why test chips on the wafer before packaging?

Packaging a defective die wastes materials and assembly cost, and in multi-die packages one bad die can ruin the whole module. Wafer test also enables memory repair, trimming and early feedback on process problems.
