What is Crosstalk?

Noise that appears on one wire when the signal on a neighboring wire changes, mainly through capacitive coupling. Wider spacing and shield wires reduce it.

Japanese name: クロストーク

Crosstalk appears in 8 practice questions across 4 topics, including Interconnect processes, A/D and D/A conversion, Image sensors and optical devices.

Field: Interconnect processes

How coupling noise arises

Two neighboring wires form a capacitor. When the aggressor wire switches, it injects charge through the coupling capacitance Cc into the victim wire. If the victim is floating or only weakly driven, the resulting glitch is roughly the aggressor swing multiplied by Cc / (Cc + Cg), where Cg is the victim's capacitance to ground and other wires. A driven victim pulls back toward its level at a rate set by its driver resistance, so weak drivers and long parallel runs give the largest and longest glitches. A glitch that reaches a latch, clock or dynamic node can flip a stored value.

Crosstalk also changes delay

Crosstalk matters even when nothing glitches, because it alters timing. If the victim and aggressor switch in the same direction at the same time, the voltage across Cc barely changes and the coupling capacitance effectively vanishes, speeding the victim. If they switch in opposite directions, the voltage across Cc changes by twice the swing, so the capacitance effectively doubles, a Miller-factor effect, slowing the victim. Static timing analysis with signal-integrity awareness therefore uses extracted coupling capacitances and switching windows to bound the best and worst cases.

Why scaling makes it worse

A wire's capacitance has lateral components to neighbors on the same layer, vertical components to layers above and below and to the substrate, and fringe components from fields curling around its edges. In a parallel-plate approximation C = εS/d, so halving spacing roughly doubles lateral capacitance. Advanced metal layers use tight pitch and tall wires, with thickness-to-width ratios around 2, to keep resistance down, which further enlarges facing sidewall area. Lateral coupling thus becomes the dominant share of total capacitance, and parasitic extraction with field solvers after place and route quantifies it.

Reducing crosstalk

The main levers are spacing, shielding and dielectric constant. Wider spacing on critical nets, power or ground shield wires between sensitive signals, and low-k dielectrics such as carbon-doped oxide (about 2.7–3.0) or porous films (about 2.2–2.5) instead of SiO2 (about 3.9) all reduce coupling. Stronger victim drivers recover faster, and avoiding long parallel runs limits the coupling length. The word is used beyond wiring: image-sensor pixels leak light and charge into neighbors, and multiplexed sample-and-hold inputs can carry residue from a previous channel.

Which correctly describes the acquisition time of a sample-and-hold circuit?

  1. AThe time, in track mode, for the hold capacitor voltage to settle to the input within the required accuracy
  2. BHow fast the held voltage gradually decreases
  3. CThe time until the conversion result is output digitally
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The time, in track mode, for the hold capacitor voltage to settle to the input within the required accuracy

Acquisition time is the time needed in track mode for the hold capacitor's voltage to approach the input within a specified error. It is RC settling, so reaching 1/2 LSB takes (N+1)·ln2 time constants—about nine for 12 bits. If it is too short, the previous sample's influence remains, causing crosstalk when multiplexing channels. Higher source resistance requires longer acquisition time.

What is the main purpose of deep trench isolation (DTI), in which deep trenches filled with insulators are formed between fine pixels?

  1. ATo prevent light or charge from leaking into neighboring pixels (crosstalk)
  2. BTo make pixels larger
  3. CTo eliminate the need for color filters
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. To prevent light or charge from leaking into neighboring pixels (crosstalk)

As pixels shrink, oblique light and charge generated deep in the silicon spill into neighbors, mixing colors and reducing resolution. Deep trench isolation (DTI) etches trenches several µm deep between pixels and fills them with oxide or metal to block light and charge, suppressing this crosstalk. Trenches are usually etched from the back side, and passivating the trench walls to limit dark current is essential.

FAQ

What are aggressor and victim nets in crosstalk analysis?

The aggressor is the switching net that injects noise, and the victim is the neighboring net that receives it through coupling capacitance. The same net can be an aggressor in one analysis and a victim in another.

How do you reduce crosstalk in chip layout?

Increase spacing, insert grounded or power shield wires, shorten parallel routes, and strengthen victim drivers. Low-k dielectrics reduce coupling capacitance at the process level.

Is on-chip crosstalk capacitive or inductive?

On-chip crosstalk in short and medium wires is dominated by capacitive coupling. Inductive coupling becomes significant for long, fast global wires, packages and circuit boards.
