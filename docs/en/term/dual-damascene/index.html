What is Dual damascene?

Forming both the trench for a wire and the hole for a via in the insulator, filling them with copper together and finishing with a single CMP step. It reduces process steps.

Japanese name: デュアルダマシン法

Field: Interconnect processes

How dual damascene builds a wiring level

A dielectric stack, usually a low-k film with thin etch-stop or cap layers, is deposited first. Lithography and etching then open two kinds of features in it: via holes that reach down to the wiring level below, and trenches shaped like the wires of the current level. A thin diffusion barrier such as TaN/Ta and a copper seed layer are sputtered, copper is electroplated to fill vias and trenches together, and chemical mechanical polishing (CMP) removes the excess copper and barrier from the top surface, leaving metal inlaid only where it was patterned.

Why copper needs a damascene process

Aluminum wiring was made by depositing a metal film and etching it into lines. Copper is difficult to pattern that way because its reaction products with common plasma etch chemistries are not volatile at normal process temperatures. Damascene processing sidesteps the problem by etching the dielectric instead and filling it with metal, much like the inlay craft of Damascus that gave the method its name. The barrier layer is essential because copper diffuses readily into silicon and dielectrics, where it degrades devices and insulation.

Single vs. dual damascene

Single damascene builds vias and lines in separate passes, each with its own dielectric patterning, barrier and seed deposition, plating and CMP. Dual damascene patterns both features before a single metallization, saving one round each of deposition, plating and CMP per wiring level. Because the via and the line above it are one continuous piece of copper, there is no barrier interface between them, which lowers via resistance and helps electromigration reliability. Advanced chips repeat this module for each of their many copper levels.

Via-first, trench-first and fill challenges

Dual damascene comes in variants that differ in which feature is opened first: via-first etches the holes before the trenches, trench-first does the reverse, and hard-mask schemes help align vias to the trench above. At advanced nodes vias are only about 20 nm across and a chip contains billions of them, so void-free filling, uniform CMP and avoiding damage to fragile low-k dielectrics during etch and resist removal strongly affect both performance and yield. A single open via can kill a circuit.

Answer: A. A conductor that electrically connects upper and lower wiring layers

A via is a vertical conductor formed by filling a hole in the interlayer dielectric with metal, linking wiring on adjacent levels. At advanced nodes vias are only about 20 nm across, and a chip contains billions of them. Connections from transistors to the first wiring level are called contacts. Dual damascene fills trenches and vias with copper together. Via resistance and open failures strongly affect performance and yield.

FAQ

What is the difference between single and dual damascene?

Single damascene forms vias and wiring lines in separate deposition, plating and CMP sequences. Dual damascene opens both in the dielectric first and fills them with copper in one sequence, saving steps and avoiding a barrier layer between via and line.

Why is copper wiring not made by etching like aluminum?

Copper does not form volatile etch products easily with standard plasma chemistries, so patterning a copper film by dry etching is impractical. Instead, the dielectric is patterned and filled with copper, then polished flat.

Where does the name damascene come from?

It comes from the traditional inlay craft associated with Damascus, in which gold or silver was set into grooves cut in a metal surface, just as copper is inlaid into grooves in the dielectric.
