What is Control chart?

A chart that plots process measurements over time against a center line and control limits (usually ±3σ) to judge whether the process is stable. Points outside the limits or runs on one side signal problems.

Japanese name: 管理図

In a control chart, which is an example that should be judged abnormal even though all points are within the control limits?

  1. APoints scattered randomly on both sides of the center line
  2. BAll points moving up and down inside the control limits
  3. CMany consecutive points on one side of the center line
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Many consecutive points on one side of the center line

Even within control limits, patterns such as many consecutive points on one side of the centerline (a run) or steadily rising or falling points (a trend) are unlikely to be chance and signal that the process mean is starting to shift. For example, the chance of nine points in a row on the same side is below 1%. Such rules catch problems before out-of-spec product appears. Points scattering randomly around the centerline indicate a normal state.

Which control chart is used to control the fraction defective?

  1. AX̄-R chart
  2. Bp chart
  3. CScatter diagram
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. p chart

A p chart plots the fraction defective for each sample, such as a lot, making it an attribute control chart. Its control limits widen or narrow as sample size changes. Related charts include the np chart for defective counts and the c chart for defect counts. For variables such as film thickness or dimensions, X̄-R charts monitor mean and spread, while a scatter plot examines correlation.
