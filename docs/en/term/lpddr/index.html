What is LPDDR?

Short for low-power DDR: a DRAM standard for smartphones and laptops with lower supply voltages and enhanced power-saving features.

Japanese name: LPDDR

Field: DRAM

Where the power savings come from

LPDDR (low-power double data rate) DRAM saves energy in several ways. Its supply and I/O voltages are lower than those of desktop DDR, and dynamic power in the interface scales with the square of voltage. When the device is idle, deep low-power states switch off most internal circuitry, partial-array self-refresh refreshes only the banks that hold data, and temperature-compensated self-refresh lengthens the refresh interval when the chip is cool, because DRAM cells leak more slowly at low temperature. Phones spend most of their time idle, so these standby features matter as much as active power.

Short, soldered connections

LPDDR is designed for short point-to-point links. The DRAM is stacked on top of the application processor (package-on-package, PoP) or soldered right next to it, so traces are only millimetres long and need little drive strength. The interface is split into narrow channels, 16 bits wide since LPDDR4, which lets a processor access several channels independently. Because the memory is soldered rather than plugged into a DIMM socket, capacity in an LPDDR-based laptop or phone is normally fixed at purchase and cannot be upgraded later.

LPDDR vs. DDR, GDDR and HBM

All four are DRAM standards defined by JEDEC, but they optimize different things. Standard DDR on DIMMs prioritizes capacity and upgradability for desktops and servers. LPDDR prioritizes energy per bit and standby power for battery-powered devices, while still reaching high data rates; LPDDR5X runs at 8533 MT/s and beyond. GDDR pushes per-pin speed for graphics cards, and HBM stacks dies with a very wide interface on a silicon interposer for maximum bandwidth. LPDDR generation numbers do not map one-to-one onto DDR: LPDDR4 and DDR4 use different interfaces and protocols.

Answer: C. Devices that require low power, such as smartphones

LPDDR (Low Power DDR) is mobile DRAM that cuts power with low supply voltages, deep power-down modes and temperature-aware refresh. Used in smartphones, tablets and thin laptops, it is stacked on the processor package (PoP) or placed right beside it to keep wiring short. Because it is soldered down rather than socketed, it usually cannot be upgraded later; LPDDR5X reaches 8533 MT/s and beyond.

FAQ

What does LPDDR stand for?

LPDDR stands for low-power double data rate, a family of mobile DRAM standards published by JEDEC. Double data rate means data is transferred on both edges of the clock.

Can LPDDR memory be upgraded?

Usually not. LPDDR is soldered to the board or stacked on the processor package to keep wiring short, so the installed capacity is fixed when the device is built.

Is LPDDR slower than DDR?

Not necessarily; recent LPDDR generations reach data rates comparable to or higher than desktop DDR. The main trade-offs are capacity, which is limited by the soldered packages, and the lack of upgradeable modules.
