What is 3D packaging?

Stacking chips vertically and connecting them with TSVs or hybrid bonding. Shorter connections improve speed and power, but heat removal becomes a challenge.

Try it: questions about 3D packaging

Which is especially likely to be a challenge in 3D packaging, which stacks chips vertically?

  1. AWiring becomes too long
  2. BChips are too light to secure
  3. CTemperature rise from heat generated by stacked chips, and removing that heat
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Temperature rise from heat generated by stacked chips, and removing that heat

3D stacking shortens wiring, but stacking heat-generating dies traps heat and makes it hard to remove from inner dies. Heat per unit area rises with the number of layers, and heat must pass through multiple bonding layers and thin silicon to escape. Higher temperature degrades performance and reliability, so thermal design, such as placing the hottest die next to the heat sink, is critical. Wiring actually gets shorter.

Which correctly describes the difference between 2.5D and 3D packaging?

  1. A2.5D places chips side by side on an intermediate substrate (such as an interposer), while 3D stacks chips vertically
  2. B2.5D stacks chips vertically, while 3D places chips side by side
  3. CThere is no difference between 2.5D and 3D
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2.5D places chips side by side on an intermediate substrate (such as an interposer), while 3D stacks chips vertically

2.5D packaging places multiple dies side by side on an intermediary substrate such as a silicon interposer and connects them through its fine wiring. 3D packaging stacks dies vertically, connecting them with TSVs or hybrid bonding. 2.5D dissipates heat more easily, while 3D gives the shortest wiring. Combined structures are also used, such as 3D-stacked HBM placed beside a GPU in a 2.5D arrangement.
