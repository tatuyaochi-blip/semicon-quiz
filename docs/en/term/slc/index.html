What is SLC (single-level cell)?

A flash format that stores one bit per cell. With only two threshold-voltage states it is faster and more durable than multi-level formats.

Japanese name: SLC(シングルレベルセル)

SLC (single-level cell) appears in 8 practice questions across 2 topics, including SRAM and flash memory, Emerging memories.

Field: SRAM and flash memory

How an SLC cell stores one bit

A flash cell is a transistor whose threshold voltage (Vt) shifts according to how much charge sits in its floating gate or charge-trap layer. Programming pushes electrons through the thin tunnel oxide, raising Vt; erasing removes them, lowering it. A single-level cell (SLC) uses only two Vt states, erased and programmed, so a read needs just one reference voltage placed in the wide gap between the two distributions. Large margin means the cell can be programmed with fewer verify steps and read reliably even as the distributions broaden with wear, age or temperature.

SLC vs. MLC, TLC and QLC

Storing N bits per cell requires 2ᴺ distinguishable Vt states: 2 for SLC, 4 for multi-level cell (MLC), 8 for triple-level cell (TLC) and 16 for quad-level cell (QLC). The usable window is only a few volts in every case, so the spacing between states shrinks rapidly as N grows. Multi-level cells therefore need finely stepped program-and-verify sequences and several read references, making them slower, and their narrow margins tolerate less degradation. In exchange they store two to four times more data on the same cells, lowering cost per bit, which is why TLC dominates mainstream SSDs.

Why SLC endures more cycles

Each program or erase drives electrons through the tunnel oxide at fields around 10 MV/cm. Over many cycles some charge becomes trapped and new defects form, narrowing the Vt window and enabling stress-induced leakage current (SILC), which lets stored charge escape. SLC's wide gap absorbs much more of this damage before reads fail. Typical endurance is about 50,000–100,000 program/erase cycles for SLC, several thousand to 10,000 for MLC, a few thousand for TLC and roughly 1,000 for QLC. SLC also retains data better and copes with higher temperatures.

Where you will meet SLC

Native SLC NAND appears in industrial, automotive and embedded systems that need high endurance and retention more than capacity, and low-latency SLC devices serve some roles between DRAM and conventional NAND. More commonly, consumer TLC and QLC SSDs run part of their free space in SLC mode, often called pseudo-SLC or an SLC cache. Writes land there quickly and are later folded into denser storage during idle time. When a long transfer fills the cache, speed drops to the slower native TLC or QLC rate, which is why large copies can slow midway.

Answer: C. To make writes faster

TLC and QLC take time to program because states must be placed precisely. SSDs therefore run part of their free space in SLC mode, writing there quickly first and migrating data to TLC/QLC areas when idle (an SLC cache). If sustained writes overflow the cache, speed drops sharply to the native rate, which is why large file copies can slow down midway.

Which orders SLC, MLC and TLC from the most rewrite cycles to the fewest?

  1. ATLC > MLC > SLC
  2. BSLC > MLC > TLC
  3. CMLC > SLC > TLC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. SLC > MLC > TLC

Fewer bits per cell mean wider gaps between threshold states, so cells still read correctly as wear broadens the distributions, giving higher endurance. Generally SLC > MLC > TLC > QLC, roughly 100,000 cycles for SLC, several thousand to 10,000 for MLC, a few thousand for TLC and about 1,000 for QLC. SLC mode is used in industrial products needing high endurance and in SSD cache areas.

FAQ

Is SLC better than TLC?

SLC is faster, more durable and more reliable, but it stores one third as much data per cell, so it costs much more per gigabyte. TLC is the better value for typical consumer and data-center use.

How many write cycles does SLC NAND last?

Typical SLC NAND is rated for roughly 50,000 to 100,000 program/erase cycles per block, an order of magnitude or more beyond TLC. Drive-level endurance also depends on wear leveling and spare area.

What is an SLC cache in an SSD?

It is a region of TLC or QLC flash temporarily operated with one bit per cell so incoming writes complete quickly. Data is later rewritten into the denser format, and once the cache is full, writes slow to the native speed.
