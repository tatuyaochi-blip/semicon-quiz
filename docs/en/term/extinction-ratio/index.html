What is Extinction ratio?

In intensity modulation, the ratio of the optical power P1 for a "1" to the power P0 for a "0", expressed in dB as 10 log(P1/P0). The larger it is, the greater the difference between 1 and 0 and the fewer decision errors at the receiver.

Japanese name: 消光比

Which correctly describes the extinction ratio, a measure of optical transmit signal quality?

  1. AThe ratio of the optical power when sending a "1" to the optical power when sending a "0"
  2. BThe ratio of the optical power emitted by the laser to the portion of it coupled into the fiber
  3. CThe ratio of the transmitted optical power to the optical power reaching the receiver through the link
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The ratio of the optical power when sending a "1" to the optical power when sending a "0"

Intensity modulation makes "1" bright and "0" dark, but in a real modulator the light does not go completely off even for "0". The extinction ratio is the ratio of the "1" power P1 to the "0" power P0, expressed in dB as 10 log(P1/P0). For example, with an extinction ratio of 6 dB, P0 is about one-quarter of P1. A small extinction ratio narrows the difference between "1" and "0", making decision errors at the receiver more likely. In a Mach-Zehnder modulator, increasing the drive voltage swing raises the extinction ratio but increases the driver circuit's power consumption. The ratio of transmitted to received power represents the link loss.
