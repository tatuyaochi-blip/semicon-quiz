What is Precursor?

A source compound that reacts to form the desired film in CVD or ALD, such as TEOS or metal-organic compounds.

Japanese name: プリカーサ(前駆体)

Try it: questions about Precursor

In epitaxial growth by CVD, the growth rate depends strongly on temperature in the relatively low-temperature regime. Why?

  1. ABecause the growth rate is determined only by the source gas supply
  2. BBecause the rate of the chemical reaction at the surface determines the growth rate (surface-reaction limited)
  3. CBecause the growth rate does not depend on temperature
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Because the rate of the chemical reaction at the surface determines the growth rate (surface-reaction limited)

At low temperature, surface reactions are slow and limit the growth rate (reaction-limited). Reaction rates follow the Arrhenius law, depending exponentially on temperature, so growth rate does too, and temperature uniformity across the wafer translates directly into thickness uniformity. At high temperature reactions are fast and the arrival rate of precursor gas limits growth (mass-transport-limited), making gas-flow uniformity critical.

In silicon epitaxial growth, what is the main role of the large flow of hydrogen (H2) supplied along with the source gas?

  1. AIt is the silicon source itself
  2. BIt is the n-type dopant
  3. CIt is a carrier gas for the source and provides a reducing atmosphere that keeps the surface clean
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It is a carrier gas for the source and provides a reducing atmosphere that keeps the surface clean

In silicon epitaxy, precursors such as trichlorosilane are diluted in large flows of hydrogen. Hydrogen carries the precursor, participates as a reducing agent, maintains a reducing atmosphere that prevents surface oxidation, and removes chlorine as HCl. It also terminates dangling bonds on surface silicon atoms, keeping the surface clean.
