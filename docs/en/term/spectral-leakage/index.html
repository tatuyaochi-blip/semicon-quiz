What is Spectral leakage?

In a DFT, the phenomenon in which the energy of a component at one frequency appears spread over many surrounding bins rather than only its own. The DFT treats the observation interval as a repeating signal, so when the interval does not hold a whole number of signal periods, the computation behaves as if the two ends of the interval joined discontinuously. Leakage from a strong component can hide nearby weak components or make amplitudes read too small. A window function that brings both ends of the interval smoothly toward 0 greatly reduces leakage into distant bins. Zero padding does not remove leakage.

Japanese name: スペクトル漏れ

Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The observation interval does not contain a whole number of signal periods, so the waveform is treated as if its two ends joined discontinuously

The DFT treats the N observed points as one period of a repeating signal. If the interval holds exactly a whole number of periods of a sine wave, the peak fits in a single bin; otherwise the waveform is discontinuous where the ends of the interval meet, and that energy spreads into many surrounding bins. This is spectral leakage, and it can be reduced with a window function that brings both ends of the interval smoothly toward 0. Aliasing (folding) is a different phenomenon that occurs at sampling.
