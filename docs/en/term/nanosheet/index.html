What is Nanosheet?

A GAA transistor structure that stacks several thin, plate-like silicon channels vertically, each surrounded by the gate. Current can be tuned by the sheet width.

Japanese name: ナノシート

Nanosheet appears in 9 practice questions across 2 topics, including Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA), Epitaxial growth.

Field: Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA)

Structure of a nanosheet transistor

A nanosheet transistor stacks typically about three horizontal silicon channels, each around 5 nm thick and from about 15 to 50 nm wide, spaced about 10 nm apart vertically. The gate dielectric and metal fill every gap, so the gate surrounds each sheet on all four sides, which is why it is called gate-all-around (GAA). It resembles a FinFET fin turned on its side and stacked. The effective channel width is the perimeter of each sheet summed over the stack, so a wider sheet carries proportionally more current.

How nanosheets are built

Fabrication begins with an epitaxial stack of alternating Si and SiGe layers, the SiGe containing about 25 to 30 percent germanium. The stack is patterned like a fin and a dummy gate is formed. The ends of the SiGe layers are recessed and filled with a dielectric such as nitride, forming inner spacers that reduce gate-to-source/drain capacitance and prevent shorts. After source/drain epitaxy and removal of the dummy gate, the SiGe is etched away with high selectivity, leaving the Si sheets suspended, and ALD fills the gaps with high-k dielectric and metal gate.

Advantages over FinFETs

In a FinFET, fin height and width are fixed by the process, so drive strength can change only by the number of fins, a constraint called width quantization. Nanosheet width can be set continuously in layout: wide sheets for high-performance logic, narrow ones for low-power cells and SRAM, which allows finer tuning of drive current and capacitance. Gating the channel from all sides also gives stronger electrostatic control and better resistance to short-channel effects, delivering more current in the same footprint. Samsung's 3 nm generation in 2022 was the first announced volume production.

Limits and challenges

Adding sheets raises current per footprint, but taller stacks make source/drain epitaxy and gate fill harder, and lower sheets see more parasitic resistance and capacitance, so about three sheets is a common compromise. At thicknesses near 5 nm, quantum confinement widens the effective band gap and shifts threshold voltage; because the shift scales inversely with thickness squared, it grows rapidly as the body thins, and at about 2–3 nm one atomic layer of variation can move it by ten-odd to tens of millivolts. The thermal budget is tight too: later steps run at about 600 to 700 °C or below to avoid Si/SiGe intermixing.

Answer: B. Drive current can be adjusted more flexibly by changing the nanosheet width

A FinFET's channel width can change only by fin count, but in nanosheet GAA the stacked sheets' width (e.g., 15–50 nm) can be varied continuously in layout. Wide sheets suit high-performance cells and narrow ones suit low-power logic and SRAM, giving flexible control of drive current and capacitance. The all-around gate also resists short-channel effects, delivering more current than a FinFET in the same footprint.

Nanosheet GAA starts from an epitaxial stack of alternating Si and SiGe (about 25–30% Ge). After the dummy gate is removed, SiGe is etched away with high selectivity, leaving the Si sheets suspended. Filling the gaps with high-k and metal gate by ALD wraps each sheet on all sides. Selective etching that removes only SiGe without damaging the sheets is the manufacturing key.

FAQ

What is the difference between a nanosheet and a nanowire transistor?

Both are gate-all-around devices. A nanowire has a narrow cross-section with width close to its thickness, while a nanosheet is much wider than it is thick, carrying more current per stacked channel and allowing width to be tuned in layout.

What is an inner spacer in a nanosheet transistor?

It is a dielectric plug formed between the gate and the source/drain in the gaps between sheets, made by recessing the SiGe layer ends and refilling with an insulator such as nitride. It cuts parasitic capacitance and prevents gate-to-source/drain shorts.

Why replace FinFETs with nanosheets?

As gate lengths shrink, a gate on three sides of a fin no longer controls the channel well enough. Wrapping the gate around each sheet improves electrostatic control, and adjustable sheet width removes the fin-count quantization of FinFET design.
