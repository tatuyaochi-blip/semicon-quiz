What is Depth of focus (DOF)?

The range of focus over which a pattern prints with acceptable quality. Raising the numerical aperture improves resolution but reduces DOF, which makes wafer planarity important.

Japanese name: 焦点深度(DOF)

Field: Lithography

The resolution–DOF trade-off

Lithography is governed by two Rayleigh relations: resolution R = k1·λ/NA and depth of focus DOF = k2·λ/NA², where λ is the exposure wavelength, NA the numerical aperture of the projection lens, and k1 and k2 process-dependent factors. Raising NA improves resolution in proportion to NA but shrinks DOF with its square. Shortening the wavelength helps resolution while reducing DOF only linearly. Leading-edge processes are left with a DOF of roughly 100 nm, which must cover every source of height and focus error.

What uses up the focus budget

The usable DOF has to absorb wafer non-flatness within each exposure field, topography left by underlying layers, resist thickness, chucking distortion, lens field curvature and the scanner's own focus-control errors. Site flatness over a field such as 26 × 33 mm is therefore held to tens of nanometres or less. Engineers measure the process window with a focus–exposure matrix: linewidth is plotted against focus for several doses (Bossung curves), and the overlapping range that keeps linewidth and profile within specification defines the practical DOF and exposure latitude.

How fabs protect depth of focus

Chemical mechanical polishing (CMP) planarizes each interconnect level globally, removing the millimetre-to-centimetre topography that reflow or etch-back cannot, which made stacks of ten or more wiring levels practical. Scanners measure wafer height and tilt before each shot and level the wafer so the image plane follows the surface. Thinner resist films reduce the vertical range that must stay in focus. Resolution enhancement techniques such as off-axis illumination can also enlarge DOF for dense periodic patterns.

Why High-NA EUV makes DOF harder

Standard EUV scanners use NA 0.33 at a 13.5 nm wavelength, and High-NA EUV raises NA to 0.55 for about 1.7× better resolution. Because DOF scales with 1/NA², the same k2 gives roughly (0.55/0.33)² ≈ 2.8 times less depth of focus. That tightens every item in the focus budget, pushing toward thinner resists, flatter wafers and more precise focus control. High resolution and generous DOF pull in opposite directions, and much of lithography engineering is managing that conflict.

Answer: B. It becomes shallower

Depth of focus is DOF = k2·λ/NA², inversely proportional to NA squared, so raising NA shrinks it rapidly—to around 100 nm in leading-edge lithography. This tightens requirements on wafer flatness, topography and resist-thickness uniformity, which is one reason CMP planarization matters. Scanners measure wafer height and tilt for each shot and adjust focus automatically.

FAQ

What is the formula for depth of focus in lithography?

The usual expression is DOF = k2·λ/NA², where λ is the wavelength, NA the numerical aperture and k2 a process factor. It shows that DOF falls with the square of NA.

Is depth of focus the same as depth of field?

No. Depth of field is the range of object distances that appear sharp, while depth of focus is the tolerance in the position of the image plane. In lithography the wafer sits in the image plane, so depth of focus is the relevant quantity.

Why does a higher NA reduce depth of focus?

A higher NA collects light over a wider cone of angles, so rays converge more steeply onto the image. A small shift away from best focus then spreads those rays more, blurring the image sooner.
