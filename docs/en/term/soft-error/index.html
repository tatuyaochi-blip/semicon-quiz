What is Soft error?

A temporary flip of data stored in memory or flip-flops caused by neutrons from cosmic rays, alpha particles and the like. The device is not damaged, so rewriting restores correct operation.

Japanese name: ソフトエラー

Field: Reliability

How a particle flips a bit

An energetic charged particle passing through silicon leaves a trail of electron-hole pairs, about one pair for every 3.6 eV it deposits. If a sensitive junction, such as the storage node of an SRAM cell or flip-flop, collects more than its critical charge, the stored value flips. Alpha particles come from trace uranium and thorium in package materials and solder. Cosmic-ray neutrons do not ionize directly but undergo nuclear reactions with silicon that produce charged fragments. Thermal neutrons captured by boron-10, once present in borophosphosilicate glass, are another historical source.

Soft errors versus hard errors

A soft error, also called a single-event upset (SEU), corrupts data without damaging the device, so rewriting the location restores correct behavior. A hard error is a permanent failure from a manufacturing defect or wear-out. As cells shrink and supply voltage falls, critical charge drops, and one particle can upset several neighboring cells at once, called a multi-cell upset. Some radiation effects are not soft: single-event latch-up can trigger a parasitic thyristor and destroy a device unless power is removed.

Measuring the soft error rate

Soft error rate is usually expressed in FIT, failures per billion device-hours, often normalized per megabit. Ground-level neutron flux is low, about 13 neutrons per square centimeter per hour above 10 MeV at the New York City sea-level reference used in JEDEC standards, so field measurements take a long time. Instead, devices are irradiated with neutron beams at accelerator facilities, far more intense than the natural flux, and alpha sources are used for package effects. Neutron flux rises steeply with altitude, reaching hundreds of times ground level at airliner cruising altitude.

Countermeasures

Error-correcting codes (ECC) are the main defense for memories: single-error-correct, double-error-detect codes fix any one flipped bit per word, and periodic scrubbing rewrites corrected data before a second upset accumulates. Physically interleaving the bits of a word spreads a multi-cell upset across several words so each remains correctable. Low-alpha materials reduce package emissions. For logic, hardened flip-flop designs, parity on registers and triple modular redundancy in critical paths are used, and silicon-on-insulator (SOI) reduces the volume from which charge is collected.

FAQ

What causes soft errors in memory?

They are caused by alpha particles from trace radioactive impurities in packaging and by secondary particles from cosmic-ray neutrons, which generate enough charge in the silicon to flip a stored bit.

What is the difference between a soft error and a hard error?

A soft error is a temporary data upset that leaves the device undamaged and is cleared by rewriting. A hard error is a permanent fault caused by a defect or wear-out.

Does ECC protect against soft errors?

Yes. ECC corrects single-bit upsets and detects double-bit errors in each word, and combining it with scrubbing and bit interleaving handles most soft errors in memories.
