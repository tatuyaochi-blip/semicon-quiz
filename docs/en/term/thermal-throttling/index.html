What is Thermal throttling?

Control that lowers frequency and voltage when a chip gets too hot, reducing heat to protect it.

Read the beginner explainer: How Are Chips Cooled? Heat Sinks, Heat Pipes and Limits

Japanese name: サーマルスロットリング

Thermal throttling appears in 1 practice question across 1 topic, including Power Management.

Field: Power Management

How thermal throttling works

A chip contains temperature sensors at many locations, based on the temperature characteristics of diodes or transistors. When the hottest spot approaches the limit set for the product, the chip's power-management circuitry, firmware or OS lowers the operating frequency and supply voltage through DVFS, or skips clock cycles to reduce the effective frequency, keeping heat within the cooling capacity. When the temperature falls, the frequency rises again. If the temperature keeps rising anyway and crosses a still higher threshold, the chip is forcibly powered off to protect it (thermal shutdown).

Why lowering frequency and voltage reduces heat

The dynamic power of CMOS circuits is roughly proportional to load capacitance × supply voltage squared × frequency. Lowering the frequency gives the circuit timing slack, allowing it to run at a lower voltage, so lowering the voltage too cuts power far more than lowering frequency alone. Chip temperature can be roughly estimated as the ambient temperature plus the product of power and thermal resistance, so lower power means lower temperature. In addition, leakage current increases with temperature, so lower temperature also reduces leakage power.

Everyday examples and power limits

When a thin laptop or smartphone slows down partway through sustained heavy work or gaming, thermal throttling is at work. The device can run at a high clock for a short time, but as heat builds up in the chassis, performance drops to a sustainable level that matches the cooling capacity. Smartphones may also impose limits to keep the surface temperature of the case held in the hand down, not just the chip temperature. Besides temperature, CPU and GPU frequencies can also be reduced by power or current limits, and many monitoring tools can show which reason is causing the limit.

Countermeasures in design and use

In chip design, sensors are placed at hot spots such as dense arithmetic units, and control is based on the highest reading. Thermal design generally targets the power that is sustained over time rather than the instantaneous peak. For users, increasing cooling capacity makes throttling less likely: keeping heat sinks, fans and thermal interface material in good condition, clearing dust from vents and lowering the ambient temperature. Components other than CPUs and GPUs, such as SSD controllers, may also have functions that limit performance according to temperature.

Answer: C. Lowering frequency and voltage when the chip gets too hot, reducing heat to protect it from damage

When on-chip temperature sensors exceed a set threshold, the power-management circuitry or OS lowers frequency and voltage to bring heat within the cooling capacity. Performance drops, but chip failure and shortened lifetime are prevented. Thin laptops and smartphones slow down partway through sustained heavy work because of this throttling. If temperature keeps rising anyway, the system finally shuts down for protection.

FAQ

What is thermal throttling?

It is control that lowers the operating frequency and voltage when a chip gets too hot, reducing heat to protect the chip. Performance drops temporarily, but failure and shortened lifetime are prevented. Once the temperature falls, performance returns.

Does thermal throttling damage the chip?

Throttling is normal behavior that protects the chip and does not damage it in itself. Frequent throttling, however, is a sign of insufficient cooling, and long periods at high temperature can affect component lifetime, so it is worth reviewing the cooling.

How can I tell whether thermal throttling is happening?

Under load, watch temperature and operating frequency together with a monitoring tool. If the temperature is pinned near its limit and the frequency drops accordingly, throttling is likely occurring. Some tools can show the reason for the limit directly.
