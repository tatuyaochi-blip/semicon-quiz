What is Arsine (AsH3)?

A gas compound of arsenic and hydrogen used to introduce n-type dopant (arsenic) and to grow compound semiconductors such as GaAs. It is extremely toxic.

Japanese name: アルシン(AsH3)

What arsine does in a fab

Arsine (AsH3) is the hydride of arsenic and the standard gas source of that element. In ion implantation, the ion source ionizes arsine to produce arsenic ions for n-type regions; arsenic is heavy and diffuses slowly in silicon, which suits shallow, heavily doped junctions. In chemical vapor deposition and epitaxy it can dope films with arsenic during growth. In compound semiconductor manufacturing it supplies the arsenic for growing GaAs, AlGaAs and InGaAs layers. Its group V partner phosphine (PH3) plays the same roles for phosphorus.

How toxic arsine is

Arsine is one of the most toxic gases used in semiconductor manufacturing. Its occupational exposure limit is set at about 0.005 ppm, roughly ten times lower than phosphine's, meaning that a barely measurable leak can already exceed safe levels. Inhaled arsine attacks red blood cells, and the faint garlic-like odor is not a reliable warning. It is also flammable. Exposure limits of this kind are guideline concentrations for repeated work-day exposure and are used to decide whether local exhaust, detection and protective equipment are needed.

Sub-atmospheric storage vs. high-pressure cylinders

A conventional high-pressure cylinder can release a large amount of gas at once if a valve or line fails. For arsine and phosphine, fabs widely use sub-atmospheric containers in which the gas is adsorbed on a porous sorbent and held below atmospheric pressure. If a leak occurs, air tends to flow in rather than gas flowing out, greatly reducing the risk of a massive release, and the tool's vacuum draws the gas out during use. The gas itself is just as toxic, so detection and exhaust are still required.

Safety systems around arsine

Arsine sources sit in exhausted gas cabinets with continuous leak detectors, emergency shutoff valves and automatic alarms, and tool exhaust passes through abatement systems that destroy or capture the hydride before release. Arsenic also appears in solid and liquid waste: GaAs is about 52% arsenic by weight, so grinding and polishing dust, spent slurries and scrapped wafers are handled as hazardous waste, with local exhaust ventilation and protective equipment preventing worker exposure during processing.

FAQ

What is arsine used for in semiconductor manufacturing?

Arsine supplies arsenic: as an ion-source gas for implanting n-type arsenic, as an in-situ dopant during deposition or epitaxy, and as the arsenic source for growing compound semiconductors such as GaAs and InGaAs.

What is the difference between arsine and phosphine?

Both are highly toxic, flammable group V hydrides used for n-type doping; arsine delivers arsenic and phosphine delivers phosphorus. Arsine's exposure limit, around 0.005 ppm, is about an order of magnitude lower than phosphine's.

Why is arsine stored in sub-atmospheric containers?

Holding the gas adsorbed on a sorbent below atmospheric pressure means a leak draws air in rather than pushing gas out, so a sudden large release is much less likely than with a high-pressure cylinder.
