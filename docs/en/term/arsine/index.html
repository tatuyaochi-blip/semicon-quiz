What is Arsine (AsH3)?

A gas compound of arsenic and hydrogen used to introduce n-type dopant (arsenic) and to grow compound semiconductors such as GaAs. It is extremely toxic.

Japanese name: アルシン(AsH3)

Which correctly describes phosphine (PH3) and arsine (AsH3), used to introduce n-type dopants?

  1. AThey are harmless and need no special control
  2. BThey are extremely toxic and require strict leak control
  3. CThey are nonflammable and odorless and safe to release into air
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. They are extremely toxic and require strict leak control

Phosphine and arsine are so toxic that their exposure limits are 0.05 ppm or lower. They are handled with cylinder cabinets, leak detectors, emergency shutoff valves and abatement systems. Sub-atmospheric containers, in which the gas is adsorbed on a porous material so that it tends not to escape in a leak, are also widely used. They are neither harmless nor nonflammable; phosphine is also flammable.

What is the safety advantage of storing and supplying toxic gases such as arsine and phosphine adsorbed on a sorbent in containers below atmospheric pressure?

  1. AEven if the container leaks, the gas is unlikely to gush out
  2. BThe gas loses its toxicity
  3. CGas detectors become unnecessary
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Even if the container leaks, the gas is unlikely to gush out

A high-pressure cylinder can release a large amount of gas at once if a valve or line fails. Sub-atmospheric containers, which store gas adsorbed on porous material below atmospheric pressure, tend to draw air in rather than leak out, greatly reducing the risk of a massive toxic release. The gas is drawn out by the tool's vacuum. Its toxicity is unchanged, so detection and exhaust measures are still required.
