What is Noise margin?

The difference between the voltage level an output guarantees and the level an input is guaranteed to interpret correctly. The low-level margin is VIL(max) − VOL(max) and the high-level margin is VOH(min) − VIH(min); noise smaller than this causes no misreading.

Japanese name: ノイズマージン

Calculating it from datasheet levels

Noise margin pairs the driver's guaranteed output levels with the receiver's guaranteed input thresholds. The low-level margin is NML = VIL(max) − VOL(max), and the high-level margin is NMH = VOH(min) − VIH(min). For a driver with VOH(min) = 2.9 V and VOL(max) = 0.4 V feeding a receiver with VIH(min) = 2.0 V and VIL(max) = 0.8 V, NML is 0.4 V and NMH is 0.9 V. The smaller of the two is the weak point of that connection.

The undefined region

Between VIL(max) and VIH(min) lies a band where the receiver's interpretation is not guaranteed; a slow or noisy edge crossing it can cause glitches or multiple transitions. Noise margins state how much disturbance, such as ground bounce, crosstalk or supply ripple, can be added to a worst-case output before it reaches that band. Because the datasheet values are worst-case limits, typical parts have more margin, but a robust design relies only on the guaranteed numbers.

Where margins shrink

Lower supply voltages compress every level and therefore the margins, one reason fast low-voltage interfaces move to differential signaling. Multi-level signaling trades margin for bandwidth: PAM4 fits four levels into the swing that NRZ uses for two, leaving about one third of the spacing between adjacent levels, a loss of roughly 9.5 dB. That is why links such as PCI Express 6.0 add forward error correction to repair the resulting bit errors.

Static noise margin in SRAM

Inside a chip the same idea measures storage stability. The static noise margin (SNM) of an SRAM cell is the side of the largest square that fits inside the butterfly curve formed by overlaying the transfer characteristics of its two cross-coupled inverters. Read SNM, with the access transistor on, is smaller than hold SNM and usually limits stability. It shrinks at low supply voltage and with transistor variation, which constrains the minimum operating voltage of SRAM arrays.

The driving IC has VOH(min) = 2.9 V and VOL(max) = 0.4 V, and the receiving IC has VIH(min) = 2.0 V and VIL(max) = 0.8 V. What is the low-level noise margin?

  1. A0.9 V
  2. B0.4 V
  3. C0.8 V

Answer: B. 0.4 V

The low-level noise margin is VIL(max) − VOL(max) = 0.8 − 0.4 = 0.4 V. The output drives an L of 0.4 V or less even in the worst case, and the input recognizes up to 0.8 V as L, so noise up to the 0.4 V difference will not cause a misread. The high-level noise margin is VOH(min) − VIH(min) = 2.9 − 2.0 = 0.9 V. Noise margin is thus found by combining the guaranteed values of the driver and receiver, and the smaller one is the weak point of that connection. 0.8 V is VIL(max) itself.

FAQ

How do you calculate noise margin?

Low-level margin is VIL(max) of the receiver minus VOL(max) of the driver, and high-level margin is VOH(min) of the driver minus VIH(min) of the receiver. Use the worst-case datasheet values and take the smaller result as the limiting margin.

Why does PAM4 have a smaller noise margin than NRZ?

With the same voltage swing, PAM4 must separate four levels instead of two, so the spacing between adjacent levels is about one third as large, roughly 9.5 dB less margin.

What is static noise margin in SRAM?

It is the largest noise voltage a cell can tolerate without flipping its stored bit, found from the butterfly curve of its cross-coupled inverters. Read SNM is usually the limiting case.
