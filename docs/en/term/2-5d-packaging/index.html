What is 2.5D packaging?

Placing several chips side by side on an intermediate substrate such as a silicon interposer and connecting them with dense, fine wiring. It is used, for example, to connect GPUs and HBM.

How a 2.5D package is built

Each die is flipped and attached with microbumps at a pitch of a few tens of micrometres onto an interposer, an intermediate substrate that carries the chip-to-chip wiring. On a silicon interposer that wiring is made with back-end-of-line processes, with line widths around 1 µm or below. Through-silicon vias (TSVs) carry power and external signals down through the interposer to larger bumps on an organic package substrate, which in turn connects to the board through a ball grid array.

Why it is used

An organic substrate with lines tens of micrometres wide cannot route thousands of signals between neighbouring dies, but an interposer can. This enables very wide, short parallel links, which is exactly what high-bandwidth memory (HBM) needs: each stack has an interface 1024 bits wide in common generations, sitting a few millimetres from the processor. Short links also cost far less energy per bit than board-level connections. The same approach lets a large design be split into chiplets, improving yield and allowing each die to use the most suitable process.

Interposer choices

Silicon interposers offer the finest wiring but are costly, and their size is tied to lithography field limits, although stitching allows interposers several times the field size. Organic interposers built from redistribution layers (RDL) of polymer dielectric and copper are cheaper, easier to make large and closer in thermal expansion to the package substrate, but their wiring is coarser. A third approach embeds small silicon bridges only under die edges where dense connections are needed, leaving the rest of the package as ordinary substrate.

2.5D vs. 3D and fan-out

In 2.5D the dies sit side by side, so each can be cooled from the top and tested before assembly. In 3D packaging, dies stack vertically and connect through TSVs or hybrid bonding, giving the shortest connections but making heat removal harder. Fan-out wafer-level packaging forms RDL directly on dies embedded in mold compound, without a separate interposer. Real products combine them: an HBM stack is itself 3D, and several stacks are placed beside a processor in a 2.5D arrangement.

Answer: B. An intermediate substrate on which several chips are placed side by side and connected at high density with fine wiring

A silicon interposer is a silicon intermediary substrate on which wiring of 1 µm or finer is formed using semiconductor back-end processes. Multiple chips, such as a processor and HBM, sit side by side on it and are connected with wiring far denser than ordinary organic package substrates, whose lines are tens of micrometers wide, can provide. The interposer connects to the package substrate through TSVs. It is unrelated to cooling parts or photomasks.

Which TSMC 2.5D packaging technology is widely used to place AI GPUs and HBM side by side on a silicon interposer and connect them?

  1. AInFO
  2. BEMIB
  3. CCoWoS
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. CoWoS

CoWoS (chip on wafer on substrate) is a 2.5D packaging technology in which dies are mounted on a wafer-form interposer (chip on wafer), which is then mounted on a package substrate (on substrate). Interposers can now be several times the lithography field size, and CoWoS is widely used to connect AI GPUs with multiple HBM stacks. Surging AI demand made expanding its capacity a major challenge. InFO is a fan-out technology and EMIB is Intel's bridge approach.

FAQ

What is the difference between 2.5D and 3D packaging?

2.5D places dies side by side on an interposer and connects them through its fine wiring. 3D stacks dies on top of each other and connects them vertically with TSVs or hybrid bonding.

Why does HBM need an interposer?

HBM uses a very wide interface with roughly a thousand data signals per stack, which an ordinary organic package substrate cannot route at that density. An interposer provides the fine, short wiring needed.

What is the difference between a silicon interposer and an organic interposer?

A silicon interposer has the finest wiring and supports TSVs but is expensive and size-limited. An organic RDL interposer is cheaper and easier to make large, at the cost of coarser line pitch.
