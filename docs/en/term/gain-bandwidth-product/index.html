What is Gain-bandwidth product (GBW)?

The product of an op-amp's gain and bandwidth, which stays roughly constant. With a GBW of 1 MHz, for example, a gain-of-10 circuit has a bandwidth of about 100 kHz.

Japanese name: 利得帯域幅積(GBW)

An op-amp with a gain-bandwidth product (GBW) of 1 MHz is used to build a non-inverting amplifier with a gain of 10. What is the approximate bandwidth?

  1. AAbout 10 MHz
  2. BAbout 10 kHz
  3. CAbout 100 kHz
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. About 100 kHz

An internally compensated op-amp's open-loop gain falls at −20 dB/decade, so gain × bandwidth is roughly constant. With a 1 MHz GBW, a gain of 10 gives about 1 MHz ÷ 10 = 100 kHz. Strictly, divide by the non-inverting (noise) gain, so an inverting gain of −10 divides by 11, about 91 kHz. To get both high gain and wide bandwidth, choose a higher-GBW op-amp or split the gain across stages.
