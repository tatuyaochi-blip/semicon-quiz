What is Mini-environment?

Keeping only the small space around the wafers highly clean. Sealed carriers such as FOUPs combined with EFEMs on the tools lower the cleanliness required of the room as a whole, reducing cost.

Japanese name: ミニエンバイロメント

Field: Cleanrooms and equipment

The mini-environment concept

People and moving equipment are major sources of the particles that contaminate wafers. Fabs once protected wafers by keeping entire rooms extremely clean, but that meant pushing huge volumes of clean air through large spaces, which made both construction and air handling expensive. A mini-environment instead encloses only the space where wafers are, keeping that small volume highly clean. Wafers sit inside sealed carriers during transport and storage and inside an EFEM while being handed to a tool, so they never touch the air of the room where people work. With a much smaller volume to keep clean, the room's cleanliness requirement and cost can be lowered, and contamination from people is reduced.

Building blocks

In a 300 mm fab the core elements are the FOUP that carries wafers sealed, the load port at the front of the tool where the FOUP is set, and the EFEM, which houses the mechanism that opens the FOUP door and a robot that takes wafers out. A fan filter unit (FFU) with HEPA or ULPA filters on top of the EFEM blows clean air downward and keeps the inside at slightly higher pressure than the room (positive pressure). On vacuum tools, the EFEM robot hands wafers to a load lock, and from there they travel through a vacuum transfer chamber. In the 200 mm era, SMIF, which sealed a cassette in a pod opened from the bottom on top of the tool, spread the concept.

Contamination beyond particles

A sealed carrier mainly keeps out particles. Moisture and oxygen in the air inside a FOUP can grow native oxide on exposed silicon or metal, and gases released by wafers just out of etch or other processes can contaminate the other wafers and the carrier's inner walls. To suppress such airborne molecular contamination (AMC), FOUPs are purged with nitrogen at the load port, or the EFEM itself is filled with nitrogen. Fabs also manage how long wafers may wait between steps (Q-time) and clean FOUPs periodically.

Benefits and cautions

A mini-environment keeps only the space that touches wafers extremely clean while relaxing the cleanliness of the space where people work, cutting construction cost and air-handling power. It also fits well with an open layout of tools in a large room served by overhead hoist transport (OHT), and makes rearranging tools easier. However, cleanliness then depends on the sealing and airflow of carriers and tools, so worn FOUP door seals, a drop in EFEM pressure and contamination when tools are opened for maintenance need attention. Monitoring pressure differentials and particles so that problems are noticed immediately is important.

Answer: B. SMIF

SMIF (standard mechanical interface) carries a wafer cassette in a sealed pod; on the tool, the pod's bottom opens downward to bring the cassette inside. Devised in the 1980s, it popularized the minienvironment concept. For 300 mm it evolved into the FOUP, which integrates cassette and pod and opens from the front. An EFEM is the tool front-end module that receives FOUPs.

FAQ

How are a mini-environment and an EFEM related?

Mini-environment is the name of the concept or approach of keeping only the small space around wafers clean. An EFEM is one piece of equipment that implements it, keeping wafers in clean air while they pass between the FOUP and the process tool. Combining FOUPs with EFEMs means wafers never touch the room air.

What are the advantages over keeping the whole room clean?

Because the volume that must be kept clean is much smaller, construction and air-handling costs drop. Separating people from wafers also reduces contamination from people. The room's cleanliness can be relaxed, but that does not mean a cleanroom is no longer needed.

Why are FOUPs purged with nitrogen?

Sealing a FOUP keeps out particles, but moisture and oxygen in the air inside, and gases released by processed wafers, remain. They can grow native oxide, corrode metals and contaminate other wafers, so for sensitive steps the air is replaced with nitrogen to suppress them.
