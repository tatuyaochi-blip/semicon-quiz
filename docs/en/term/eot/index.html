What is EOT (equivalent oxide thickness)?

The thickness of SiO2 that would give the same gate capacitance as a gate insulator such as a high-k film. A physically thick film can have a small EOT, giving high capacitance while limiting tunneling current.

Japanese name: EOT(等価酸化膜厚)

How EOT is calculated

Gate capacitance per unit area is ε/t, so any dielectric can be converted to the SiO2 thickness (relative permittivity 3.9) that gives the same capacitance: EOT = physical thickness × 3.9/ε. For a high-k film with ε = 20, about 5 nm of physical film gives 1 nm of EOT. A real gate stack is several layers in series, so their EOTs simply add: a thin SiO2 interfacial layer counts one-for-one, while the high-k layer on top contributes only its thickness scaled by 3.9/ε.

Why gate dielectrics moved to high-k

Shrinking a transistor requires more gate capacitance so the gate keeps control of the channel. With pure SiO2 that means a thinner film, but direct tunneling current through the oxide rises exponentially as the film thins, and below roughly 2 nm the gate leakage becomes a serious power and reliability problem. A high-k material such as hafnium oxide (HfO2, ε about 20–25) delivers the same capacitance from a physically much thicker film, so tunneling is suppressed while the EOT keeps shrinking. Silicon oxynitride (SiON, ε about 5–6) was the intermediate step, also valued for blocking boron penetration.

The interfacial layer limits scaling

High-k films placed directly on silicon hurt channel mobility through interface defects, fixed charge and remote phonon scattering. A thin SiO2 or SiON interfacial layer of about 0.5–1 nm is therefore kept underneath. Because it has low permittivity, this layer contributes a large share of the total EOT, so modern stacks of about 0.8–1 nm EOT depend on making the interfacial layer as thin as possible without degrading the interface. Plasma nitridation of the oxide surface is one way to raise its permittivity without disturbing the Si/SiO2 interface.

EOT vs. CET

EOT is a property of the dielectric stack alone. The capacitance-equivalent thickness (CET) extracted from a measured capacitance in inversion is larger, because the inversion-layer charge peaks about 1 nm below the interface due to quantum confinement, adding roughly 0.3–0.4 nm in SiO2 terms. With polysilicon gates, depletion inside the gate electrode adds more; metal gates remove that term, which is one reason high-k is paired with metal gates. As EOT shrinks, these fixed penalties become a growing fraction of the electrical thickness.

FAQ

What does EOT stand for in semiconductors?

EOT stands for equivalent oxide thickness: the thickness of SiO2 that would give the same gate capacitance as the actual gate dielectric. It lets stacks made of different materials be compared on one scale.

How do you convert a high-k film thickness to EOT?

Multiply the physical thickness by 3.9 divided by the film's relative permittivity. For example, 3 nm of a film with ε = 22 corresponds to about 0.53 nm of EOT, to which the interfacial layer's own EOT must be added.

What is the difference between EOT and CET?

EOT describes only the dielectric stack, while CET is the electrical thickness inferred from measured capacitance in inversion. CET is larger because of the quantum-mechanical setback of inversion charge and, with polysilicon gates, gate depletion.
