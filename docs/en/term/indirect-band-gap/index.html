What is Indirect band gap?

A band structure in which the bottom of the conduction band and the top of the valence band occur at different momenta. Transitions need a phonon, so light emission is inefficient. Silicon and germanium are typical examples.

Japanese name: 間接遷移型

  1. ABecause the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum have different momenta, so the transition requires a phonon
  2. BBecause the hole mobility is lower than the electron mobility
  3. CBecause the band gap is too large
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Because the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum have different momenta, so the transition requires a phonon

In an indirect-gap semiconductor, the conduction-band minimum and valence-band maximum are separated in momentum (wave-vector) space. Photons carry almost no momentum, so a phonon must take part in the transition, making radiative recombination orders of magnitude less likely than in direct-gap materials. Carriers instead recombine non-radiatively, e.g. via SRH, releasing heat, so silicon is a poor light emitter. This is why silicon photonics bonds materials such as InP for the light source.
