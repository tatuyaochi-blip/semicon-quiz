What is Systems audit?

An activity in which specialist auditors independent of the audited area (systems auditors) inspect and evaluate whether controls over the risks associated with information systems are properly designed and operated, and report the results to management and others as assurance or as advice for improvement. It proceeds through audit planning, a preliminary survey, the main audit, evaluation and conclusion, audit reporting and follow-up. In Japan, the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry publishes the System Audit Standards and the System Management Standards.

Japanese name: システム監査

How an audit proceeds

A systems audit begins with an audit plan that sets the objectives, scope and timing. In the preliminary survey, the auditor reviews documents and interviews people to understand the actual state and risks of the audited area, and then defines concretely the audit procedures for the main audit. In the main audit, audit evidence is gathered through procedures such as matching records, observation and testing, and recorded in the audit working papers. A conclusion is reached by evaluating the evidence, findings and improvement recommendations are reported in the audit report, and the status of improvements is checked in the follow-up.

Independence and expertise

Systems auditors are required to be independent of what they audit. If they are involved in developing or operating the audited system, they would be evaluating their own work or their own department's work, so they could not judge objectively and would not appear fair to others. Auditors do not carry out improvements themselves; they recommend them, and the audited department (and the management directing it) is responsible for carrying them out. Specialist knowledge of information systems, risks and controls is also needed.

Audit techniques

In addition to reviewing documents, interviewing, observing operations and matching records, auditors also use computer-based techniques. These include the test data method, in which a program processes data prepared by the auditor and the results are checked; the ITF method, which sets up fictitious audit accounts in the production system; the snapshot method, which writes out data midway through processing; and analysis of all records with generalized audit software. Together they are called CAAT (computer-assisted audit techniques).

A systems audit generally proceeds in the order of audit planning, preliminary survey, main audit, evaluation and conclusion, audit reporting and follow-up. In the main audit, those procedures are carried out to gather audit evidence, which is then evaluated to reach a conclusion.

FAQ

How does a systems audit differ from an information security audit?

A systems audit covers the full range of risks associated with information systems, such as effectiveness, efficiency, reliability and security. An information security audit focuses on information security among these. Because the scopes overlap, some organizations carry them out together.

Can an employee of the internal audit department be a systems auditor?

Yes. An internal audit by employees of the organization's internal audit department can be carried out as a systems audit as long as they are independent of the audited business and systems. There are also external audits commissioned from outside specialists, and the two are used according to the purpose.
