What is Redistribution layer (RDL)?

A wiring layer formed on a chip or wafer surface to route pads out to the positions of the external terminals. It is used in fan-out packages and interposers.

Japanese name: 再配線層(RDL)

How an RDL is built

An RDL is a stack of polymer dielectric and copper wiring built on top of a finished chip, wafer or reconstituted wafer. A typical sequence spins on a dielectric such as polyimide or PBO (polybenzoxazole), opens vias by photolithography and cures it, sputters a thin Ti/Cu seed layer, patterns resist, electroplates copper into the openings, then strips the resist and etches away the exposed seed. Repeating this builds several wiring layers. Finally an under-bump metallization and solder balls or bumps are formed at the new terminal positions.

RDL vs. on-chip BEOL wiring

On-chip back-end-of-line (BEOL) interconnect is made by copper damascene in hard dielectrics with pitches down to tens of nanometers. RDL is much coarser, with line widths and spaces of several micrometers moving toward about 1 µm, and thicker copper, so resistance per length is low. It sits between the chip and the package substrate, whose build-up wiring is coarser still, roughly ten micrometers or more. This middle ground is what lets RDL bridge fine chip pads to board-scale solder ball pitches.

Fan-in, fan-out and interposers

In fan-in wafer-level packaging, RDL reroutes pads to a ball array inside the die outline. In fan-out packaging, dies are embedded in mold compound to form a reconstituted wafer and RDL extends wiring beyond the die edge, allowing more I/O than the die area alone could hold and enabling multi-die connections. Organic RDL interposers for 2.5D packaging are cheaper than silicon interposers, scale more easily to large areas and better match the substrate's thermal expansion, but their wiring density is lower, so silicon bridges are sometimes embedded where density is needed.

Process challenges

Polymer layers shrink during cure and differ in thermal expansion from silicon and mold compound, so warpage and stress grow with layer count and package size. Fine lines require good lithography on surfaces that may not be perfectly flat, and stacked vias need reliable adhesion between copper and polymer. Moisture uptake and adhesion of the dielectric also affect long-term reliability under temperature cycling. These limits are why RDL pitches shrink more slowly than on-chip wiring.

Answer: C. It keeps cost down more easily and makes large-area interposers easier to build

Organic RDL interposers avoid silicon wafer processing, so they are cheaper, more compliant and easier to scale up in size. Their thermal expansion is closer to the package substrate's, which also reduces joint stress. Their wiring pitch and density usually fall short of silicon interposers, however, so they are chosen per application or combined with silicon bridges only where high density is needed.

FAQ

What is a redistribution layer in packaging?

It is copper wiring in polymer dielectric layers built on a chip or wafer surface that reroutes the chip's pads to new positions where solder balls or bumps are placed.

What line width do RDLs use?

Typically several micrometers, with advanced fan-out and interposer processes pushing toward about 1 µm, far coarser than on-chip interconnect.

What is the difference between an RDL interposer and a silicon interposer?

An RDL interposer uses organic dielectric and plated copper, so it is cheaper and easier to make large, while a silicon interposer uses chip-style wiring that achieves finer pitch and higher wiring density.
