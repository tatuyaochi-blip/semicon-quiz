What is Positive photoresist?

A photoresist whose exposed areas dissolve and are removed during development. One whose exposed areas remain is a negative resist.

Japanese name: ポジ型フォトレジスト

Positive photoresist appears in 1 practice question across 1 topic, including Lithography.

Field: Lithography

Which correctly describes positive photoresist?

  1. AExposed areas remain after development
  2. BExposed areas dissolve and are removed during development
  3. CEverything dissolves during development regardless of exposure
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Exposed areas dissolve and are removed during development

In a positive resist, exposed areas become soluble in developer and are removed, leaving the pattern where the mask blocked light. Representative types are novolak resins for i-line and chemically amplified resists for KrF and ArF, developed in alkaline TMAH solution. Their superior resolution makes them the mainstream. Negative resists keep the exposed areas, analogous to photographic negatives and positives.
