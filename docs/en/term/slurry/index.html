What is Slurry?

The liquid used in CMP, with fine abrasive particles dispersed in chemicals. It flattens surfaces by combining chemical action with mechanical polishing.

Japanese name: スラリー

Slurry appears in 17 practice questions across 3 topics, including CMP and cleaning, Silicon wafers, Process materials (gases and chemicals).

Field: CMP and cleaning

What is in a CMP slurry

A CMP (chemical mechanical polishing) slurry is a suspension of abrasive particles, typically tens to over a hundred nanometers across, in a chemical solution. Common abrasives are silica (SiO2), ceria (CeO2) and alumina. The liquid contains pH adjusters, oxidizers for metals, corrosion inhibitors such as benzotriazole for copper, and dispersants that keep particles from clumping. Particle size, hardness, concentration and chemistry are tuned for each film, and slurry is one of the largest consumable costs in CMP.

How chemistry and abrasion work together

The chemistry converts the top of the film into a softer or more soluble layer, and the abrasive particles, pressed by the pad, wipe that layer away, exposing fresh material to react again. Tungsten CMP shows this clearly: tungsten is too hard to polish efficiently by abrasion alone, so oxidizers such as hydrogen peroxide or iron salts form a softer oxide that the particles remove. Removal rate roughly follows Preston's equation, rate = k × pressure × relative velocity, where k depends on slurry, pad and film; raised areas see higher local pressure and polish faster, which planarizes.

Matching slurry to the film

Oxide and interlayer dielectric polishing commonly use silica or ceria slurries. Ceria slurries can polish oxide much faster than silicon nitride, which makes nitride a natural stop layer in shallow trench isolation (STI). Copper CMP is done in stages with different slurries: a fast bulk copper step, a gentler clearing step and a barrier step that removes Ta/TaN and a little dielectric. Final polishing of bare silicon wafers uses alkaline colloidal silica, whose soft, fine particles give damage-free mirror surfaces with roughness around 0.1 nm.

Defects, filtration and cleaning

Slurry particles can agglomerate into micron-sized clusters through drying, temperature swings, pH changes or shear in pumps, and these clusters scratch wafers, which can short or open copper lines and is especially harmful to fragile low-k films. Slurry is therefore circulated, temperature-controlled and passed through filters of a few micrometers just before the tool. After polishing, residual particles, metal ions and inhibitors must be removed by brush scrubbing and chemical cleans while the wafer is still wet, because dried slurry bonds firmly to the surface.

Answer: B. Colloidal silica (fine silica particles)

Final wafer polishing uses colloidal silica—silica particles tens of nm across dispersed in an alkaline solution. The alkali chemically converts the silicon surface into a softer layer that the particles mechanically remove, and this combined chemical-mechanical action yields a damage-free mirror finish with roughness around 0.1 nm. The soft particles avoid deep scratches and metal contamination.

Particles and chemistry are chosen per film: silica or ceria for oxide, oxidizer-containing silica slurries for tungsten and copper. Slurry is a major CMP consumable cost, and Japanese companies are strong suppliers.

FAQ

What abrasive is used in CMP slurry?

The most common abrasives are silica (fumed or colloidal) and ceria, with alumina used in some metal slurries; the choice depends on the film being polished and the selectivity required.

Why is ceria slurry used for STI CMP?

Ceria slurries can remove silicon oxide much faster than silicon nitride, so polishing effectively stops on the nitride layer that protects the active areas.

What causes scratches in CMP?

Most scratches come from large agglomerated slurry particles or hard debris trapped in the pad, which is why slurry is filtered and circulated and the pad is conditioned regularly.
