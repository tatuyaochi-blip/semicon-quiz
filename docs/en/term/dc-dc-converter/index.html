What is DC-DC converter?

A circuit that converts one DC voltage into another. Using switches, inductors and capacitors, buck types lower the voltage and boost types raise it.

Japanese name: DC-DCコンバータ

Try it: questions about DC-DC converter

The output-voltage feedback loop of a buck DC-DC converter with a switching frequency of 500 kHz is being designed. Which of the following is the usual guideline for choosing the gain crossover frequency?

  1. AAt least ten times the switching frequency (5 MHz), so that it responds quickly to sudden load changes
  2. BEqual to the switching frequency, 500 kHz, so that the loop is synchronized with the switching and suppresses the output-voltage ripple best
  3. CWell below the switching frequency, for example at or below about 1/10 to 1/5 of it (50 to 100 kHz)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Well below the switching frequency, for example at or below about 1/10 to 1/5 of it (50 to 100 kHz)

A switching converter can change its duty cycle only once per period, so it is a system that contains a kind of sampling. Bringing the gain crossover frequency close to the switching frequency loses phase margin through this delay and the ripple, so the usual guideline is about 1/10 to 1/5 of the switching frequency or lower, 50 to 100 kHz or lower in this example. Within that range, the higher the crossover frequency, the smaller the output-voltage excursion on a sudden load change. The output LC filter has a second-order resonance with a phase lag of up to 180°, so phase compensation is used to secure the phase margin.

Which DC-DC converter outputs a DC voltage higher than its input?

  1. AStep-up chopper (boost converter)
  2. BStep-down chopper (buck converter)
  3. CBridge rectifier
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Step-up chopper (boost converter)

A boost converter stores energy in the inductor while the switch is on, then adds the inductor's voltage to the input when it turns off. Ideally the output is input ÷ (1 − duty cycle)—twice the input at a duty of 0.5. Applications include driving LEDs from a single battery cell, boosting solar-panel voltage, raising motor-drive voltage in hybrid cars, and PFC circuits.
