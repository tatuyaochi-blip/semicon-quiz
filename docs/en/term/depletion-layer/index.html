What is Depletion layer?

A region near a PN junction boundary that contains almost no carriers. It widens under reverse bias.

Japanese name: 空乏層

Depletion layer appears in 4 practice questions across 2 topics, including PN junctions and diodes, MOSFET.

Field: PN junctions and diodes

How the depletion layer forms

When p-type and n-type regions meet, electrons diffuse from the n side and holes from the p side, recombining near the junction. They leave behind fixed charges: positive ionized donors on the n side and negative ionized acceptors on the p side. These charges set up a built-in electric field pointing from n to p that pushes carriers back and stops further net diffusion. The resulting built-in potential is typically about 0.6 to 0.9 V for silicon junctions, depending on doping. The region is also called the space-charge region, because its defining feature is uncompensated fixed charge.

Width and how doping divides it

Charge neutrality requires NA·xp = ND·xn, so the depletion layer extends further into the more lightly doped side. For an abrupt junction the total width is W = √(2ε(Vbi + V)/q × (1/NA + 1/ND)), where V is the reverse bias. In a p+n junction this reduces to W ≈ √(2ε(Vbi + V)/(qND)), and almost the whole layer lies on the n side. Typical widths are about 0.1 to 1 µm; for example, ND = 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ at zero bias gives roughly 0.3 µm. Quadrupling Vbi + V doubles the width.

Depletion capacitance

Because the region contains almost no mobile carriers, it behaves like the insulator of a parallel-plate capacitor with C = εA/W. As reverse bias widens W, the capacitance falls, roughly as (Vbi + V)^-1/2 for an abrupt junction. Varactor diodes exploit this voltage-controlled capacitance for tuning. The same relation is used in capacitance-voltage profiling: plotting 1/C² against reverse bias gives a slope from which the doping on the lightly doped side can be extracted. Junction capacitance also loads every transistor's drain and source, adding to circuit delay.

Depletion beyond the PN diode

The concept appears throughout device physics. A gate voltage depletes the silicon under a MOS gate before inversion occurs. Polysilicon gate electrodes deplete too, adding roughly 0.3 to 0.5 nm of equivalent oxide thickness, which is why high-k/metal gate technology replaced them. Photodiodes collect light-generated carriers efficiently within the depletion region, so PIN diodes insert a wide intrinsic layer. Power devices use lightly doped drift layers so a wide depletion region can hold high voltage. Note that the sharp-edged picture is the depletion approximation; real edges taper over a few Debye lengths.

Try it: questions about Depletion layer

What happens to the width of the depletion layer when the reverse bias applied to a PN junction is increased?

  1. AIt narrows
  2. BIt does not change
  3. CIt widens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It widens

Under reverse bias the applied voltage adds to the built-in potential, pulling carriers further from the junction, so the depletion region widens. Its width scales roughly as √(Vbi + V), so when V is much larger than Vbi, quadrupling the voltage about doubles it. A wider depletion region lowers the junction capacitance (C = εA/W), the property used in varactor diodes, and spreading the field over a wider region also helps withstand voltage.

In a PN junction where the p side is far more heavily doped than the n side (a p+n junction), how does the depletion layer spread?

  1. AIt spreads mostly into the lightly doped n side
  2. BIt spreads mostly into the heavily doped p side
  3. CIt always spreads equally into both sides
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It spreads mostly into the lightly doped n side

On both sides of the depletion region the ionized-impurity charge must balance (NA·xp = ND·xn). The lightly doped side must deplete over a wider distance to supply the same charge, so in a p+n junction the depletion region extends almost entirely into the n side. Breakdown voltage and capacitance are then set mostly by the lightly doped side, allowing a one-sided abrupt-junction approximation. Power devices use lightly doped drift layers in this way to hold high voltages.

FAQ

Why does the depletion region widen under reverse bias?

Reverse bias adds to the built-in potential, so more fixed charge must be uncovered to support the larger voltage. Since the dopant density is fixed, the only way to expose more charge is to deplete a wider region, with width scaling roughly as the square root of the total voltage.

Is the depletion region the same as the space-charge region?

Yes. Both names describe the same zone around the junction where mobile carriers are nearly absent and the ionized dopants leave a net fixed charge.

Does a PN junction have a depletion layer with no voltage applied?

Yes. It forms in thermal equilibrium through diffusion and recombination of carriers, supported by the built-in potential. Forward bias narrows it and reverse bias widens it.
