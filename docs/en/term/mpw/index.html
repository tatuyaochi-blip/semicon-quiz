What is MPW (multi-project wafer)?

Prototyping several customers' designs together on one wafer (one mask set). Sharing the expensive mask cost makes it popular with universities and startups.

Japanese name: MPW(マルチプロジェクトウエハ)

How an MPW shuttle works

A foundry or aggregator announces runs on a given process at scheduled dates. Each participant submits a finished layout for a die area of agreed size by the deadline. The designs are tiled together on a shared reticle, one mask set is made, and the wafers are processed as a single lot. Afterwards the wafers are diced and each participant receives its own dies, often a few dozen, sometimes packaged. Mask and wafer costs are split among the participants, usually in proportion to silicon area, which is why the scheme is also called a shuttle.

Why MPWs matter

A full mask set at an advanced node can cost tens of millions of dollars, and even mature nodes cost far more than a small team can spend on a first prototype. Sharing that cost lets universities, research groups and startups fabricate real chips for a small fraction of the price. Established companies use MPWs too, for test chips that characterize new circuits, validate IP blocks or check a new process before committing to a dedicated tape-out.

Limitations

An MPW serves prototyping and evaluation, not production. Each participant gets only a limited number of dies, because neighboring designs share the wafer and dicing one design out can sacrifice its neighbors. Runs follow the foundry's calendar, so a missed deadline means waiting for the next shuttle, and die size, process options and turnaround are fixed by the organizer. Wafer-level testing and yield data are also limited compared with a dedicated lot.

From MPW to production

Once a prototype works, the design moves to its own mask set for engineering lots and then volume production. For low-volume products, a multi-layer reticle, which places several mask layers on one reticle, can cut mask cost further at the expense of scanner throughput. For digital logic alone, an FPGA can validate function without any fabrication, but analog, RF and process-specific behavior can only be confirmed on real silicon, which is what an MPW run provides.

An MPW (multi-project wafer) combines several designs on one mask set and wafer run, sharing mask and fabrication costs among participants; it is also called a shuttle, like a shared bus. Answer: C. Last-time buy

When a chipmaker discontinues an old product, it accepts final orders for a set period. Makers of equipment needing ten or more years of supply and maintenance, such as automotive, industrial and medical equipment, use this chance to buy all the quantity they will need (a last-time buy) and store it long-term. Afterward they redesign around substitutes. An MPW is a prototyping service and just-in-time is a low-inventory philosophy.

FAQ

What does MPW stand for?

MPW stands for multi-project wafer: a fabrication run in which designs from several customers share one mask set and one set of wafers.

Why do startups and universities use MPW runs?

Because mask and wafer costs are divided among all participants, an MPW lets a small team obtain real prototype chips at a fraction of the cost of a dedicated mask set.

Can an MPW be used for volume production?

No. Each participant receives only a limited number of dies on a fixed schedule, so MPWs are used for prototypes and test chips; production requires a dedicated mask set.
