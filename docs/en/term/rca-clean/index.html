What is RCA clean?

A cleaning method that removes particles and organics with SC-1 (ammonia and hydrogen peroxide) and metal contamination with SC-2 (hydrochloric acid and hydrogen peroxide).

Japanese name: RCA洗浄

Field: CMP and cleaning

Origin and basic sequence

The RCA clean is a silicon wafer cleaning method developed by Werner Kern and colleagues at RCA in the US and published in 1970. It basically uses two hydrogen peroxide-based solutions in turn: alkaline SC-1 (Standard Clean 1) removes particles and organics, and acidic SC-2 (Standard Clean 2) removes metal contamination. Where needed, a sulfuric acid-hydrogen peroxide mixture (SPM) first strips heavy organics, and dilute hydrofluoric acid (DHF) removes oxide between SC-1 and SC-2. Each step is followed by an ultrapure water rinse, and the wafers are dried at the end. More than 50 years on, it remains the foundation of wafer cleaning, with ongoing refinements.

What SC-1 (APM) does

SC-1 is a mixture of ammonium hydroxide, hydrogen peroxide and water, also called APM (ammonia-peroxide mixture). A typical ratio is 1:1:5, used warm at around 70 °C. Hydrogen peroxide oxidizes the silicon surface to form a thin oxide about 1 nm thick, and ammonia slowly dissolves that oxide, so the surface is etched away very slightly and attached particles are undercut and lifted off. In the alkaline solution both the wafer surface and the particles carry negative charge and repel each other, which also discourages redeposition. On the other hand, metals in the solution such as iron and aluminum tend to deposit on the surface, which is why SC-2 follows.

What SC-2 (HPM) does

SC-2 is a mixture of hydrochloric acid, hydrogen peroxide and water, also called HPM (hydrochloric-peroxide mixture). A typical ratio is 1:1:6, used heated. In the acidic solution, metals such as iron, nickel and sodium and metal hydroxides on the surface are dissolved and removed in soluble forms such as chlorides. Because SC-2 barely etches silicon or oxide, it does little to remove particles, and the standard order is to use it after SC-1. Afterward the surface is covered with a thin chemical oxide that makes it hydrophilic and protects it from subsequent contamination.

Modern refinements

The original RCA clean was a batch process, immersing many wafers in tanks of concentrated chemicals at high temperature. Today, heavily diluted chemicals at lower temperatures combined with megasonic agitation are widely used to remove particles. Dilution reduces surface roughening of silicon and chemical consumption. Single-wafer tools that spin each wafer while dispensing chemicals have also become common, and the chemicals are optimized step by step, for example using ozonated ultrapure water to remove organics. After metal wiring is exposed, other cleaning solutions are used because SC-1 and SC-2 can dissolve metals.

The RCA clean, published by Kern and colleagues at RCA in 1970, first removes particles and organics with alkaline SC-1, then removes metals—which SC-1 tends to leave behind—as chlorides with acidic SC-2 (HCl:H2O2:H2O). A dilute-HF step may be inserted to strip oxide, with ultrapure-water rinses between steps. More than 50 years on, refined versions remain the foundation of wafer cleaning.

FAQ

What is the difference between SC-1 and SC-2?

SC-1 is a mixture of ammonium hydroxide and hydrogen peroxide (APM) that removes particles and organics in an alkaline solution. SC-2 is a mixture of hydrochloric acid and hydrogen peroxide (HPM) that dissolves and removes metal contamination in an acidic solution. The standard order is SC-1 followed by SC-2.

What is an APM clean?

APM stands for ammonia-peroxide mixture; an APM clean uses the same mixture of ammonium hydroxide, hydrogen peroxide and water as SC-1 in the RCA clean. It is often used on its own, diluted and combined with megasonics, to remove particles. By the same naming, SC-2 is called HPM and the sulfuric acid-hydrogen peroxide mixture is called SPM.

Why is SC-2 used after SC-1?

SC-1 is excellent at removing particles, but in an alkaline solution metals such as iron and aluminum tend to deposit on the surface. Following it with acidic SC-2 dissolves and removes metals left or deposited during SC-1. In the reverse order, metals deposited by SC-1 would remain.
