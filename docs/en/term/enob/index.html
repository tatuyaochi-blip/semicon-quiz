What is ENOB (effective number of bits)?

The real performance of an ADC, including noise and distortion (SINAD), expressed as the bit count of an ideal ADC. A 12-bit ADC with an ENOB of around 10 bits is not unusual.

Japanese name: ENOB(有効ビット数)

Which is the approximate formula for the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of an ideal N-bit ADC, set by quantization noise? (For a full-scale sine-wave input)

  1. A6.02N + 1.76 dB
  2. B3N dB
  3. C20N dB
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 6.02N + 1.76 dB

An ideal ADC's SNR is 6.02N + 1.76 dB, improving about 6 dB per bit: roughly 74 dB at 12 bits and 98 dB at 16 bits. It follows from the ratio of a full-scale sine's rms value to the quantization noise rms, LSB/√12. Real ADCs fall short due to thermal noise, distortion and clock jitter, so actual performance is compared by the effective number of bits (ENOB) calculated from measurements.

Which metric converts the performance of a real ADC, including noise and distortion, into the bit count of an ideal ADC?

  1. AENOB (effective number of bits)
  2. BLSB
  3. CDNL
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ENOB (effective number of bits)

ENOB is calculated from measured SINAD (signal-to-noise-and-distortion ratio) as ENOB = (SINAD − 1.76)/6.02, expressing how many bits of an ideal ADC the real one equals. A 12-bit ADC with 62 dB SINAD has an ENOB of about 10 bits. ENOB falls at higher input frequencies due to jitter and bandwidth limits, so check the frequency at which it is specified. This is why nominal resolution alone does not reveal real performance.
