What is Risk assessment?

Identifying the hazards of tasks or chemicals, estimating the size of the risks and deciding countermeasures in order of priority.

Japanese name: リスクアセスメント

Risk assessment appears in 3 practice questions across 3 topics, including Automotive Semiconductors and Functional Safety, Yield and quality control, Environment, health and safety.

Field: Environment, health and safety

Hazard vs. risk

A hazard is anything that can cause harm, such as a corrosive acid, a toxic gas or a moving robot arm. Risk combines how severe the harm could be with how likely it is to happen under the actual working conditions. A highly toxic chemical used in a closed, interlocked system can carry lower risk than a milder chemical poured by hand. Risk assessment is the structured process of identifying hazards, estimating these risks and deciding which countermeasures to apply first, before an accident rather than after it.

How an assessment is carried out

The team first gathers information: safety data sheets (SDSs), quantities handled, task steps and who is exposed. Each hazard is then rated, often with a matrix scoring severity and likelihood, and the result is compared with what the organization accepts. Countermeasures are chosen, the residual risk is re-evaluated and everything is documented. The assessment is repeated whenever a new chemical, tool or procedure is introduced, after incidents and near misses, and periodically so that changes in practice are caught.

The hierarchy of controls

Countermeasures are prioritized from most to least reliable. First comes elimination or substitution with a less hazardous material. Next are engineering controls such as enclosures, local exhaust ventilation, gas detectors and interlocks, which work regardless of human behavior. Then come administrative measures: procedures, training, permits and limited exposure time. Personal protective equipment (PPE) such as gloves, face shields and respirators is the last line of defense because it protects only when chosen correctly, worn properly and maintained.

Related methods: FMEA, HARA and TARA

The same idea appears under other names. Failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA) rates each possible failure of a process or design by severity, occurrence and detectability. In automotive functional safety under ISO 26262, hazard analysis and risk assessment (HARA) assigns safety integrity levels, while ISO/SAE 21434 uses threat analysis and risk assessment (TARA) for cyberattacks. All are proactive, unlike statistical process control (SPC), which monitors a running process and reacts when it drifts.

  2. BISO 26262 addresses protection against threats from cyberattacks, while ISO/SAE 21434 addresses hazards caused by component failures and design errors
  3. CThe two have the same content; ISO/SAE 21434 is the new number assigned when ISO 26262 was revised
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ISO 26262 addresses hazards caused by component failures and design errors, while ISO/SAE 21434 addresses protection against threats from malicious attacks (cyberattacks)

ISO 26262 is a functional safety standard for preventing hazards caused by random hardware failures and systematic failures, evaluating risk through HARA. ISO/SAE 21434 is a cybersecurity standard for protecting vehicles from intentional attacks such as hijacking communications or tampering with software, evaluated through threat analysis and risk assessment (TARA). Their scopes differ, but if an attack disables safety mechanisms, safety is also compromised, so the two activities are carried out in coordination.

Answer: C. APC (run-to-run control, etc.)

APC (advanced process control) uses metrology data to adjust tool settings automatically and reduce process variation. In run-to-run control, for example, if the incoming film measured thick, the next CMP time is extended. Whereas SPC monitors and stops on abnormalities, APC actively corrects toward target. FMEA is proactive risk assessment and Pareto analysis finds priorities.

FAQ

What are the steps of a chemical risk assessment?

Identify the hazards using SDSs and task information, estimate the risk from severity and likelihood of exposure, decide and prioritize controls, then record the results and review them when conditions change.

Why is PPE the last option in the hierarchy of controls?

PPE does not remove the hazard; it only works if the right equipment is selected, worn correctly every time and kept in good condition. Substitution and engineering controls reduce risk without relying on individual behavior.

Is FMEA a kind of risk assessment?

Yes. FMEA is a risk assessment focused on failure modes of a product or process, scoring severity, occurrence and detection to decide which failures need design or process changes first.
