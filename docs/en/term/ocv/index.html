What is OCV (on-chip variation)?

Variation in transistor and wire delay from place to place within a single chip. STA applies separate factors to launch and capture paths (for example 1.1× and 0.9×) to analyze the most pessimistic combination.

Japanese name: OCV(オンチップばらつき)

Sources of on-chip variation

Even within one die, transistors and wires do not match exactly. Random dopant fluctuation, line-edge roughness and local lithography effects shift threshold voltage and channel length; wire width and thickness vary with local pattern density; and IR drop and hot spots make local supply voltage and temperature differ. Global PVT corners (process, voltage, temperature) cover chip-to-chip shifts, but two paths on the same chip can still differ, and setup and hold checks compare exactly such pairs of paths.

Derating launch and capture paths

With flat OCV, STA multiplies delays by derating factors chosen pessimistically for each check. For setup, the launch clock and data paths are made slow (for example ×1.1) and the capture clock path fast (×0.9). With a 1.0 ns launch clock, 1.5 ns data path, 1.0 ns capture clock, 3.0 ns period and 0.1 ns setup time, arrival is 2.75 ns and required time is 3.8 ns, giving 1.05 ns slack instead of 1.40 ns without derating. For hold the factors are swapped.

CRPR: removing impossible pessimism

When launch and capture clocks share a common path from the clock root to a branch point, flat derating treats that same buffer chain as both slow and fast in one check, which cannot happen for a single clock edge. Common path pessimism removal (CRPR, also called CPPR) adds the difference back to the slack. For a 1.0 ns common segment with 1.1 and 0.9 factors, it recovers 0.2 ns. It does not set the common delay to zero; it only removes the double counting.

AOCV and POCV

Flat derating is crude. Random variation partly averages out along deep paths, while cells placed far apart see more systematic difference. Advanced OCV (AOCV) uses tables indexed by logic depth and distance, so long paths receive smaller derates. Parametric or statistical OCV (POCV, SOCV) gives each cell a delay mean and standard deviation and combines them statistically along the path. Both recover margin that flat OCV wastes while still covering variation to a chosen confidence level.

Try it: questions about OCV (on-chip variation)

For OCV derating in setup analysis, the launch clock path and data path delays are multiplied by 1.1 and the capture clock path delay by 0.9. With a launch clock path of 1.0 ns, a data path (including clock-to-output) of 1.5 ns, a capture clock path of 1.0 ns (no common portion), a period of 3.0 ns and a setup time of 0.1 ns, what is the setup slack?

  1. A1.05 ns
  2. B1.40 ns
  3. C1.25 ns
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1.05 ns

OCV derating accounts for delay varying with location even within the same chip, and for setup assumes the most pessimistic combination (slow launch side, early capture clock). The arrival time is 1.1 × (1.0 + 1.5) = 2.75 ns and the required time is 3.0 + 0.9 × 1.0 − 0.1 = 3.8 ns, so the slack is 1.05 ns. 1.40 ns is the value without derating, and 1.25 ns applies the factor to the data path only. In hold analysis the factors are applied the other way around.

In STA with OCV derating, when the launch and capture clock paths share a common portion, which correctly describes CRPR (common path pessimism removal)?

  1. AIt treats the delay of the common portion as zero and recalculates the clock arrival times
  2. BIt removes the impossible extra pessimism caused by applying separate slow and fast values to the common portion
  3. CIt applies an even larger derating factor to the common portion to stay on the safe side
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It removes the impossible extra pessimism caused by applying separate slow and fast values to the common portion

OCV estimates the launch clock path as slow and the capture clock path as fast, but the common portion from the root to the branch point is physically the same buffers and wires, so it cannot be both slow and fast within a single edge. If the common portion has a delay of 1.0 ns and the factors are 1.1 and 0.9, the 0.2 ns difference between 1.1 ns and 0.9 ns is extra pessimism. CRPR adds this difference back to the slack, bringing the result closer to reality. It does not set the common delay to zero, and enlarging the factor is the opposite operation.

FAQ

What is the difference between OCV and PVT corners?

PVT corners model the whole chip shifting together to a slow or fast condition. OCV models differences between paths on the same chip at a given corner, so it is applied on top of the corner analysis.

What is CRPR in static timing analysis?

Common path pessimism removal credits back the slack lost when OCV derating treats the shared part of the launch and capture clock paths as both slow and fast at the same time.

Why are OCV derates applied differently for setup and hold?

Each check assumes its own worst case: setup is hurt by a late launch and an early capture, while hold is hurt by an early launch and a late capture, so the slow and fast factors are swapped.
