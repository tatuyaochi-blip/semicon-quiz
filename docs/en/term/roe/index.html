What is ROE (return on equity)?

Net income divided by equity: how much profit a company earned relative to the funds provided by shareholders. It equals ROA (return on assets) multiplied by financial leverage (total assets ÷ equity).

Japanese name: ROE(自己資本利益率)

Try it: questions about ROE (return on equity)

A company had net income of ¥12 billion, equity of ¥80 billion and total assets of ¥200 billion. Which is its ROE (return on equity)?

  1. A6%
  2. B10%
  3. C15%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 15%

ROE is an indicator of how much net income a company earned relative to its equity, the funds provided by shareholders: 12 ÷ 80 = 15%. The ratio to total assets, 12 ÷ 200 = 6%, is ROA (return on assets), which shows how efficiently all assets, including those funded by liabilities, are used. ROE equals ROA multiplied by financial leverage (total assets ÷ equity); here the financial leverage is 200 ÷ 80 = 2.5 times, so ROE is 2.5 times ROA.
