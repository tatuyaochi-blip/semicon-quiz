What is Self-information?

The amount of information obtained on being told that an event of probability p has occurred. It is I = −log₂ p, measured in bits. It is larger for less likely events and 0 for an event that is certain (p = 1). An event of probability 1/2 carries 1 bit and one of probability 1/1024 carries 10 bits. The information from two independent events both occurring is the sum of their individual amounts. Averaging the information per symbol of a source, weighted by probability, gives its entropy. Also called information content.

Japanese name: 情報量

Try it: questions about Self-information

Let the amount of information obtained on being told that an event of probability p has occurred be −log₂ p bits. Which of the following is the amount of information obtained on being told that an event of probability 1/8 has occurred?

  1. A0.125 bits
  2. B0.375 bits
  3. C3 bits
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 3 bits

The amount of information is −log₂(1/8) = log₂ 8 = 3 bits. Since 1/8 = 1/2³, it is the same amount of information as being told the answer to a "one of two" question of probability 1/2 three times over. The less likely an event, the more information we receive on being told that it has occurred.

Which of the following correctly describes a property of the amount of information (−log₂ p on being told that an event of probability p has occurred)?

  1. AThe amount of information on being told that an event of probability 1, which always occurs, has occurred is 1 bit
  2. BThe amount of information on being told that two independent events have both occurred is the sum of their individual amounts of information
  3. CThe more probable an event, the more information we receive on being told that it has occurred
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The amount of information on being told that two independent events have both occurred is the sum of their individual amounts of information

Two independent events both occur with probability p₁ × p₂, so the amount of information is −log₂(p₁ × p₂) = (−log₂ p₁) + (−log₂ p₂), the sum of the individual amounts. Information is defined with a logarithm precisely to give it this additive property. Being told that a certain event has occurred tells us nothing new, so its amount of information is −log₂ 1 = 0.
