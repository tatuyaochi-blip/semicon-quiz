What is Back EMF?

An electromotive force produced by electromagnetic induction that acts against the change of current or the rotation that caused it. Abruptly interrupting the current in a coil produces a high voltage V = L di/dt (about 1000 V when 1 A is cut in 1 µs in a 1 mH coil), which can destroy the switch, so a diode or snubber circuit diverts it. A motor generates a back EMF proportional to its speed, and in a DC motor the speed follows from applied voltage = back EMF + voltage drop across the winding resistance.

Japanese name: 逆起電力

Try it: questions about Back EMF

A voltage of 24 V is applied to a DC motor with an armature resistance of 0.5 Ω and a back-EMF constant of 0.1 V/(rad/s), and the armature current is 8 A. Which of the following is the angular speed of rotation at this point?

  1. A40 rad/s
  2. B200 rad/s
  3. C240 rad/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 200 rad/s

In a DC motor the applied voltage equals the voltage drop across the armature resistance plus the back EMF (V = R × I + E). The back EMF is E = 24 − 0.5 × 8 = 20 V, and from E = K_e × ω, ω = 20/0.1 = 200 rad/s (about 1910 min⁻¹). In SI units the torque constant has the same value as the back-EMF constant, so the torque is 0.1 × 8 = 0.8 N·m. When the load and therefore the current increase, the resistive drop increases and the back EMF decreases, so the motor slows slightly.
