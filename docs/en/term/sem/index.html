What is SEM (scanning electron microscope)?

A microscope that scans a finely focused electron beam over a sample and detects secondary electrons and other signals to image the surface. It resolves features of a few nanometers, far beyond optical microscopes.

Japanese name: SEM(走査電子顕微鏡)

How an SEM builds an image

A scanning electron microscope (SEM) has no image-forming lens in the optical sense. An electron gun, usually a field-emission source in high-end tools, produces a beam that electromagnetic lenses focus to a spot only nanometers wide, and scan coils raster it across the sample in vacuum. At each point a detector measures the electrons emitted, and that count becomes the brightness of one pixel. Magnification is simply the ratio of display size to scanned area. Because electrons at kilovolt energies have picometer-scale wavelengths, resolution is limited by spot size and beam–sample interaction rather than diffraction, reaching around 1 nm.

Secondary electrons, backscattered electrons and X-rays

Secondary electrons have low energies, conventionally below 50 eV, so only those generated within a few nanometers of the surface escape; their yield depends on local tilt and edges, giving crisp topographic images. Backscattered electrons are beam electrons deflected back with high energy, and their yield rises with atomic number, so heavier materials appear brighter, giving compositional contrast. The beam also knocks out inner-shell electrons, producing characteristic X-rays analyzed by energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) to identify elements, with detection limits of about 0.1 wt%. Trace impurities need more sensitive methods such as SIMS.

SEM in the fab

Critical-dimension SEMs (CD-SEMs) measure linewidths and hole diameters top-down for process control, operating at low accelerating voltage to limit charging and beam damage to resist. Review SEMs revisit defects found by optical inspection tools and image them at high resolution, and automatic defect classification (ADC) sorts those images into categories such as particles, pattern defects and scratches. For internal structure, a wafer is cleaved or milled with a focused ion beam (FIB) and the cross-section imaged; this is destructive, so it is done on sampled or test wafers. Dual-beam FIB-SEM tools image while milling.

Charging, and SEM vs. TEM

On insulating samples, incoming electrons cannot drain away, so charge builds up and distorts brightness and geometry. Remedies include lowering the accelerating voltage until emitted and incoming electrons roughly balance, coating the surface with a thin gold or carbon film, or imaging in low-vacuum mode, where gas ions neutralize charge. Raising the voltage usually makes charging worse. An SEM images the surface of a bulk sample from reflected and emitted signals, whereas a transmission electron microscope (TEM) sends electrons through a lamella thin enough to transmit them, reaching atomic resolution but requiring careful sample preparation.

Answer: A. A finely focused electron beam is scanned over the sample surface, and secondary electrons and other emissions are detected to observe surface shape

An SEM scans a finely focused electron beam over the sample and forms images from the emitted secondary and backscattered electrons. Low-energy secondary electrons escape only from very shallow depths, so they reveal topography, while backscattered electrons increase with heavier elements and show compositional contrast. Resolution is around 1 nm. Fabs use CD-SEMs, operated at low accelerating voltage, to measure linewidths for process control. X-ray diffraction and optical interference are different techniques.

What can be determined with EDX (energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy), used together with SEM and TEM?

  1. ASurface roughness
  2. BThe kinds and amounts of elements in the sample
  3. CInternal stress in films

Answer: B. The kinds and amounts of elements in the sample

When an electron beam ejects an inner-shell electron, an outer electron dropping into the vacancy emits a characteristic X-ray whose energy is unique to the element. EDX analyzes these energies to identify elements and their amounts. Because it reveals composition right where the microscope is looking, it is widely used to analyze particles and failure sites. Its detection limit is about 0.1 wt%, so trace impurities require techniques such as SIMS.

FAQ

What is the difference between SEM and TEM?

An SEM scans a beam across a bulk sample and detects emitted electrons, giving surface images down to about a nanometer. A TEM passes electrons through an ultra-thin slice and can resolve atomic columns, but the sample must be specially thinned.

Why are SEM samples coated with gold or carbon?

Insulating samples accumulate charge under the electron beam, which distorts the image. A thin conductive coating drains the charge to ground; low accelerating voltage or low-vacuum mode are alternatives when coating is unacceptable.

What is a CD-SEM?

A CD-SEM is an SEM specialized for automatically measuring critical dimensions, such as linewidths and hole sizes, on production wafers. It uses low beam energy to minimize charging and damage to resist patterns.
