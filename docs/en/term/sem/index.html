What is SEM (scanning electron microscope)?

A microscope that scans a finely focused electron beam over a sample and detects secondary electrons and other signals to image the surface. It resolves features of a few nanometers, far beyond optical microscopes.

Japanese name: SEM(走査電子顕微鏡)

Which correctly describes the principle of the scanning electron microscope (SEM)?

  1. AA finely focused electron beam is scanned over the sample surface, and secondary electrons and other emissions are detected to observe surface shape
  2. BCrystal structure is examined from X-ray diffraction
  3. CFilm thickness is measured from light interference
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A finely focused electron beam is scanned over the sample surface, and secondary electrons and other emissions are detected to observe surface shape

An SEM scans a finely focused electron beam over the sample and forms images from the emitted secondary and backscattered electrons. Low-energy secondary electrons escape only from very shallow depths, so they reveal topography, while backscattered electrons increase with heavier elements and show compositional contrast. Resolution is around 1 nm. Fabs use CD-SEMs, operated at low accelerating voltage, to measure linewidths for process control. X-ray diffraction and optical interference are different techniques.

What can be determined with EDX (energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy), used together with SEM and TEM?

  1. ASurface roughness
  2. BThe kinds and amounts of elements in the sample
  3. CInternal stress in films
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The kinds and amounts of elements in the sample

When an electron beam ejects an inner-shell electron, an outer electron dropping into the vacancy emits a characteristic X-ray whose energy is unique to the element. EDX analyzes these energies to identify elements and their amounts. Because it reveals composition right where the microscope is looking, it is widely used to analyze particles and failure sites. Its detection limit is about 0.1 wt%, so trace impurities require techniques such as SIMS.
