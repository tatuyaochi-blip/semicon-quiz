What is Junction capacitance (depletion capacitance)?

Capacitance arising because the depletion layer of a PN junction acts like a parallel-plate capacitor. It decreases as reverse bias widens the depletion layer; varactor diodes use this property.

Japanese name: 接合容量(空乏層容量)

Which value is closest to the relative permittivity of silicon?

  1. AAbout 11.7
  2. BAbout 80
  3. CAbout 3.9
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. About 11.7

Silicon's relative permittivity is about 11.7, used to calculate depletion width (W = √(2εV/qN)) and junction capacitance. SiO2 is about 3.9, and gate dielectrics are compared by their equivalent oxide thickness (EOT) relative to this value. High-k materials such as HfO2 (about 20–25) can be physically thicker to suppress tunneling while matching the capacitance of thin SiO2. Between interconnects, low-k materials are used instead.

What happens to the width of the depletion layer when the reverse bias applied to a PN junction is increased?

  1. AIt narrows
  2. BIt does not change
  3. CIt widens
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It widens

Under reverse bias the applied voltage adds to the built-in potential, pulling carriers further from the junction, so the depletion region widens. Its width scales roughly as √(Vbi + V), so quadrupling the voltage about doubles it. A wider depletion region lowers the junction capacitance (C = εA/W), the property used in varactor diodes, and spreading the field over a wider region also helps withstand voltage.
