What is AFM (atomic force microscope)?

A microscope that traces a sharp tip over a sample and measures fine surface topography from the forces between tip and surface. It is used, for example, to evaluate surface roughness.

Japanese name: AFM(原子間力顕微鏡)

How an AFM works

An atomic force microscope (AFM) carries a sharp tip at the free end of a microfabricated cantilever. As a piezoelectric scanner moves the tip and sample relative to each other in a raster, forces between tip and surface bend the cantilever. A laser beam reflected off the back of the cantilever onto a position-sensitive photodetector magnifies that bending. A feedback loop raises or lowers the tip to keep the deflection, or oscillation amplitude, constant, and the recorded height at each point builds a three-dimensional topography map with vertical resolution around 0.1 nm.

Contact and tapping modes

In contact mode the tip is dragged across the surface at constant deflection, which is simple but applies lateral forces that can scratch soft films or blunt the tip. In tapping mode the cantilever oscillates near its resonance and touches the surface only briefly at the bottom of each cycle; the feedback holds the oscillation amplitude constant. Tapping mode minimizes damage and is the usual choice for resists, polymers and delicate thin films, while still delivering sub-nanometer height sensitivity.

Tip shape and measurement limits

An AFM image is never the pure surface: it is the surface convolved with the tip. A tip cannot reach the bottom of a trench narrower than itself, and on steep sidewalls its flank touches first, so trenches appear shallower and narrower and lines appear wider. Sharper tips, flared tips designed for sidewall profiling, and software that estimates and removes the tip shape all reduce the error. Scanning is mechanical and slow, and fields of view are small, so AFM samples selected sites rather than whole wafers.

AFM vs. other metrology tools

AFM's strengths are surface roughness after chemical mechanical polishing (CMP), thin-film surfaces and small step heights. A stylus profiler works on the same tracing principle with coarser resolution but can scan millimeters, so it suits etch depth and long-range planarity. AFM measures shape only: composition and chemical state come from XPS (X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy), and sheet resistance from a four-point probe. In advanced nodes, AFM data is often combined with optical and CD-SEM results in hybrid metrology to constrain dimensions no single tool determines well.

Answer: C. Fine surface topography (shape and roughness)

An AFM scans the surface with a sharp tip on a cantilever, detecting its deflection by atomic forces via a reflected laser beam, to map surface topography. It resolves height differences of about 0.1 nm, so it is used to evaluate surface roughness after CMP and the surfaces of thin films. Answer: B. The tip has a finite size and shape, so its shape gets superimposed on the measured profile

Because an AFM traces the surface with a tip, it cannot reach the bottom of trenches narrower than the tip, and on steep sidewalls the side of the tip makes contact first. The measured profile is thus a convolution of the real shape with the tip shape, making trenches look shallower and narrower and lines wider. Sharper tips or flared tips for sidewall measurement are used, along with analysis that estimates and removes the tip shape.

FAQ

What does an atomic force microscope measure?

Surface topography, meaning height variations and roughness, with vertical resolution around 0.1 nm, from the forces between a sharp tip and the sample.

What is the difference between contact mode and tapping mode AFM?

Contact mode drags the tip along the surface continuously; tapping mode oscillates the cantilever so the tip touches only intermittently, greatly reducing lateral force and sample damage.

Why does AFM make trenches look narrower than they are?

The tip has finite size and shape, so it cannot reach into narrow trenches or follow steep walls. The image is the true profile convolved with the tip shape.
