What is Donor?

An impurity that supplies electrons to a semiconductor. For silicon, group V elements such as phosphorus (P) and arsenic (As) act as donors and make n-type semiconductors.

Japanese name: ドナー

What is the approximate hole density in n-type silicon with a donor concentration of 1×10¹⁶ cm⁻³ (all donors ionized, intrinsic carrier density ni = 1×10¹⁰ cm⁻³)?

  1. A1×10⁴ cm⁻³
  2. B1×10⁶ cm⁻³
  3. C1×10¹⁰ cm⁻³
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 1×10⁴ cm⁻³

With all donors ionized, the electron density is n ≈ 1×10¹⁶ cm⁻³. From the law of mass action n × p = ni², p = (1×10¹⁰)² ÷ 1×10¹⁶ = 1×10⁴ cm⁻³. These minority carriers are a trillionth of the majority carriers, yet they carry the reverse current of a PN junction and the operation of a bipolar transistor. Since ni rises steeply with temperature, the minority carrier density also grows sharply when hot.

Where in the band gap is the donor level created by phosphorus in silicon?

  1. AJust below the bottom of the conduction band (about 0.045 eV below)
  2. BJust above the top of the valence band
  3. CNear the middle of the band gap
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Just below the bottom of the conduction band (about 0.045 eV below)

The phosphorus donor level lies only about 0.045 eV below the conduction band, so even room-temperature thermal energy (about 0.026 eV) ionizes nearly all donors. Arsenic is about 0.054 eV, and the boron acceptor level is about 0.045 eV above the valence band. These shallow levels are weakly bound because the host's dielectric constant screens them, so they can be estimated with a hydrogen-atom model. Deep levels near midgap come from impurities such as gold and act as recombination centers.
