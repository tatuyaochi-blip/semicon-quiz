What is LPCVD (low-pressure CVD)?

Thermal CVD performed under reduced pressure. Faster gas diffusion gives good thickness uniformity and step coverage, and many wafers can be processed at once in a vertical furnace. It is used for polysilicon and nitride films.

Japanese name: LPCVD(減圧CVD)

Field: Deposition (CVD and PVD)

Why low pressure gives uniform films

Low-pressure chemical vapor deposition (LPCVD) runs thermal CVD at roughly tens to hundreds of pascals. Gas diffusivity rises as pressure falls, so reactants reach every surface quickly and the growth rate is set by the surface reaction rather than by gas transport. A surface-reaction-limited process depends mainly on temperature, and a hot-wall tube furnace holds temperature very uniform. That is why wafers can stand only a few millimetres apart, often more than a hundred per run, and still receive uniform, conformal films with good step coverage.

Typical films and chemistry

Polysilicon is deposited by decomposing silane (SiH4) at about 600 to 650 °C; below about 580 °C the film is amorphous. Silicon nitride comes from dichlorosilane and ammonia at about 700 to 800 °C, and oxide from TEOS decomposition at similar furnace temperatures. Polysilicon can be doped in situ by adding phosphine or diborane, which is useful for films lining deep trenches. Deposition rates are modest, typically a few to tens of nanometres per minute, which the large batch size compensates for.

LPCVD vs. PECVD

Plasma-enhanced CVD (PECVD) uses a plasma to drive reactions at a few hundred degrees, which makes it usable after metallization, but its films are less dense, contain more hydrogen and etch faster. LPCVD films are dense and close to stoichiometric; LPCVD nitride holds only a few percent hydrogen compared with around 20% in PECVD nitride. Stoichiometric LPCVD nitride also has high tensile stress, so silicon-rich low-stress variants are often used in MEMS membranes. The price of LPCVD quality is its thermal budget.

Where you will meet it

Because it needs 600 to 800 °C, LPCVD belongs to the front end, before aluminum or copper wiring exists. Typical uses are polysilicon gates and resistors, nitride hard masks and spacers, pad nitride for isolation, capacitor electrodes in DRAM and floating gates or electrodes in flash, and structural layers in MEMS. Film also deposits on the furnace tube and on both sides of every wafer, so tubes need periodic cleaning and backside films sometimes must be stripped.

Answer: B. Silane (SiH4)

It serves as gate electrodes (before high-k), resistors, electrodes in DRAM and flash, and MEMS structural layers, doped during or after deposition to lower resistance.

FAQ

What is the difference between LPCVD and PECVD?

LPCVD uses heat alone at about 600 to 800 °C and gives dense, low-hydrogen, uniform films in large batches. PECVD uses a plasma to deposit at a few hundred degrees, enabling use over metal at the cost of film density and purity.

Why is LPCVD deposition so uniform?

At low pressure gas diffusion is fast, so growth is limited by the surface reaction, which depends mainly on temperature. A hot-wall furnace keeps temperature uniform across all wafers in the load.

At what temperature is LPCVD polysilicon deposited?

Typically about 600 to 650 °C from silane. Depositing below roughly 580 °C produces amorphous silicon, which can be crystallized by a later anneal.
