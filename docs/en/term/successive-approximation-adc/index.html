What is Successive-approximation ADC?

An ADC that uses an internal DAC and comparator to determine one bit at a time, starting from the most significant. It balances speed, accuracy and power well.

Japanese name: 逐次比較型ADC

What is the main reason for placing a driver circuit, such as an op-amp, in front of a successive-approximation ADC?

  1. ATo change the color of the input signal
  2. BTo charge the ADC's internal sampling capacitor quickly and capture the input voltage accurately
  3. CTo reduce the ADC's power consumption to zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. To charge the ADC's internal sampling capacitor quickly and capture the input voltage accurately

A SAR ADC connects its internal capacitance (a few to tens of pF) to the input at sampling, so a high-impedance source cannot charge it within the short acquisition time and the captured voltage is inaccurate. Settling to N-bit accuracy takes about (N+1)·ln2 time constants. The fix combines a low-output-impedance driver with an RC filter (charge bucket) that supplies the instantaneous charge.

How many comparisons does an N-bit successive-approximation ADC basically perform per conversion?

  1. A1
  2. B2ᴺ
  3. CN
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. N

A SAR ADC performs a binary search, comparing the input with its internal DAC from the MSB down and settling one bit per comparison, so N bits basically need N comparisons. At 12 bits and 1 MS/s, each comparison gets only tens of nanoseconds. Some designs add redundant bits so that early decision errors can be corrected later, trading a few extra comparisons for higher speed and accuracy.
