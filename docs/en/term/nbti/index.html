What is NBTI (negative bias temperature instability)?

A degradation phenomenon in which the magnitude of a pMOS threshold voltage gradually increases under negative gate voltage at high temperature.

Japanese name: NBTI

Which phenomenon increases off-state leakage because band-to-band tunneling current flows due to the strong field where the gate overlaps the drain?

  1. ADIBL
  2. BNBTI
  3. CGIDL (gate-induced drain leakage)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. GIDL (gate-induced drain leakage)

Even when a transistor is off, a large gate-to-drain voltage bends the bands sharply near the drain edge of the gate, and band-to-band tunneling causes current to flow—GIDL, which notably grows as the gate is driven more negative. It is a problem in devices requiring ultra-low leakage such as DRAM cell transistors and is controlled by engineering the drain-edge doping and field. DIBL is threshold lowering by drain voltage, and NBTI is a pMOS degradation mechanism.

Which degradation phenomenon causes the absolute value of a pMOS transistor's threshold voltage to gradually increase under sustained negative gate voltage and high temperature?

  1. ANBTI
  2. BElectromigration
  3. CESD
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. NBTI

NBTI (negative bias temperature instability) arises when negative gate bias and high temperature on a pMOS break Si–H bonds at the Si/SiO2 interface, creating interface traps, while positive charge accumulates in the dielectric, increasing the threshold voltage magnitude. Circuits slow gradually over years, so designers build in margin for the degradation. Partial recovery when stress is removed is another characteristic.
