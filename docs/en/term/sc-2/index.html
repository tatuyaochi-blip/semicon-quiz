What is SC-2 (HPM)?

A mixture of hydrochloric acid, hydrogen peroxide and water used after SC-1 in RCA cleaning to remove metal contamination.

Japanese name: SC-2(HPM)

SC-2 (HPM) appears in 4 practice questions across 1 topic, including CMP and cleaning.

Field: CMP and cleaning

Composition and conditions

SC-2, Standard Clean 2, is a mixture of hydrochloric acid, hydrogen peroxide and water, also called HPM (hydrochloric acid/peroxide mixture). The classic RCA recipe uses volume ratios around 1:1:6 at about 70 to 80 °C for several minutes. It is the acidic, oxidizing companion to SC-1, the ammonia/peroxide mixture (APM). SC-2 does not measurably etch silicon or silicon dioxide, and it leaves a thin chemical oxide that makes the surface hydrophilic.

How it removes metals

Hydrogen peroxide oxidizes metallic contaminants on the surface to ions, and the acidic chloride solution keeps them dissolved, partly as soluble chloride complexes, so they are rinsed away rather than redepositing. This targets alkali metals such as sodium and transition metals such as iron, nickel and zinc, as well as aluminum. In the alkaline SC-1 bath some of these metals, for example iron and aluminum, tend to form hydroxides that stay on or return to the wafer, which is why the acidic step follows.

Its place in RCA cleaning

The RCA clean, published by Kern and colleagues in 1970, runs SC-1 first to remove particles and organics, then SC-2 to strip metals, with ultrapure-water rinses between steps and a dilute hydrofluoric acid (HF) dip optionally inserted to remove oxide. A sulfuric acid/peroxide mixture (SPM) may precede everything when heavy organics such as resist residue are present. SC-2 is not effective for particles; particles adhere more readily in acidic solutions, so particle removal relies on SC-1 and physical action such as megasonics.

Modern practice

Refined versions of the sequence remain the basis of front-end cleaning, especially before gate oxidation and other high-temperature steps where metal contamination degrades oxide integrity and carrier lifetime. Fabs often use much more dilute mixtures and lower temperatures than the original recipe, which cut chemical use while still removing metals effectively, and many cleans now run in single-wafer spin processors instead of immersion baths. HCl fumes are corrosive, so baths and tools need good exhaust.

Answer: A. SC-1 removes particles and organics, then SC-2 removes metal contamination

The RCA clean, published by Kern and colleagues at RCA in 1970, first removes particles and organics with alkaline SC-1, then removes metals—which SC-1 tends to leave behind—as chlorides with acidic SC-2 (HCl:H2O2:H2O). A dilute-HF step may be inserted to strip oxide, with ultrapure-water rinses between steps. More than 50 years on, refined versions remain the foundation of wafer cleaning.

FAQ

What does SC-2 remove in wafer cleaning?

SC-2 removes metal contamination, such as sodium, iron, nickel and aluminum, by oxidizing the metals and keeping them dissolved as ions in the acidic chloride solution. It does not remove particles effectively.

Why is SC-2 done after SC-1?

SC-1 removes particles and organics but can leave some metals behind as hydroxides in its alkaline solution. The acidic SC-2 step then dissolves those metals.

What do HPM, APM and SPM stand for?

HPM is the hydrochloric acid/hydrogen peroxide mixture (SC-2), APM is the ammonia/hydrogen peroxide mixture (SC-1), and SPM is the sulfuric acid/hydrogen peroxide mixture used to strip heavy organics.
