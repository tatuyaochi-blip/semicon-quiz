What is Silicide?

A compound of silicon and a metal, such as NiSi or TiSi2. It is formed on source, drain and gate surfaces to reduce resistance and contact resistance.

Japanese name: シリサイド

Silicide appears in 4 practice questions across 4 topics, including PN junctions and diodes, Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA), CMP and cleaning.

Field: Interconnect processes

The self-aligned silicide (salicide) process

After gates and sidewall spacers are formed, a thin metal film is deposited over the whole wafer, often with a cap layer to prevent oxidation. A rapid thermal anneal makes the metal react only where it touches silicon, on the source, drain and, in older flows, the polysilicon gate; it does not react with oxide or nitride spacers and isolation. A selective wet etch then removes the unreacted metal, and a second anneal may convert the film to its low-resistivity phase. No mask is needed, hence the name salicide.

Why the material changed: TiSi2, CoSi2, NiSi

Titanium disilicide must transform into a low-resistivity phase, and that transformation becomes difficult on narrow lines, so resistance rose as dimensions shrank. Cobalt disilicide avoided this linewidth problem but consumes a relatively large amount of silicon. Nickel monosilicide (NiSi) forms at lower temperature, consumes less silicon and has low resistivity, around 15 µΩ·cm. Its weakness is thermal stability: at higher temperatures it can agglomerate or convert to the high-resistivity NiSi2 phase, so later process steps must stay cool.

Silicide and contact resistance

A metal on silicon forms a Schottky barrier. Heavy doping, around 10²⁰ cm⁻³ at the surface, thins that barrier enough for carriers to tunnel, and the silicide provides an intimate, low-resistance metal interface right at that doped silicon. Silicide also lowers the sheet resistance of source and drain regions by over an order of magnitude compared with doped silicon. In advanced transistors, where contacts account for a large share of total resistance, specific contact resistivities near 10⁻⁹ Ω·cm² are targeted, and titanium-based silicide formed at the bottom of contact trenches is common.

Silicide vs. other conductors

A silicide is an intermetallic compound grown by consuming silicon, unlike a deposited metal or barrier film such as TiN. Silicided polysilicon gate lines were once called polycide. In replacement metal gate processes, the gate is metal from the start, so silicide is needed only on source and drain contacts. Silicides are not used for long interconnects, which rely on copper or other metals with much lower resistivity.

FAQ

What does salicide mean?

Salicide stands for self-aligned silicide: metal is deposited everywhere, reacts only where it contacts silicon, and the unreacted metal is etched away, so the silicide aligns to the silicon regions without a lithography step.

Why is NiSi used instead of TiSi2?

NiSi keeps low resistance on narrow lines, forms at lower temperature and consumes less silicon. TiSi2 has trouble reaching its low-resistivity phase on very narrow lines.

Does silicide make a contact ohmic by itself?

Not by itself; a silicide-silicon interface still has a Schottky barrier. Heavy doping under the silicide thins that barrier so carriers tunnel through, giving a low-resistance ohmic contact.
