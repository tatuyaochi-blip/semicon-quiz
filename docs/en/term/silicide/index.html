What is Silicide?

A compound of silicon and a metal, such as NiSi or TiSi2. It is formed on source, drain and gate surfaces to reduce resistance and contact resistance.

Japanese name: シリサイド

Silicide appears in 4 practice questions across 4 topics, including PN junctions and diodes, Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA), CMP and cleaning.

Field: Interconnect processes

What is a common way to make a metal-semiconductor contact ohmic (resistive)?

  1. AHeavily dope the semiconductor surface at the contact so carriers can tunnel through the barrier easily
  2. BMake the semiconductor surface at the contact lightly doped
  3. CInsert a thick oxide between the metal and semiconductor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Heavily dope the semiconductor surface at the contact so carriers can tunnel through the barrier easily

A Schottky barrier forms between metal and semiconductor, but doping the semiconductor heavily (about 10¹⁹ cm⁻³ or more in silicon) thins the barrier to a few nm so carriers can tunnel through. The result is a low-resistance ohmic contact in either current direction. In advanced transistors contact resistance makes up a large share of total resistance, so it is reduced with silicide formation and extremely high doping.

What is the basic way to reduce metal-semiconductor contact resistance?

  1. AHeavily dope the semiconductor at the contact to thin the barrier and make tunneling easier
  2. BMake the semiconductor at the contact extremely lightly doped
  3. CInsert a thick oxide between the metal and semiconductor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Heavily dope the semiconductor at the contact to thin the barrier and make tunneling easier

A Schottky barrier forms at metal-semiconductor interfaces. Raising semiconductor doping above 10²⁰ cm⁻³ thins the barrier to a few nm or less so carriers tunnel through easily, lowering contact resistance. Silicide interfaces such as titanium silicide, low-barrier materials and enlarged contact areas are also used, as advanced nodes demand specific contact resistivities around 10⁻⁹ Ω·cm².
