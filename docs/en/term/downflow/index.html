What is Downflow?

A cleanroom airflow method that sends clean air in one direction from the entire ceiling to the floor, quickly carrying generated particles away under the floor.

Japanese name: ダウンフロー

How downflow works

In a downflow (vertical unidirectional flow) cleanroom, nearly the whole ceiling is covered with high-efficiency HEPA or ULPA filters, often as fan filter units. Filtered air flows straight down through the working space, passes through perforated floor panels into the underfloor plenum, and returns through side chases or return shafts to the ceiling plenum to be filtered again. Particles shed by people and tools are swept downward and out of the room before they can drift sideways or rise toward wafers.

Unidirectional vs. mixed flow

A conventional cleanroom supplies filtered air through only part of the ceiling and relies on mixing to dilute particles, so contamination lingers and spreads. Downflow instead moves the whole room's air in parallel streamlines, so a particle generated at one spot is carried away rather than diluted. This is why the cleanest classes, roughly ISO Class 5 and better, generally require unidirectional flow, while mixed-flow rooms suit less demanding areas. The price is enormous air volume and fan energy.

Typical numbers

Supply velocity is usually about 0.3 to 0.5 m/s. Faster flow becomes turbulent around people and equipment and stirs particles up, while slower flow can be overcome by thermal plumes rising from hot tools. At 0.4 m/s, air travels about 1,440 m per hour, so a room 3 to 4 m tall is fully replaced several hundred times per hour, compared with a few changes per hour in an ordinary office. Moving that much air is a major share of a fab's electricity use.

Floors, slabs and mini-environments

The floor must pass air, so it is built from perforated panels or gratings laid over a waffle slab, a thick concrete floor with a grid of holes that also gives the stiffness lithography scanners need for vibration control. Modern fabs carry wafers in sealed pods and load them through tool front ends with their own filter units, so the highest cleanliness is needed only inside these mini-environments. The surrounding bays can then use lower filter coverage, saving energy while protecting the wafer.

What is the main purpose of the downflow method, in which air flows in one direction from ceiling to floor in a cleanroom?

  1. ATo quickly carry generated particles below the floor so they do not linger around wafers
  2. BTo raise the room temperature
  3. CTo lower noise in the room

Answer: A. To quickly carry generated particles below the floor so they do not linger around wafers

Clean air from high-efficiency ceiling filters flowing uniformly downward carries particles from people and tools to the underfloor before they can rise, keeping them from lingering around wafers. It achieves higher cleanliness than mixing and diluting room air, but moving such large air volumes consumes much energy. Adjusting temperature or noise is not the purpose.

In downflow cleanrooms, why are perforated floor panels (gratings) used?

  1. ATo make the floor non-slip
  2. BTo make the floor lighter
  3. CTo pass air blown from the ceiling into the space below the floor for recirculation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. To pass air blown from the ceiling into the space below the floor for recirculation

In downflow cleanrooms, air from ceiling filters is drawn through perforated floor panels into the underfloor space, then returns through side chases to the ceiling. If the floor did not pass air, the flow would deflect sideways at the floor and stir particles up. Because air keeps moving in one direction, particles are swept to the underfloor immediately. Slip resistance or lighter weight is not the purpose.

FAQ

What air velocity is used in a downflow cleanroom?

Typically about 0.3 to 0.5 m/s. That is fast enough to overcome thermal updrafts but slow enough to stay smooth rather than turbulent around people and tools.

Why do cleanrooms have perforated floors?

The holes let the downward airflow pass into the underfloor space and return for recirculation. A solid floor would turn the flow sideways and stir particles back up into the room.

Why do cleanrooms use so much energy?

Unidirectional downflow replaces the room's air several hundred times per hour, so large fans run continuously, and the air must also be kept at tightly controlled temperature and humidity.
