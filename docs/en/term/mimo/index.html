What is MIMO (multiple-input multiple-output)?

A wireless technique that uses multiple antennas at both the transmitter and the receiver. It raises the transmission rate through spatial multiplexing, sending different data on the same frequency at the same time, or raises reliability through diversity, sending the same data over several paths. The number of streams that spatial multiplexing can send is limited by the smaller of the numbers of transmit and receive antennas.

Japanese name: MIMO(多入力多出力)

How spatial multiplexing works

The way radio waves travel (the propagation channel) differs slightly for each pair of transmit and receive antennas. The receiver estimates these differences from known reference signals and separates the signals that arrive overlapped at each receive antenna, much like solving simultaneous equations. In this way several data flows (streams) can be sent at the same frequency and time, raising the transmission rate without increasing bandwidth.

When spatial multiplexing works well

Spatial multiplexing needs propagation channels that differ sufficiently. In environments with many reflections, such as among buildings, the channels differ greatly and many streams can be separated. Conversely, when the paths are similar, as in places with clear line of sight, the channels are highly correlated and fewer streams can be used. Because power is divided among the streams, a sufficiently high S/N is also needed.

Multi-user MIMO and massive MIMO

Whereas single-user MIMO sends several streams to one terminal, multi-user MIMO has a base station or access point send separate streams to several terminals at the same time. Massive MIMO, with several tens or more antenna elements at the base station, uses beamforming to form a beam toward each terminal and communicates with many terminals simultaneously. It is used in 5G NR base stations and elsewhere.

Answer: A. 2

In MIMO spatial multiplexing, different data are sent simultaneously on the same frequency from several transmit antennas, and the receiver separates them using the differences in the propagation channels between each pair of transmit and receive antennas. The number of separable streams is limited by the smaller of the numbers of transmit and receive antennas, here min(4, 2) = 2. In practice, when the propagation channels are similar (highly correlated), as in places with clear line of sight, fewer streams than this limit can be used.

FAQ

How do MIMO and beamforming differ?

Beamforming is a technique that adjusts the phases of several antenna elements to concentrate radio waves in a particular direction. MIMO refers to the whole approach of transmitting and receiving with multiple antennas, within which spatial multiplexing, diversity and beamforming are used. Massive MIMO forms a beam per terminal by beamforming and combines it with spatial multiplexing.

What does a notation such as 4×4 MIMO mean?

It generally means the number of transmit antennas × the number of receive antennas. 4×4 MIMO can send up to 4 streams by spatial multiplexing. If the terminal has only 2 antennas, at most 2 streams can be sent to it even if the access point has 4.
