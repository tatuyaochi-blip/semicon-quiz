What is Ultrapure water?

Water from which ions, particles and organic matter have been removed to the extreme. Used in large amounts for wafer cleaning, with purity controlled by resistivity and other measures.

Japanese name: 超純水

Field: Process materials (gases and chemicals)

How ultrapure water is made

Ultrapure water (UPW) is produced in stages, each targeting a different contaminant. After pretreatment, reverse osmosis removes most dissolved salts, organics and particles. Ion-exchange resins then strip the remaining ions, ultraviolet oxidation breaks down residual organic molecules so they can be removed, and membrane degassing lowers dissolved oxygen. Ultrafiltration near the point of use catches the finest particles. The finished water is kept circulating through the distribution loop rather than standing in pipes, because stagnant water picks up contamination from surfaces and allows bacteria to grow.

Resistivity and what it misses

Purity is tracked mainly by resistivity, because fewer dissolved ions means lower conductivity. Pure water still contains H+ and OH− from its own dissociation, which sets a theoretical limit of about 18.2 MΩ·cm at 25 °C; semiconductor UPW is kept very close to it, and readings are temperature-compensated to 25 °C. Resistivity is blind to uncharged contaminants, so total organic carbon (TOC), particles, dissolved oxygen and silica are monitored separately at parts-per-billion levels, particles are counted with liquid particle counters, and metals are held at parts-per-trillion levels.

Where it is used in a fab

The largest use is rinsing wafers after chemical cleans, and cleaning recurs before and after deposition, etching, implantation, CMP and ashing, on the order of 200 times or more for an advanced chip. UPW also dilutes process chemicals and carries dissolved ozone for room-temperature organic removal. In ArF immersion lithography, a roughly 1 mm film of UPW between the final lens and the wafer, with a refractive index of about 1.44 at 193 nm, lets the numerical aperture reach about 1.35. Any metal it carries would shorten carrier lifetime and degrade gate oxides.

Water and energy footprint

A large fab can consume tens of thousands of tonnes of UPW per day, so water is a design constraint rather than a commodity. Relatively clean rinse water is reclaimed, treated and returned as UPW feed or cooling water to raise the recovery rate, and water availability influences site selection in dry regions. Producing UPW also takes considerable electricity for pumps, membranes and UV lamps, so the UPW plant sits alongside cleanroom air handling and exhaust treatment among the utility systems that dominate a fab's energy use.

What is the main purpose of cleaning with ozone dissolved in ultrapure water (ozonated water)?

  1. ATo deposit metal films
  2. BTo polish the wafer
  3. CTo oxidize, decompose and remove organics
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. To oxidize, decompose and remove organics

Ozonated water with a few to tens of ppm dissolved ozone is a strong oxidizer that decomposes organics and grows thin oxide on silicon at room temperature. It reverts to oxygen after use, simplifying waste treatment, and compared with SPM, which consumes large amounts of sulfuric acid and peroxide, it reduces chemical use and CO2 emissions. It is also used in cleans that alternate with dilute HF.

Which is a typical indicator used to control the purity of ultrapure water in semiconductor manufacturing?

  1. ADepth of color
  2. BResistivity
  3. CViscosity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Resistivity

The fewer dissolved ions, the less water conducts, so ultrapure water is controlled by resistivity. The theoretical limit, set only by water's own dissociation, is about 18.2 MΩ·cm at 25 °C, and semiconductor UPW is kept close to it. Because resistivity does not respond to non-ionic contamination, TOC (total organic carbon), particles, dissolved oxygen and silica are monitored separately at ppb levels. Color or viscosity cannot reveal purity differences.

FAQ

What is the resistivity of ultrapure water?

Close to 18.2 MΩ·cm at 25 °C, which is the theoretical maximum set by water's own dissociation into H+ and OH− ions.

Is ultrapure water the same as deionized water?

Deionized water has had its ions removed and may be fairly pure, but semiconductor UPW goes further, also removing organics, particles, dissolved gases and silica to parts-per-billion or lower levels.

Why can't ordinary pure water be used to rinse wafers?

Traces of metals, ions, particles or organics left on the wafer would create leakage, lifetime loss and defects, so rinse water must be cleaner than the surface it is meant to clean.
