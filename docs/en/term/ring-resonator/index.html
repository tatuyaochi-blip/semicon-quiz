What is Ring resonator?

A looped waveguide that resonates only at wavelengths for which one round trip's optical length is an integer number of wavelengths. It is used as a wavelength filter or compact modulator. In silicon the resonance shifts by about 0.08 nm per °C, so heaters are often used to control its temperature.

Japanese name: リング共振器

Field: Silicon photonics

How resonance arises

A ring resonator is a closed-loop waveguide placed next to a straight bus waveguide. Light couples across the narrow gap into the ring and circulates many times. When the round-trip optical length, effective index times circumference, equals an integer number of wavelengths, each pass adds in phase and power builds up. At those wavelengths the light passing along the bus shows sharp dips, while other wavelengths pass almost unaffected. Adding a second bus waveguide makes an add-drop filter that routes the resonant wavelength to a separate drop port.

FSR, linewidth and Q

Resonances repeat in wavelength. Their spacing, the free spectral range, is approximately FSR ≈ λ²/(ng·L), where ng is the group index and L the circumference. A silicon ring of about 10 µm radius (L ≈ 63 µm, ng ≈ 4.2) at 1.55 µm has an FSR near 9 nm; halving L roughly doubles it, helping WDM channels avoid neighboring resonances, though tighter bends add loss. Linewidths of about 0.1–0.3 nm are common, corresponding to quality factors Q, resonant wavelength divided by linewidth, of roughly 5,000 to 15,000.

Thermal sensitivity and tuning

Silicon's thermo-optic coefficient of about 1.8×10⁻⁴ per kelvin shifts a C-band ring's resonance toward longer wavelengths by roughly 0.07–0.08 nm per °C. With linewidths of a few tenths of a nanometer, a few degrees of drift detunes it from the laser. Fabrication is also unforgiving: a few nanometers of error in waveguide width or thickness moves the resonance by nanometers. Integrated heaters with feedback control therefore lock each ring to its wavelength, at the cost of extra power.

Ring modulators vs. Mach-Zehnder modulators

In a ring modulator, a PN junction in the ring changes the refractive index as carriers are depleted, shifting the resonance so that transmitted power at a fixed laser wavelength swings between high and low. Because the resonance magnifies a small index change, a ring is only tens of micrometers across with low capacitance, whereas a silicon Mach-Zehnder modulator is typically millimeters long. The tradeoffs are a narrow optical bandwidth, strong temperature sensitivity and the need for active wavelength locking.

Answer: C. At wavelengths where the optical path length of one round trip is an integer multiple of the wavelength, the circulating light interferes constructively

In a ring resonator, part of the light in an adjacent bus waveguide transfers into the ring and circulates many times. At wavelengths where the optical length of one round trip (effective index × circumference) is an integer multiple of the wavelength, the phase lines up on every pass and the light builds up (resonance), causing a sharp dip in the light passing through the bus waveguide at that wavelength only. This property is used for filters that extract specific wavelengths and for compact modulators that shift the resonant wavelength electrically. There are multiple discrete resonant wavelengths, and rings with radii from a few µm to several tens of µm are used.

  2. BThe spacing between adjacent resonant wavelengths (FSR) becomes narrower
  3. CResonance no longer occurs and all wavelengths pass through

Answer: A. The spacing between adjacent resonant wavelengths (FSR) becomes wider

The FSR (free spectral range) is the spacing between adjacent resonant wavelengths, approximately FSR ≈ λ² / (ng × L) (ng is the group index, L the circumference), so it is inversely proportional to the circumference. For example, with λ = 1.55 µm, ng ≈ 4.2 and L ≈ 63 µm (radius about 10 µm), the FSR is about 9 nm. Halving the circumference roughly doubles the FSR, making it easier in WDM, which handles many wavelengths, to keep one ring from also responding to another wavelength. However, sharper bends increase bending loss, so there is a limit to how small the ring can be.

FAQ

What is the free spectral range of a ring resonator?

It is the wavelength spacing between adjacent resonances, approximately λ²/(ng·L). Smaller rings have a larger FSR.

Why do silicon ring resonators need heaters?

Their resonance shifts by about 0.08 nm per °C and also varies with fabrication, while resonances are only tenths of a nanometer wide. Heaters with feedback keep the resonance aligned to the laser wavelength.

What is the difference between an all-pass and an add-drop ring?

An all-pass ring couples to one bus waveguide and produces dips in its through transmission. An add-drop ring couples to two waveguides, sending the resonant wavelength out of a second drop port.
