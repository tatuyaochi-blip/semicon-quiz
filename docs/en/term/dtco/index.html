What is DTCO (design-technology co-optimization)?

Optimizing cell area and performance by considering circuit design and process technology together, as scaling becomes harder.

Japanese name: DTCO(設計・技術協調最適化)

DTCO (design-technology co-optimization) appears in 1 practice question across 1 topic, including Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA).

Field: Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA)

Why DTCO became necessary

DTCO (design-technology co-optimization) means developing process technology and circuit design together rather than separately, to optimize area, performance, power and cost. In the past, shrinking gate and interconnect pitches substantially reduced cell area each generation without major changes to how designs were done. At advanced nodes, however, lithography and physical transistor limits have slowed pitch scaling, and shrinking dimensions alone no longer meets area targets. So density is now raised by changing cell structures and layout rules together with the process.

Representative techniques

Standard-cell height is expressed as the number of horizontal routing tracks that fit through a cell (the track count), and it has fallen generation by generation. DTCO is what made this possible. Examples include fin depopulation, which reduces the number of fins per transistor; contact over active gate (COAG), which places contacts above the gate; single diffusion break, which narrows the isolation at cell boundaries; and buried power rails, which embed power lines in the substrate, or backside power delivery, which moves them to the back of the wafer. Each is introduced as a pairing of a process change with changes to standard-cell and SRAM design rules.

How DTCO is carried out

For each candidate process, DTCO prepares TCAD device analysis, compact models and a predictive PDK bundling provisional design rules, then actually lays out standard cells and SRAM bitcells. These are evaluated from small circuits such as ring oscillators up to place-and-route of blocks such as CPU cores, to confirm whether power, performance and area (PPA) improve. If targets are missed, the team goes back to revisit transistor structure, interconnect pitch and design rules. Repeating this from early in development weeds out options that are hard to manufacture yet deliver little benefit.

STCO and what comes next

Whereas DTCO is co-optimization at the level of transistors, cells and blocks, STCO (system-technology co-optimization) optimizes the whole system, including partitioning into chiplets, 3D stacking, advanced packaging and memory placement. GAA (nanosheet) transistors allow drive strength to be tuned by varying sheet width, and structures that bring nMOS and pMOS closer together or stack them, such as forksheets and CFETs (complementary FETs), have been proposed. How much such new structures improve area and performance is also evaluated through DTCO.

FAQ

What does DTCO stand for and mean?

It stands for design-technology co-optimization. It means developing process technology and circuit design together to optimize area, performance and power.

What is the difference between DTCO and STCO?

DTCO covers co-optimization of design and process within the chip, such as transistors, standard cells and SRAM. STCO is a broader concept that covers co-optimization of the whole system and technology, including chiplet partitioning, 3D stacking and packaging.

Why has DTCO become important?

At advanced nodes, physical limits have slowed the scaling of gate and interconnect pitches, making it hard to meet area and performance targets by shrinking dimensions alone. Improvements that combine design and process, such as reducing cell height, have become a major pillar of density scaling.
