What is Standard cell?

A pre-designed, pre-verified building block such as a logic gate or flip-flop. Cells share a common height so automatic place-and-route can line them up to build large circuits.

Japanese name: スタンダードセル

Standard cell appears in 2 practice questions across 2 topics, including Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA), IC design flow and EDA.

Try it: questions about Standard cell

When standard cell height is expressed in the number of routing tracks, what is the effect of reducing the number of tracks?

  1. ACell area shrinks and density increases
  2. BCell area grows
  3. CThe number of wires increases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Cell area shrinks and density increases

Standard-cell height is expressed in tracks—the number of minimum-pitch wires that fit over it; six tracks at a 28 nm metal pitch is about 170 nm. Fewer tracks mean shorter cells and smaller circuit area. Advanced nodes have gone from about 7.5T to 6T to 5T, but less routing room and fewer fins (or sheets) per device raise congestion and reduce drive strength.

What is the task called of circuit-simulating standard cell delay and power across various input transition times, load capacitances, voltages and temperatures and compiling the results into tables?

  1. ATape-out
  2. BDicing
  3. CLibrary characterization
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Library characterization

Characterization simulates each cell's delay and power over combinations of input transition time and output load capacitance and tabulates the results, for example in Liberty format. Doing this for every PVT corner requires an enormous number of simulations. Synthesis and STA then look up and interpolate these tables to compute delays of large circuits quickly.
