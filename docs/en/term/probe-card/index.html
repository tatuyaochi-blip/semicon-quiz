What is Probe card?

A fixture that presses many needles (probes) onto chip electrodes on the wafer to connect them electrically to a tester.

Japanese name: プローブカード

Probe card appears in 3 practice questions across 2 topics, including Testing and inspection, Packaging and assembly.

Field: Testing and inspection

How wafer probing works

In wafer test, also called wafer sort or circuit probe, a prober holds the wafer on a movable chuck and uses cameras to align the chip pads with the needles of a probe card. The chuck then rises until the pads touch the needles and moves a little further, called overdrive, so the tips scrub through the thin native oxide on the aluminum pads and make low-resistance contact. The tester behind the card supplies power and signals and measures each chip's responses. Results are stored in a wafer map, and only chips that pass are packaged, which avoids spending assembly cost on defective dies.

Types of needles

Every probe card is built for a specific product because its needles must match that chip's pad layout. Cantilever cards use angled needles, traditionally of tungsten-based alloys, that reach in from the side and suit pads arranged around the chip edge. Vertical probe cards use straight, spring-like pins that push down from above, which suits area-array pads and bumps. MEMS (micro-electro-mechanical systems) probe cards form the contacts with lithographic processes, allowing very fine pitches and thousands of contacts, so many chips can be tested in parallel in a single touchdown to raise throughput.

Pads, probe marks and maintenance

Probe pads are typically tens of micrometers on a side and often double as the later wire-bond or bump sites. Each touchdown leaves a probe mark, and inspection checks its size and position so that damage does not impair bonding reliability. Needles cannot touch transistors directly because those lie beneath many wiring layers; all electrical access goes through the pads. With repeated touchdowns, pad material and debris build up on the tips and contact resistance rises, so probe cards are cleaned periodically on abrasive pads and their tip planarity and alignment are rechecked.

Probe card vs. other test hardware

The probe card is the interface between tester and bare wafer, whereas packaged chips are tested at final test through a load board and socket. The prober is the machine that handles and aligns wafers; the probe card is the replaceable, product-specific fixture mounted in it. Wafer test catches gross defects and parametric failures early, while final test re-checks chips after assembly, often at temperature and full speed.

FAQ

What is overdrive in wafer probing?

Overdrive is the extra vertical travel applied after the pads first touch the needles. It lets the tips scrub through surface oxide to make reliable contact, but too much overdrive enlarges probe marks and can damage pads.

What is the difference between a prober and a probe card?

The prober is the general-purpose machine that loads, aligns and steps the wafer, while the probe card is the product-specific board of needles that touches the chip pads and connects them to the tester.

Why test chips on the wafer before packaging?

Screening dies at wafer level means only good chips go through assembly, saving packaging cost and giving early yield feedback to the fab.
