What is Qubit?

The unit that represents information in a quantum computer. It can be in a superposition α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ of |0⟩ and |1⟩, and a measurement gives 0 with probability |α|² and 1 with probability |β|². Qubits are realized with superconducting circuits, ions, neutral atoms, electron spins in silicon, photons and more.

Japanese name: 量子ビット

Describing the state and measuring it

The state of a single qubit can be written α|0⟩ + β|1⟩, where α and β are complex numbers (probability amplitudes) satisfying |α|² + |β|² = 1. A measurement gives 0 with probability |α|² and 1 with probability |β|², and the state changes to match the result. A single measurement does not reveal the probabilities themselves, so the same circuit is repeated many times (each repetition is called a shot) and the probabilities are estimated from the fraction of each result. For a probability of 0.5, even 10,000 repetitions leave a standard deviation of about 0.5% in the estimate. The state can be shown as a point on the Bloch sphere, and quantum gate operations correspond to rotations of that sphere.

n qubits and 2ⁿ amplitudes

The state of n qubits has 2ⁿ probability amplitudes, about 10¹⁵ for 50 qubits. Storing them as double-precision complex numbers on a classical computer takes about 18 PB, and each additional qubit doubles that. However, a measurement yields only n bits, and the amplitudes cannot all be read out. Quantum algorithms are designed so that interference enlarges the amplitude of the correct answer and cancels the amplitudes of wrong ones.

Physical implementations

Superconducting circuits (transmons) use two energy levels about 5 GHz apart as a qubit and are operated after cooling to about 10 mK. Trapped ions and neutral atoms use atomic energy levels, silicon spin qubits use the spin direction of electrons confined in quantum dots, and photonic approaches use properties of photons such as polarization or path. In every platform, performance is set not only by the number of qubits but also by gate error rates, coherence times and how the qubits are connected. In error correction, many physical qubits are combined into one logical qubit.

A qubit is in the state (√3/2)|0⟩ + (1/2)|1⟩. What is the probability of getting 1 when it is measured?

  1. A25%
  2. B50%
  3. C75%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 25%

The probability of getting 1 is the squared magnitude of the amplitude of |1⟩: (1/2)² = 1/4 = 25%. The probability of getting 0 is (√3/2)² = 3/4 = 75%, and the two add up to 100%. Taking the amplitude 1/2 itself as the probability leads to the wrong answer of 50%.

FAQ

How many bits of information can be extracted from n qubits?

At most n bits of classical information can be obtained by measurement. Even though the qubits hold 2ⁿ amplitudes, they cannot all be read out. The speed of a quantum computer comes not from reading out more information but from using interference to reduce the number of steps needed to reach the answer.

What is the difference between a physical qubit and a logical qubit?

A physical qubit is an individual qubit actually built into a device. A logical qubit is a single, more error-resistant qubit made by combining many physical qubits with quantum error correction. With the surface code, one logical qubit can require several hundred or more physical qubits.
