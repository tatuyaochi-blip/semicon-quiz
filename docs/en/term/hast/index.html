What is HAST (highly accelerated stress test)?

A test that applies bias in pressurized steam at high humidity and temperatures above 100 °C to evaluate moisture resistance quickly. For example, 96 hours at 130 °C/85% RH is treated as equivalent to 1000 hours of temperature-humidity-bias testing (THB) at 85 °C/85% RH.

Japanese name: HAST(高度加速ストレス試験)

How HAST speeds up moisture failures

HAST places packaged devices in a sealed pressure vessel filled with unsaturated steam, typically at 110 to 130 °C and 85% relative humidity (RH). Because the water vapor pressure at these temperatures is roughly two to five times that at 85 °C, moisture diffuses through the plastic mold compound and along interfaces to the die much faster. Electrical bias is applied during the test so that, once moisture and ionic contaminants arrive, electrochemical reactions proceed. Common conditions are 96 hours at 130 °C/85% RH, used as a substitute for 1000 hours of temperature-humidity-bias testing (THB) at 85 °C/85% RH.

Failure mechanisms it targets

The main targets are corrosion of aluminum bond pads and metal lines, especially when chloride or other ionic contaminants are present; ion migration that grows conductive paths between biased conductors; and rising leakage currents across surfaces and passivation defects. Delamination between mold compound and die or lead frame can open paths that let moisture in faster. Bias is usually arranged to put voltage between adjacent pins while keeping power dissipation low, since self-heating would dry out the die surface and hide humidity-driven failures.

HAST vs. THB, unbiased HAST and autoclave

THB uses the same 85% RH at 85 °C and atmospheric pressure, so it needs about 1000 hours to reach similar damage; HAST trades that time for a pressure chamber. Unbiased HAST uses the same temperature and humidity without bias, focusing on purely chemical corrosion and package integrity. The autoclave, or pressure cooker test, uses saturated steam at 100% RH, commonly 121 °C, also without bias, and is harsher on packaging materials. Results from these tests are not interchangeable, because condensation and bias change which mechanisms dominate.

Converting results to field life

Acceleration is commonly estimated with Peck's model, which multiplies a humidity term, relative humidity to the power of minus n with n around 3, by an Arrhenius temperature term. Using it, a qualification test can be mapped to an expected lifetime in a given climate. The conversion has limits: if the test temperature is so high that the mold compound's properties change markedly, or condensation forms, the test can trigger failure modes that would never occur in service. Test conditions are therefore chosen within ranges established by industry standards such as those from JEDEC.

Answer: A. It accelerates failures such as corrosion caused by moisture ingress, shortening a 1000-hour test to around 100 hours

HAST (Highly Accelerated Stress Test) is a method that tests at temperatures above 100 °C under pressure while maintaining high humidity. Raising the temperature and water vapor pressure speeds the movement of moisture through the package mold compound to the chip, accelerating electrode corrosion, increased leakage current and similar failures. For example, it is common to run about 96 hours at 130 °C and 85% RH as an evaluation equivalent to 1000 hours at 85 °C and 85% RH. It has nothing to do with evaluating soft errors or dry conditions.

Which failures are mainly evaluated by temperature-humidity-bias tests (THB and HAST)?

  1. ASoft errors caused by cosmic rays
  2. BClock delay
  3. CElectrode corrosion and increased leakage current due to moisture ingress
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Electrode corrosion and increased leakage current due to moisture ingress

Applying bias in hot, humid conditions lets moisture and ionic impurities that penetrate the package corrode aluminum pads and drive ion migration, increasing leakage or causing opens. THB runs at 85 °C/85% RH for about 1,000 hours, while HAST uses pressurized steam around 130 °C to accelerate moisture ingress, evaluating humidity resistance in roughly 100 hours. Soft errors and clock delays are targets of other tests.

FAQ

What is the difference between HAST and THB?

Both apply bias in 85% relative humidity, but THB runs at 85 °C and atmospheric pressure for about 1000 hours, while HAST uses pressurized steam at around 110 to 130 °C to reach a comparable result in about 100 to 300 hours.

Why is bias applied during HAST?

Voltage between conductors drives the electrochemical reactions behind corrosion and ion migration once moisture reaches the die. Without bias the test mainly checks chemical attack and package integrity, which is what unbiased HAST does.

Is HAST the same as a pressure cooker test?

No. HAST uses unsaturated steam, typically 85% RH, and usually bias, while the pressure cooker or autoclave test uses saturated steam at 100% RH, commonly at 121 °C, without bias.
