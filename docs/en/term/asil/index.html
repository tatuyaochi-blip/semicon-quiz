What is ASIL (Automotive Safety Integrity Level)?

In ISO 26262, the level of safety requirements assigned to each hazardous event from three factors: severity S, exposure E and controllability C. There are four levels, A to D, with D the most stringent; low-risk items that need no ASIL are classed as QM.

Japanese name: ASIL(自動車安全度水準)

How an ASIL is assigned

During hazard analysis and risk assessment (HARA), each hazardous event is rated for severity of injury (S1–S3), exposure to the driving situation in which it can occur (E1–E4) and controllability by the driver or others (C1–C3). A table in ISO 26262 maps the combination to ASIL A, B, C or D, or to QM (Quality Management) when no ASIL is needed. A handy rule: S + E + C equal to 10 gives D, 9 gives C, 8 gives B and 7 gives A. Only S3/E4/C3 reaches D, and lowering any one factor by a step lowers the ASIL by one.

What the letter changes

The ASIL attaches to the safety goal and is inherited by the requirements derived from it, then by the hardware and software that implement them. A higher ASIL demands more rigorous development and verification and tighter hardware metrics. Target guidelines for the single-point fault metric (SPFM) are 90% for B, 97% for C and 99% for D; for the latent fault metric (LFM), 60%, 80% and 90%. The probabilistic metric for random hardware failures (PMHF) target is below 100 FIT for B and C and below 10 FIT, or 10⁻⁸ per hour, for D. QM still requires normal quality work.

A worked metric example

SPFM is one minus the fraction of safety-related failure rate that is single-point or residual, meaning faults that by themselves violate a safety goal or slip past safety mechanisms undetected. If a block has 1000 FIT of safety-related failures and 5 FIT of them are single-point or residual, SPFM = 1 − 5/1000 = 99.5%, which meets the ASIL D target. Improving it means adding safety mechanisms with higher diagnostic coverage, such as ECC on memories, lockstep cores or monitoring circuits, so that fewer faults remain undetected.

Decomposition and mixed-criticality designs

A requirement can be split across sufficiently independent redundant elements, written with the original level in parentheses: ASIL D may become B(D) + B(D), C(D) + A(D), or D(D) + QM(D). Dependent failure analysis must show the two cannot fail together from a common cause, and the random hardware metrics still follow the original ASIL. When software of different ASILs shares one SoC, freedom from interference must be shown with spatial partitioning, such as memory protection units or a hypervisor, and temporal partitioning by execution-time monitoring; otherwise everything must be developed to the highest ASIL.

Answer: A. Severity (S), exposure (E), controllability (C)

The ASIL is determined for each hazardous event by evaluating three factors: severity S, representing how serious the injuries are; exposure E, representing the frequency or duration of exposure to driving situations in which the hazard can occur; and controllability C, representing how likely the driver or others are to avoid the hazard. For example, sudden loss of braking at high speed has high severity and is hard to avoid, so it receives a high ASIL. Failure rate, diagnostic coverage and FTTI are quantities used after the ASIL is determined, to evaluate whether a design meets its requirements.

Answer: B. It ranges from A to D, with ASIL D representing the most stringent requirements. When the risk is sufficiently low, the classification is QM and it is handled by ordinary quality management

ASIL (Automotive Safety Integrity Level) has four levels, A, B, C and D, with D the most stringent, imposing high requirements on development processes, safety mechanisms, failure rate targets and so on. For example, parts of airbag, brake and steering control are typical cases that often require ASIL D. When the risk is low and no ASIL needs to be assigned, the classification is QM (Quality Management) and development is carried out under ordinary quality management. Quality still has to be assured under QM, and testing cannot be skipped.

FAQ

Which ASIL is the most stringent?

ASIL D is the highest and ASIL A the lowest; QM means the risk is low enough that ordinary quality management suffices. Parts of brake, steering and airbag control are typical ASIL D examples.

What does ASIL B(D) mean?

It marks a requirement developed to ASIL B as a result of decomposing an original ASIL D requirement across two independent redundant elements. The parentheses keep the original level visible because some obligations, such as hardware metric targets, still follow ASIL D.

Is ASIL the same as SIL?

No. SIL levels 1–4 come from the generic standard IEC 61508, while ASIL A–D are defined by ISO 26262 for road vehicles using severity, exposure and controllability. They are related in spirit but not formally interchangeable.
