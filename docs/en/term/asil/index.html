What is ASIL (Automotive Safety Integrity Level)?

In ISO 26262, the level of safety requirements assigned to each hazardous event from three factors: severity S, exposure E and controllability C. There are four levels, A to D, with D the most stringent; low-risk items that need no ASIL are classed as QM.

Japanese name: ASIL(自動車安全度水準)

In ISO 26262, which correctly lists the three factors evaluated when determining the ASIL through hazard analysis and risk assessment (HARA)?

  1. ASeverity (S), exposure (E), controllability (C)
  2. BFailure rate (λ), diagnostic coverage (DC), fault tolerant time interval (FTTI)
  3. CComponent price, production volume, development period
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Severity (S), exposure (E), controllability (C)

The ASIL is determined for each hazardous event by evaluating three factors: severity S, representing how serious the injuries are; exposure E, representing the frequency or duration of exposure to driving situations in which the hazard can occur; and controllability C, representing how likely the driver or others are to avoid the hazard. For example, sudden loss of braking at high speed has high severity and is hard to avoid, so it receives a high ASIL. Failure rate, diagnostic coverage and FTTI are quantities used after the ASIL is determined, to evaluate whether a design meets its requirements.

Which correctly describes the ASIL in ISO 26262?

  1. AIt ranges from A to D, with ASIL A representing the most stringent requirements. QM represents even more stringent requirements than ASIL D
  2. BIt ranges from A to D, with ASIL D representing the most stringent requirements. When the risk is sufficiently low, the classification is QM and it is handled by ordinary quality management
  3. CIt ranges from A to D, with ASIL D representing the components with the highest failure rate. QM represents components with no quality issues, for which all testing may be skipped
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It ranges from A to D, with ASIL D representing the most stringent requirements. When the risk is sufficiently low, the classification is QM and it is handled by ordinary quality management

ASIL (Automotive Safety Integrity Level) has four levels, A, B, C and D, with D the most stringent, imposing high requirements on development processes, safety mechanisms, failure rate targets and so on. For example, parts of airbag, brake and steering control are typical cases that often require ASIL D. When the risk is low and no ASIL needs to be assigned, the classification is QM (Quality Management) and development is carried out under ordinary quality management. Quality still has to be assured under QM, and testing cannot be skipped.
