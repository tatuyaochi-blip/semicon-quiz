What is STT-MRAM?

MRAM that writes by flipping the magnetization of the MTJ's free layer using the spin-transfer torque of a spin-polarized current. It is in volume production as embedded non-volatile memory.

Japanese name: STT-MRAM

Try it: questions about STT-MRAM

How is information written in STT-MRAM?

  1. AOnly with a magnetic field generated by an external coil
  2. BBy irradiating it with ultraviolet light
  3. CBy reversing the magnetization with spin-transfer torque from a spin-polarized current
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. By reversing the magnetization with spin-transfer torque from a spin-polarized current

In STT-MRAM, current flows through the MTJ, and electrons spin-polarized by the reference layer transfer angular momentum to the free layer (spin-transfer torque), flipping its magnetization; the current direction determines 0 or 1. Early field-written MRAM needed more current as cells shrank, but STT write current scales down with cell area, so it suits scaling and is now mainstream.

Why have MTJs with magnetization perpendicular to the film plane (perpendicular MTJs) become mainstream in recent STT-MRAM?

  1. ABecause no magnetic materials are needed to manufacture them
  2. BBecause they maintain thermal stability more easily when scaled and allow lower write currents
  3. CBecause light can be used for reading
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Because they maintain thermal stability more easily when scaled and allow lower write currents

In-plane MTJs rely on cell shape, such as an ellipse, to stabilize magnetization, so stability is lost as they shrink. Perpendicular MTJs use perpendicular magnetic anisotropy arising at interfaces such as CoFeB/MgO, keeping even small round cells thermally stable while lowering write current. This materials advance made volume production of STT-MRAM possible in the late 2010s.
