What is MEMS?

Micro-electro-mechanical systems. Devices that integrate tiny mechanical structures and electronic circuits using semiconductor microfabrication.

Japanese name: MEMS

MEMS appears in 45 practice questions across 9 topics, including MEMS and sensors, Etching, Packaging and assembly.

Field: MEMS and sensors

How MEMS structures are made

MEMS (micro-electro-mechanical systems) borrow IC fabrication but add steps that free parts to move. Surface micromachining stacks a sacrificial film such as SiO2 under a structural film such as LPCVD polysilicon, then etches the sacrificial layer away to leave beams suspended a few µm above the substrate. Bulk micromachining carves the wafer itself, using KOH etching, which stops on (111) planes at about 54.7°, or deep RIE (the Bosch process) for vertical trenches with aspect ratios above 20. SOI wafers, anodic bonding and wafer-level vacuum sealing complete many devices.

Sensing and actuation principles

Most MEMS sensors turn motion into a capacitance change. In an accelerometer a spring-suspended proof mass shifts by nanometers or less, changing comb-electrode capacitance by under a femtofarad. A gyroscope vibrates a mass at several to tens of kHz and senses the Coriolis displacement caused by rotation, usually in vacuum to raise Q. Resonant frequency is f = (1/2π)√(k/m), so stiffer springs raise bandwidth but lower sensitivity. Electrostatic actuators produce force proportional to V²/d², and parallel plates snap together (pull-in) after travelling about one third of the gap.

Physics at the micrometer scale

With structures from micrometers to millimeters, surface forces dominate over mass. Capillary forces during release drying, van der Waals forces and humidity can pin parts to the substrate, called stiction, prevented by vapor-phase HF release, supercritical drying, hydrophobic coatings and dimples. Gas trapped in narrow gaps causes squeeze-film damping, which scales inversely with the cube of the gap and is useful in accelerometers but harmful in resonators. Thermomechanical (Brownian) noise grows as the proof mass shrinks, limiting the resolution of very small sensors.

Where you will meet MEMS

A smartphone contains several MEMS devices: accelerometers, gyroscopes, pressure sensors precise enough to resolve a change of one building floor, and microphones. Cars use MEMS accelerometers to trigger airbags and gyroscopes for stability control. Other examples include inkjet print heads, scanning mirrors for LiDAR and projection, silicon oscillators replacing quartz, RF switches, uncooled infrared microbolometers, ultrasonic transducers and MEMS probe cards for wafer test. MEMS are a typical More than Moore technology, often built on 200 mm or smaller wafers with mature equipment.

Answer: A. Devices that integrate tiny mechanical structures with electronic circuits using semiconductor microfabrication

MEMS (micro-electro-mechanical systems) uses semiconductor deposition, lithography and etching to build tiny mechanical structures with moving parts—springs, proof masses, membranes, mirrors—that act as sensors or actuators. Batch fabrication on wafers makes them small and cheap, and a single smartphone contains several, including accelerometers, gyroscopes, pressure sensors and microphones.

What is the principle by which a capacitive MEMS accelerometer detects acceleration?

  1. AChange in resistance due to temperature change
  2. BChange in capacitance between electrodes caused by displacement of a movable mass
  3. CChange in optical interference fringes
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Change in capacitance between electrodes caused by displacement of a movable mass

Under acceleration, a spring-suspended proof mass moves by inertia, changing its gap to comb-shaped fixed electrodes; the resulting capacitance change is measured to determine acceleration. Displacements are nanometers or less and capacitance changes below a femtofarad, read with differential electrodes and sensitive circuits. Simple, low-power and temperature-stable, capacitive accelerometers are ubiquitous in smartphones.

FAQ

What does MEMS stand for?

MEMS stands for micro-electro-mechanical systems: tiny mechanical structures such as springs, membranes and mirrors built with semiconductor microfabrication and combined with electronics. Even smaller nanometer-scale mechanical devices are called NEMS.

What is stiction in MEMS?

Stiction is the permanent sticking of a movable MEMS part to the substrate or another surface, caused by capillary, electrostatic or van der Waals forces. It is fought with liquid-free release methods, anti-stiction coatings and small contact bumps.

How does a MEMS accelerometer measure acceleration?

A proof mass on springs lags behind when the package accelerates, changing its gap to fixed electrodes. The resulting capacitance change is converted to an acceleration reading, and at rest the sensor reads about 1 g because of gravity.
