What is Underfill?

Resin filled into the gap between the chip and the substrate in flip-chip assembly. It spreads out stress from thermal expansion mismatch and protects the bump joints.

Japanese name: アンダーフィル

Underfill appears in 4 practice questions across 3 topics, including Advanced packaging (3D and chiplets), Packaging and assembly, Reliability.

Field: Packaging and assembly

Why flip chips need underfill

Silicon expands at about 3 ppm/K, while organic package substrates expand at about 15 to 17 ppm/K. In a flip-chip assembly the solder or copper-pillar bumps are the only link between the two, so every temperature swing shears them. The mismatch displacement grows with distance from the chip center, so corner bumps are strained most and tend to fail first by fatigue cracking. Underfill bonds the chip to the substrate across the whole gap, so the pair flexes together and the strain is carried by the resin rather than concentrated in a few joints, extending thermal-cycling life many times over.

How underfill is applied

In the classic capillary process, the chip is first reflowed onto the substrate, then a low-viscosity epoxy loaded with fine silica particles is dispensed along one or two die edges. Capillary action draws it through the gap between the bumps, after which it is cured. The silica filler lowers the resin's expansion coefficient and stiffens it. Flow needs adequate standoff height, which is one reason copper pillars, which keep their height during reflow, help at fine pitch. Alternatives include no-flow underfill applied before placement, pre-applied non-conductive film for thermocompression bonding, and molded underfill formed during encapsulation.

Failure modes and trade-offs

Underfill only helps if it fills completely and stays attached. Voids left by trapped air, filler settling, or delamination at the chip or substrate interface, often driven by absorbed moisture during reflow, can leave bumps unsupported and become crack starters. A cured underfill also makes rework nearly impossible, so a faulty die usually means scrapping the package. Material choice balances flow speed, cure temperature, modulus, expansion coefficient and adhesion against package warpage.

Board-level use and related materials

Underfill is also applied between BGA or chip-scale packages and the circuit board in handheld products, where drop impact and board bending can crack solder joints or the brittle intermetallic layer at the pad. It should not be confused with mold compound, which encapsulates the whole package, or with thermal interface material (TIM), which fills the gap between the die back and a heat spreader to conduct heat rather than to hold joints together.

FAQ

What is underfill used for in flip-chip packages?

It fills the gap between chip and substrate so that stress from their different thermal expansion is shared by the resin instead of concentrating on the bumps. This greatly improves solder-joint fatigue life under temperature cycling.

Is underfill used on BGAs on circuit boards?

Yes. Phones and other handheld devices often underfill BGA and chip-scale packages on the board to help solder joints survive drop impact and board bending.

Can a flip chip with underfill be reworked?

Generally not in practice. Cured underfill bonds the chip firmly to the substrate, so removing and replacing the die is very difficult, which is why assemblies are tested before underfill when possible.
