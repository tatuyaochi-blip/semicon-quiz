What is GaAs (gallium arsenide)?

A III-V compound semiconductor of gallium and arsenic with a direct band gap of about 1.42 eV. Its high electron mobility makes it useful for high-frequency transistors and infrared LEDs and lasers.

Japanese name: GaAs(ガリウムヒ素)

What type of band structure does gallium arsenide (GaAs) have?

  1. ADirect band gap
  2. BIndirect band gap
  3. CNo band gap
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Direct band gap

GaAs is a direct-gap semiconductor: its conduction-band minimum and valence-band maximum coincide in momentum space, so electrons and holes recombine radiatively without phonons and emit light efficiently. It is used in infrared LEDs and lasers (optical discs, communications, VCSELs for face authentication). Silicon's indirect gap requires phonons, making emission very inefficient. GaAs also absorbs strongly, so thin layers suffice in high-efficiency solar cells.

What is the main reason GaAs has been used in RF amplifiers for mobile phones and similar applications?

  1. AIts wafers are cheap and easy to make in large diameters
  2. BIt readily forms a high-quality thermal oxide
  3. CIts electron mobility is higher than Si's
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Its electron mobility is higher than Si's

GaAs has an electron mobility of about 8500 cm²/V·s—roughly six times silicon's—and a small effective mass, so it operates well at high frequencies and has served in phone power amplifiers and switches and satellite receivers. Its semi-insulating substrate also minimizes parasitic capacitance. But GaAs wafers are costlier and harder to scale up, and it lacks a good native oxide, making it unsuitable for large digital circuits.
