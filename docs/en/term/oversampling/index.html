What is Oversampling?

Sampling at a rate well above what the signal requires (twice its bandwidth). It spreads quantization noise over a wider band and reduces noise in the signal band; it is the basis of delta-sigma ADCs.

Japanese name: オーバーサンプリング

Field: A/D and D/A conversion

Why sampling faster lowers in-band noise

Quantization adds an error whose total power is fixed by the step size, about one step squared over twelve, and is roughly spread evenly from DC to half the sampling frequency. The oversampling ratio (OSR) is the sampling rate divided by twice the signal bandwidth. Raising the OSR spreads the same noise over a wider band, so the portion inside the signal band falls by 10 log10(OSR) dB, and a digital filter removes the rest. Quadrupling the rate gives about 6 dB, or one extra bit. This assumes the error is noise-like rather than correlated with the signal, which sometimes requires added dither.

Noise shaping in delta-sigma converters

Plain oversampling is inefficient: one extra bit costs a fourfold rate increase and two bits cost sixteenfold. A delta-sigma (ΔΣ) modulator adds a feedback loop with integrators that pushes quantization noise toward high frequencies, away from the signal band. For a modulator of order L, each doubling of the OSR improves in-band signal-to-noise ratio by about 6L + 3 dB, roughly 9 dB for first order and 15 dB for second order. With OSRs in the tens to hundreds, even a 1-bit quantizer can deliver more than 20 bits of resolution at low frequencies, with little dependence on analog component matching.

Decimation and the anti-aliasing filter

An oversampling converter does not deliver its raw samples. A digital decimation filter removes the noise above the signal band and reduces the output rate to roughly the Nyquist rate, turning many coarse samples into fewer high-resolution ones. This filter accounts for the latency of ΔΣ converters, which makes them unsuitable for fast control loops and multiplexed channels. A side benefit is on the analog side: because the sampling rate is far above the signal band, frequencies that would alias are far away, so the analog anti-aliasing filter can be a simple, gentle one.

Oversampling vs. Nyquist-rate ADCs

Nyquist-rate converters such as flash, pipelined and successive-approximation register (SAR) ADCs sample at about twice the signal bandwidth and convert each sample independently. A SAR ADC, for instance, resolves one bit per comparison, giving 8 to 18 bits at rates from kilosamples to tens of megasamples per second, which suits microcontrollers. Oversampling converters trade conversion speed for resolution and are found in audio, precision instrumentation and sensor readout. The same principle appears in software too: averaging many samples from a Nyquist-rate ADC and decimating can add resolution, provided enough noise is present to act as dither.

Answer: B. About 6 dB (about 1 bit)

Total quantization noise is fixed, so raising the sampling rate spreads it thinly over a wider band. In-band noise falls by 10 log₁₀(4) ≈ 6 dB, about one bit. Plain oversampling is therefore inefficient: one extra bit needs 4× the rate, and two bits need 16×. ΔΣ converters add noise shaping to obtain far greater improvement.

Which ADC architecture is classified as oversampling?

  1. AFlash
  2. BDelta-sigma
  3. CSuccessive approximation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Delta-sigma

ADCs are classed as Nyquist-rate converters, which sample at about twice the signal bandwidth (flash, SAR, pipelined, etc.), and oversampling converters, which sample far faster. The ΔΣ ADC is the classic oversampling type, with oversampling ratios (OSR) typically in the tens to hundreds. Nyquist-rate converters convert each sample independently, which also suits multiplexing many channels.

FAQ

How much does oversampling improve ADC resolution?

Without noise shaping, each doubling of the sampling rate lowers in-band quantization noise by about 3 dB, so a fourfold increase gains roughly 6 dB, or one bit. Delta-sigma converters add noise shaping to gain much more per doubling.

What is the difference between oversampling and noise shaping?

Oversampling spreads a fixed amount of quantization noise over a wider band so less of it falls in the signal band. Noise shaping uses feedback to move the noise itself toward high frequencies, multiplying the benefit of oversampling.

What is the oversampling ratio?

The oversampling ratio is the sampling frequency divided by the Nyquist rate, that is, twice the signal bandwidth. Delta-sigma ADCs typically use ratios from tens to hundreds.
