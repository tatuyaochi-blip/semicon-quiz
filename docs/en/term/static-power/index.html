What is Static power?

Power consumed continuously by leakage currents through off transistors even when signals do not switch, also called leakage power. It rises steeply with temperature.

Japanese name: 静的消費電力

Try it: questions about Static power

Why is the static (steady-state) power consumption of a CMOS inverter small?

  1. ABecause the supply voltage is high
  2. BBecause current always flows into the gate
  3. CBecause whether the input is 0 or 1, either the pMOS or the nMOS is off, so almost no current flows from supply to ground
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Because whether the input is 0 or 1, either the pMOS or the nMOS is off, so almost no current flows from supply to ground

In a CMOS inverter, input 0 turns the pMOS on and nMOS off, and input 1 does the reverse, so in steady state there is always an off transistor between supply and ground and almost no through current flows. Power is consumed mainly by charging and discharging load capacitance during switching: P = α·C·V²·f. With scaling, however, off-state leakage has grown, and static power is no longer negligible in advanced chips.

Assume DVFS can lower the supply voltage in proportion to frequency. When frequency is halved, roughly what happens to dynamic power and to the dynamic energy for a fixed amount of computation?

  1. APower falls to one-eighth and energy to one-quarter
  2. BBoth power and energy halve
  3. CPower falls to a quarter and energy is unchanged
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Power falls to one-eighth and energy to one-quarter

Power is proportional to V²f, so halving both V and f gives (1/2)² × 1/2 = 1/8. The computation takes twice as long, so energy is 1/8 × 2 = 1/4. Equivalently, since energy per switching event is proportional to CV², energy scales with the square of voltage. In practice the voltage range is limited and static power exists, so the reduction is not this ideal.
