What is Static power?

Power consumed continuously by leakage currents through off transistors even when signals do not switch, also called leakage power. It rises steeply with temperature.

Japanese name: 静的消費電力

Static power appears in 3 practice questions across 2 topics, including Power Management, MOSFET.

Field: Power Management

Where leakage current comes from

An ideal CMOS gate draws no current at rest, because one transistor between the supply and ground is always off. Real transistors leak. The largest component is usually subthreshold leakage: below threshold, drain current falls exponentially with gate voltage, at best by a factor of ten for every 60 mV or so at room temperature, so lowering the threshold voltage for speed raises off-state current sharply. Gate leakage tunnels through very thin gate dielectrics and was reduced by switching to high-k materials, and junction leakage and gate-induced drain leakage add smaller contributions.

Why it rises steeply with temperature

Higher temperature increases the thermal energy of carriers, so more of them surmount the source barrier, and it also lowers the threshold voltage. Both effects multiply subthreshold leakage, so a chip running hot can leak several times more than the same chip when cool. This creates a feedback loop: leakage generates heat, and heat raises leakage. Power budgets and thermal design must therefore be checked at the worst-case junction temperature, and in extreme cases the loop can lead to thermal runaway if cooling is insufficient.

Static power vs. dynamic power

Dynamic power is spent charging and discharging capacitance when signals switch, P = α·C·V²·f, so it falls when activity or clock frequency falls and responds strongly to voltage. If dynamic voltage and frequency scaling (DVFS) halves both voltage and frequency, dynamic power drops to one-eighth and energy for a fixed task to one-quarter. Static power behaves differently: it flows whenever the circuit is powered, even with the clock stopped, and since a slowed task runs longer, leakage energy grows. In scaled technologies with billions of transistors, static power is a substantial fraction of the total, especially in standby.

How designers reduce it

Because clock gating cannot stop leakage, the most effective tool is power gating: header or footer sleep transistors disconnect idle blocks from the supply entirely, with retention registers or memories keeping essential state. Multi-threshold libraries place high-threshold, low-leakage cells on non-critical paths and reserve fast low-threshold cells for critical ones. Reverse body biasing raises the threshold during idle periods, and lowering the supply in standby reduces leakage further. At the device level, FinFETs and gate-all-around transistors cut off-state leakage through stronger gate control and a steeper subthreshold slope than late planar devices.

  1. ABecause the supply voltage is high
  2. BBecause current always flows into the gate
  3. CBecause whether the input is 0 or 1, either the pMOS or the nMOS is off, so almost no current flows from supply to ground
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Because whether the input is 0 or 1, either the pMOS or the nMOS is off, so almost no current flows from supply to ground

In a CMOS inverter, input 0 turns the pMOS on and nMOS off, and input 1 does the reverse, so in steady state there is always an off transistor between supply and ground and almost no through current flows. Power is consumed mainly by charging and discharging load capacitance during switching: P = α·C·V²·f. With scaling, however, off-state leakage has grown, and static power is no longer negligible in advanced chips.

Assume DVFS can lower the supply voltage in proportion to frequency. When frequency is halved, roughly what happens to dynamic power and to the dynamic energy for a fixed amount of computation?

  1. APower falls to one-eighth and energy to one-quarter
  2. BBoth power and energy halve
  3. CPower falls to a quarter and energy is unchanged
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Power falls to one-eighth and energy to one-quarter

Power is proportional to V²f, so halving both V and f gives (1/2)² × 1/2 = 1/8. The computation takes twice as long, so energy is 1/8 × 2 = 1/4. Equivalently, since energy per switching event is proportional to CV², energy scales with the square of voltage. In practice the voltage range is limited and static power exists, so the reduction is not this ideal.

FAQ

What is the difference between static and dynamic power?

Dynamic power is consumed when signals switch and charge or discharge capacitance, so it depends on activity, frequency and voltage squared. Static power is the leakage current through transistors that are nominally off and flows continuously whenever the circuit is powered.

Why does leakage power increase with temperature?

Higher temperature lets more carriers overcome the source barrier and also lowers the threshold voltage, both of which raise subthreshold leakage exponentially. As a result, leakage can multiply between a cool and a hot chip.

Does clock gating reduce static power?

No. Clock gating stops switching and so saves dynamic power, but leakage continues as long as the supply is connected; power gating is needed to remove static power from an idle block.
