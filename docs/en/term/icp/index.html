What is ICP (inductively coupled plasma)?

A method that generates a high-density plasma with RF current in a coil. It makes it easy to control plasma density and the energy of ions reaching the wafer independently.

Japanese name: ICP(誘導結合プラズマ)

Field: Etching

How an ICP source works

An inductively coupled plasma (ICP) source drives RF current, commonly at 13.56 MHz, through a flat spiral or cylindrical coil placed outside a dielectric window of quartz or ceramic. The coil's oscillating magnetic field induces a circulating electric field inside the chamber, which accelerates electrons and keeps the gas ionized. Because power is transferred to the electrons inductively instead of through a high-voltage sheath at an electrode, the discharge reaches densities of about 10¹¹–10¹² cm⁻³ while operating at low pressures of a few pascals or less.

Separate knobs for density and ion energy

An ICP etcher uses two RF supplies. Source power to the coil sets plasma density, and therefore the flux of ions and radicals. A separate bias supply on the wafer chuck sets the DC self-bias and therefore the energy of ions striking the wafer. Engineers can etch fast with low ion energy to protect the underlayer and limit damage, or raise bias for more vertical profiles. Low pressure gives a long mean free path, so ions cross the sheath with few collisions and arrive nearly perpendicular, which supports anisotropic etching.

ICP vs. CCP

In a capacitively coupled plasma (CCP), RF power is applied between parallel-plate electrodes, so the same power that sustains the plasma also sets the sheath voltage; density and ion energy rise together. CCP densities are typically around 10⁹–10¹⁰ cm⁻³ with higher ion energies, and CCP tools, often with two frequencies to partly decouple the two, are widely used for oxide and nitride etching with fluorocarbon gases. ICP tools are favored for silicon, polysilicon gates, metals and compound semiconductors, where high radical flux and modest ion energy are wanted.

Where you will meet it

In fabs, ICP etchers handle shallow trench isolation, gate and fin etching, and metal lines. For GaN, chlorine-based ICP plasmas (Cl2, BCl3) etch LED and laser mesas and HEMT gate recesses at low ion energy to keep damage from degrading devices. High-density plasma CVD for gap fill also uses inductive sources. Outside processing, the same principle appears in ICP-MS, where an atmospheric-pressure argon plasma ionizes samples for trace-metal analysis, for example of droplets collected from wafer surfaces by vapor phase decomposition (VPD).

Answer: B. It generates high-density plasma with a coil, and plasma density and ion energy are easy to control separately

An ICP etcher drives RF current through a coil above or around the chamber to induce an electric field that sustains a dense plasma of 10¹¹–10¹² cm⁻³, while a separate RF supply biases the wafer. Plasma density (radical and ion flux) and ion energy at the wafer can be set independently, enabling fast, highly anisotropic etching at pressures of a few Pa or less; ICP tools are widely used for silicon and metal etching.

FAQ

What is the difference between ICP and CCP etching?

ICP sustains the plasma with an inductive coil and biases the wafer with a separate RF supply, so plasma density and ion energy can be set independently and density is high. CCP drives parallel-plate electrodes, so density and ion energy are coupled and density is lower, which suits energetic dielectric etching.

What plasma density does an ICP etcher reach?

Typical ICP etch plasmas reach about 10¹¹–10¹² cm⁻³ at pressures of a few pascals or below, roughly one to two orders of magnitude denser than a conventional capacitively coupled plasma.

Is the ICP in ICP-MS the same as in ICP etching?

Both use an inductively coupled plasma, but ICP-MS runs an atmospheric-pressure argon plasma to ionize a liquid sample for mass spectrometry, while an ICP etcher runs a low-pressure reactive plasma to remove material from a wafer.
