What is Mass flow controller (MFC)?

A device in deposition and etch tools that precisely measures the mass flow of a gas and holds it at a set value.

Japanese name: マスフローコントローラ(MFC)

How a thermal MFC measures flow

The most common type splits the incoming gas: a fixed small fraction passes through a narrow heated sensor tube, while the rest goes through a bypass element designed to keep the split ratio constant. Temperature sensors upstream and downstream on the sensor tube detect how much heat the flowing gas carries downstream. That temperature difference is proportional to mass flow times the gas's specific heat. Electronics compare the measured flow with the setpoint and drive a proportional control valve, usually solenoid or piezoelectric, to close the loop, typically settling within about a second.

Why mass flow and what sccm means

Reaction rates depend on how many molecules arrive per second, not on volume at whatever pressure and temperature the line happens to have. Measuring mass flow makes the delivered amount insensitive to such changes. Flow is usually expressed in sccm, standard cubic centimeters per minute, meaning the volume the gas would occupy at a reference temperature and pressure, commonly 0 °C and 1 atm, so 1 sccm corresponds to a fixed number of molecules per minute. Larger flows use slm, standard liters per minute.

Gas correction and range limits

Because a thermal sensor responds to heat capacity, an MFC calibrated with nitrogen reads incorrectly for other gases unless a gas conversion factor is applied, and the factors are less accurate for reactive or unusual gases and mixtures, so process MFCs are often calibrated for the actual gas. Accuracy also degrades near the bottom of the full-scale range. That is why tiny dopant flows such as diborane and phosphine are diluted to percent or ppm levels in hydrogen or nitrogen, letting the MFC operate in its accurate range while also reducing leak hazards.

Where you will meet it

Every gas line on CVD, ALD, etch, epitaxy and diffusion tools has its own MFC, because deposition rate, film composition, uniformity and etch selectivity depend directly on flow ratios. Pressure-based MFCs, which infer flow from pressure across a calibrated orifice, are also used where fast response or insensitivity to gas type matters. Do not confuse an MFC with a mass flow meter, which only measures, or with the chamber pressure controller, which sets pressure with a throttle valve while the MFCs set gas flow.

Answer: B. Because only tiny amounts are needed, dilution makes the flow easier to control precisely and lowers toxicity and reactivity risks

Dopant quantities are tiny, so pure gas would require flows too small for a mass flow controller to control precisely. Diluting to a few percent or ppm levels in hydrogen or nitrogen lets the required amount be delivered accurately at manageable flows. Dilution also lowers concentrations in a leak, reducing toxicity and ignition risk. Pure diborane slowly decomposes even at room temperature, so dilution aids stability too.

FAQ

How does a thermal mass flow controller work?

A small fraction of the gas flows through a heated sensor tube, and the temperature difference between upstream and downstream sensors gives the mass flow. A control valve then adjusts the flow to match the setpoint.

What does sccm mean?

Standard cubic centimeters per minute: the gas flow expressed as the volume it would occupy at reference conditions, commonly 0 °C and 1 atm, which makes it a measure of molecules delivered per minute.

Why do MFCs need a gas correction factor?

A thermal MFC responds to how much heat the gas carries, which depends on its heat capacity, so a unit calibrated on nitrogen must be corrected when used with a different gas.
