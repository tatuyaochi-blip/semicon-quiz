What is Dry etching?

Removing material with plasma or gas instead of liquid chemicals. Combining ion bombardment allows anisotropic etching, which is essential for fine patterns.

Japanese name: ドライエッチング

Field: Etching

What does the etching in a plasma

Etch plasmas are weakly ionized, typically about 0.01% or less, and contain electrons, positive ions, highly reactive radicals and neutral molecules. Radicals supply the chemistry and ions supply directional energy. Etching works only if the reaction product is volatile: silicon leaves as SiF4, SiCl4 or SiBr4, silicon dioxide etched with fluorocarbon gases such as CF4 or C4F8 leaves as SiF4 plus CO or CO2, and GaN etched with Cl2 and BCl3 leaves as GaCl3. Copper chlorides are not volatile at normal process temperatures, which is why copper lines are formed by damascene instead.

Where anisotropy comes from

Ions and radicals together etch much faster than either alone; in a classic experiment, XeF2 gas plus an argon ion beam etched silicon about ten times faster than the sum of the two separately. Because ions arrive vertically, feature bottoms etch quickly while sidewalls do not. In addition, polymer from fluorocarbon gases or HBr-derived byproducts coats the sidewalls and blocks lateral attack, while ion bombardment clears it from the bottom. Too much passivation tapers the profile and too little causes bowing, so chemistry, bias and temperature are balanced.

Metrics, endpoint and defects

Key figures are etch rate, uniformity, profile and selectivity; Cl2 and HBr etch polysilicon with selectivity above 100 to thin gate oxide. Endpoint is detected by optical emission spectroscopy (OES), watching emission lines that change when the film clears, such as CO at 483 nm dropping at the end of an oxide etch; interferometry helps when the open area is too small for a clear signal. Typical defects include microtrenching at feature corners and sidewall residues, which are removed by ashing and wet cleans to avoid corrosion and high contact resistance.

Dry etching vs. wet etching

Wet etching dissolves films in liquid, usually isotropically, undercutting the mask by roughly the film thickness, but it is cheap, can process many wafers at once and gives high selectivity. Dry etching, including reactive ion etching (RIE) and high-density ICP etching, transfers mask patterns with little dimensional loss and is essential below a few micrometers. Fabs therefore use dry etching for patterning and wet processes for blanket film removal and cleaning. Many etch gases, especially fluorocarbons, have very high global warming potentials, so exhaust passes through abatement systems.

Which method is commonly used to detect the endpoint of dry etching?

  1. AWeighing the wafer
  2. BMonitoring changes in the plasma's emission spectrum
  3. CChecking visually after etching
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Monitoring changes in the plasma's emission spectrum

When the film being etched clears and the underlayer is exposed, the species and amounts of reaction products in the plasma change, altering emission intensity at specific wavelengths—for oxide etching, CO emission at 483 nm drops. Monitoring this with optical emission spectroscopy (OES) detects the endpoint in real time. When the exposed area is very small the signal is weak, so interferometric thickness monitoring is also used.

Which gases are commonly used for dry etching of polysilicon gates?

  1. ANitrogen (N2)
  2. BHelium (He)
  3. CHalogen gases such as chlorine (Cl2) and hydrogen bromide (HBr)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Halogen gases such as chlorine (Cl2) and hydrogen bromide (HBr)

Polysilicon is commonly etched with halogen gases such as chlorine and hydrogen bromide. They react with silicon to form volatile SiCl4 and SiBr4 while barely attacking oxide, giving selectivity above 100 to the thin underlying gate oxide. HBr also readily forms sidewall passivation for vertical profiles, and small oxygen additions tune the amount of passivation.

FAQ

What is the difference between dry etching and wet etching?

Dry etching removes material with plasma or reactive gas and, with ion bombardment, can etch vertically for fine patterns. Wet etching uses liquid chemicals, is usually isotropic and undercuts the mask, but is simple, high-throughput and highly selective.

Why can copper not be patterned by dry etching?

Copper chlorides and fluorides do not evaporate below several hundred degrees Celsius, so reaction products stay on the surface instead of being pumped away. Copper wiring is therefore made by etching trenches in the dielectric, filling them with copper and polishing off the excess.

How is the endpoint of a dry etch detected?

Most tools monitor the plasma's optical emission at wavelengths tied to reaction products, which change sharply when the film clears and the underlayer is exposed. Laser or broadband interferometry of film thickness is used when the exposed area is small.
