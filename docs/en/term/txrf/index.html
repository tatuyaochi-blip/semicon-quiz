What is TXRF (total reflection X-ray fluorescence)?

An analysis method that shines X-rays at a grazing angle so they totally reflect, then sensitively measures the fluorescent X-rays from trace metal contamination on the wafer surface.

Japanese name: TXRF(全反射蛍光X線分析)

To analyze metal contamination on a wafer surface with high sensitivity, which preparation method dissolves the surface oxide with hydrofluoric acid vapor and collects it in a droplet for analysis?

  1. AFour-point probe
  2. BSEM observation
  3. CVPD (vapor phase decomposition)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. VPD (vapor phase decomposition)

In VPD, hydrofluoric acid vapor decomposes the native oxide, and a small droplet is scanned across the wafer to collect surface metal impurities. Analyzing the collected liquid by ICP-MS or total-reflection X-ray fluorescence (TXRF) measures whole-wafer metal contamination with sensitivity around 10⁸–10⁹ atoms/cm². It is essential for monitoring contamination from tools and chemicals and evaluating cleaning processes.

Which method is often used to analyze trace metal contamination on wafer surfaces?

  1. ATotal reflection X-ray fluorescence (TXRF)
  2. BFour-point probe method
  3. CEllipsometry
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Total reflection X-ray fluorescence (TXRF)

TXRF directs X-rays at the wafer at a grazing angle of about 0.1° so they totally reflect, and measures fluorescent X-rays from atoms near the surface. Because the X-rays barely penetrate, substrate background is low, allowing non-destructive detection of metals at about 10⁹–10¹⁰ atoms/cm². Dissolving the surface oxide with HF vapor and collecting it into a single spot before measuring raises sensitivity further. Four-point probe and ellipsometry cannot detect contamination.
