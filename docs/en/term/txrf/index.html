What is TXRF (total reflection X-ray fluorescence)?

An analysis method that shines X-rays at a grazing angle so they totally reflect, then sensitively measures the fluorescent X-rays from trace metal contamination on the wafer surface.

Japanese name: TXRF(全反射蛍光X線分析)

Field: Metrology and analysis

How total reflection helps

When an X-ray beam strikes a flat, polished surface below a small critical angle, on the order of 0.1° for silicon, it is totally reflected and penetrates only a few nanometers. Atoms sitting on the surface are excited by both the incoming and reflected beams and emit fluorescent X-rays at energies characteristic of each element. An energy-dispersive detector placed close above the wafer collects this fluorescence. Because the beam barely enters the substrate and the scattered primary beam mostly leaves along the reflection direction, the background is very low.

What it detects and how well

TXRF identifies elements by the energy of their fluorescence lines and quantifies them from line intensity, calibrated against reference samples. Transition metals such as iron, nickel, copper and zinc are measured non-destructively at roughly 10⁹–10¹⁰ atoms/cm², and a wafer can be checked at several points. Light elements such as sodium and aluminum are much harder to detect because their low-energy fluorescence is weak and easily absorbed. These metals matter because they create deep levels in silicon, shortening carrier lifetime, increasing junction leakage and degrading gate oxide integrity.

VPD-TXRF for higher sensitivity

Standard TXRF samples only the area under the beam. In vapor phase decomposition (VPD), hydrofluoric acid vapor dissolves the native oxide, and a small droplet is scanned across the wafer to collect the metals from the whole surface. The droplet is dried into a single spot and measured by TXRF, concentrating the signal and pushing sensitivity to roughly 10⁸–10⁹ atoms/cm². The same collected liquid can instead be analyzed by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS). Unlike direct TXRF, this approach consumes the surface oxide and gives no spatial information.

Where you will meet it

Fabs use TXRF to monitor metal contamination from process tools, chemicals and handling, for example by running bare test wafers through a tool after maintenance or by evaluating whether a cleaning step removes metals as intended. It is often confused with other metrology: four-point probes measure sheet resistance and ellipsometers measure film thickness, neither of which sees trace contamination. Secondary ion mass spectrometry (SIMS) is also highly sensitive but sputters the sample to give depth profiles, whereas TXRF looks only at the outermost surface.

Answer: C. VPD (vapor phase decomposition)

In VPD, hydrofluoric acid vapor decomposes the native oxide, and a small droplet is scanned across the wafer to collect surface metal impurities. Analyzing the collected liquid by ICP-MS or total-reflection X-ray fluorescence (TXRF) measures whole-wafer metal contamination with sensitivity around 10⁸–10⁹ atoms/cm². Answer: A. Total reflection X-ray fluorescence (TXRF)

TXRF directs X-rays at the wafer at a grazing angle of about 0.1° so they totally reflect, and measures fluorescent X-rays from atoms near the surface. Because the X-rays barely penetrate, substrate background is low, allowing non-destructive detection of metals at about 10⁹–10¹⁰ atoms/cm². Dissolving the surface oxide with HF vapor and collecting it into a single spot before measuring raises sensitivity further. Four-point probe and ellipsometry cannot detect contamination.

FAQ

What is the detection limit of TXRF?

For transition metals on a silicon wafer, direct TXRF typically reaches about 10⁹–10¹⁰ atoms/cm²; combining it with VPD preconcentration improves this to roughly 10⁸–10⁹ atoms/cm².

Why does TXRF use a grazing incidence angle?

Below the critical angle the X-rays are totally reflected and penetrate only nanometers, so they excite surface contaminants strongly while producing little background signal from the silicon substrate.

Can TXRF detect sodium and aluminum?

Only poorly. Light elements emit low-energy fluorescence that is weak and strongly absorbed, so methods such as VPD-ICP-MS are generally preferred for them.
