What is Ellipsometry?

A measurement method that shines polarized light on a sample and determines thin-film thickness and refractive index from the change in polarization on reflection. It is nondestructive.

Japanese name: エリプソメトリ

Ellipsometry appears in 3 practice questions across 2 topics, including Metrology and analysis, Oxidation and diffusion.

Field: Metrology and analysis

What an ellipsometer actually measures

Ellipsometry shines polarized light onto a sample at an oblique angle, often around 65–75° for silicon, and analyzes the polarization of the reflected beam. The light is split into p-polarized (in the plane of incidence) and s-polarized (perpendicular) components, which reflect differently from a film stack. The instrument measures two quantities: Ψ, related to the ratio of their reflection amplitudes, and Δ, their phase difference, defined by tanΨ·exp(iΔ) = rp/rs. Because it measures a ratio and a phase rather than absolute intensity, it is insensitive to source fluctuations.

From Ψ and Δ to thickness and index

Ψ and Δ do not give thickness directly. The analyst builds an optical model of the stack, for example a silicon substrate, a thin interfacial layer and an oxide film, with each layer's thickness and optical constants n and k, then adjusts the unknowns until the calculated Ψ and Δ match the measurement. Spectroscopic ellipsometry measures across many wavelengths, giving enough data to fit multilayer stacks. For films only a few nanometers thick, thickness and refractive index become strongly correlated, so the index is usually fixed at a known value.

Strengths and limits in a fab

Ellipsometry is fast, non-contact and non-destructive, with thickness repeatability better than 0.1 nm, which makes it the workhorse for monitoring gate oxides, nitrides and other thin films. Its accuracy is only as good as the model: interface layers, surface roughness, absorbing films and unknown composition all complicate the fit. The beam spot is relatively large, so measurements on product wafers are made on dedicated test pads, typically in the scribe lines, rather than on dense circuit patterns.

Ellipsometry vs. other thickness methods

Oxide interference colors give only a rough estimate of thickness. Spectral reflectometry measures reflected intensity versus wavelength; it is simpler and works well for thicker films, but carries less information than ellipsometry's phase data for very thin layers. X-ray reflectivity and cross-sectional TEM serve as references, the latter destructive. Ellipsometry measures film thickness and optical constants only; sheet resistance needs a four-point probe, and surface metal contamination needs a method such as TXRF.

FAQ

What does ellipsometry measure?

It measures how reflection changes the polarization of light, expressed as the amplitude ratio Ψ and phase difference Δ. Fitting these to an optical model yields film thickness and refractive index.

What is the difference between ellipsometry and reflectometry?

Reflectometry measures only the intensity of reflected light, while ellipsometry also measures the phase difference between two polarizations. The extra phase information makes ellipsometry more sensitive for very thin films.

Can ellipsometry measure films only a few nanometers thick?

Yes, with sub-0.1 nm repeatability, which is why it is used for gate dielectrics. For such thin films, though, the refractive index is usually fixed in the model because it cannot be separated reliably from thickness.
