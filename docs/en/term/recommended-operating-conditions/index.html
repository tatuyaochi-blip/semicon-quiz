What is Recommended operating conditions?

The range of conditions, such as supply voltage and ambient temperature, under which an IC is meant to operate. The min/max values in the electrical characteristics table are guaranteed only within this range. For a 3.3 V part, for example, the supply might be specified as 3.0 to 3.6 V.

Which correctly describes the difference between "absolute maximum ratings" and "recommended operating conditions" in a datasheet?

  1. AWithin the absolute maximum ratings the device will not be damaged, and all values in the electrical characteristics table are also guaranteed as written
  2. BAbsolute maximum ratings are limits beyond which damage may occur; characteristics are guaranteed only within the recommended operating conditions
  3. CRecommended operating conditions are the limits for continuous operation, while absolute maximum ratings are limits that may be used routinely for short periods
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Absolute maximum ratings are limits beyond which damage may occur; characteristics are guaranteed only within the recommended operating conditions

Absolute maximum ratings are limit values that, if exceeded even momentarily, may cause damage or degradation; staying within them only means the device will not be damaged—it does not guarantee correct operation. The values in the electrical characteristics table are guaranteed only within the recommended operating conditions. For example, a 3.3 V device might specify an absolute maximum supply voltage of −0.3 to 4.6 V and recommended operating conditions of 3.0 to 3.6 V. In design, use the device within the recommended operating conditions, and confirm that the absolute maximum ratings are not exceeded even including noise and surges.

What is the main reason a logic IC's recommended operating conditions specify an upper limit on how slow input rise and fall can be (for example, 10 ns/V)?

  1. ABecause a slow input change overcharges the capacitance of the input pin, damaging the input pin
  2. BBecause an input lingering near the threshold increases shoot-through current or causes the output to oscillate
  3. CBecause the slower the input change, the shorter the propagation delay becomes, causing malfunction in later stages
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Because an input lingering near the threshold increases shoot-through current or causes the output to oscillate

A slowly changing input lingers at intermediate voltages where H/L recognition is undefined. During that time, the pMOS and nMOS of the input stage are both partially on, and shoot-through current flows from the supply to ground, increasing current consumption and heat. Noise can also make the decision flip H, L, H repeatedly, causing the output to oscillate. For this reason, the recommended operating conditions set a lower limit on input transition speed. When slow signals must be received, choose a product with Schmitt-trigger inputs that have hysteresis.
