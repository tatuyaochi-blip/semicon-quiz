What is Silane (SiH4)?

A gas compound of silicon and hydrogen used as the source for CVD of polysilicon, silicon oxide and silicon nitride. It ignites spontaneously in air, so it is handled under strict control.

Japanese name: シラン(SiH4)

When silicon films are deposited by thermally decomposing silane, what kind of film forms if the deposition temperature is lowered?

  1. ASingle-crystal silicon
  2. BAmorphous silicon
  3. CSilicon dioxide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Amorphous silicon

Silicon films from thermal decomposition of silane are amorphous below about 580 °C and polycrystalline above it. Depositing amorphous silicon and then annealing near 600 °C for a long time grows crystals from few nuclei, yielding larger grains (and higher mobility) than direct poly deposition. Thin-film transistors in LCDs also use laser crystallization.

What is the main hazard in handling silane (SiH4), used as a source for CVD and other processes?

  1. AIt is pyrophoric and may ignite if it leaks into air
  2. BIt is nonflammable and nontoxic, so no special control is needed
  3. CIt emits strong radiation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It is pyrophoric and may ignite if it leaks into air

Silane is pyrophoric: it ignites spontaneously on contact with air, and a high-concentration leak can accumulate before igniting explosively. It is therefore kept in exhausted cylinder cabinets and supplied through lines with leak detectors and emergency shutoff valves. It is not radioactive, nor is it a benign gas needing no special care. Silane is a common CVD precursor for polysilicon, SiO2 and SiN.
