What is Siemens process?

A method that reduces trichlorosilane with hydrogen to deposit high-purity polycrystalline silicon on heated silicon rods. It is the main way to make raw silicon for semiconductors.

Japanese name: シーメンス法

Which is the typical method for producing high-purity polycrystalline silicon?

  1. ABosch process
  2. BSiemens process (deposition by hydrogen reduction of trichlorosilane)
  3. CDamascene process
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Siemens process (deposition by hydrogen reduction of trichlorosilane)

The Siemens process reacts metallurgical-grade silicon (about 98% pure) with hydrogen chloride to make trichlorosilane (SiHCl3, boiling point about 32 °C), purifies it by distillation, then reduces it with hydrogen onto silicon rods heated to about 1100 °C, depositing high-purity silicon. The resulting polysilicon, about 11N (99.999999999%) pure, feeds CZ growth. The process is very energy-intensive.

What is the most appropriate approximate purity required of polycrystalline silicon (polysilicon) feedstock for semiconductors?

  1. AAbout 99%
  2. BAbout 99.99%
  3. CAbout 99.999999999% (11N)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. About 99.999999999% (11N)

Semiconductor-grade polysilicon must reach about eleven nines (11N), meaning total impurities of only around 0.01 ppb. Even trace metals shorten carrier lifetime and increase leakage. It is made by the Siemens process, in which trichlorosilane is reduced with hydrogen and deposited on heated rods. Solar-grade silicon can be 6N–9N; 99% or 99.99% is far from adequate for semiconductors.
