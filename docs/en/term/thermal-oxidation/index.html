What is Thermal oxidation?

A method of forming an SiO2 film on silicon by heating it in oxygen or water vapor. There are dry and wet oxidation.

Japanese name: 熱酸化

Which correctly compares dry oxidation (O2) and wet oxidation (H2O) in the thermal oxidation of silicon?

  1. ADry oxidation grows oxide faster
  2. BWet oxidation grows oxide faster
  3. CBoth grow oxide at the same rate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Wet oxidation grows oxide faster

Wet oxidation uses steam; H2O dissolves in SiO2 several hundred times more than O2, so it grows oxide several to ten times faster than dry oxidation—at 1000 °C for an hour, roughly 70 nm dry versus 400 nm wet. It suits thick isolation and masking oxides. Dry oxidation is slower but yields dense, low-interface-state films with precise thickness control, so it was used for thin gate oxides.

Once the oxide has become sufficiently thick in thermal oxidation, how does its thickness relate to oxidation time?

  1. AThickness is roughly proportional to the square root of time (parabolic law)
  2. BThickness is proportional to the square of time
  3. CThickness is constant regardless of time
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Thickness is roughly proportional to the square root of time (parabolic law)

The Deal-Grove model gives x² + Ax = B(t + τ). For thin oxides the interface reaction limits growth and thickness rises nearly linearly with time (x ≈ (B/A)t); for thick oxides oxidant diffusion through the film limits it and thickness follows the square root of time (x ≈ √(Bt)), so doubling thickness takes about four times as long. Thick oxides are therefore sped up with high temperature, steam and high pressure.
