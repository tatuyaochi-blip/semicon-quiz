What is 6T SRAM cell?

The SRAM memory cell made of two cross-coupled inverters (four transistors) and two access transistors.

Japanese name: 6T SRAMセル

What is the advantage of an 8T SRAM cell, which adds read-only transistors to a 6T SRAM cell?

  1. AThe cell area is smaller than 6T
  2. BData remains when power is turned off
  3. CCell data is less likely to be disturbed during reads, so it operates stably even at low voltage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Cell data is less likely to be disturbed during reads, so it operates stably even at low voltage

In a 6T cell, bit-line charge can push up the storage node during a read and flip the data. An 8T cell adds a two-transistor read-only path; the storage node only drives a gate there, so reads cannot disturb it. Read and write transistors can be optimized separately, improving low-voltage stability, and separate read and write ports can operate simultaneously. The cost is a larger cell.

In a 6T SRAM cell, which design is needed so that the cell contents are not flipped during a read (to prevent read upset)?

  1. AMake the access transistor stronger than the pull-down transistor
  2. BRemove the pull-up transistor
  3. CMake the pull-down (driver) transistor stronger than the access transistor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Make the pull-down (driver) transistor stronger than the access transistor

During a read, current flows from the precharged bit line through the access transistor into the node holding 0, raising its voltage slightly. If the pull-down transistor is sufficiently stronger than the access transistor (a cell ratio β = pull-down/access of about 1.5–2.5), the rise stays below the opposite inverter's switching threshold and the data does not flip. 8T cells and read-assist circuits also address this.
