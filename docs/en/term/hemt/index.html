What is HEMT (high-electron-mobility transistor)?

A transistor that uses the high-mobility two-dimensional electron gas formed at a heterojunction interface as its channel. It is used in high-frequency and high-power applications.

Japanese name: HEMT(高電子移動度トランジスタ)

How the 2DEG channel forms

A HEMT is grown epitaxially as a heterojunction between a wide-band-gap barrier, such as AlGaAs or AlGaN, and a narrower-gap channel material, such as GaAs or GaN. The conduction-band offset creates a thin triangular potential well on the channel side, where electrons are confined in a sheet only a few nanometers thick: the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG). A Schottky metal gate on the barrier modulates the sheet density, so the device is a field-effect transistor whose channel lies at a buried, nearly perfect crystal interface rather than at an oxide surface as in a MOSFET.

Why the electrons are fast

In GaAs-based HEMTs the electrons come from donors placed in the barrier, often behind an undoped spacer, so the ionized donors are separated from the undoped channel where electrons travel. This modulation doping sharply reduces impurity scattering, giving higher mobility and lower noise than a doped channel; mobility rises further on cooling as phonon scattering falls. In AlGaN/GaN no doping is needed at all: the difference in spontaneous and piezoelectric polarization induces a sheet density of about 10¹³ cm⁻², with room-temperature mobility around 2000 cm²/V·s.

GaAs HEMT vs. GaN HEMT

GaAs HEMTs, including pseudomorphic versions with an InGaAs channel, are the classic low-noise amplifiers in satellite receivers and radio telescopes. GaN HEMTs trade some noise performance for power: GaN's wide band gap gives a breakdown field roughly ten times that of silicon, its electron saturation velocity is about 2.5×10⁷ cm/s, and the dense 2DEG supplies high current. Together these give several times the power density of GaAs, so GaN HEMTs serve base-station, radar and satellite power amplifiers and, increasingly, fast power-conversion switches.

The normally-on problem

Because the 2DEG exists with zero gate bias, a basic HEMT is normally on (depletion mode) and needs a negative gate voltage to turn off. Power circuits prefer normally-off devices for fail-safe behavior. Common fixes are a p-type GaN layer under the gate that depletes the 2DEG beneath it, a recessed gate that thins the barrier, or a cascode in which a low-voltage silicon MOSFET in series switches the HEMT off by pulling its source above its grounded gate, at the cost of an extra die and its parasitics.

Answer: C. A two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) formed by polarization at the AlGaN/GaN interface

In a GaN HEMT, the difference in spontaneous and piezoelectric polarization at the AlGaN/GaN interface creates a dense two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) of about 10¹³ cm⁻². Because no doping is needed to supply the electrons, scattering is low and mobility reaches about 2000 cm²/V·s, combining low on-resistance with fast switching. The 2DEG exists even at 0 V gate bias, so the basic device is normally on.

  1. ABridge rectifier
  2. BDarlington connection
  3. CCascode configuration

Answer: C. Cascode configuration

In a cascode, a low-voltage (about 30 V) silicon MOSFET is placed in series on the source side of a GaN HEMT, with the HEMT's gate tied to the MOSFET's source. Turning the MOSFET off negatively biases the HEMT gate, switching it off automatically, so the pair behaves as a normally-off device driven by a standard silicon MOSFET gate driver. Drawbacks are the extra component and the MOSFET's parasitics at high speed.

FAQ

What is the difference between a HEMT and a MOSFET?

A MOSFET's channel is an inversion layer at the silicon/oxide interface, while a HEMT's channel is a two-dimensional electron gas at an epitaxial heterojunction with little impurity or roughness scattering. HEMTs therefore reach higher electron mobility and suit high-frequency, low-noise and high-power RF work.

Why are GaN HEMTs normally on?

Polarization at the AlGaN/GaN interface creates the 2DEG without any gate voltage, so the channel conducts at 0 V. Normally-off operation needs a p-GaN gate, a recessed gate or a cascode with a silicon MOSFET.

What does 2DEG mean in a HEMT?

2DEG stands for two-dimensional electron gas, a sheet of electrons confined in a quantum well at a heterojunction so they can move freely only in the plane of the interface. It forms the HEMT's channel.
