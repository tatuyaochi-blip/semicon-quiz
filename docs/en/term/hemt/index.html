What is HEMT (high-electron-mobility transistor)?

A transistor that uses the high-mobility two-dimensional electron gas formed at a heterojunction interface as its channel. It is used in high-frequency and high-power applications.

Japanese name: HEMT(高電子移動度トランジスタ)

Which is the channel through which current flows in a GaN HEMT?

  1. AA p-type inversion layer formed under the gate
  2. BA thick, heavily doped n-type layer
  3. CA two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) formed by polarization at the AlGaN/GaN interface
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) formed by polarization at the AlGaN/GaN interface

In a GaN HEMT, the difference in spontaneous and piezoelectric polarization at the AlGaN/GaN interface creates a dense two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) of about 10¹³ cm⁻². Because no doping is needed to supply the electrons, scattering is low and mobility reaches about 2000 cm²/V·s, combining low on-resistance with fast switching. The 2DEG exists even at 0 V gate bias, so the basic device is normally on.

Which configuration combines a normally-on GaN HEMT in series with a low-voltage silicon MOSFET so it can be used safely with normally-off behavior?

  1. ABridge rectifier
  2. BDarlington connection
  3. CCascode configuration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Cascode configuration

In a cascode, a low-voltage (about 30 V) silicon MOSFET is placed in series on the source side of a GaN HEMT, with the HEMT's gate tied to the MOSFET's source. Turning the MOSFET off negatively biases the HEMT gate, switching it off automatically, so the pair behaves as a normally-off device driven by a standard silicon MOSFET gate driver. Drawbacks are the extra component and the MOSFET's parasitics at high speed.
