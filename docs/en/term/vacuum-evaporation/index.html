What is Vacuum evaporation?

Forming a film by heating a material in vacuum until it evaporates and condenses on the substrate. Electron-beam heating handles high-melting-point materials, but step coverage is poor.

Japanese name: 真空蒸着

Which correctly describes vacuum evaporation?

  1. AFilms are deposited by chemical reactions of source gases
  2. BMetal is deposited from an electrolyte
  3. CMaterial is heated in vacuum to evaporate it and deposit it on the substrate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Material is heated in vacuum to evaporate it and deposit it on the substrate

Vacuum evaporation heats material by resistance or electron beam until it evaporates, and the atoms travel through vacuum (about 10⁻⁴ Pa or better) to coat the substrate. The equipment is relatively simple and films are pure, but line-of-sight travel gives poor step coverage and alloy compositions drift, so sputtering dominates IC wiring. Evaporation is used for lift-off electrode patterning on compound semiconductors and for depositing OLED emissive layers.

What is an advantage of vacuum evaporation that heats material with an electron beam?

  1. AIt can evaporate even high-melting-point materials
  2. BIts step coverage is very high
  3. CIt forms films through chemical reactions of source gases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It can evaporate even high-melting-point materials

Electron-beam evaporation focuses an electron beam accelerated through several kV onto the source material, vaporizing refractory metals such as tungsten and molybdenum and oxides that resistive heating cannot, with little crucible contamination. Atoms travel in straight lines, giving poor step coverage—which is exploited in lift-off, where film deposited over patterned resist is removed along with the resist to define electrodes.
