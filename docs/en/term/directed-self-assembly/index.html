What is Directed self-assembly (DSA)?

A technique that uses the tendency of block copolymers to form regular fine patterns on their own, aligning their position and orientation with guides made by lithography to create patterns finer than the resolution limit. Reducing defects to extremely low levels is the challenge for volume production.

Japanese name: 誘導自己組織化(DSA)

Field: Lithography

Phase separation of block copolymers

A block copolymer joins chains (blocks) of two different polymers end to end. A typical example, PS-b-PMMA, consists of polystyrene (PS) and poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA). The two blocks do not want to mix, but because they are joined they cannot separate far, so on annealing they spontaneously form stripes (lamellae) or cylinders with a period set by the molecular length. The period can be tuned by molecular weight and ranges from a few to several tens of nanometers.

Aligning the pattern with guides

Left alone, the pattern's orientation is random, so guides made by lithography set its position and direction. Graphoepitaxy confines the material in trenches, while chemoepitaxy patterns the surface chemistry in stripes. If the guide period is an integer multiple of the self-assembly period, the space between guides fills with finer stripes and the pattern pitch is multiplied. Cylinder patterns can also be used to make contact holes whose position and size variations are rectified.

The χ parameter and defects

The tendency of the two blocks to separate is expressed by the χ (chi) parameter; the larger χ, the finer the period that still separates cleanly. PS-b-PMMA has a low χ and a practical pitch limit of about 20 nm, so high-χ materials are being developed for finer pitches. The biggest problem for volume production is defects: dislocations and other defects where stripes shift must be reduced to the extremely low densities required in semiconductor manufacturing and verified by inspection.

Combining with EUV lithography

In the 2020s, combining DSA with EUV lithography has drawn attention. In EUV, the small number of photons causes stochastic variation in line width and line-edge roughness. Using EUV patterns as guides for self-assembly is expected to snap them to the regular period set by the material and reduce that variation. imec, Intel and others have reported patterning results combining EUV and DSA since the early 2020s.

FAQ

What does "directed" mean in directed self-assembly?

It means aligning the position and orientation of the block copolymer pattern with guides made by lithography, such as trenches or chemical patterns. Without guides, the pattern is regular but randomly oriented.

What determines the feature size in DSA?

Mainly the molecular length (molecular weight) of the block copolymer. How small it can go depends on the χ parameter, which expresses how strongly the two blocks separate.

How do DSA and EUV lithography relate?

They are not only competitors but can be combined. Using EUV patterns as guides and refining them with DSA is being studied as a way to reduce line-width and roughness variation caused by EUV stochastics.
