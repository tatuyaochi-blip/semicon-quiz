What is Annealing?

Heat treatment. Used to repair crystal damage after ion implantation and to activate dopants, among other purposes.

Japanese name: アニール

Annealing appears in 36 practice questions across 14 topics, including Ion implantation, Oxidation and diffusion, Silicon wafers.

Field: Ion implantation

Why implanted wafers must be annealed

Ion implantation knocks silicon atoms out of place and, at high doses, can make the surface layer amorphous. Most implanted atoms also sit in interstitial positions where they do not donate or accept carriers. Annealing gives the lattice enough thermal energy to regrow its crystal order and move dopants onto substitutional sites, which is called activation. The catch is that heat also makes dopants diffuse: source and drain regions spread laterally under the gate, shortening the effective channel length and adding overlap capacitance. Every anneal therefore trades activation and damage repair against unwanted diffusion.

Furnace, RTP and millisecond anneals

The total heat a wafer sees is called the thermal budget, set by both temperature and time. Vertical furnaces process about 100 wafers at once over many minutes and suit oxidation, diffusion and long anneals. Rapid thermal processing (RTP) heats one wafer at a time with lamps for seconds, and millisecond anneals using lasers or flash lamps go further, activating dopants while allowing almost no diffusion; advanced nodes rely on these to keep junctions shallow. Fast ramps have a cost: temperature gradients across a large wafer can cause slip dislocations, so ramp rates and wafer support are optimized.

Anneals with other purposes

Many anneals have nothing to do with implant damage. A forming gas anneal at about 400 to 450 °C in hydrogen-containing nitrogen passivates dangling bonds at the Si/SiO2 interface, cutting interface states to about 10¹⁰ cm⁻²eV⁻¹. Annealing densifies CVD oxide so its etch rate approaches that of thermal oxide. Magnesium-doped GaN becomes p-type only after annealing near 700 °C in nitrogen drives out the hydrogen that passivates the acceptors. Wafer makers anneal above 1100 °C to form a denuded zone, and briefly above about 650 °C to eliminate oxygen thermal donors.

Material-specific conditions

Required temperatures vary widely with material. Dopants barely diffuse in SiC, so its implants are often done hot, at about 300 to 600 °C, and activation needs 1,600 to 1,800 °C, far beyond silicon practice. At the other extreme, the Smart Cut process for SOI wafers needs only a few hundred degrees for implanted hydrogen to form microcavities that split the wafer. Unintended anneals matter too: holding oxygen-rich CZ silicon near 450 °C generates thermal donors that shift resistivity.

What is the main purpose of annealing (heat treatment) after ion implantation?

  1. ATo evaporate and remove the implanted dopants
  2. BTo repair crystal damage and place dopants on lattice sites to activate them electrically
  3. CTo grow a thick oxide on the wafer surface
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. To repair crystal damage and place dopants on lattice sites to activate them electrically

Implanted ions disrupt the silicon lattice and mostly sit at interstitial sites where they do not act as donors or acceptors, so they are largely electrically inactive right after implantation. Annealing repairs the damage and moves dopants onto lattice sites, activating them. To avoid excessive diffusion, lamp-based RTA lasting seconds or flash-lamp and laser anneals lasting milliseconds or less are used.

What is the phenomenon called in which diffusion of boron and other dopants is temporarily accelerated early in annealing because of interstitial silicon created by implantation?

  1. ATransient enhanced diffusion (TED)
  2. BGettering
  3. CSegregation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Transient enhanced diffusion (TED)

Excess silicon interstitials created by implantation pair with boron and assist its diffusion, so early in annealing diffusion is temporarily enhanced by factors of tens to a thousand. It subsides once the interstitials annihilate at the surface and elsewhere—hence "transient" enhanced diffusion (TED)—and is worst for long, relatively low-temperature anneals. Because it thwarts shallow junctions, it is countered with rapid spike anneals and carbon co-implantation.

FAQ

What does dopant activation mean?

It means moving implanted dopant atoms onto substitutional lattice sites, where they can donate electrons or accept them to create holes. Annealing supplies the energy for this while also repairing implant damage.

What is the difference between RTA and furnace annealing?

Furnace annealing treats a large batch of wafers for minutes to hours, while rapid thermal annealing (RTA) heats a single wafer with lamps for seconds. The shorter time of RTA gives a much smaller thermal budget and less dopant diffusion.

What is a forming gas anneal used for?

It is a low-temperature anneal, around 400 to 450 °C, in nitrogen with a few percent hydrogen. The hydrogen passivates dangling bonds at the Si/SiO2 interface, improving threshold stability, subthreshold slope and mobility.
