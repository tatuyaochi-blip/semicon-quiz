What is Annealing?

Heat treatment. Used to repair crystal damage after ion implantation and to activate dopants, among other purposes.

Japanese name: アニール

Annealing appears in 33 practice questions across 13 topics, including Ion implantation, Oxidation and diffusion, Silicon wafers.

Field: Ion implantation

Try it: questions about Annealing

What is the main purpose of annealing (heat treatment) after ion implantation?

  1. ATo evaporate and remove the implanted dopants
  2. BTo repair crystal damage and place dopants on lattice sites to activate them electrically
  3. CTo grow a thick oxide on the wafer surface
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. To repair crystal damage and place dopants on lattice sites to activate them electrically

Implanted ions disrupt the silicon lattice and mostly sit at interstitial sites where they do not act as donors or acceptors, so they are largely electrically inactive right after implantation. Annealing repairs the damage and moves dopants onto lattice sites, activating them. To avoid excessive diffusion, lamp-based RTA lasting seconds or flash-lamp and laser anneals lasting milliseconds or less are used.

What is the phenomenon called in which diffusion of boron and other dopants is temporarily accelerated early in annealing because of interstitial silicon created by implantation?

  1. ATransient enhanced diffusion (TED)
  2. BGettering
  3. CSegregation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Transient enhanced diffusion (TED)

Excess silicon interstitials created by implantation pair with boron and assist its diffusion, so early in annealing diffusion is temporarily enhanced by factors of tens to a thousand. It subsides once the interstitials annihilate at the surface and elsewhere—hence "transient" enhanced diffusion (TED)—and is worst for long, relatively low-temperature anneals. Because it thwarts shallow junctions, it is countered with rapid spike anneals and carbon co-implantation.
