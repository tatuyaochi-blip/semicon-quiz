What is Speedup?

Execution time before an improvement divided by the time after; a value of 2 means twice as fast.

Japanese name: 速度向上率

Speedup appears in 12 practice questions across 4 topics, including Performance Analysis, CPUs and architecture, Multicore & Synchronization.

Field: Performance Analysis

Try it: questions about Speedup

If 90% of a program can be parallelized, what is the theoretical maximum speedup when it runs on an unlimited number of processors?

  1. A10 times
  2. B90 times
  3. CInfinite
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 10 times

Amdahl's law gives speedup as 1/((1 − p) + p/N) for parallel fraction p and N processors. With p = 0.9 the limit as N grows is 1/0.1 = 10×, and even N = 10 yields only about 5.3×. Even a small serial portion caps the speedup, so reducing it is crucial. Gustafson's law takes the complementary view of scaling the problem size to grow the parallel part.

How is speedup, which expresses a performance improvement, calculated?

  1. AExecution time before ÷ execution time after
  2. BExecution time after ÷ execution time before
  3. CExecution time before − execution time after
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Execution time before ÷ execution time after

Speedup is the time before divided by the time after. A task that took 10 seconds and now takes 4 has a speedup of 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5×. "60% shorter" and "2.5× faster" describe the same change but are easily confused. For parallelization, speedup is shown relative to the single-core time as the core count grows.
