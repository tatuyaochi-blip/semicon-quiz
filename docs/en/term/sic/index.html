What is SiC (silicon carbide)?

A wide-band-gap semiconductor with a band gap of about 3.3 eV. Its high breakdown field makes it suitable for high-voltage, low-loss power devices.

Japanese name: SiC(炭化ケイ素)

Field: Power semiconductors

Why the wide band gap matters

SiC's band gap of about 3.3 eV gives it a critical breakdown field near 3 MV/cm, roughly ten times silicon's. For a given voltage rating the drift layer can be about one tenth as thick and about 100 times more heavily doped, so its resistance falls by a theoretical factor of several hundred. Baliga's figure of merit, εμEc³, captures this because the breakdown field enters cubed. The wide gap also keeps intrinsic carrier density low at high temperature, and SiC conducts heat about three times better than silicon, around 400 W/(m·K).

SiC devices in power converters

The workhorses are SiC MOSFETs and SiC Schottky barrier diodes. Being unipolar, they avoid the tail current of silicon IGBTs and the reverse-recovery current of silicon pn diodes, so switching losses drop sharply; SiC Schottky diodes are made at 600–1700 V. Lower losses let designers raise switching frequency and shrink inductors, capacitors and cooling, which is why SiC spread into electric-vehicle inverters, especially 800 V systems. The price of speed is steep dv/dt, tens of volts per nanosecond, which turns small parasitic inductances into voltage surges and EMI, so layout and gate drive matter.

Gate drive and robustness

SiC MOSFETs are usually driven at about +15 to +18 V when on and often a negative bias of −3 to −5 V when off. Their threshold voltage is low, around 2–3 V, so the Miller current caused by the opposite switch's dv/dt could otherwise turn them on falsely. Small chips with low thermal capacity mean short-circuit withstand time is typically a few microseconds, versus about 10 µs for silicon IGBTs, so protection must act faster. Kelvin-source packages separate the gate-drive return from the main current path to avoid common-source inductance problems.

Why SiC is hard to make

SiC does not melt at normal pressure, so boules are grown by sublimation (physical vapour transport) at 2000–2500 °C at only about 0.1–1 mm per hour, and the extremely hard crystal is slow to slice and polish. Power devices use the 4H-SiC polytype on substrates cut about 4° off-axis for step-flow epitaxy at 1500–1650 °C. Dopants barely diffuse, so junctions are formed by epitaxy and hot ion implantation followed by 1600–1800 °C anneals. Reliability concerns include low channel mobility from carbon-related oxide-interface defects and bipolar degradation, where basal-plane dislocations grow into stacking faults.

Answer: A. Its breakdown electric field is about 10 times that of Si

SiC has a wide band gap of about 3.3 eV and a breakdown field about ten times silicon's (about 3 MV/cm). For the same voltage rating the drift layer can be about one tenth as thick and about 100 times more heavily doped, cutting its resistance theoretically by a factor of several hundred. Its thermal conductivity is about three times silicon's, so it handles heat well, and it is spreading in EV inverters. SiC wafers remain costlier, and the move to 8-inch is still under way.

What is the general term for semiconductors with a larger band gap than silicon, such as SiC and GaN?

  1. AWide-band-gap semiconductors
  2. BNarrow-gap semiconductors
  3. CAmorphous semiconductors
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Wide-band-gap semiconductors

Semiconductors with bandgaps of roughly 3 eV or more are called wide-bandgap semiconductors; SiC (about 3.3 eV) and GaN (about 3.4 eV) are the main examples. High breakdown fields and few intrinsic carriers let them operate at high temperature, suiting high-voltage, low-loss power and RF devices. Even wider-gap Ga2O3 (about 4.8 eV) and diamond (about 5.5 eV) are called ultra-wide-bandgap semiconductors.

FAQ

Why is SiC better than silicon for high-voltage power devices?

SiC's breakdown field is about ten times silicon's, so a thinner, more heavily doped drift layer blocks the same voltage with far lower resistance. It also tolerates higher temperatures and conducts heat better, which cuts both losses and cooling requirements.

What is the difference between SiC and GaN power devices?

Both are wide-band-gap semiconductors with gaps of about 3.3 and 3.4 eV. SiC is grown as native substrates and is used mainly for vertical MOSFETs and diodes at high voltage and current, while GaN power transistors are usually lateral HEMTs grown on foreign substrates such as silicon and excel at very fast switching at moderate voltages.

What is 4H-SiC?

4H-SiC is the polytype of silicon carbide with a four-layer hexagonal stacking sequence. It is the standard choice for power devices because it combines a band gap of about 3.26 eV with electron mobility around 1000 cm²/V·s and relatively little anisotropy.
