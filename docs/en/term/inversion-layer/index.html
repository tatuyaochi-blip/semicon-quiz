What is Inversion layer?

A layer formed at the semiconductor surface by the gate voltage, where carriers of the type opposite to the substrate gather. It becomes the MOSFET's channel.

Japanese name: 反転層

In a MOSFET with a very thin gate insulator, what is the effect of the electron distribution in the inversion layer peaking slightly away from the interface?

  1. AThe threshold voltage becomes zero
  2. BThe gate insulator appears effectively thicker, reducing gate capacitance (and drive current)
  3. CThe gate insulator appears effectively thinner and the gate capacitance increases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The gate insulator appears effectively thicker, reducing gate capacitance (and drive current)

Electrons in the inversion layer are confined in a narrow well by the strong interface field, and quantum effects place their distribution peak about 1 nm inside rather than right at the interface. With the charge centroid set back, the dielectric acts as if it were about 0.3–0.4 nm thicker in SiO2 terms, reducing gate capacitance and drive current. Together with poly depletion, this makes the electrical thickness (CET) exceed the physical EOT, and it matters more as EOT shrinks.
