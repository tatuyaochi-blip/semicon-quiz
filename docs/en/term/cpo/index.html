What is CPO (co-packaged optics)?

Technology that mounts the optical transceiver parts (optical engines) in the same package as a switch IC or similar. Shorter electrical connections lower the energy per bit, but the optics cannot be swapped out by unplugging, so testing before assembly and the use of external laser sources become important.

Japanese name: CPO(コパッケージドオプティクス)

CPO (co-packaged optics) appears in 8 practice questions across 3 topics, including Silicon photonics, Compound semiconductors, Advanced packaging (3D and chiplets).

Field: Silicon photonics

Pluggable vs. co-packaged

In a conventional switch, the switch IC drives electrical signals across the circuit board to pluggable optical transceivers in the front panel. These traces run from roughly a dozen to several tens of centimetres, and at high data rates the circuits that compensate their loss consume considerable power. Co-packaged optics (CPO) moves the optical engines into the same package as the switch IC, shrinking the electrical path to a few millimetres. That lowers energy per bit, reduces signal degradation and allows many optical links to be packed densely around the chip.

Thinking in energy per bit

Link power is energy per bit multiplied by bit rate. A switch with 51.2 Tb/s of total bandwidth whose optical links consume 5 pJ/bit spends 5×10⁻¹² × 51.2×10¹² = 256 W on them, and at 15 pJ/bit it would spend about 768 W, so the difference is roughly 500 W per switch. Watch the prefixes, since mixing up T and G or p and n gives answers off by a factor of a thousand. CPO and linear-drive approaches both aim to cut pJ/bit by reducing long electrical paths and digital signal processing (DSP).

Serviceability and yield

The price of integration is that a failed optical component can no longer be unplugged and replaced; a fault can take out the whole package, including an expensive switch IC. CPO therefore depends on known good die (KGD): photonic chips are tested at wafer level by bringing fiber probes down onto grating couplers alongside electrical probes, measuring waveguide loss, modulators and photodetectors before assembly. Final test after assembly is still required. Attaching many fibers precisely and keeping optics stable next to a hot processor are further engineering challenges.

Why the laser often sits outside

A semiconductor laser's threshold current rises and its efficiency falls as temperature increases, and degradation accelerates. Next to a switch IC dissipating several hundred watts, an in-package laser would face harsh conditions and would be the most failure-prone part. An external laser source places lasers in a cooler spot, often as front-panel modules that can still be swapped, and feeds continuous light to the optical engines by fiber, where modulators impose data. The cost is extra coupling loss, which must be offset by higher laser output.

Answer: A. Failed optical components cannot be replaced as modules, so high reliability and thorough pre-assembly testing are required

In CPO, the optical engines sit right next to the switch IC, so the electrical paths are short, lowering energy per bit and allowing many optical links to be packed densely. On the other hand, a failed optical component cannot be swapped out by unplugging it from the front panel, and the failure affects the entire package. Therefore, testing at the wafer or chip level before assembly and using only good parts, and moving the failure-prone lasers outside the package, become important. Operating optical components near the hot switch IC and precisely connecting many fibers are also challenges.

  3. CSilicon modulators can generate light from electrical signals alone, without laser light

Answer: A. Lasers tend to lose efficiency and lifetime at high temperatures, so they are kept away from the hot switch IC and made easier to replace

As temperature rises, a semiconductor laser's threshold current increases, its efficiency drops and degradation accelerates. In CPO, the optical engines are placed right next to a switch IC consuming several hundred W, so a laser inside the package would have to operate at harsh temperatures. An external laser source can run in a relatively cool location and can be replaced by plugging and unplugging, for example from the front panel. In exchange, the coupling loss of guiding the laser light to the optical engine through fiber increases, and the laser output must be raised accordingly. Modulators do not generate light.

FAQ

What is the difference between co-packaged optics and pluggable transceivers?

Pluggable transceivers sit in the front panel and connect to the switch IC through long board traces, but they are easy to replace and mix. CPO puts the optics inside the switch package to shorten the electrical path and save power, at the cost of field replaceability.

Why does co-packaged optics use an external laser source?

Lasers lose efficiency and lifetime at high temperature and are among the least reliable components, so keeping them away from the hot switch IC and in a replaceable module improves both performance and serviceability.

How do you calculate optical interconnect power from pJ/bit?

Multiply energy per bit by total bit rate: for example, 5 pJ/bit × 51.2 Tb/s = 256 W. Keep units consistent, since pico is 10⁻¹² and tera is 10¹².
