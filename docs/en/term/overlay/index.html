What is Overlay?

How accurately a new pattern is placed relative to the patterns made in earlier steps. Misalignment causes defects such as missed contacts, so it is controlled at the nanometer level.

Japanese name: オーバーレイ(重ね合わせ精度)

Field: Lithography

How overlay is measured

Overlay is checked with dedicated targets, usually placed in the scribe lines between dies, that combine a feature printed in the previous layer with one printed in the current layer. Image-based tools find the center of each pattern and report the offset between them, while diffraction-based tools infer the shift from intensity differences in light diffracted by stacked gratings. Measurements from many sites are fitted to a wafer-level model, and the correction terms are fed back to the exposure tool so that later lots are printed closer to the ideal position.

Where misregistration comes from

Error accumulates from many sources: stage and reticle positioning, lens distortion, differences between the tools that exposed each layer, and distortion of the wafer itself. Film stress bows the wafer, high-temperature steps can introduce slip dislocations, and temperature matters directly: silicon expands by about 2.6 ppm/K, so a 0.1 °C change moves the edge of a 300 mm wafer by tens of nanometers. Scanners therefore measure alignment marks on every wafer and correct translation, rotation, magnification and higher-order terms, and the exposure area is held within about ±0.1 °C.

Typical overlay budgets

A common rule is that overlay must be held to roughly a quarter or a fifth of the minimum feature size, which means a few nanometers or less at advanced nodes. The budget is tightest in multiple patterning. In litho-etch-litho-etch (LELE) double patterning, any shift of the second exposure changes the spacing to neighboring lines directly: a 2 nm error on a 20 nm space is a 10% change in spacing, affecting wire capacitance and short risk. Spacer-based self-aligned double patterning (SADP) avoids this because line positions are set by deposition, not by a second alignment.

Overlay vs. CD, and design for misalignment

Overlay describes where a pattern lands; critical dimension (CD) describes how large it is. Both consume the same edge-placement margin, since a contact can miss its target because it is misplaced or because it is too small. Designers and process engineers buy back margin with self-aligned schemes. In a self-aligned contact (SAC), the gate is capped with silicon nitride, so a misaligned contact etch stops on the nitride instead of shorting to the gate. This lets gate-to-contact spacing shrink without demanding impossible alignment.

Answer: B. How accurately a new pattern can be positioned relative to the pattern formed in a previous step

An IC stacks dozens of patterned layers, so each must align precisely to the previous ones; overlay accuracy measures how small that misregistration is. Requirements run to about a quarter or fifth of the linewidth—a few nm or less (a dozen-odd atoms) at advanced nodes. Alignment marks on the wafer are measured to correct position, rotation and expansion, with thermal distortion of mask and wafer and stresses from prior steps also modeled.

Answer: C. Dedicated overlay measurement marks

To measure overlay, dedicated targets combining patterns from the previous and current layers are placed in scribe lines and similar areas. Optical tools measure the offset between the centers of the two patterns, or derive it from differences in diffraction from stacked gratings. Results, mapped across the wafer, are fed back to the scanner to correct subsequent lots. Bond pads and notches serve other purposes.

FAQ

What is the difference between overlay and alignment in lithography?

Alignment is the act of measuring wafer marks and positioning the wafer before exposure; overlay is the resulting placement error between the new layer and earlier layers, measured afterwards on dedicated targets.

How tight is overlay at advanced nodes?

Requirements are roughly one quarter to one fifth of the smallest feature size, which works out to a few nanometers or less, and even tighter between the two exposures of LELE double patterning.

Why does temperature affect overlay?

Wafers, reticles and tool structures expand with temperature; for silicon at about 2.6 ppm/K, a 0.1 °C change shifts features at the edge of a 300 mm wafer by tens of nanometers, which is larger than the overlay budget.
