What is Airborne molecular contamination (AMC)?

Gaseous contaminants in the air such as acids, alkalis and organics. Particle filters cannot remove them, so chemical filters are used.

Japanese name: 分子状汚染(AMC)

Main classes of AMC

Molecular contaminants are commonly grouped into acids, bases, condensable organics and dopants. Acids include hydrogen fluoride, hydrogen chloride, sulfur oxides and nitrogen oxides; bases include ammonia and amines. Condensables are high-boiling organic compounds, such as siloxanes and plasticizers outgassed from polymers, that stick to surfaces. Dopants are boron- or phosphorus-containing species, for example boron released from glass-fiber filter media or organophosphate flame retardants. Each class harms different steps, so fabs set separate limits for each class at critical locations.

How AMC damages processes

Bases are the classic problem in lithography: chemically amplified resists rely on a small amount of photogenerated acid, and airborne ammonia or amines neutralize it at the resist surface, causing T-shaped line tops or incomplete development. Acids corrode metal lines and pads. Organic films on wafers degrade gate-oxide quality, film adhesion and wafer bonding. Dopant species shift doping in sensitive layers, and ammonium and sulfate compounds can form haze on photomasks and optics, reducing transmission over time.

Chemical filtration

HEPA and ULPA filters stop particles but let gas molecules pass, because gas molecules are far too small to be captured mechanically by the fibers. Chemical filters remove AMC by adsorption or reaction, using activated carbon for organics, media impregnated with reagents that neutralize acids or bases, or ion-exchange materials. Each filter type targets particular species and has finite capacity, so filters are monitored and replaced periodically. They are installed at outside-air intakes, in recirculation paths and inside critical tools such as lithography scanners.

Sources and control beyond filters

AMC comes from outside air, process chemicals and exhaust leaks, outgassing from construction materials, sealants, cables and plastics, cleaning agents and people. Control therefore also relies on choosing low-outgassing materials, keeping chemical areas isolated and under correct pressure, and shielding wafers in closed carriers such as front-opening unified pods (FOUPs), which may be purged with nitrogen. Concentrations are monitored with methods such as impinger sampling with ion chromatography for ions and thermal desorption gas chromatography-mass spectrometry for organics.

Answer: B. Chemical filter (using activated carbon or ion exchangers)

ULPA and HEPA filters capture particles but let gas molecules through. Chemical filters use activated carbon, media impregnated with reagents that react with acids or bases, or ion-exchange resins to adsorb or react away molecular contaminants. Types are chosen for the target species, and because their capacity is finite they are replaced periodically. They are used at outside-air intakes and in critical tools such as scanners.

FAQ

Can a HEPA filter remove airborne molecular contamination?

No. HEPA and ULPA filters capture particles, but gas molecules pass through them; chemical filters based on activated carbon, impregnated media or ion exchangers are needed.

Why is ammonia a problem in semiconductor lithography?

Chemically amplified resists depend on photogenerated acid, and ammonia or amines in the air neutralize that acid at the resist surface, distorting the developed pattern.

What are the main sources of AMC in a cleanroom?

Outside air, chemical handling and exhaust leaks, outgassing from building and tool materials, cleaning agents and personnel.
