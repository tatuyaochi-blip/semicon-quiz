What is Velocity saturation?

Under strong electric fields, carrier drift velocity stops increasing in proportion to the field and levels off at about 1×10⁷ cm/s. In short-channel MOSFETs it makes drain current smaller than the square law predicts.

Japanese name: 速度飽和

What is the drift velocity of electrons with a mobility of 1000 cm²/V·s in an electric field of 100 V/cm?

  1. A1×10³ cm/s
  2. B1×10⁵ cm/s
  3. C1×10⁷ cm/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1×10⁵ cm/s

Drift velocity is v = μE, so 1000 cm²/V·s × 100 V/cm = 1×10⁵ cm/s. The units work out as cm²/V·s × V/cm = cm/s, which is a handy check. The proportionality holds only at low fields; for electrons in silicon the increase slows above roughly 10⁴ V/cm and the velocity saturates at about 1×10⁷ cm/s (velocity saturation). The channels of scaled MOSFETs operate in this high-field regime.

What happens to the drift velocity of electrons in silicon when a strong electric field is applied?

  1. AIt keeps increasing in proportion to the field
  2. BIt approaches a certain value (about 1×10⁷ cm/s) and hardly increases further
  3. CIt decreases as the field becomes stronger
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It approaches a certain value (about 1×10⁷ cm/s) and hardly increases further

At low fields drift velocity is proportional to field, but at high fields carriers lose energy by emitting optical phonons, and electron velocity in silicon levels off at about 1×10⁷ cm/s (velocity saturation). As a result, short-channel MOSFET current becomes roughly linear in gate voltage rather than quadratic. In GaAs, electrons at high field transfer to a heavier valley and slow down, an effect used for Gunn-diode oscillators.
