What is Velocity saturation?

Under strong electric fields, carrier drift velocity stops increasing in proportion to the field and levels off at about 1×10⁷ cm/s. In short-channel MOSFETs it makes drain current smaller than the square law predicts.

Japanese name: 速度飽和

Velocity saturation appears in 4 practice questions across 2 topics, including Semiconductor physics, MOSFET.

Field: MOSFET

The physics behind it

At low electric field, carrier drift velocity is v = μE, so 1000 cm²/V·s at 100 V/cm gives 1×10⁵ cm/s. As the field rises, carriers gain energy faster than ordinary scattering removes it, until they lose energy mainly by emitting optical phonons. The velocity then levels off. For electrons in silicon the departure from linearity becomes noticeable above roughly 10⁴ V/cm, and the saturated velocity is about 1×10⁷ cm/s at room temperature; holes saturate at a somewhat lower velocity. A common empirical fit is v = μE/(1 + E/Ec), with critical field Ec = vsat/μ.

What it does to MOSFET current

Lateral fields in short channels are large: 1 V across 50 nm is 2×10⁵ V/cm, well beyond the critical field. A long-channel MOSFET follows the square law, ID = (1/2)μCox(W/L)(VGS − Vth)². When carriers are fully velocity saturated, the current approaches ID ≈ W·Cox·vsat·(VGS − Vth), linear in overdrive and nearly independent of channel length. Real devices sit between the two limits, which the alpha-power law captures with an exponent between 1 and 2, closer to 1 for very short channels.

Velocity saturation vs. pinch-off

In a long-channel device, current saturates when VDS reaches VGS − Vth and the channel pinches off at the drain end. In a short-channel device, carriers reach saturation velocity first, so the current saturates at a smaller VDS than VGS − Vth. Transconductance stops rising with overdrive and tends toward W·Cox·vsat, and shortening the gate gives less extra current than simple scaling predicts. Mobility degradation and parasitic source and drain resistance add further deviations, so practical design relies on compact models such as BSIM rather than the square law.

Other materials

Saturation velocity is a key material figure of merit for high-frequency and power devices. GaN's electron saturation velocity, about 2.5×10⁷ cm/s, contributes to its strength in high-frequency power transistors. GaAs behaves differently: at high field, electrons transfer to a higher, heavier conduction-band valley and actually slow down, giving a region of negative differential mobility that Gunn diodes exploit to build microwave oscillators.

Answer: B. 1×10⁵ cm/s

Drift velocity is v = μE, so 1000 cm²/V·s × 100 V/cm = 1×10⁵ cm/s. The units work out as cm²/V·s × V/cm = cm/s, which is a handy check. The proportionality holds only at low fields; for electrons in silicon the increase slows above roughly 10⁴ V/cm and the velocity saturates at about 1×10⁷ cm/s (velocity saturation). The channels of scaled MOSFETs operate in this high-field regime.

As a result, short-channel MOSFET current becomes roughly linear in gate voltage rather than quadratic. FAQ

What is the saturation velocity of electrons in silicon?

About 1×10⁷ cm/s at room temperature. Drift velocity stops being proportional to field at fields above roughly 10⁴ V/cm and approaches this value.

Why does velocity saturation make MOSFET drain current linear in gate voltage?

Once carriers move at a fixed maximum velocity, current equals channel charge times that velocity. The channel charge is proportional to VGS − Vth, so the current becomes roughly linear rather than quadratic in overdrive.

What is the difference between velocity saturation and pinch-off?

Pinch-off is the long-channel mechanism in which the channel vanishes at the drain end once VDS reaches VGS − Vth. Velocity saturation is a field-driven limit on carrier speed that makes short-channel current saturate at a lower VDS.
