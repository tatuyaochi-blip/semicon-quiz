What is MTBF (mean time between failures)?

The average interval between failures. For a synchronizer it is the average time between metastable events that fail to resolve and propagate onward, given by MTBF = e^(t_r/τ) ÷ (T_0 × f_clk × f_data). Adding stages lengthens the resolution time t_r and increases it exponentially.

Japanese name: MTBF(平均故障間隔)

MTBF, MTTF and MTTR

Mean time between failures (MTBF) is the average operating time between successive failures of a repairable system. For parts that are not repaired, the matching figure is mean time to failure (MTTF), and the average time to restore service is mean time to repair (MTTR). With a constant failure rate λ, MTBF = 1/λ, and since 1 FIT is one failure per 10⁹ device-hours, 100 FIT corresponds to 10⁷ hours. An average is not a guaranteed life: under a constant failure rate about 63% of units have failed by the time t equals MTBF.

Reliability, not quality

Quality describes whether a product meets specification at shipment, tracked with figures such as DPPM (defective parts per million) or controlled by sampling with an acceptable quality level (AQL). Reliability is the time-dependent ability to keep working under stated conditions, expressed with FIT, MTTF and MTBF. Quality can be checked by testing finished parts, but reliability has to be predicted, usually from accelerated stress tests at elevated temperature or voltage whose results are extrapolated to use conditions.

MTBF of a synchronizer

In digital design MTBF has a special meaning: the average time between metastable events that fail to resolve before the next stage samples them. It is approximated by MTBF = e^(t_r/τ) / (T_0 × f_clk × f_data). Here t_r is the resolution time available, roughly a clock period minus overheads; τ is the flip-flop's regeneration time constant; and T_0 relates to the width of its metastability window. More sampling events from a faster clock or a busier data input reduce MTBF linearly, while t_r acts exponentially.

Working with the numbers

Because t_r sits in the exponent, adding one synchronizer stage, which adds roughly one clock period, typically multiplies MTBF by an astronomical factor; with τ = 20 ps, one extra nanosecond gives e^50, more than 10^21. Doubling the receive clock from 500 MHz to 1 GHz halves the period and cuts MTBF by far more than two. Failure rates add for parts in series, so a chip with many synchronizers has a system MTBF lower than any single one, and well-designed crossings aim for thousands of years or more.

MTBF (mean time between failures) is the average operating time between failures of a system that is repaired and kept in service; higher means more reliable. Mean time from failure to completed repair is MTTR, and mean time to failure for non-repaired parts is MTTF. Because MTBF is an average, it does not mean nothing fails before then, nor is it the shortest time to first failure.

Answer: A. The time available for metastability to resolve increases by about one period, and MTBF grows exponentially

Synchronizer MTBF is approximately MTBF = e^(t_r/τ) ÷ (T_0 × f_clk × f_data), where t_r is the time available for metastability to resolve and τ and T_0 are constants determined by the flip-flop's characteristics. Adding a stage extends t_r by about one period. For example, with τ = 20 ps, increasing t_r by 1 ns multiplies MTBF by e^50, more than 10^21 times. The probability of the first stage going metastable is unchanged, but the probability that it resolves before propagating further rises sharply.

FAQ

Does an MTBF of 1,000,000 hours mean a device will last 114 years?

No. MTBF describes a population's failure rate during its useful life, not the lifetime of a single unit. With a constant failure rate, about 63% of units would have failed by one MTBF, and wear-out mechanisms usually end life much earlier.

How do you convert FIT to MTBF?

Under a constant failure rate, MTBF in hours equals 10⁹ divided by the FIT value. For example, 50 FIT corresponds to 2×10⁷ hours.

What is the difference between MTBF and MTTF?

MTBF applies to repairable systems and measures average operating time between failures, while MTTF applies to non-repairable parts and measures average time until their single failure. For a chip that is replaced rather than repaired, MTTF is the appropriate term.
