What is Step coverage?

A measure of how uniformly a film covers the sidewalls and bottoms of steps and holes compared with flat areas. ALD gives excellent step coverage.

Japanese name: ステップカバレッジ

Which correctly describes step coverage in deposition?

  1. AHow thick the film deposited on the sidewalls and bottom of a step is compared with flat areas
  2. BThe electrical resistance of the film
  3. CThe deposition rate per minute
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. How thick the film deposited on the sidewalls and bottom of a step is compared with flat areas

Step coverage expresses how much film lands on the sidewalls and bottoms of trenches and holes relative to flat areas, e.g., bottom thickness ÷ top thickness as a percentage. PVD such as sputtering scores low (below 10% at the bottom of deep holes) because atoms travel in straight lines; CVD scores higher as surface reactions wrap around features; ALD reaches nearly 100%. For barriers and gate films that must be thin and uniform in tiny holes, this metric drives the choice of method.

Under what condition does CVD give good step coverage (conformality)?

  1. ASurface-reaction-limited conditions (such as relatively low temperature and low pressure)
  2. BConditions limited by the supply of source gas
  3. CConditions with source gas blown at high speed from one direction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Surface-reaction-limited conditions (such as relatively low temperature and low pressure)

Under reaction-limited conditions, precursor gas reaches deep into trenches before reacting slowly at the surface, so deposition rate varies little with location and sidewalls and bottoms are coated uniformly. Lower temperatures slow the reaction and lower pressures speed gas diffusion, both pushing toward this regime. Under supply-limited conditions, by contrast, openings where gas arrives easily get thicker film and tend to pinch off.
