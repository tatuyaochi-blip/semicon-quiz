What is Selective epitaxial growth?

A method that grows crystal only where silicon is exposed, not on areas covered by insulating film. It is used for SiGe in transistor sources and drains and elsewhere.

Japanese name: 選択エピタキシャル成長

Field: Epitaxial growth

How selectivity is achieved

Selective epitaxy is usually done by chemical vapor deposition with chlorine-containing chemistry, for example dichlorosilane with added hydrogen chloride (HCl). On exposed silicon, adatoms join the crystal readily; on oxide or nitride, nucleation is much slower, and the HCl etches away the small nuclei before they can grow into polycrystalline deposits. Selectivity holds only within a window of temperature, pressure and gas ratios, and some processes alternate short deposition and etch steps to keep the dielectric surfaces clean.

Uses in advanced CMOS

Advanced CMOS uses selective epitaxy to fill etched source/drain recesses. Embedded SiGe in p-type transistors compresses the channel and raises hole mobility, while phosphorus-doped silicon is used on the n-type side; heavy in-situ doping also lowers resistance. On FinFETs and nanosheet transistors, the epitaxial source/drain grows on the exposed fin or sheet ends and forms raised regions that give room for low-resistance contacts. Silicon photonics uses selective germanium growth in oxide windows to build photodetectors.

Process sensitivities

The silicon surface must be free of native oxide, so wafers receive a dilute HF-last clean and often an in-situ hydrogen bake before growth. Growth rate and composition can vary with the local ratio of exposed silicon to dielectric, a pattern-loading effect that designers and process engineers must account for. Crystal facets, often along {111} or {311} planes, form at the edges of the mask opening, and any loss of selectivity leaves polysilicon nodules on the dielectric that become defects.

Selective growth vs. blanket epitaxy

Blanket epitaxy covers the whole wafer and is used for uniform layers, such as epi wafers or the Si/SiGe superlattice that is later patterned into nanosheets. Selective epitaxy grows only in openings, avoiding extra patterning and etch steps. If selective growth continues past the top of the mask window, the crystal spreads sideways over the dielectric; deliberately exploited, this is epitaxial lateral overgrowth, which can also reduce defect density in mismatched materials.

Answer: C. Growing crystal only where silicon is exposed and not on insulating films

Selective epitaxy grows crystal only where silicon is exposed, often adding HCl so nuclei forming on oxide or nitride are etched away. Advanced CMOS uses it to embed SiGe or SiP in source/drain regions, straining the channel and lowering resistance, to form raised source/drains on FinFETs, and to build the stacked layers for GAA nanosheets.

FAQ

Why is HCl added in selective epitaxial growth?

HCl etches the small nuclei that form on oxide or nitride before they grow, so deposition continues only on exposed silicon where crystal growth is fast.

What is selective epitaxial growth used for?

Mainly for embedded SiGe and doped silicon source/drain regions in CMOS, raised source/drains on FinFETs and nanosheets, and germanium photodetectors in silicon photonics.

What is the difference between selective epitaxy and epitaxial lateral overgrowth?

Selective epitaxy grows only in the exposed openings; epitaxial lateral overgrowth continues the growth so the crystal spreads sideways over the mask.
