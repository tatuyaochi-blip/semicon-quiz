What is Refresh?

The periodic reading and rewriting of DRAM data before the charge in its capacitors is lost through leakage.

Japanese name: リフレッシュ

Refresh appears in 22 practice questions across 5 topics, including DRAM, SRAM and flash memory, Power Management.

Field: DRAM

Why DRAM needs refresh

A DRAM cell stores a bit as charge on a small capacitor reached through one access transistor (a 1T1C cell). Even when the transistor is off, charge leaks away through its subthreshold current and through junction leakage at the storage node, including gate-induced drain leakage (GIDL) and defect-assisted generation. Retention time varies from cell to cell, and a small tail of the weakest cells sets the refresh period. Standards therefore require every row to be refreshed within about 64 ms in the normal temperature range. SRAM, by contrast, holds data in a cross-coupled latch and needs no refresh while powered.

How refresh is performed

Reading a DRAM cell is destructive: the cell shares its charge with the much larger bit-line capacitance, and the sense amplifier resolves a difference of tens of millivolts while writing the full level back. Simply activating a row and closing it therefore refreshes every cell in that row. In DDR4, the controller issues 8,192 refresh commands per 64 ms window, one about every 7.8 µs (tREFI), each refreshing several rows internally. Each command keeps the bank busy for tRFC, which grows with density to several hundred nanoseconds for 16 Gbit devices.

Temperature, capacity and power

Leakage rises with temperature, and retention roughly halves for every 10 °C as a rule of thumb, so DDR standards halve the refresh interval above 85 °C, to 3.9 µs in DDR4. Larger chips have more rows to refresh, so refresh eats a growing share of time and power, costing several percent up to about 10 percent of performance in dense devices; per-bank refresh keeps other banks available meanwhile. During standby, self-refresh lets the DRAM refresh itself from an internal timer while the controller sleeps, and low-power DRAM adds temperature-aware and partial-array refresh.

Reliability issues tied to refresh

Some cells show variable retention time (VRT): a defect switching states makes their leakage jump between values, so a cell that passed testing can later become weak; refresh margin and ECC cover this. Row hammer is another threat: activating one row tens of thousands of times within a refresh window can drain charge from neighboring rows and flip bits, which has been exploited in security attacks. DRAMs and controllers counter it by refreshing the neighbors of heavily activated rows, through mechanisms such as target row refresh and DDR5's refresh management commands.

Answer: C. Because the charge stored in the capacitor is gradually lost through leakage current

Charge on the capacitor gradually leaks away through the cell transistor's subthreshold and junction leakage. Refresh reads and rewrites each cell before its data becomes unreadable; every row must be refreshed within 64 ms up to DDR4, and within 32 ms in DDR5. During refresh, the bank cannot be accessed and power is consumed, a burden that grows with capacity. A limit on write cycles is a characteristic of flash memory.

As DRAM capacity grows, what tends to become a problem with respect to refresh?

  1. AMore rows must be refreshed, so a larger share of time and power goes to refresh
  2. BThe refresh period must be lengthened
  3. CRefresh becomes unnecessary
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. More rows must be refreshed, so a larger share of time and power goes to refresh

As capacity grows, more cells must be refreshed in the same 64 ms, lengthening each refresh operation (tRFC). Access is blocked meanwhile, so 16 Gbit-class devices can lose several percent to about 10% of performance, and power rises too. Mitigations include per-bank refresh, which keeps other banks available, and research into refreshing long-retention rows less often.

FAQ

How often does DRAM need to be refreshed?

Standard DDR DRAM must refresh every row within about 64 ms at normal temperatures, spread across 8,192 refresh commands. Above 85 °C the interval is typically halved.

Does SRAM need refresh?

No. SRAM stores each bit in a cross-coupled latch that holds its state actively as long as power is applied. DRAM needs refresh because it stores bits as charge on a leaky capacitor.

What is DRAM self-refresh mode?

It is a low-power mode in which the DRAM refreshes itself using an internal timer, so the external clock and memory controller can be stopped. It preserves data while a phone or PC sleeps.
