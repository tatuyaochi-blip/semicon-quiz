What is Debouncing?

Processing that removes switch bounce, by software that accepts a change only after repeated identical readings or by hardware filtering.

Japanese name: デバウンス

Debouncing appears in 2 practice questions across 2 topics, including Analog and electronic circuits, Embedded Systems & Firmware.

Try it: questions about Debouncing

In the step response of an RC circuit, how long does it take for the capacitor voltage to reach about 63% of its final value?

  1. ARC / 2
  2. BRC (the time constant)
  3. C5RC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. RC (the time constant)

The capacitor voltage in an RC circuit follows V(1 − e^(−t/RC)), reaching 1 − 1/e ≈ 63% at t = RC. This RC is the time constant τ: about 86% at 2τ, 95% at 3τ and over 99% at 5τ, so charging is considered complete after 5τ. For 10 kΩ and 1 µF, τ = 10 ms. It is used to estimate reset-delay circuits, switch debouncing and flash charging times.

What is the phenomenon called in which a mechanical switch's contacts bounce, turning on and off many times in a short period when pressed?

  1. ALatch-up
  2. BCrosstalk
  3. CContact bounce (chattering)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Contact bounce (chattering)

When a mechanical switch closes, the metal contacts bounce and turn on and off for a few milliseconds. A microcontroller reads its pins very fast, so counting naively would register one press as several. The bounce is removed in software by waiting and rereading, or in hardware with a capacitor or Schmitt trigger to smooth the waveform. This processing is called debouncing.
