What is Miller effect?

The effect by which a capacitance between the input and output of an inverting amplifier appears (1 + gain) times larger from the input. It degrades the high-frequency response of common-emitter and common-source stages.

Japanese name: ミラー効果

  2. BThe emitter resistance changes with temperature
  3. CThe collector current fluctuates due to thermal noise
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The base-collector capacitance appears, from the input, as a much larger capacitance in proportion to the voltage gain

In a common-emitter stage the collector swings strongly opposite to the base, so the base-collector capacitance Cμ appears about (1 + |Av|) times larger from the input. With Cμ = 2 pF and a gain of 100 it looks like about 200 pF, forming a low-pass filter with the source resistance and narrowing the bandwidth. Remedies include a cascode, which stacks a common-base stage to take the gain at the collector side, and lowering the source resistance.
