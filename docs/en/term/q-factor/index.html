What is Q factor (quality factor)?

A dimensionless quantity expressing the sharpness of resonance. It is the resonant frequency divided by the bandwidth, Q = f0/BW, and also corresponds to the ratio of the energy stored to the energy lost per cycle. For a series RLC circuit Q = (1/R)√(L/C), and for a parallel RLC circuit Q = R√(C/L); the smaller the losses, the larger Q. Crystal resonators and MEMS resonators reach tens of thousands or more.

Japanese name: Q値(クオリティファクタ)

Answer: B. Low loss and a sharp resonant peak (narrow bandwidth)

Q expresses the sharpness of resonance: Q = f0/Δf (resonant frequency ÷ bandwidth). Higher Q means lower loss, a sharper peak and a narrower bandwidth; f0 = 1 MHz with Q = 100 gives 10 kHz of bandwidth. For a series RLC circuit, Q = (1/R)√(L/C), so lower resistance (loss) raises it. Highly selective filters and low-phase-noise oscillators require high Q.
