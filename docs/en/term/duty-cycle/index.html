What is Duty cycle?

The fraction of each period during which a periodic pulse signal is high. In PWM it sets the average power.

Japanese name: デューティ比

Duty cycle appears in 6 practice questions across 5 topics, including Power semiconductors, Embedded Systems & Firmware, Logic circuits.

Field: Embedded Systems & Firmware

Try it: questions about Duty cycle

In an ideal step-down chopper (buck converter) with an input voltage of 12 V and a switch duty cycle of 0.25, what is the output voltage?

  1. A3 V
  2. B9 V
  3. C48 V
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 3 V

An ideal buck converter outputs input voltage × duty cycle: 12 V × 0.25 = 3 V. Varying the switch's on-time ratio adjusts the output, and an inductor and capacitor average it into smooth DC. Synchronous rectification, using a MOSFET instead of a diode, pushes efficiency above 90%. The low supply voltages of CPUs and memory are produced by multiphase buck converters on the motherboard.

A PWM signal has a period of 1000 counts, with the output high (1) for 250 counts of each period. What is the duty cycle?

  1. A75%
  2. B25%
  3. C250%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 25%

The duty cycle is the fraction of each period during which the output is high: 250 ÷ 1000 = 25%. PWM (pulse-width modulation) adjusts average voltage or power by changing the high time while keeping the period constant. A microcontroller timer switches the output by comparing its counter with a compare value, so it can produce PWM continuously without CPU involvement.
