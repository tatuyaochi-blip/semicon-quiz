What is Duty cycle?

The fraction of each period during which a periodic pulse signal is high. In PWM it sets the average power.

Japanese name: デューティ比

Duty cycle appears in 8 practice questions across 6 topics, including Power semiconductors, Timing analysis and CDC, Embedded Systems & Firmware.

Try it: questions about Duty cycle

In an ideal step-down chopper (buck converter) with an input voltage of 12 V and a switch duty cycle of 0.25, what is the output voltage?

  1. A3 V
  2. B9 V
  3. C48 V
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 3 V

An ideal buck converter outputs input voltage × duty cycle: 12 V × 0.25 = 3 V. Varying the switch's on-time ratio adjusts the output, and an inductor and capacitor average it into smooth DC. Synchronous rectification, using a MOSFET instead of a diode, pushes efficiency above 90%. The low supply voltages of CPUs and memory are produced by multiphase buck converters on the motherboard.

With a 2.0 ns clock at 50% duty cycle, a path launches on the rising edge and captures on the falling edge. With a setup time of 0.1 ns and zero skew, what is the maximum allowed sum of the clock-to-output delay and combinational logic delay?

  1. A1.9 ns
  2. B1.0 ns
  3. C0.9 ns
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 0.9 ns

A path launched on the rising edge and captured on the falling edge is called a half-cycle path. The capture edge comes half a period, 1.0 ns, after launch, so the allowed delay is 1.0 − 0.1 = 0.9 ns. 1.9 ns assumes a full period is available, and 1.0 ns forgets to subtract the setup time. A half-cycle path is tight for setup, but hold is checked against the falling edge half a period earlier, which gives a large hold margin.
