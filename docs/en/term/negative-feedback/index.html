What is Negative feedback?

Returning part of the output to the input with opposite polarity. Gain drops, but it becomes stable, and distortion and bandwidth improve.

Japanese name: 負帰還

Try it: questions about Negative feedback

What is the effect of applying negative feedback to an amplifier?

  1. AGain is reduced, but gain stability improves, bandwidth widens and distortion decreases
  2. BGain becomes infinite
  3. CIt always starts to oscillate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gain is reduced, but gain stability improves, bandwidth widens and distortion decreases

Negative feedback subtracts part of the output from the input, reducing gain. In return, closed-loop gain becomes about 1/β (the inverse of the feedback fraction), largely independent of device variation and temperature; bandwidth widens; and distortion and output impedance shrink by a factor of (1 + loop gain). Op-amp circuits are built on this. At frequencies where the phase shifts enough to make feedback positive, however, the loop oscillates, so phase margin must be secured.

In an ideal op-amp circuit with negative feedback, what is the property called by which the two input terminals have almost the same voltage?

  1. ASlew rate
  2. BOffset
  3. CVirtual short
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Virtual short

When an op-amp with huge open-loop gain A has negative feedback, the output settles at a finite value, so the voltage between its inputs is Vout/A—essentially zero. The inputs look shorted without being connected, hence "virtual short"; with the non-inverting input grounded, the inverting input sits at 0 V (virtual ground). This is the foundation of op-amp analysis, but it fails when the output saturates or in positive-feedback circuits such as comparators.
