What is Thermal resistance?

A measure of how hard it is for heat to flow, in °C/W. Multiplying dissipated power by thermal resistance gives the temperature rise. Junction-to-ambient thermal resistance is written θJA.

Japanese name: 熱抵抗

Try it: questions about Thermal resistance

If a power device with a thermal resistance of 2 °C/W dissipates 10 W, what is the temperature rise?

  1. A5 °C
  2. B12 °C
  3. C20 °C
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 20 °C

Temperature rise is thermal resistance × power: 2 °C/W × 10 W = 20 °C, so at 50 °C ambient the junction reaches 70 °C. In practice the junction-to-case, case-to-heatsink and heatsink-to-air resistances are in series and are summed. Thermal resistance behaves like electrical resistance (a thermal Ohm's law), forming the basis of cooling design that keeps junction temperature within limits.

Which symbol denotes the thermal resistance from the chip junction to the surrounding air, indicating a package's heat-dissipation performance?

  1. AθJA (Rth(j-a))
  2. BCpk
  3. CFIT
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. θJA (Rth(j-a))

θJA is the thermal resistance from the chip junction to the ambient air, in °C/W. Junction temperature is Tj = Ta + P×θJA; a chip dissipating 2 W in a package with θJA = 40 °C/W runs 80 °C above ambient. Because θJA depends on the board and airflow, the junction-to-case value θJC is often given too. Cpk is a process capability index and FIT is a unit of failure rate.
