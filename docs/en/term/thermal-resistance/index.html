What is Thermal resistance?

A measure of how hard it is for heat to flow, in °C/W. Multiplying dissipated power by thermal resistance gives the temperature rise. Junction-to-ambient thermal resistance is written θJA.

Japanese name: 熱抵抗

Thermal resistance appears in 10 practice questions across 5 topics, including Power semiconductors, How to read datasheets, Packaging and assembly.

Field: Packaging and assembly

The thermal Ohm's law

Thermal resistance treats heat flow like current in a circuit: power plays the role of current, temperature difference plays the role of voltage, and the temperature rise across a path is power multiplied by thermal resistance. Because only differences matter, °C/W and K/W are the same unit. Resistances along one path add in series, so a device on a heatsink sees junction-to-case, case-to-heatsink and heatsink-to-air terms summed. Parallel heat paths, such as heat leaving through both the package top and the board, combine like parallel resistors and lower the total.

θJA, θJC and ψJT

θJA is junction-to-ambient resistance measured on a standardized test board in still air, so it depends strongly on board copper area, layer count and airflow, and is best used for comparing packages. θJC is junction-to-case resistance measured with the case held at a fixed temperature, and is the starting point when a heatsink is attached. ψJT is not a true resistance but a characterization parameter: it relates junction temperature to the measured top-of-package temperature when most heat actually flows into the board, so Tj ≈ Ttop + P × ψJT.

Worked numbers

A transistor dissipating 0.5 W in a small TO-92 package with θJA around 200 °C/W rises about 100 °C above ambient, which can exceed a typical 150 °C junction limit on a warm day. A power device with θJC = 1.0 °C/W, 0.5 °C/W of thermal grease and a 3.0 °C/W heatsink has 4.5 °C/W in total, so 10 W of loss raises the junction 45 °C above ambient. A 50 W processor on a 0.5 K/W cooler runs about 25 °C above the surrounding air.

Steady state vs. transients, and reliability

Thermal resistance describes steady state. Real packages also store heat, so for short power pulses designers use transient thermal impedance curves, which start low and approach the steady-state value as pulse length grows. Over a product's life, thermal resistance can increase: in power modules, repeated heating and cooling cracks solder layers and lifts bond wires because silicon, aluminum and copper expand at different rates. Heat spreaders, thermal interface materials (TIMs) and better die attach keep resistance low, and lower junction temperature generally extends lifetime.

Try it: questions about Thermal resistance

If a power device with a thermal resistance of 2 °C/W dissipates 10 W, what is the temperature rise?

  1. A5 °C
  2. B12 °C
  3. C20 °C
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 20 °C

Temperature rise is thermal resistance × power: 2 °C/W × 10 W = 20 °C, so at 50 °C ambient the junction reaches 70 °C. In practice the junction-to-case, case-to-heatsink and heatsink-to-air resistances are in series and are summed. Thermal resistance behaves like electrical resistance (a thermal Ohm's law), forming the basis of cooling design that keeps junction temperature within limits.

Which symbol denotes the thermal resistance from the chip junction to the surrounding air, indicating a package's heat-dissipation performance?

  1. AθJA (Rth(j-a))
  2. BCpk
  3. CFIT
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. θJA (Rth(j-a))

θJA is the thermal resistance from the chip junction to the ambient air, in °C/W. Junction temperature is Tj = Ta + P×θJA; a chip dissipating 2 W in a package with θJA = 40 °C/W runs 80 °C above ambient. Because θJA depends on the board and airflow, the junction-to-case value θJC is often given too. Cpk is a process capability index and FIT is a unit of failure rate.

FAQ

How do you calculate junction temperature from thermal resistance?

Add the ambient temperature to power times the junction-to-ambient thermal resistance: Tj = Ta + P × θ. For example, 2 W in a 40 °C/W package runs 80 °C above ambient.

Can I use the datasheet θJA for my own board?

Only as a rough guide. It is measured on a standard test board in still air, and a real board with different copper area, layer count or airflow can change it by up to around a factor of two, so verify by measurement.

Is °C/W the same as K/W?

Yes. Thermal resistance relates a temperature difference to power, and a difference of 1 °C equals a difference of 1 K.
