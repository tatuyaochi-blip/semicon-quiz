What is RC delay?

Signal delay determined by the product of wire resistance (R) and capacitance (C). It grows as scaling makes wires thinner, limiting performance.

Japanese name: RC遅延

RC delay appears in 4 practice questions across 1 topic, including Interconnect processes.

Field: Interconnect processes

Where RC delay comes from

A wire has resistance R = ρL/(W × T), set by resistivity, length, width and thickness, and capacitance to neighboring wires and to layers above and below. A signal must charge that capacitance through that resistance. Because both are spread along the line, the 50% delay of a distributed RC wire is roughly 0.4 RC, versus about 0.7 RC for a single lumped resistor and capacitor. As an example, a 1 mm wire with a 100 nm × 100 nm cross-section and ρ = 2 µΩ·cm has about 2 kΩ; with roughly 0.2 pF per mm, RC is around 400 ps.

Why delay grows with length squared

Both R and C scale with length, so their product, and hence delay, scales with length squared: doubling an unbuffered wire roughly quadruples its delay. Breaking a long wire into segments driven by repeaters (buffers) turns this into a roughly linear dependence on length, because each segment is short and the repeater delays add rather than multiply. The price is area and power, which is significant on large chips with very many repeaters, so long nets are also routed on thick upper metal levels with lower resistance per unit length.

Why scaling makes it worse

Shrinking width and thickness raises resistance per unit length, while capacitance per unit length stays roughly constant because spacing shrinks too, so local wires get slower even as transistors get faster. At narrow widths copper's effective resistivity also rises above its bulk value of about 1.7 µΩ·cm due to electron scattering at surfaces and grain boundaries, and barrier and liner layers occupy a growing share of the cross-section. Making wires taller lowers R but enlarges the facing sidewall area and raises coupling capacitance, so aspect ratios around 2 are a compromise.

How the industry fights it

Resistance was cut by replacing aluminum with copper interconnect. Capacitance is cut with low-k interlayer dielectrics: carbon-doped oxide (SiOC) at about 2.7 to 3.0 and porous films at about 2.2 to 2.5, compared with about 3.9 for SiO2, although these softer films complicate CMP and packaging. Design measures include repeater insertion, a wiring hierarchy with thin local levels and thick global levels, and wider spacing on critical nets. Lower capacitance also cuts switching power and crosstalk, not only delay.

FAQ

Why does wire delay increase with the square of length?

A wire's resistance and its capacitance both grow in proportion to its length, and the delay depends on their product. Doubling the length therefore roughly quadruples the RC delay unless repeaters are inserted.

How do low-k dielectrics reduce RC delay?

Capacitance between wires is proportional to the dielectric constant of the insulator between them. Replacing SiO2 (about 3.9) with materials of about 2.2 to 3.0 lowers C and therefore the RC product.

Why are repeaters inserted on long wires?

Repeaters split a long wire into short segments, each with a small RC, so total delay grows roughly linearly with length instead of quadratically. They cost area and power, so they are placed only where needed.
