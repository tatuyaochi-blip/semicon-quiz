What is FP8?

8-bit floating-point formats: E4M3 (4 exponent, 3 significand bits) and E5M2 (5 exponent, 2 significand bits), chosen to trade precision against range. Used to make AI training and inference faster and more power-efficient.

How are the two FP8 formats, E4M3 (4 exponent, 3 significand bits) and E5M2 (5 exponent, 2 significand bits), usually used?

  1. AE4M3 for weights and activations, E5M2 for gradients
  2. BBoth have the same precision and range, so they are used interchangeably
  3. CE4M3 is used as an integer and E5M2 as a floating-point number
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. E4M3 for weights and activations, E5M2 for gradients

How the scarce 8 bits are split between exponent and significand determines the format's character. E4M3 has one more significand bit, giving more precision but less range; E5M2 has one more exponent bit, giving more range but less precision. E4M3 is therefore typically used for weights and activations, whose magnitudes are fairly uniform, and E5M2 for gradients, whose magnitudes vary widely. Both are floating-point formats, not integers.
