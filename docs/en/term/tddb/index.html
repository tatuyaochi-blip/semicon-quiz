What is TDDB (time-dependent dielectric breakdown)?

A phenomenon in which an insulating film under sustained voltage eventually breaks down even below its breakdown voltage. It is used to evaluate the lifetime of gate oxides and the like.

Japanese name: TDDB(経時絶縁破壊)

What is the time-dependent dielectric breakdown of the insulator between wires called, which is becoming more important as wire spacing shrinks?

  1. ABEOL TDDB (inter-wire TDDB)
  2. BNBTI
  3. CHCI
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. BEOL TDDB (inter-wire TDDB)

As wire spacing shrinks to around ten-odd nm, even about 0.7 V produces fields of hundreds of kV/cm or more across the dielectric, risking breakdown over long use. Low-k films are less robust than conventional oxide, and copper-ion migration and post-CMP interface damage also contribute. BEOL TDDB is therefore a key reliability metric, and local spacing reductions from via misalignment are tightly controlled.

Which correctly describes TDDB (time-dependent dielectric breakdown)?

  1. AA phenomenon in which current moves metal atoms in wiring
  2. BA phenomenon in which solder joints fatigue under repeated temperature changes
  3. CA phenomenon in which a gate dielectric under a continuously applied electric field breaks down after a certain time
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A phenomenon in which a gate dielectric under a continuously applied electric field breaks down after a certain time

TDDB (time-dependent dielectric breakdown) occurs when a field too weak to cause immediate breakdown, applied for a long time, gradually creates defects in the dielectric until they link up and bridge the electrodes, causing breakdown. Thinner gate dielectrics see higher fields, making it a key reliability concern; it also affects low-k dielectrics between wires. Atom migration in wires is electromigration, and solder fatigue is a temperature-cycling failure.
