What is Electroplating?

Depositing metal by passing current through a plating bath with the wafer as the cathode. It fills copper interconnect trenches, with additives making the fill proceed from the bottom up.

Japanese name: 電解めっき

Electroplating appears in 1 practice question across 1 topic, including Interconnect processes.

Field: Deposition (CVD and PVD)

Depositing metal with current

Electroplating immerses the wafer in a bath containing metal ions, makes the wafer the cathode and an opposing electrode the anode, and passes current so that metal ions are reduced and deposited on the wafer surface. For copper interconnects, a bath based on copper sulfate is used, building up copper through the reaction Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. The amount deposited is proportional to the charge passed (Faraday's law), so thickness is controlled by current and time. Because current must flow across the surface, a barrier layer and a copper seed layer are first sputtered onto the dielectric.

Bottom-up fill

Plating narrow trenches and vias in the ordinary way deposits faster at the top corners, pinching off the opening and leaving a void inside. Three additives are therefore added to the bath. The suppressor adsorbs on the surface and slows deposition, while the accelerator speeds it up. At the bottom of a trench, the area shrinks as copper builds up and the accelerator becomes concentrated, so the bottom fills first and fastest. The leveler keeps copper from bulging too much above the trenches once they are filled. This bottom-up filling is also called superfilling.

Film properties and later steps

As-plated copper incorporates additives and has small grains of a few tens of nanometers; even at room temperature the grains grow over hours to days and resistivity drops (self-annealing). So that properties do not depend on how long the wafer waits, the film is annealed after plating to complete grain growth before excess copper is removed by CMP. Also, current flows from contacts at the wafer edge through the thin seed layer, so the seed's resistance tends to make the film thicker near the edge (the terminal effect); tool designs that shape the current distribution and power-supply control keep the film uniform.

Applications and contrast with electroless plating

Because electroplating makes thick metal films quickly and cheaply at room temperature and atmospheric pressure, it is used not only for damascene copper interconnects but widely in back-end processes, filling TSVs with copper and forming copper pillars, solder bumps and redistribution layers (RDL). The related electroless plating passes no external current and deposits metal through a chemical reaction with a reducing agent in the bath. Given a catalytic surface, it can coat isolated patterns that cannot carry current, or insulators, uniformly, but deposition is generally slow, and it is used for thin films of nickel, gold, cobalt and similar metals.

  2. BA conductive layer that carries plating current (seed layer)
  3. CInsulation between layers
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A conductive layer that carries plating current (seed layer)

Electroplating deposits copper by passing current through the wafer surface to reduce copper ions, so a continuous conductive layer must line the trenches. Barrier layers (TaN/Ta) are too resistive for uniform plating initiation, so a thin copper seed tens of nm thick is sputtered on top before plating. In narrow trenches, making the seed thin enough not to pinch off the opening yet continuous to the bottom is difficult, and ruthenium liners are also used.

FAQ

What is electroplating, in simple terms?

A method that makes the wafer the cathode in a plating bath and passes current so that metal ions in the bath are reduced on the surface, forming a metal film. In semiconductors it is widely used to fill trenches and vias for copper interconnects.

Why is a seed layer needed before plating?

Electroplating deposits metal by passing current across the surface, so a continuous conductive layer must line the trenches. Barrier layers are too resistive for plating to start uniformly, so a thin copper seed layer is sputtered on top.

What is the difference between electroplating and electroless plating?

Electroplating deposits metal with current from an external power supply and can build thick films quickly. Electroless plating deposits metal through a chemical reaction with a reducing agent in the bath, needing no current, so it can coat isolated patterns, but it is generally slower.
