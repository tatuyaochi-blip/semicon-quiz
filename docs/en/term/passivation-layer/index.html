What is Passivation layer?

A film formed as the topmost layer of a chip to protect the circuitry from moisture, contamination and mechanical damage. Silicon nitride and polyimide are typical materials.

Japanese name: パッシベーション膜

Passivation layer appears in 1 practice question across 1 topic, including Deposition (CVD and PVD).

Field: Interconnect processes

Role of the passivation layer

The passivation layer is a protective film covering the chip's top metal layer, also called final passivation. It keeps moisture and mobile ions such as sodium from reaching the circuitry, preventing wiring corrosion and shifts in transistor characteristics. It also protects the circuitry from scratches and particles during wafer handling and assembly. Only over the bond pads used for wire bonding or bumps is the film opened by lithography and etching (pad opening) to expose the metal. The layer is essential for surviving high-temperature, high-humidity reliability tests.

Materials and deposition

The typical material is silicon nitride, which is hard and resists moisture and ions, at a thickness of several hundred nanometers to about 1 µm. Because the aluminum or copper wiring underneath cannot tolerate high temperatures, plasma CVD, which deposits films at a low 300–400 °C, is used. Plasma CVD nitride contains a lot of hydrogen and deviates from Si3N4 stoichiometry, but it performs well as a protective film. Oxide and nitride may be stacked, or oxynitride used, to tune film stress and optical transmission. A resin film such as polyimide is often added on top to relieve stress from the package molding compound.

Relation to reliability

Cracks or pinholes in the passivation let moisture in and corrode aluminum wiring and pads. Moisture resistance is therefore checked with temperature-humidity-bias (THB) testing, which applies voltage at 85 °C and 85% RH, and with HAST, which accelerates the test under harsher conditions. Excessive film stress can crack the film itself or cause stress migration, in which metal in the wiring below moves and forms voids. Hydrogen in the nitride can also affect transistor characteristics, so film composition and process order are taken into account in the design.

Other meanings of 'passivation'

In semiconductors, the word passivation is used more broadly for any treatment that makes a surface inactive and stable. For example, forming gas annealing, in which hydrogen terminates unbonded orbitals (dangling bonds) left at the silicon-oxide interface to reduce interface states, is also called passivation. In crystalline silicon solar cells, surface passivation with films such as aluminum oxide, which reduces recombination at the surface, strongly affects efficiency. In GaN HEMTs, nitride films suppress the effect of surface states and reduce current collapse. Forming protective films on sidewalls during dry etching is also called passivation.

  3. CIt conducts electricity like a metal

Answer: A. It contains relatively much hydrogen and tends to be less dense than thermal CVD film

PECVD silicon nitride forms from silane and ammonia at 300–400 °C, so it contains about 10–30% hydrogen as Si-H and N-H bonds and deviates from stoichiometry (SiNx). It is therefore less dense and etches faster than thermal CVD nitride. Still, it blocks moisture and sodium and deposits at low temperature, making it the final passivation layer on chips and a diffusion barrier over copper wiring.

FAQ

Why is silicon nitride so often used for passivation?

It resists moisture and ions such as sodium and is hard and scratch-resistant. Plasma CVD can also deposit it at a low 300–400 °C, so it can cover the top of the chip without damaging the aluminum or copper wiring already formed.

Why is polyimide added on top?

It relieves the stress on the chip surface when the package molding compound shrinks during curing and temperature changes, preventing nitride cracks and shifting of the wiring. It also protects against scratches during assembly. It is sometimes used as a stress-buffer layer under flip-chip bumps as well.

Are surface passivation and a passivation layer the same thing?

Their aims are similar, but they refer to different things. A passivation layer is the protective film covering the top of the chip against moisture, contamination and scratches. Surface passivation is the treatment of defects at semiconductor surfaces and interfaces with hydrogen or films to make them inactive, reducing carrier recombination and interface states; it is important for solar cells and transistor interfaces.
