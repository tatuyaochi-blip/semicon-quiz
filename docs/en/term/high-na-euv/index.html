What is High-NA EUV?

Next-generation EUV lithography that raises the projection optics' numerical aperture from 0.33 to 0.55 to improve resolution.

Japanese name: High-NA EUV

High-NA EUV appears in 3 practice questions across 1 topic, including Lithography.

Field: Lithography

Why raise the numerical aperture

Resolution follows R = k1 × λ / NA, where λ is wavelength and NA is the numerical aperture of the projection optics. EUV already uses the 13.5 nm wavelength, so High-NA EUV improves resolution by raising NA from 0.33 to 0.55, about 1.7 times finer for the same k1. That brings pitches in the low 20 nm range within reach of a single exposure, instead of splitting a layer into several exposures with standard EUV. The wavelength, the reflective mirror optics and the vacuum environment stay the same as in standard EUV.

Anamorphic optics and a half-size field

EUV masks are reflective multilayer mirrors illuminated at an oblique angle. A larger NA would require light cones at angles the mask cannot reflect efficiently with 4× reduction in both directions. High-NA optics therefore reduce by 4× in one direction and 8× in the other, keeping the standard 6-inch mask. The cost is that the exposure field on the wafer shrinks to half of the usual 26 × 33 mm. Dies larger than this half field must be split and stitched from two exposures, or the design must be partitioned, for example into chiplets.

Trade-offs that come with higher NA

Depth of focus shrinks roughly with the square of NA, so higher-NA patterning tolerates less wafer non-flatness and calls for thinner resists. Thinner resists absorb fewer photons, and smaller features contain fewer photons per edge, so stochastic effects such as line edge roughness and random missing or bridging features become harder to control without higher dose, which costs throughput. The tools are larger and far more expensive than standard EUV scanners, so whether High-NA is cheaper than multi-patterning depends on the layer and the number of exposures it replaces.

Answer: A. The numerical aperture of the projection optics was increased from 0.33 to 0.55 to improve resolution

Standard EUV scanners have an NA of 0.33; High-NA EUV raises it to 0.55, improving resolution about 1.7× and enabling single-exposure patterning of pitches in the low 20 nm range at the same 13.5 nm wavelength. Depth of focus shrinks, and because reduction differs by direction, the exposure field is halved. Developed by ASML, each tool was reported as of 2024 to cost on the order of hundreds of millions of dollars.

FAQ

What is the numerical aperture of High-NA EUV?

High-NA EUV uses projection optics with an NA of 0.55, compared with 0.33 for standard EUV scanners, at the same 13.5 nm wavelength.

Why is the High-NA EUV exposure field half size?

The optics use 8× reduction in one direction and 4× in the other to keep mask reflection angles workable with standard 6-inch masks. With 8× in one direction, the same mask covers only half the usual 26 × 33 mm field on the wafer.

Does High-NA EUV use a shorter wavelength?

No. It keeps the 13.5 nm EUV wavelength and improves resolution solely by increasing the numerical aperture of the optics.
