What is Refractive index?

The speed of light in vacuum divided by its speed in a material. At a wavelength of 1.55 µm, silicon is about 3.48 and oxide (SiO2) about 1.45; the larger this contrast, the more tightly light can be confined in a small waveguide.

Japanese name: 屈折率

Field: Silicon photonics

What the number tells you

Refractive index n is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its phase speed in a material. The optical frequency does not change on entering a material, so the wavelength inside shrinks to λ/n: 1.55 µm light has a wavelength of about 0.45 µm in silicon. Index varies with wavelength (dispersion) and temperature, so values must be quoted with conditions. Useful reference points are silicon about 3.48 and SiO2 about 1.45 at 1.55 µm, silicon nitride about 2.0, and water about 1.44 at 193 nm.

Snell's law and total internal reflection

At an interface, n1 sinθ1 = n2 sinθ2. Going from high to low index, rays beyond the critical angle, sinθc = n2/n1, cannot escape and are totally reflected. For silicon to oxide, 1.45 / 3.48 gives θc ≈ 25° from the normal, so most rays are trapped and waveguides can be tiny and sharply bent. In fiber the contrast is under 1%, θc exceeds 80°, and only near-grazing rays are guided. The reverse direction, low to high index, never gives total reflection. A guided mode sees an effective index between the core and cladding values.

Changing the index on purpose

Silicon's thermo-optic coefficient is large, about 1.8×10⁻⁴ per kelvin, which shifts a C-band ring resonance by roughly 0.07–0.08 nm per °C; heaters exploit this for tuning. For fast modulation, silicon lacks the Pockels effect because its crystal is centrosymmetric, so modulators rely on the plasma dispersion effect (free-carrier dispersion): adding electrons and holes lowers the index and raises absorption. The change is small, about 10⁻³ or less, so phase shifters are millimeters long. Lithium niobate, by contrast, has a strong Pockels effect.

Where index shows up in the fab

Index is central to several process tools. Ellipsometry fits the polarization change of reflected light to a model to extract both film thickness and index. FTIR measures epitaxial-layer thickness from interference, relying on the slight infrared index difference that free carriers create between a lightly doped epi layer and a heavily doped substrate. Immersion lithography fills the lens–wafer gap with water so that NA = n sinθ can exceed 1, reaching about 1.35. Silicon oxynitride films have a tunable index and serve as antireflective layers.

Which correctly describes a silicon waveguide (refractive index about 3.5) surrounded by oxide (SiO2, refractive index about 1.45)?

  1. AThe large index contrast lets light leak out easily, so the waveguide can only be bent at radii larger than an optical fiber's, making it unsuitable for integration
  2. BThe large index contrast confines light tightly, so it can be bent at small radii of a few µm and devices can be packed densely
  3. CThe larger the index contrast, the less sensitive it is to sidewall roughness, so propagation loss becomes as low as an optical fiber's
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The large index contrast confines light tightly, so it can be bent at small radii of a few µm and devices can be packed densely

Light is confined by total internal reflection at the boundary between the high-index core and the low-index surroundings (cladding), and the larger the index contrast, the tighter the confinement. The difference between silicon and oxide is about 2, orders of magnitude larger than the core–cladding difference in optical fiber (less than 1%), allowing thin waveguides with cross-sections of about 0.5 µm × 0.22 µm and bend radii down to a few µm. On the other hand, the larger the index contrast, the more sidewall roughness scatters light, so propagation loss—though condition-dependent—is about 1–3 dB/cm for a typical strip waveguide (lower for rib waveguides or low-loss processes), far greater than fiber's (about 0.2 dB/km).

When light travels from silicon (refractive index 3.48) toward oxide (refractive index 1.45), which is closest to the critical angle for total internal reflection (measured from the normal to the interface)?

  1. AAbout 65°
  2. BAbout 25°
  3. CAbout 45°
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. About 25°

The critical angle θc is given by sinθc = n2 / n1; 1.45 ÷ 3.48 ≈ 0.417 gives θc ≈ 24.6°, or about 25°. Light striking the interface at angles greater than 25° from the normal does not exit into the oxide and is totally reflected. Total internal reflection occurs only when light travels from the higher-index side to the lower-index side, not in the reverse direction. In optical fiber, where the index contrast is small, the critical angle is large, 80° or more, so only light striking the boundary at shallow angles is confined. About 65° is the value obtained by mistakenly measuring the angle from the interface.

FAQ

What is the refractive index of silicon at 1550 nm?

About 3.48 at room temperature, rising slightly with temperature at roughly 1.8×10⁻⁴ per kelvin. Silicon dioxide is about 1.45 at the same wavelength.

Why does a higher index contrast allow smaller optical waveguides?

A larger contrast lowers the critical angle and pulls the mode tightly into the core, so the core can be sub-micron and bends of a few µm radius do not radiate. The trade-off is greater sensitivity to sidewall roughness and dimensional variation.

What is effective index?

It is the index that a guided mode experiences as a whole, defined from its propagation constant, and it always lies between the cladding and core indices. It sets the phase accumulated along a waveguide and the resonance condition of rings and gratings.
