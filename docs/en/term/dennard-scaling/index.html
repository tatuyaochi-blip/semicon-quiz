What is Dennard scaling?

The rule that shrinking transistor dimensions and supply voltage by the same factor gives faster, denser circuits at constant power density. It broke down in the mid-2000s when voltages could no longer be lowered easily.

Japanese name: デナードスケーリング

Which correctly describes Dennard scaling?

  1. AThe number of transistors doubles about every two years
  2. BShrinking transistor dimensions and supply voltage by the same ratio allows higher speed and integration while keeping power density constant
  3. CThe semiconductor market repeats booms and busts on a cycle of about four years
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Shrinking transistor dimensions and supply voltage by the same ratio allows higher speed and integration while keeping power density constant

Dennard scaling, described by Robert Dennard and colleagues in 1974, states that shrinking transistor dimensions and voltage by the same factor k keeps power per unit area constant while making devices faster and smaller. In the mid-2000s, rising leakage made it hard to lower threshold and supply voltages, and scaling no longer followed this rule. That is why clock frequency growth stalled and the industry shifted to multicore.
