What is Confidence interval?

An interval that estimates an unknown value, such as the population mean, from a sample with a range. A 95% confidence interval is made by a method such that, if sampling were repeated, about 95% of the resulting intervals would contain the true value. When the population standard deviation σ is known, the 95% confidence interval for the population mean is the sample mean ± 1.96 × σ/√n, where σ/√n is called the standard error. Making the sample size n four times as large halves the width of the interval.

Japanese name: 信頼区間

A confidence interval estimates an unknown value, such as the population mean, from a sample with a range. A 95% confidence interval is an interval made by a method such that, if sampling were repeated, about 95% of the resulting intervals would contain the true population mean. The width of the interval is proportional to the standard error σ/√n, so it narrows as the sample grows.

The standard deviation of a population is known to be 10. A sample of size 100 drawn from it has a sample mean of 50.0. Taking the two-sided 95% value of the standard normal distribution to be 1.96, which of the following is the 95% confidence interval for the population mean?

  1. A30.4 to 69.6
  2. B48.04 to 51.96
  3. C49.804 to 50.196
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 48.04 to 51.96

The standard error of the sample mean is 10 ÷ √100 = 1. The 95% confidence interval is "sample mean ± 1.96 × standard error", so 50.0 ± 1.96, which is 48.04 to 51.96. With a sample four times as large, 400, the half-width of the interval narrows to 1.96 × 10 ÷ 20 = 0.98.
