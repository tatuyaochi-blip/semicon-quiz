What is Significand?

The bits of a floating-point number holding its significant digits. For normalized numbers the leading 1 is implied, so single precision stores 23 bits but has 24 bits of precision.

Japanese name: 仮数

Where the significand sits in IEEE 754

An IEEE 754 binary floating-point number stores a sign bit, an exponent field and a significand field, and a normalized value equals (−1)^sign × 1.f × 2^(exponent − bias), where f is the stored significand. Single precision (FP32) allots 1, 8 and 23 bits; double precision (FP64) allots 1, 11 and 52. Because a normalized binary number always begins with 1, that leading bit is implied rather than stored, so FP32 has 24 bits of precision and FP64 has 53. When the exponent field is all zeros, the number is subnormal and the leading digit is 0 instead.

Significand sets precision, exponent sets range

The exponent decides how large or small a number can be; the significand decides how many digits are kept. With 24 bits, FP32 resolves about 7 decimal digits, since 2²⁴ is about 1.7×10⁷, and FP64's 53 bits give about 15 to 16. Machine epsilon, the gap between 1 and the next representable number, is 2⁻²³ ≈ 1.19×10⁻⁷ for FP32 and 2⁻⁵² for FP64. Storing 123456789 in FP32 yields 123456792. Values whose fractions do not have power-of-two denominators, like 0.1, become repeating binary fractions and are rounded.

A worked encoding

To encode −6.25 in FP32, write 6.25 in binary as 110.01 and normalize it to 1.1001 × 2². The sign bit is 1, the stored exponent is 2 + 127 = 129 (1000 0001), and the significand field holds the bits after the binary point, 1001 followed by zeros. The full pattern 1 10000001 1001000…0 is 0xC0C80000 in hexadecimal. For 1.0 the significand field is all zeros and the stored exponent is 127, giving 0x3F800000. With the exponent field all ones, a zero significand means infinity and a nonzero one means NaN.

Trading significand bits in AI formats

Machine-learning hardware often shortens the significand to halve memory and arithmetic cost. FP16 uses 1 sign, 5 exponent and 10 significand bits, keeping reasonable precision but a narrow range, so small gradients can underflow and training uses loss scaling. bfloat16 uses 1, 8 and 7 bits: the same exponent width and nearly the same range as FP32, but only 8 bits of precision. Training tolerates this coarse precision, so bfloat16 trades significand bits for range, while FP16 does the opposite.

Answer: B. 1, 8, 23

Single precision (FP32) uses 1 sign bit, 8 exponent bits and 23 significand bits, 32 in total. For normalized numbers the leading 1 of the significand is implied, so precision is 24 bits, about seven decimal digits. Double precision (FP64) uses 1, 11 and 52 bits, with a wider exponent and higher precision. 1, 11, 20 confuses it with double precision's exponent width, and 1, 5, 26 with half precision's.

  3. C11-bit exponent, 52-bit significand

Answer: C. 11-bit exponent, 52-bit significand

Double precision (FP64) uses 1 sign bit, 11 exponent bits and 52 significand bits, 64 in total. The exponent bias is 1023, covering roughly 10⁻³⁰⁸ to 10³⁰⁸, and the 53 bits of precision including the implied 1 correspond to about 15–16 decimal digits. Both fields are wider than in single precision, and it is the standard for scientific computing. An 8-bit exponent keeps the single-precision width while widening only the significand, which is not a standard format.

FAQ

Is the significand the same as the mantissa?

In everyday use, yes; mantissa is the older term for the stored fraction of a floating-point number. IEEE 754 prefers significand, partly because mantissa traditionally means the fractional part of a logarithm.

Why does single precision have 24 bits of precision when only 23 are stored?

Normalized binary numbers always start with 1, so that leading bit is implied rather than stored. The 23 stored bits plus the hidden 1 give 24 bits of precision.

How many decimal digits does a float have?

Single precision keeps about 7 significant decimal digits and double precision about 15 to 16. The limit comes from the significand width: 24 and 53 bits including the hidden bit.
