What is Synchronizer?

A circuit that aligns an asynchronous signal to the receiving clock. The basic form is two flip-flops in series, which raises the probability that metastability in the first stage resolves before the next edge.

Japanese name: 同期化回路

Which path between two clock domains requires a synchronizer?

  1. ABetween a 400 MHz clock generated by a PLL and the 200 MHz clock obtained by dividing it by two
  2. BBetween clocks obtained by distributing the same clock to two regions through the clock tree
  3. CBetween clocks generated from separate oscillators, with no defined frequency or phase relationship
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Between clocks generated from separate oscillators, with no defined frequency or phase relationship

Clocks generated from the same source with a defined frequency ratio and phase relationship are synchronous clocks, and once STA checks setup and hold, they can be treated like ordinary paths. Clocks from separate oscillators, even if both are labeled 100 MHz, actually differ slightly in frequency, so their edge alignment changes over time and a moment inevitably comes when setup or hold cannot be met. STA cannot guarantee paths between such asynchronous clocks, so a synchronizer is needed.

How does the mean time between failures (MTBF) due to metastability change when a two-flip-flop synchronizer is extended to three stages?

  1. AThe time available for metastability to resolve increases by about one period, and MTBF grows exponentially
  2. BMTBF increases 1.5 times, in proportion to the number of stages
  3. CHow likely metastability is depends on the input side, so MTBF does not change
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The time available for metastability to resolve increases by about one period, and MTBF grows exponentially

Synchronizer MTBF is approximately MTBF = e^(t_r/τ) ÷ (T_0 × f_clk × f_data), where t_r is the time available for metastability to resolve and τ and T_0 are constants determined by the flip-flop's characteristics. Adding a stage extends t_r by about one period. For example, with τ = 20 ps, increasing t_r by 1 ns multiplies MTBF by e^50, more than 10^21 times. The probability of the first stage going metastable is unchanged, but the probability that it resolves before propagating further rises sharply.
