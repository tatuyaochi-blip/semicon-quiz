What is LOCOS?

A method that isolates devices by thickly oxidizing only areas not covered by nitride. It forms a beak-like encroachment at the edges (the bird's beak) and has been replaced by STI.

Japanese name: LOCOS

Field: Oxidation and diffusion

How LOCOS works

LOCOS (local oxidation of silicon) starts with a thin pad oxide, followed by a silicon nitride film that is patterned to cover the future active areas. The wafer is then oxidized, usually in steam at high temperature. Nitride blocks oxygen and water, so only the exposed silicon grows a thick field oxide, typically hundreds of nanometers. Because oxidation consumes silicon, part of that oxide sits below the original surface. Finally the nitride is stripped in hot phosphoric acid (around 160 °C) and the pad oxide is removed, leaving islands of bare silicon surrounded by field oxide.

Bird's beak and other side effects

Oxidant diffuses sideways through the pad oxide under the nitride edge, so oxide wedges in beneath the mask and lifts its edge. This bird's beak can extend roughly half the field-oxide thickness on each side, shrinking the usable active width and setting a floor on isolation pitch. The pad oxide cannot simply be removed to stop it, because it protects silicon from the nitride's stress. LOCOS also leaves a surface step that complicates later lithography, and the Kooi effect (white ribbon) can nitride the silicon at the mask edge; a sacrificial oxidation and strip is used to remove that nitride before the gate oxide is grown.

Why the field oxide is thick

Any wire running over the isolation region acts like a gate, with the field oxide as its dielectric and neighboring diffusions as source and drain. Making the field oxide hundreds of nanometers thick, more than 100 times a typical gate oxide, pushes the threshold voltage of this parasitic field transistor well above the supply voltage so it never turns on. A channel-stop implant under the field oxide is often added to raise that threshold further.

LOCOS vs. STI

Shallow trench isolation (STI) etches trenches about 200–300 nm deep, fills them with CVD oxide and planarizes by chemical mechanical polishing (CMP), using nitride as the polish stop. The result is flat and has no lateral encroachment, so isolation can be made much narrower, and the same approach isolates FinFET fins. STI displaced LOCOS around the 0.25 µm generation. LOCOS survives in older, larger-geometry processes where its simplicity matters more than density, and variants such as poly-buffered LOCOS were developed to shorten the beak without eliminating it.

Answer: C. Bird's beak

In LOCOS, nitride-covered areas resist oxidation, but oxidant diffuses laterally through the pad oxide beneath the nitride edge, so oxide encroaches in a bird's-beak shape. The encroachment can reach about half the oxide thickness on each side, eating into active area, so STI replaced LOCOS around the 0.25 µm generation. The pad oxide is needed to protect silicon from the nitride's stress.

  2. BBoth have the same thickness
  3. CThe field oxide is much thicker

Answer: C. The field oxide is much thicker

Field oxide is made thick—hundreds of nm—so that wiring crossing over it cannot turn on parasitic transistors; thicker oxide raises their threshold voltage. Gate oxide, by contrast, is only a few nm thick to couple the gate field strongly into the channel—a difference of more than 100×. Chips may also use thicker gate oxides for I/O transistors alongside thin core oxides.

FAQ

What is the bird's beak in LOCOS?

It is the wedge of oxide that grows laterally under the edge of the nitride mask because oxidant diffuses through the pad oxide. It can reach about half the field-oxide thickness per side and eats into the active area.

Why was LOCOS replaced by shallow trench isolation?

The bird's beak and the surface step prevented narrow, planar isolation as devices shrank. STI gives vertical, planarized isolation with no encroachment and became standard around the 0.25 µm generation.

What is the Kooi effect in LOCOS?

During steam oxidation, ammonia formed from the nitride mask can nitride the silicon surface near the mask edge, which later causes locally thin or weak gate oxide. A sacrificial oxide is grown and stripped to remove it.
