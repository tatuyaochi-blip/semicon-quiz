What is Harmonic mean?

The reciprocal of the mean of reciprocals, used to average rates over equal amounts of work.

Japanese name: 調和平均

Harmonic mean appears in 2 practice questions across 2 topics, including Machine Learning Basics, Performance Analysis.

Field: Performance Analysis

Try it: questions about Harmonic mean

A two-class classification model was evaluated on 200 test samples, giving TP = 20, FP = 5, FN = 30 and TN = 145. Which is closest to the F1 score?

  1. A0.40
  2. B0.53
  3. C0.60
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 0.53

First, precision is 20 ÷ (20 + 5) = 0.8 and recall is 20 ÷ (20 + 30) = 0.4. The F1 score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall: 2 × 0.8 × 0.4 ÷ (0.8 + 0.4) = 0.64 ÷ 1.2 ≈ 0.53. The harmonic mean is pulled toward the smaller value, so the F1 score is not high when only one of the two is high. Note that the accuracy, (20 + 145) ÷ 200 = 0.825, looks high, yet this model misses 60% of the positives.

Two programs with equal instruction counts run back to back, one at 1000 MIPS (1 billion instructions per second) and the other at 500 MIPS. What is the overall average rate?

  1. A750 MIPS
  2. BAbout 707 MIPS
  3. CAbout 667 MIPS
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. About 667 MIPS

With I instructions each, the times are I ÷ 1000 and I ÷ 500, totaling 3I ÷ 1000. Executing 2I instructions in that time gives an average rate of 2I ÷ (3I ÷ 1000) ≈ 667 MIPS. The slower program takes longer, pulling the average toward the slower rate. When averaging rates over equal amounts of work, use the harmonic mean, not the arithmetic mean.
