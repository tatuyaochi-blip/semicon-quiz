What is Bit line?

The wire used to read and write data in memory cells. In DRAM, the small change in bit-line potential caused by a cell's charge is amplified by a sense amplifier.

Japanese name: ビット線

Bit line appears in 12 practice questions across 4 topics, including SRAM and flash memory, DRAM, Logic circuits.

Field: DRAM

What happens on a DRAM bit line during a read

Before a read, the bit line is precharged to VDD/2. When the word line turns on a cell's access transistor, the cell capacitor Cs (about 10 fF) shares its charge with the much larger bit-line capacitance Cb, several to about ten times Cs. The bit line therefore moves only about (VDD/2)·Cs/(Cs + Cb), typically tens to about a hundred millivolts, up for a stored 1 and down for a 0. A sense amplifier, a cross-coupled latch, amplifies this small difference with positive feedback and simultaneously writes the full level back, because the read destroyed the cell's original charge.

Why bit-line length is limited

The signal depends on the transfer ratio Cs/(Cs + Cb), and every cell attached to the bit line adds capacitance. A long bit line serving many cells would dilute the signal below what the sense amplifier can resolve reliably, so arrays are divided into segments with a limited number of cells per bit line, each served by its own sense amplifiers. This trades area for signal margin. Precharging back to VDD/2 after a row is closed takes time, specified as tRP, which adds to the latency of switching rows.

Folded vs. open bit lines

A sense amplifier compares two bit lines, one connected to the selected cell and one used as reference. In the folded architecture the pair runs side by side in the same array, so noise couples onto both lines equally and cancels; however, each word line can contact cells on only one line of the pair, which makes the cell 8F², where F is the minimum feature size. In the open architecture the pair lies in separate arrays. It is more sensitive to noise but permits a 6F² cell, and it is the mainstream arrangement today.

Bit lines in SRAM and flash

Other memories use bit lines differently. An SRAM column has a complementary bit-line pair precharged to VDD; the selected cell discharges one side slightly, and the sense amplifier fires at a difference of tens to about a hundred millivolts. Writing pulls one bit line low, and at low voltage a negative bit-line assist drives it slightly below ground. In NOR flash each cell connects in parallel to the bit line for fast random reads, while NAND flash connects only the end of a series string of cells to it.

  3. CBecause the cell capacitor's capacitance is much smaller than the bit line's parasitic capacitance

Answer: C. Because the cell capacitor's capacitance is much smaller than the bit line's parasitic capacitance

On readout, the cell's charge is shared with the bit line. The cell capacitance Cs (about 10 fF) is much smaller than the bit-line capacitance Cb (several to about ten times larger), so the swing is only about (VDD/2)·Cs/(Cs + Cb), tens to about a hundred mV. To preserve this transfer ratio, the number of cells per bit line is limited to keep Cb small. The sense amplifier then rapidly amplifies the tiny difference with positive feedback to decide 0 or 1.

When writing to an SRAM cell, one bit line is pulled down to 0 V to flip the cell's state. What condition is required of the access transistor?

  1. AIt must have sufficiently stronger drive than the pull-up transistor (pMOS) in the cell
  2. BIt must be weaker than every transistor in the cell
  3. CIt must always remain off
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It must have sufficiently stronger drive than the pull-up transistor (pMOS) in the cell

To write, the access transistor must pull the storage node to 0, overpowering the pull-up pMOS trying to hold 1, so the access transistor is made substantially stronger than the pMOS (the pull-up ratio). For read stability it must also be weaker than the pull-down nMOS (the cell ratio). Balancing these three drive strengths is the heart of SRAM cell design.

FAQ

What is the difference between a word line and a bit line?

A word line runs along a row and drives the gates of the access transistors, selecting which cells are connected. A bit line runs along a column and carries data between the selected cell and the sense amplifier or write driver.

Why is the DRAM bit line precharged to VDD/2?

Starting midway lets the bit line move up for a stored 1 and down for a stored 0 by similar amounts, so the sense amplifier can compare it against a reference line held at VDD/2.

Why is a DRAM read destructive?

Connecting the small cell capacitor to the much larger bit-line capacitance redistributes its charge, so the cell no longer holds its original voltage. The sense amplifier restores the data by writing the amplified level back into the cell.
