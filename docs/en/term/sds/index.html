What is SDS (safety data sheet)?

A document describing the hazards of a chemical, how to handle it, first-aid measures and more.

Japanese name: SDS(安全データシート)

The 16-section structure

Under the Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals (GHS), an SDS always uses the same 16 sections in the same order: identification, hazard identification, composition, first-aid measures, fire-fighting measures, accidental release measures, handling and storage, exposure controls and personal protection, physical and chemical properties, stability and reactivity, toxicological information, ecological information, disposal, transport, regulatory information and other information. The fixed layout means you can jump straight to the section you need, such as first aid during an emergency, without reading the whole document.

Reading an SDS before handling a fab chemical

Before using a chemical, check section 2 for the GHS pictograms, the signal word (Danger or Warning) and the hazard statements, then sections 7 and 8 for storage rules, exposure limits and the required protective equipment, including suitable glove materials. Section 10 lists incompatible materials, which matters in fabs where acids, bases, oxidizers and solvents share exhaust and drain systems. For hydrofluoric acid, for example, the SDS explains that burns may not hurt at first and that calcium gluconate gel is part of first aid.

SDS vs. container label

The label on a bottle or drum is a condensed summary: product name, pictograms, signal word, key hazard and precautionary statements and the supplier. The SDS is the full reference behind it, supplied by the manufacturer or distributor whenever the chemical, or a product containing it, is transferred. The workplace must keep current SDSs where workers can reach them quickly. An SDS is not a tool maintenance manual or an inspection report; it describes the substance, not the equipment that uses it.

First aid example: chemical in the eye

Section 4 typically tells you to flush an eye immediately with plenty of running water, ideally at an eyewash station, holding the eyelids open for at least about 15 minutes, and then to get medical attention. Rubbing the eye can spread corneal damage, and every minute of delay lets the injury progress. Alkalis are particularly dangerous because they penetrate deeper into tissue than most acids. Because some chemicals need specific measures, read the first-aid section before work starts, not after an accident.

Answer: B. A document describing a chemical's hazards, handling methods, first aid and more

An SDS is the document a supplier provides when transferring a chemical or a product containing one. It covers 16 sections in a fixed order, including composition, hazards, handling and storage, exposure controls, first aid and disposal. Reviewing it before handling a chemical and keeping it readily accessible at the workplace are basic practice. It is different from a tool maintenance manual or a shipment inspection report.

FAQ

What is the difference between an SDS and an MSDS?

MSDS (material safety data sheet) is the older name. The GHS standardized the content into the 16-section SDS format, and most regulations now use the term SDS.

How many sections does a safety data sheet have?

A GHS-compliant SDS has 16 sections in a fixed order, from identification and hazards through first aid, handling, exposure controls, disposal and transport to regulatory and other information.

Who is responsible for providing an SDS?

The supplier (manufacturer, importer or distributor) must provide it when the chemical is transferred, and the employer must keep it accessible to the people who handle the chemical.
