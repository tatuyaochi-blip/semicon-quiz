What is LDO?

Short for low-dropout regulator: a linear regulator that works with only a small difference between input and output voltage. It is low-noise, but the voltage difference times the current is lost as heat.

Japanese name: LDO

LDO appears in 5 practice questions across 3 topics, including Analog and electronic circuits, How to read datasheets, Power Management.

Field: Analog and electronic circuits

How an LDO regulates

A low-dropout regulator (LDO) is a linear regulator: an error amplifier compares a fraction of the output voltage, taken from a resistor divider, with a stable reference such as a bandgap, and drives the pass transistor so the output stays at the set value. The pass device behaves like a continuously adjusted resistor. A PMOS (or PNP) pass device can be turned fully on simply by pulling its gate toward ground, which lets it stay in regulation with only 0.1 to 0.3 V across it, whereas classic NPN-follower regulators need roughly 1.5 to 2 V or more of headroom.

Dropout, efficiency and heat

The minimum input is the output voltage plus the dropout voltage at the actual load current and temperature; a 3.3 V LDO with 300 mV maximum dropout needs at least 3.6 V in. Because input and output currents are essentially equal, efficiency is roughly Vout divided by Vin, so 1.8 V to 1.0 V gives about 56%. The rest, (Vin - Vout) times the load current, becomes heat, which must be checked against the package's thermal resistance. At light loads the regulator's own quiescent current dominates battery drain.

PSRR and output noise

Power supply rejection ratio (PSRR) expresses how much input ripple is suppressed, as a voltage ratio in dB using 20·log10: 60 dB means ripple is divided by 1000. PSRR is highest at low frequency and falls as the error amplifier's gain rolls off, sometimes to 20 to 30 dB at several hundred kHz. When an LDO cleans up a switching regulator's output, check PSRR at the switching frequency. Output noise, largely from the reference, matters for PLLs, ADC references and RF circuits, and some LDOs provide a noise-reduction capacitor pin.

LDO vs. switching regulator

A switching regulator chops the input at hundreds of kHz to several MHz and transfers energy through an inductor, reaching roughly 80 to 95% efficiency even for large voltage steps, and can also boost or invert. It produces switching ripple and electromagnetic noise. An LDO only steps down and wastes the difference as heat, but it is small, quiet and fast to respond to load changes. A common design steps down efficiently with a switcher, then follows it with an LDO for noise-sensitive loads. LDOs are also integrated on chip to supply separate voltage domains.

  3. CHigh power-conversion efficiency, but switching tends to produce noise

Answer: C. High power-conversion efficiency, but switching tends to produce noise

A switching regulator turns transistors on and off at hundreds of kHz to several MHz and transfers energy through inductors and capacitors, achieving about 80–95% efficiency even with large voltage differences, and it can also boost or invert. A linear regulator burns the difference as heat, so its efficiency is roughly Vout ÷ Vin (about 28% for 12 V to 3.3 V). Noise-sensitive circuits commonly follow a switcher with an LDO.

What does the dropout voltage of an LDO (low-dropout linear regulator) mean?

  1. AThe maximum output voltage
  2. BThe change in output voltage when the load is removed
  3. CThe minimum voltage difference between input and output needed to keep the output at its specified voltage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The minimum voltage difference between input and output needed to keep the output at its specified voltage

Dropout voltage is the minimum input-output difference at which a regulator can still hold its output. LDOs use a PMOS (or similar) pass transistor to bring it down to about 0.1–0.3 V—for example, producing 2.8 V from a lithium-ion cell (3.0–4.2 V). Loss is (Vin − Vout) × current, so a small difference means better efficiency. With high ripple rejection, LDOs are widely used to power noise-sensitive circuits.

FAQ

What is the dropout voltage of an LDO?

It is the smallest input-to-output voltage difference at which the regulator can still hold its output at the specified value. It rises with load current and often with temperature, so use the datasheet value at your operating conditions.

How do I calculate LDO efficiency and power loss?

Efficiency is approximately Vout divided by Vin, ignoring quiescent current. The power lost as heat is (Vin - Vout) times the load current; for example, 5 V to 3.3 V at 0.5 A dissipates about 0.85 W.

Should I use an LDO or a switching regulator?

Use a switching regulator when the voltage drop or current is large and efficiency matters. Use an LDO when the drop is small, the load is noise-sensitive, or simplicity and low ripple are more important than efficiency.
