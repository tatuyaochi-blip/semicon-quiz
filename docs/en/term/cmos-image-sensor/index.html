What is CMOS image sensor?

An image sensor made with a CMOS process that has an amplifier in each pixel. It is low-power, can integrate peripheral circuits, and dominates smartphone cameras and more.

Field: Image sensors and optical devices

From photon to voltage in a 4T pixel

Each pixel's photodiode absorbs light and collects electrons in proportion to the exposure; quantum efficiency for visible light is typically about 50–80%. In the common four-transistor (4T) pixel, a transfer transistor moves this charge to a small node called the floating diffusion (FD), a source-follower amplifier buffers the FD voltage onto a column line, a select transistor connects the row, and a reset transistor clears the FD. Conversion gain is q/C_FD, about 160 µV per electron for a 1 fF node. Neighboring pixels often share the FD and readout transistors to save area.

Pinned photodiodes and CDS

Interface states at the silicon/oxide surface generate dark current. The pinned photodiode buries the n-type collection region under a heavily doped p+ surface layer, which fills those states with holes and suppresses that current. The buried region also fully depletes, so charge transfer is complete, avoiding image lag. Readout uses correlated double sampling (CDS): the FD is sampled right after reset and again after transfer, and the difference removes kTC reset noise and amplifier offset, bringing read noise down to a few electrons or less. Column-parallel ADCs then digitize the signal on chip.

Front-side vs. back-side illumination

In a front-side illuminated (FSI) sensor, light must pass through gaps between metal wiring layers before reaching the photodiode, losing intensity and angle tolerance. A back-side illuminated (BSI) sensor thins the wafer to a few micrometers and receives light from the side opposite the wiring, which keeps sensitivity high for pixels around 1 µm and became standard in smartphone cameras. Stacked sensors go further, bonding the pixel wafer to a separate logic wafer that carries ADCs and image processing. Epitaxial silicon substrates are used for low-defect pixel regions.

CMOS vs. CCD sensors

A charge-coupled device (CCD) shifts charge packets across the chip to a single output amplifier, which needs several clock voltages and more power. A CMOS image sensor amplifies in each pixel and reads rows through column circuits, so it runs at lower power, reads out faster, and integrates timing, ADCs and processing on the same die. Row-by-row readout causes rolling-shutter distortion of fast motion, which global-shutter pixels avoid with in-pixel storage. Dual-conversion-gain pixels switch extra FD capacitance to cover both dim and bright scenes.

Answer: A. Photodiode

In an image-sensor pixel, a photodiode absorbs light, generating electron-hole pairs, and accumulates electrons in proportion to the light during exposure. A CMOS image sensor converts that charge to a voltage with in-pixel transistors for readout. The fraction of photons converted to electrons (quantum efficiency) is about 50–80% for visible light. An LED converts electricity to light, and a Zener diode provides a voltage reference.

  3. CIt no longer needs color filters

Answer: B. Light reaches the photodiode without passing through the wiring layers, making it easier to increase sensitivity

In front-side illumination, light must pass through gaps in several metal layers above the photodiode, where it is blocked or reflected. Back-side illumination thins the wafer to a few µm and admits light from the side opposite the wiring, collecting it efficiently even at oblique angles. Commercialized by Sony in 2009, it preserves sensitivity in pixels around 1 µm and became standard in smartphone cameras.

FAQ

What is the difference between a CMOS image sensor and a CCD?

A CCD moves charge across the chip to one shared output amplifier, while a CMOS image sensor converts charge to voltage inside each pixel and reads it out row by row. CMOS sensors use less power and integrate ADCs and logic, which is why they dominate phones and most cameras.

What does BSI mean in an image sensor?

BSI stands for back-side illumination: the wafer is thinned and light enters from the back, so it reaches the photodiodes without passing through the metal wiring layers, improving sensitivity for small pixels.

What is correlated double sampling in a CMOS image sensor?

It reads the floating diffusion once after reset and once after charge transfer, then subtracts the two. Reset (kTC) noise and amplifier offsets appear in both samples and cancel, leaving the true signal.
