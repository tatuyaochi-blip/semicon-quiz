What is Electrostatic chuck?

A mechanism that holds the wafer to its stage by electrostatic force. It works in vacuum and allows backside gas for temperature control; it is widely used in deposition and etch tools.

Japanese name: 静電チャック

Field: Deposition (CVD and PVD)

How clamping works

An electrostatic chuck (ESC) embeds electrodes inside a platen of ceramic or another dielectric and applies hundreds of volts to several kV, clamping the wafer by the electrostatic attraction between them. In the monopolar type, a single electrode is used and the wafer potential is set through the plasma; in the bipolar type, the electrode is split in two and given positive and negative voltages. The bipolar type can clamp without plasma, which makes it convenient, for example right after wafer transfer. Unlike a mechanical clamp, it holds the whole wafer evenly without touching the front surface.

Coulomb and Johnsen-Rahbek types

Electrostatic chucks fall into two types according to the dielectric's resistivity. The Coulomb type uses a very high-resistivity dielectric, so electrode and wafer act as a capacitor with the dielectric between them. The Johnsen-Rahbek type uses a dielectric resistive enough to pass only a small current; charge gathers across the tiny gap at the contact surface with the wafer, giving strong clamping force even at low voltage. On the other hand, leakage current flows and the resistivity changes with temperature, which requires care. As a rule of thumb, clamping force is proportional to the square of the voltage and inversely proportional to the square of the electrode-to-wafer distance.

Temperature control

In vacuum, almost no heat passes between wafer and platen, so grooves are cut in the chuck surface and a gas such as helium is flowed between it and the wafer backside to conduct heat. Because the wafer is held firmly, it does not lift off even under the gas pressure. In plasma etching, ion bombardment delivers a great deal of heat to the wafer, so chucks incorporate coolant channels and heaters and control temperature separately in multiple zones, such as inner and outer, to even out etch rate and profile across the wafer.

Dechucking and applications

If charge remains in the dielectric or wafer when the wafer is released after processing (dechucking), the wafer can stick, and it may crack or shift when the lift pins push it up. Residual charge is therefore removed, for example by briefly applying a voltage of opposite polarity. Electrostatic chucks are used in many vacuum tools, including dry etch, CVD, PVD and ion implantation. Wafer stages in lithography tools that expose in air mainly use vacuum chucks, but EUV scanners, which expose in vacuum, hold the mask and wafer with electrostatic chucks.

Answer: B. Electrostatic chuck (ESC)

An electrostatic chuck applies hundreds of volts to several kV to electrodes buried in a ceramic platen, clamping the wafer by electrostatic attraction. It works in vacuum and holds the whole wafer evenly, allowing helium flowed between wafer and platen to conduct heat for precise temperature control—directly affecting etch and deposition uniformity. Managing residual charge during dechucking is also important.

FAQ

What is an electrostatic chuck, in simple terms?

A mechanism that applies voltage to electrodes embedded in the platen and holds the wafer in place by electrostatic attraction. It works in vacuum and holds the whole wafer evenly, so it is widely used in etch and deposition tools.

Why use an electrostatic chuck rather than a vacuum chuck?

A vacuum chuck holds the wafer by lowering the pressure behind it below the surroundings, so it cannot work in tools whose process chamber is itself under vacuum. An electrostatic chuck clamps in vacuum and also allows backside gas for temperature control.

What problems arise when dechucking?

If charge remains after processing, the wafer stays clamped even after the voltage is turned off, and lifting it by force can crack or shift it. Residual charge is removed, for example by applying a voltage of opposite polarity, before lift pins raise the wafer.
