What is Segregation of duties?

An internal control principle that divides responsibilities and authority so that no single person can complete, alone, the initiation (input), approval, recording and custody of assets for a transaction. Errors and fraud cannot be completed by one person's decision, and they are easier to find through mutual checks. In information systems, it is applied by separating the people who develop programs from those who deploy them to production and operate them, and by separating the authority to enter data from the authority to approve it.

Japanese name: 職務分掌

Answer: A. Having different people enter purchase order data and approve those orders

Segregation of duties is the principle of dividing responsibilities and authority so that no single person can carry out a transaction alone from initiation through approval, recording and custody of assets. If input and approval are done by different people, errors and fraud cannot be completed by one person's decision and are easier to find through mutual checks. The same principle is applied in information systems, for example by separating the people who develop programs from those who operate the production environment.
