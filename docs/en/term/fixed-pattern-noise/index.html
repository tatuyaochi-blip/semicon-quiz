What is Fixed-pattern noise?

Noise that appears in the same place every time, caused by variations between pixels or readout circuits. It is removed by correlated double sampling or calibration.

Japanese name: 固定パターンノイズ

Field: Image sensors and optical devices

Noise in the same place every time

Fixed-pattern noise (FPN) is noise that appears in the same way at the same positions in every shot, caused by slight differences in the characteristics of pixels and readout circuits. Even under uniform illumination, each pixel's output differs a little, overlaying graininess or patterns on the image. Because it does not change over time, averaging many frames does not remove it, which distinguishes it from temporal noise such as shot noise and thermal noise that changes from shot to shot. Conversely, if the pattern is measured in advance, it can be removed by correction.

Main causes and how it appears

The causes fall into two broad groups. One is variation in the output even without light, due to offsets of the pixel amplifier transistors and pixel-to-pixel differences in dark current (DSNU). The other is variation in sensitivity to light, due to slight differences in photodiodes, microlenses and color filters (PRNU). In CMOS image sensors, variation among the per-column amplifiers and ADCs tends to appear as vertical stripes (column FPN), which the eye notices more than point-like variation. Because dark current changes greatly with temperature, FPN in the dark varies with temperature and exposure time.

How it is removed

Per-pixel offsets can be removed by correlated double sampling (CDS). The output is read right after reset and again after the charge is transferred, and the difference is taken as the signal, canceling offsets common to both. Variation among column circuits is reduced by similar differencing in the column circuits and by correction referenced to the output of light-shielded pixels (optical black). Sensitivity variation is corrected with per-pixel coefficients measured under uniform illumination (flat-field correction). In astrophotography and similar work, a dark frame taken with the shutter closed is subtracted to remove the pattern caused by dark current.

Differences between CCD and CMOS

CCD image sensors convert the charge from every pixel to voltage with the same output amplifier, so FPN from gain variation was characteristically small. CMOS image sensors have an amplifying transistor in each pixel and circuits for each column, so large FPN was an early weakness, but CDS and refinements to the column circuits improved it greatly. Even so, in very dark scenes or at high-sensitivity settings, residual vertical stripes or patterns can become visible. FPN is often expressed as the standard deviation relative to the mean output, or as a number of electrons, and is used together with temporal noise to evaluate sensors.

Answer: C. Fixed pattern noise

Fixed-pattern noise comes from pixel-to-pixel variations in amplifier offset and dark current and from column-to-column variations in readout circuits and ADCs, appearing at the same places in every frame; column variations show up as vertical stripes. Because it is time-invariant, it can be removed with CDS, correction using optically black pixels and factory calibration data. It differs from temporal noise such as shot and thermal noise.

FAQ

What is the difference between fixed-pattern noise and random noise?

Fixed-pattern noise is caused by variation in the characteristics of pixels and circuits and appears in the same way at the same positions every time. Random noise such as shot noise and thermal noise changes from shot to shot. Fixed-pattern noise can be measured in advance and corrected, while random noise is reduced by methods such as averaging multiple frames.

Why do vertical stripes appear in CMOS sensors?

CMOS image sensors often have an amplifier and ADC for each column, and variation in their characteristics is added equally along an entire column, appearing as vertical stripes. Because these are more noticeable than point-like variation, they are suppressed by correction using light-shielded pixels and by refinements to the column circuits.

Does correlated double sampling eliminate fixed-pattern noise?

Variation common to both the reset and signal readings, such as pixel amplifier offsets, is canceled by CDS. However, CDS cannot remove variation in dark current or sensitivity, so it is combined with dark-frame subtraction, flat-field correction and similar methods.
