What is APC (advanced process control)?

Automatically adjusting the process settings for the next lot or wafer based on earlier measurements, as in run-to-run control. It reduces variation.

Japanese name: APC(先進的プロセス制御)

Field: Yield and quality control

How run-to-run control works

Advanced process control (APC) closes a loop between metrology and the process tool. In run-to-run (R2R) control, a simple model links a recipe knob to the result, for example polish time to removed thickness in CMP. After each lot or wafer is measured, the controller updates its estimate of the tool's current behaviour, often with an exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) to filter measurement noise, and computes the setting for the next run. A heavier weight on recent data tracks drift quickly but also chases noise; a lighter weight is smoother but slower to react.

Feedforward and feedback

APC uses information from two directions. Feedforward control uses measurements taken before the step on the same wafer: if the incoming film is measured thick, the next CMP polish time is extended so the final thickness still hits target. Feedback control uses results measured after processing earlier wafers to correct slow changes in the tool, such as a falling removal rate as a pad or chamber ages. Lithography overlay and critical-dimension control, where exposure dose, focus and alignment offsets are adjusted lot by lot, are classic examples of combining both.

APC versus SPC

Statistical process control (SPC) watches a process with control charts and raises an alarm or stops the tool when a parameter drifts outside its limits; it detects problems but does not fix them. APC actively steers the process back toward target on every run, which shrinks variation without waiting for an excursion. The two work together: SPC limits are still applied to APC-controlled outputs, and in many fabs the APC umbrella also includes fault detection and classification (FDC), which monitors tool sensor traces in real time.

Confusable terms nearby

Several yield-related acronyms sit close to APC but do different jobs. Automatic defect classification (ADC) sorts review-SEM images of defects into categories such as particles or scratches. Part average testing (PAT) rejects chips whose test results are statistical outliers within their lot even though they meet specification, a technique used for automotive quality. ATPG, automatic test pattern generation, creates test vectors for logic. Only APC changes how wafers are processed; the others classify defects or screen finished chips.

FAQ

What is the difference between APC and SPC in semiconductor manufacturing?

SPC monitors process data with control charts and flags or stops the process when it goes out of control. APC uses the same kind of measurements to adjust the recipe automatically for the next lot or wafer, actively reducing variation.

What is run-to-run control?

Run-to-run control is a form of APC that updates a tool's recipe between runs based on measurements of previous runs, for example adjusting CMP polish time from the measured removal of earlier wafers. It corrects slow drift without changing anything during a single run.

What is feedforward control in APC?

Feedforward control uses a measurement of the incoming wafer, taken at an earlier step, to choose settings for the current step. A thicker-than-normal incoming film, for example, leads to a longer polish so the final thickness stays on target.
