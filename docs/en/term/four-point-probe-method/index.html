What is Four-point probe method?

A method that passes current through the outer two of four collinear needles and measures voltage across the inner two to find the sheet resistance of wafers and thin films.

Japanese name: 四探針法

Field: Metrology and analysis

How measuring with four probes works

In the four-point probe method, four equally spaced, collinear needles are pressed onto the sample; a constant current I is passed between the outer two, and the voltage V between the inner two is measured. Because the voltmeter's input resistance is high, almost no current flows through the voltage-sensing needles, so voltage drops from the needle-sample contact resistance and the leads do not enter the measurement. Separating the current-carrying and voltage-sensing terminals is the same idea as four-terminal (Kelvin) sensing. Measuring current and voltage through only two needles would include a large contact resistance, so the four-point probe is the standard method for evaluating diffused layers and metal films.

Obtaining sheet resistance and resistivity

For a thin film that is wide compared with the probe spacing, the sheet resistance is Rs = (π/ln2) × V/I ≈ 4.53 × V/I, in units of Ω/□ (ohms per square). If the film thickness t is known, the resistivity is ρ = Rs × t. Conversely, for a metal film of known resistivity, the thickness can be estimated from the sheet resistance. For a bulk sample much thicker than the probe spacing s, ρ = 2πs × V/I is used instead. When the sample is small, the measurement is near the wafer edge, or the thickness is not negligible compared with the probe spacing, a correction factor for the geometry is applied.

Use in manufacturing

The four-point probe is widely used to control the sheet resistance of diffused layers after ion implantation and annealing, of silicides, of metal films and more. Under the same anneal, a higher dose gives a lower sheet resistance; as a guide, an implant of 10¹⁵ cm⁻² gives around 100 Ω/□. Because the reading reflects both the implanted dose and the degree of activation, it can be used to monitor implanters and anneal tools. Automated tools measure many points across the wafer to draw a map and evaluate in-wafer uniformity. On wafers that have completed front-end processing, sheet resistance is also measured on TEG structures in WAT (parametric testing).

Caveats and comparison with other methods

Because the needles are pressed directly onto the surface, they can leave slight scratches or contamination, so measurements are usually made in dedicated test areas or on monitor wafers rather than on product dies. On very thin films the needles may punch through the film or current may leak into the layer below, preventing an accurate reading. For a diffused layer, the pn junction to the substrate blocks the current, so the layer alone is measured. When non-contact measurement is needed, eddy-current methods are available and are used to measure metal film thickness and wafer resistivity. When the depth profile of carrier concentration is needed, methods such as spreading resistance profiling (SRP) are used.

Answer: A. Sheet resistance

The four-point probe places four collinear needles on the sample, passes current through the outer pair and measures voltage across the inner pair. Since almost no current flows through the voltage probes, sheet resistance is measured without contact-resistance error. For a sufficiently large thin film, Rs = (π/ln2)×V/I ≈ 4.53×V/I. It is used for diffused layers and metal films. Stress is measured from wafer bow and roughness by AFM and similar tools.

FAQ

Why use four needles instead of two?

Separating the needles that carry current from those that sense voltage removes the effect of the needle-sample contact resistance. Almost no current flows through the inner two voltage-sensing needles, so the voltage drop across the contact resistance is not included in the reading.

Where does the 4.53 factor in the four-point probe come from?

Solving for the potential distribution in a wide, thin film with four equally spaced needles gives Rs = (π/ln2) × V/I, and π/ln2 is about 4.53. For small samples or measurements near an edge the factor deviates from this value, so a correction factor is applied.

What is the difference between sheet resistance and resistivity?

Resistivity (Ω·cm) is a property of the material itself, while sheet resistance (Ω/□) is resistivity divided by film thickness, a property of the film. For a square of film, the resistance between opposite edges equals the sheet resistance regardless of the square's size, which makes it convenient for evaluating thin films and diffused layers.
