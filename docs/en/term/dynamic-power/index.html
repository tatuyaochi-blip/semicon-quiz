What is Dynamic power?

Power consumed in CMOS circuits charging and discharging capacitance when signals switch, expressed as αCV²f with activity factor α, capacitance C, voltage V and frequency f.

Japanese name: 動的消費電力

Field: Power Management

Where the energy goes

Every CMOS gate output drives a load capacitance made of wire and the gates it feeds. When the output rises, the supply delivers CV² of energy: half is stored on the capacitor and half is dissipated in the pull-up transistors. When the output falls, the stored half is dumped to ground through the pull-down. Multiplying by how often this happens gives dynamic power, P = αCV²f, where α is the activity factor, the fraction of the capacitance switching per clock cycle. Some texts write ½αCV²f and count each edge as a transition, so check the convention before comparing α values.

Voltage, frequency and energy

Because of the V² term, voltage is the strongest lever: a 10% supply reduction cuts dynamic power by about 19%, and halving it leaves a quarter. Frequency is different. Halving f halves power, but a fixed task takes twice as long, so the energy for the task is unchanged; energy per switching event is set by CV². Lowering f is valuable because it lets the circuit run correctly at a lower voltage. If voltage can fall in proportion to frequency, halving both cuts power to one-eighth and task energy to one-quarter, which is the basis of dynamic voltage and frequency scaling (DVFS).

Cutting activity and capacitance

Since logic that does not toggle consumes no dynamic power, reducing α is a major design tool. The clock network switches every cycle, so clock gating, which stops the clock to registers and blocks whose values will not change, removes switching in both the flip-flops and the clock wiring. Capacitance falls with smaller transistors, shorter wires and low-k dielectrics. A worked example: α = 0.1, C = 1 nF, V = 1 V and f = 1 GHz gives 0.1 W.

Dynamic versus static power

Dynamic power is only part of the total. Short-circuit, or crowbar, current flows while both transistors are partly on during an input transition; with sharp edges it is roughly a tenth of dynamic power or less. Leakage, or static power, flows whenever the circuit is powered, so clock gating does not reduce it and power gating, which cuts the supply to idle blocks, is needed instead. Leakage also explains why lowering voltage stops paying off: near or slightly below threshold, circuits slow so much that leakage energy per operation rises, creating a minimum-energy voltage.

Try it: questions about Dynamic power

In a CMOS circuit, if only the supply voltage is halved with everything else unchanged, what happens to dynamic power consumption?

  1. AIt does not change
  2. BIt halves
  3. CIt becomes one quarter
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. It becomes one quarter

CMOS dynamic power P = αCV²f scales with the square of supply voltage, so halving the voltage cuts it to a quarter. Lower voltage also reduces transistor current and speed, so the same frequency can no longer be sustained. Still, lowering frequency together with voltage reduces power nearly with the cube, which is why DVFS—scaling both down under light load—is the most effective power-saving technique.

In the CMOS dynamic power formula P = αCV²f, what does α represent?

  1. AThe number of transistors
  2. BThe fraction of signals switching per clock cycle (the activity factor)
  3. CThe chip temperature
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The fraction of signals switching per clock cycle (the activity factor)

Dynamic power is consumed charging and discharging the capacitance of wires and gates. C is the switched capacitance, V the supply voltage, f the clock frequency and α the activity factor, the fraction of signals that switch per clock. Parts whose data does not change, or whose clocks are stopped, do not switch, so reducing α is also an important power-saving lever.

FAQ

What is the formula for dynamic power in CMOS?

Dynamic power is P = αCV²f, where α is the activity factor, C the switched capacitance, V the supply voltage and f the clock frequency. It comes from repeatedly charging and discharging load capacitance.

Why does lowering voltage save more power than lowering frequency?

Power depends on the square of voltage but only linearly on frequency, and reducing frequency alone does not change the energy needed to finish a task. Reducing voltage lowers the energy of every switching event, so it saves both power and energy.

What is the difference between clock gating and power gating?

Clock gating stops the clock to idle logic, which removes its dynamic power but leaves leakage. Power gating disconnects the block from its supply with a switch transistor, eliminating nearly all leakage as well, at the cost of losing state and needing time to wake up.
