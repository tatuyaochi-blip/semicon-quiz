What is Slack?

A value expressing timing margin. For setup it is required time minus arrival time, and for hold it is arrival time minus required time; a negative value means a violation.

Japanese name: スラック

Field: Timing analysis and CDC

Computing setup slack

Static timing analysis (STA) compares when data arrives with when it is required. For setup, the data launched at one clock edge must arrive before the next capture edge minus the setup time, so slack = required − arrival. With a 2.0 ns period, 0.2 ns clock-to-output, 1.4 ns of logic and 0.1 ns setup, arrival is 1.6 ns, required is 1.9 ns and slack is +0.3 ns. Clock uncertainty for jitter and unbuilt clock trees is also subtracted from the required time, and a multicycle path of 2 moves the capture edge one extra period later.

Hold slack and why frequency does not help

Hold asks the opposite question: does new data launched by an edge arrive so early that it corrupts the value the same edge is capturing? Here slack = arrival − required, using minimum delays and required = capture clock arrival + hold time. Because both checks refer to the same edge, the clock period does not appear, so slowing the clock never fixes a hold violation; in silicon it is usually fatal. Hold problems appear on short paths such as shift registers and are normally fixed by inserting delay cells on the data path.

Skew, OCV and CRPR

Clock skew moves the two checks in opposite directions. If the capture clock arrives later than the launch clock (positive skew), setup slack grows by that amount and hold slack shrinks by the same amount; negative skew does the reverse. On-chip variation (OCV) derating makes the analysis pessimistic, for setup slowing the launch clock and data paths and speeding the capture clock. Where both clock paths share buffers from the root, that shared section cannot be fast and slow at once, so common path pessimism removal (CRPR) adds the difference back.

Reading a timing report

Reports summarize slack per endpoint. WNS (worst negative slack) is the single worst value and indicates how far the clock is from closing; TNS (total negative slack) sums the negative slacks of all violating endpoints and indicates how much work remains. Three endpoints at −0.1 ns give WNS −0.1 ns and TNS −0.3 ns. Both are reported separately for setup and hold, and per clock group. Setup violations are attacked by shortening logic, upsizing cells or restructuring; a multicycle exception is legitimate only if the logic really captures every other cycle.

Try it: questions about Slack

In a synchronous circuit with a clock period of 2.0 ns, the launching flip-flop's clock-to-output delay is 0.2 ns, the combinational logic delay is 1.4 ns and the capturing flip-flop's setup time is 0.1 ns. With zero clock skew, what is the setup slack?

  1. A0.3 ns
  2. B0.4 ns
  3. C0.5 ns
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 0.3 ns

Setup slack is calculated as "required time − arrival time." The data arrival time is 0.2 + 1.4 = 1.6 ns and the required time is the next clock edge minus the setup time, 2.0 − 0.1 = 1.9 ns, so the slack is 1.9 − 1.6 = 0.3 ns. Being positive, it meets the constraint. 0.4 ns is the value obtained by forgetting to subtract the setup time, and 0.5 ns by adding the setup time instead. A negative slack means a violation.

A path has a clock period of 1.0 ns, a clock-to-output delay of 0.1 ns, a combinational logic delay of 0.85 ns and a setup time of 0.08 ns. If the clock arrives at the capturing flip-flop 0.05 ns later than at the launching flip-flop, what is the setup slack?

  1. A−0.03 ns
  2. B+0.02 ns
  3. C−0.08 ns
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. +0.02 ns

When the capture clock arrives late (positive skew), the capturing edge shifts later, giving more time for setup. The required time is 1.0 + 0.05 − 0.08 = 0.97 ns and the arrival time is 0.1 + 0.85 = 0.95 ns, so the slack is +0.02 ns. −0.03 ns ignores the skew, and −0.08 ns treats the skew in the opposite direction (as if the capture clock arrived early). Note that the same skew works against hold.

FAQ

What does negative slack mean in timing analysis?

It means the path violates its constraint: for setup the data arrives too late for the capture edge, and for hold it arrives too early and could overwrite the value being captured. The magnitude tells how much delay must be removed or added.

What is the difference between WNS and TNS?

WNS is the slack of the single worst path, while TNS is the sum of all negative endpoint slacks. A design can have a small WNS but a large TNS when many paths fail by a little.

How do you fix a hold violation?

Add delay to the short data path, typically with buffer or delay cells, or reduce the skew that pushes the capture clock late. Lowering the clock frequency does not help because the hold check does not involve the period.
