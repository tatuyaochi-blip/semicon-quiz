What is Overetch?

Continuing to etch for a set time after endpoint so the film is removed everywhere on the wafer. It is done under conditions with high selectivity to the underlying layer.

Japanese name: オーバーエッチング

Try it: questions about Overetch

After the etch endpoint is detected, what is it called to continue etching for a set time to ensure the film is removed across the whole wafer?

  1. AOver-etching
  2. BAshing
  3. CDeposition
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Over-etching

Because of etch-rate and thickness variation across the wafer and film remaining on step sidewalls, some film may remain when the endpoint is detected. An overetch—often about 20–50% of the main etch time—ensures complete removal. Since exposed underlayers keep etching during this step, conditions are usually switched to ones with high selectivity to the underlayer.

If the oxide etch rate is 100 nm/min and the mask is consumed at 20 nm/min, what is the selectivity to the mask?

  1. A0.2
  2. B5
  3. C80
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 5

Selectivity is the etch rate of the target film divided by that of the mask (or underlayer): 100 ÷ 20 = 5. Etching 500 nm of oxide consumes 100 nm of mask, so allowing for overetch and edge erosion the mask should be at least about 150–200 nm thick. If selectivity is insufficient, a thicker mask or a hard mask is used.
