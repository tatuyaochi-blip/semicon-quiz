What is Power capping?

Setting and enforcing an upper limit on server or CPU power, used in data centers to stay within power and cooling limits.

Japanese name: パワーキャップ

Power capping appears in 1 practice question across 1 topic, including Power Management.

Field: Power Management

Try it: questions about Power capping

Servers that draw up to 500 W each are installed in a rack with a power limit of 8 kW. If power capping sets a 400 W limit on each server, how does the number of servers the rack can hold change? (Ignore the power of other equipment.)

  1. AIt increases from 16 to 20
  2. BIt stays at 16
  3. CIt increases from 16 to 80
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It increases from 16 to 20

The rack's power limit must hold even if every server draws its maximum power at the same time. Without a cap, 8000 W ÷ 500 W = 16 servers fit, but if power capping guarantees that no server exceeds 400 W, up to 8000 W ÷ 400 W = 20 servers fit. In general, it is rare for all servers to draw their maximum power at once, so the performance impact can often be kept small. However, a server that reaches its cap has its CPU frequency and voltage lowered and runs more slowly, so the cap is set by looking at the workload.
