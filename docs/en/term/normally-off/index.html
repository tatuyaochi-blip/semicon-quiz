What is Normally-off?

The property of passing no current when the gate voltage is 0 V. It is required of power devices for safety, since current stops even if the drive circuit fails.

Japanese name: ノーマリーオフ

Normally-off appears in 2 practice questions across 2 topics, including Compound semiconductors, Power semiconductors.

Field: Power semiconductors

Why power circuits demand it

A power transistor sits directly across a high-voltage bus, so its state when the gate drive is absent matters. If the driver loses its supply or fails, the gate typically falls to 0 V. A normally-on (depletion-mode) device would then conduct, and in a half-bridge both switches could turn on together, shorting the bus and drawing destructive current. Start-up is also awkward, because the switch conducts before the driver supply is established. Silicon MOSFETs and IGBTs are enhancement-mode devices with a positive threshold voltage, so they are naturally normally-off; the issue arises mainly with some wide-bandgap devices.

Why GaN HEMTs start out normally-on

In an AlGaN/GaN high-electron-mobility transistor (HEMT), polarization charge at the heterointerface creates a two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) without any gate bias, so the basic device has a negative threshold voltage and conducts at 0 V. One fix is a p-type GaN layer under the gate, which lifts the conduction band and depletes the 2DEG beneath it, giving a positive but fairly low threshold of about a volt or two. That low margin, together with a limited allowed gate voltage, means the gate drive must be designed carefully against noise and overshoot.

How the cascode works

The cascode places a low-voltage silicon MOSFET, rated around 30 V, in series on the source side of a normally-on GaN HEMT, with the HEMT gate tied to the MOSFET source. When the MOSFET turns off, its drain voltage rises, and because that node is the HEMT source, the HEMT sees a negative gate-to-source voltage and turns off as well. The pair therefore behaves as one normally-off switch driven by an ordinary silicon gate driver. The costs are an extra die, interconnect parasitics that can affect fast switching, and the silicon MOSFET's body diode in the reverse-conduction path.

Normally-off computing: a different meaning

The same phrase appears in system design with a different meaning. Volatile SRAM and DRAM must stay powered to keep data and leak current even when idle. If caches and registers are built from non-volatile memory, the system can remove power at every idle moment without losing state and wake up quickly, cutting standby power to nearly zero. This style of operation, called normally-off computing, suits IoT sensor nodes and similar devices that spend most of their time waiting. It concerns power management, not the threshold voltage of a transistor.

Answer: C. Cascode configuration

In a cascode, a low-voltage (about 30 V) silicon MOSFET is placed in series on the source side of a GaN HEMT, with the HEMT's gate tied to the MOSFET's source. Turning the MOSFET off negatively biases the HEMT gate, switching it off automatically, so the pair behaves as a normally-off device driven by a standard silicon MOSFET gate driver. Drawbacks are the extra component and the MOSFET's parasitics at high speed.

FAQ

What is the difference between normally-on and normally-off transistors?

A normally-off (enhancement-mode) transistor conducts no current with 0 V on the gate and needs a positive gate voltage to turn on. A normally-on (depletion-mode) transistor conducts at 0 V and needs a negative gate voltage to turn off.

Are GaN HEMTs normally-on?

The basic AlGaN/GaN HEMT is normally-on because its 2DEG exists without gate bias. Commercial power parts are made normally-off with a p-GaN gate or by pairing the HEMT with a silicon MOSFET in a cascode.

What is normally-off computing?

It is a system approach that uses non-volatile memory for caches and registers so power can be cut whenever the processor is idle without losing data, greatly reducing standby power.
