What is 3D NAND?

NAND flash in which memory cells are stacked vertically in many layers to increase capacity. It was introduced to get past the limits of planar scaling, and layer counts now exceed 200.

Japanese name: 3D NAND

Which correctly describes 3D NAND flash memory?

  1. AMemory cells are stacked vertically in many layers to increase capacity
  2. BIt operates on three different supply voltages
  3. CIt stores 3 bits per cell
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Memory cells are stacked vertically in many layers to increase capacity

As planar scaling neared its limit around 15 nm, 3D NAND, which stacks cells vertically, entered mass production around 2013 and became mainstream. Deep cylindrical channel holes are etched through a thick stack of alternating conductive and insulating layers, and cells are formed on the hole walls. Capacity grows without shrinking cells aggressively, and interference drops. Three bits per cell is TLC, commonly combined with 3D NAND.

In charge-trap cells, widely used in 3D NAND, which material is commonly used for the layer that stores charge?

  1. ACopper
  2. BSilicon nitride (SiN)
  3. CAluminum
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Silicon nitride (SiN)

A charge-trap cell stores electrons in trap states within an insulating silicon nitride film instead of a conductive floating gate. Because the charge stays localized, a single defect in the dielectric cannot drain all of it, and interference with neighboring cells is small. The SiO2/SiN/SiO2 (ONO) stack can be deposited continuously on channel-hole walls, making it well suited to vertically stacked 3D NAND.
