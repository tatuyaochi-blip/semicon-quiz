What is Switching loss?

Loss that occurs while a power device switches between on and off, when voltage and current are present at the same time. It grows with switching frequency.

Japanese name: スイッチング損失

Try it: questions about Switching loss

In power devices, which loss mainly increases when the switching frequency is raised?

  1. AConduction loss
  2. BSwitching loss
  3. COnly standby leakage loss
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Switching loss

Switching loss arises because voltage and current coexist during each transition; it equals energy per switching event × switching frequency, so ten times the frequency means ten times the switching loss. Conduction loss (I²R while on) does not depend on frequency. Higher frequency shrinks inductors and capacitors, so the trend is to raise frequency using SiC and GaN, whose switching losses are small.

What is the main advantage of raising the switching frequency of a power converter?

  1. ASwitching loss always decreases
  2. BPassive components such as inductors and capacitors can be made smaller
  3. CElectromagnetic noise always decreases
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Passive components such as inductors and capacitors can be made smaller

Raising switching frequency reduces the energy inductors and capacitors must store each cycle, so they can be smaller and supplies become compact and light; transformer size, for example, shrinks roughly in inverse proportion to frequency. Switching loss and noise rise with frequency, however, so low-loss, fast SiC and GaN devices are what make high-frequency operation practical.
