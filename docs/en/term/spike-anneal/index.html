What is Spike anneal?

An anneal that ramps the wafer quickly to a high temperature and cools it again with almost no hold time. It activates dopants while limiting diffusion, for shallow junctions.

Which is a correct technique for forming shallow junctions for the sources and drains of scaled transistors?

  1. AImplanting at high energy followed by a long furnace anneal
  2. BForming them by thermal oxidation alone, without ion implantation
  3. CImplanting at low energy followed by very short anneals (such as spike or laser annealing)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Implanting at low energy followed by very short anneals (such as spike or laser annealing)

Shallow junctions start with low-energy implants, often below 1 keV. To prevent dopant diffusion during annealing from deepening the junction, spike anneals (about 1050 °C with no dwell time) or flash-lamp and laser anneals that heat only the surface for milliseconds or less activate dopants while limiting diffusion to a few nm. Co-implantation and pre-amorphization are also combined.

What is the phenomenon called in which diffusion of boron and other dopants is temporarily accelerated early in annealing because of interstitial silicon created by implantation?

  1. ATransient enhanced diffusion (TED)
  2. BGettering
  3. CSegregation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Transient enhanced diffusion (TED)

Excess silicon interstitials created by implantation pair with boron and assist its diffusion, so early in annealing diffusion is temporarily enhanced by factors of tens to a thousand. It subsides once the interstitials annihilate at the surface and elsewhere—hence "transient" enhanced diffusion (TED)—and is worst for long, relatively low-temperature anneals. Because it thwarts shallow junctions, it is countered with rapid spike anneals and carbon co-implantation.
