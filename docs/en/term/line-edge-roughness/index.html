What is Line edge roughness (LER)?

Nanometer-scale roughness along the edges of fine resist lines. It causes line-width variation (LWR) and leads to variation in transistor characteristics.

Japanese name: ラインエッジラフネス(LER)

Line edge roughness (LER) appears in 3 practice questions across 2 topics, including Lithography, Etching.

Field: Lithography

LER versus LWR

Line edge roughness (LER) measures how far a single pattern edge wanders from a straight line, usually reported as three standard deviations (3σ) of the edge position. Line width roughness (LWR) measures the variation of the distance between the two edges of a line. If the two edges roughen independently, LWR is about √2 times LER; correlated edges give less. Roughness is also characterized by its spatial frequency content: long-wavelength variation changes the effective width of a transistor or wire, while very short-wavelength wiggles tend to average out over the device.

Where the roughness comes from

Several sources add together. Resist polymers and their aggregates are a few nanometers across, setting a floor on edge smoothness. In chemically amplified resists, photogenerated acid diffuses during the post-exposure bake, blurring the latent image. The number of photons absorbed and acid generator molecules activated in each small area fluctuates statistically, called shot noise. A low-contrast aerial image makes all of these worse, because a small change in local dose then moves the edge further. Etch can then transfer, amplify or partly smooth the resulting roughness.

Why EUV makes it harder

Photon energy is inversely proportional to wavelength, so a 13.5 nm EUV photon carries about 92 eV, roughly 14 times the 6.4 eV of a 193 nm ArF photon. At the same dose, only about one fourteenth as many photons arrive, perhaps a few hundred to a few thousand in a 20 nm square, and the relative fluctuation scales as 1/√N. These stochastic effects show up as LER, missing or bridging features and unopened contacts. Raising dose reduces them but lowers throughput, an instance of the trade-off between resolution, roughness and sensitivity.

Why it matters for devices

Roughness of 2-3 nm is minor at large dimensions but exceeds 10 percent of a 20 nm line. In transistors, gate-length variation along the width causes threshold-voltage and leakage variation between nominally identical devices, and in interconnects it raises wire resistance and its spread. Trimming or shrinking a pattern does not remove roughness, so it becomes a larger fraction of the final width. LER is typically measured from top-down critical-dimension scanning electron microscope (CD-SEM) images, with corrections for image noise, which otherwise inflates the result.

FAQ

What is the difference between LER and LWR?

LER describes the roughness of one edge of a line, while LWR describes the variation in the line's width, combining both edges. For uncorrelated edges LWR is about √2 times LER.

Why is line edge roughness worse with EUV?

Each EUV photon carries about 14 times the energy of an ArF photon, so far fewer photons deliver the same dose, and the statistical fluctuation in photon count shows up as rough edges and random defects.

How is LER measured?

It is usually extracted from top-down CD-SEM images by detecting edge positions along a line and computing their 3σ deviation, with care taken to remove the contribution of SEM image noise.
