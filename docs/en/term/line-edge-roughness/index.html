What is Line edge roughness (LER)?

Nanometer-scale roughness along the edges of fine resist lines. It causes line-width variation (LWR) and leads to variation in transistor characteristics.

Japanese name: ラインエッジラフネス(LER)

Which metric describes nanometer-scale roughness on the sidewalls of fine resist patterns?

  1. ADepth of focus (DOF)
  2. BNumerical aperture (NA)
  3. CLine edge roughness (LER)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Line edge roughness (LER)

LER quantifies the roughness of pattern edges (usually as 3σ); expressed as linewidth variation it is called LWR. At linewidths near 20 nm, 2–3 nm of roughness exceeds 10% of the width, causing variation in transistor thresholds and wire resistance. Causes include resist molecular size, acid diffusion and photon shot noise, and roughness can be reduced by smoothing during etch.

What is the main cause of the stochastic fluctuations that are a problem in EUV lithography?

  1. AEUV light has a longer wavelength than ArF
  2. BEUV lithography uses lenses
  3. CEach EUV photon carries much energy, so there are fewer photons for the same dose
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Each EUV photon carries much energy, so there are fewer photons for the same dose

Photon energy is inversely proportional to wavelength: EUV (13.5 nm, about 92 eV) carries about 14 times the energy of ArF (193 nm, about 6.4 eV). At equal dose, only 1/14 as many photons arrive—perhaps a few hundred to a few thousand in a 20 nm square—fluctuating statistically by about √N. These stochastic effects cause missing or bridging features, LER and unopened contacts. Raising dose helps but lowers throughput.
