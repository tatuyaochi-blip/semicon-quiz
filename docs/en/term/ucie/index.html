What is UCIe?

Short for Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express: an industry-standard die-to-die interface intended to let chiplets from different companies connect to each other.

Japanese name: UCIe

Field: Advanced packaging (3D and chiplets)

Purpose of UCIe

UCIe (Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express) is an industry-standard die-to-die interface for connecting chiplets within a single package. It was announced in 2022 by a group of major semiconductor companies, cloud providers and others, and a consortium to manage the specification was later established. Until then, chiplet connections were mostly proprietary to each company, making it hard to combine chiplets other than those from the same company. By defining a common standard, UCIe aims to let chiplets from different companies and different fabrication processes be combined like components.

Structure of the specification

UCIe is defined in several layers. The physical layer specifies bump arrangement and pitch, electrical signaling, lane counts, clock forwarding, and a mechanism for repairing faulty lanes by switching to spare lanes. The die-to-die adapter above it handles link initialization and error detection with retry. At the top, the protocol layer can carry PCI Express and CXL, and a streaming mode is also provided for carrying custom protocols. Because the PCI Express and CXL software mechanisms can be reused as well, existing investments are easy to leverage.

Support for two kinds of package

UCIe provides two physical-layer specifications according to the type of package. The standard-package version connects chiplets on an ordinary organic substrate, with a wider bump pitch and room for longer traces. The advanced-package version uses fine wiring such as a silicon interposer or silicon bridge, with a bump pitch as narrow as a few tens of micrometers, achieving high bandwidth density and low energy per bit over short distances. By the mid-2020s, a specification for 3D stacking with hybrid bonding had also been added.

Compared with other interfaces

PCI Express and CXL mainly connect CPUs with peripherals and memory devices through printed circuit boards or cables. UCIe can carry those protocols, but its physical layer is designed for short in-package wiring, achieving low latency and power with many parallel signal lines instead of the complex equalization of board-level SerDes. Other chiplet interconnects include BoW from the OCP (Open Compute Project) and various proprietary interfaces. Even with a common link standard, challenges remain in combining chiplets from different companies, including power, thermal design, testing and assembly.

FAQ

What is UCIe, in simple terms?

An industry-standard die-to-die interface for connecting chiplets within a package. It was announced in 2022 with the aim of making it easier to combine chiplets made by different companies and in different processes.

How does UCIe differ from PCI Express and CXL?

PCI Express and CXL are standards for connecting devices mainly through boards and cables. UCIe is a standard for short in-package connections, with a physical layer designed for dense wiring between chiplets. PCI Express and CXL protocols can also be carried on top of it.

With UCIe, can chiplets from any company be freely combined?

A common connection scheme makes combining them easier, but that alone does not make free combination possible. Package assembly methods, power and thermal design, test methods and functional verification must also be aligned, and an ecosystem covering all of these is being developed.
