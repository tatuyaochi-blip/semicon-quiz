What is WLP (wafer-level package)?

A package in which redistribution wiring and external terminals are formed while the chips are still in wafer form, before dicing. The result is a small package about the size of the chip.

Japanese name: WLP(ウエハレベルパッケージ)

How a wafer-level package is built

In a wafer-level package (WLP), packaging steps run on the whole wafer at once instead of on individual dies. On top of the chip's passivation, a polymer dielectric is coated, and copper redistribution layers (RDL) are plated to route signals from the fine-pitch bond pads to a coarser array of terminal positions. Under-bump metallization is formed at those positions and solder balls are attached. Only then is the wafer diced. Because every die is processed in parallel by wafer-fab-like lithography and plating, the package adds almost no area and the cost per die falls as die count per wafer rises.

Fan-in vs. fan-out WLP

In fan-in WLP the redistribution wiring and solder balls must fit inside the die outline, so the package is exactly die-sized and the number of terminals is limited by the die area and ball pitch. Fan-out WLP removes that limit: diced, known-good dies are placed on a carrier with space between them and embedded in mold compound to form a reconstituted wafer. The RDL can then extend beyond the die edge onto the mold area, allowing more I/O. Fan-out needs no laminate substrate and is thin, so it is used even for smartphone processors.

WLP vs. other chip-scale packages

A chip-scale package (CSP) is any package no larger than about 1.2 times the die area, usually with BGA-style ball terminals underneath. Many CSPs still mount the die on a small substrate with wire bonds or flip-chip bumps. A fan-in WLP is the limiting case of a CSP: there is no separate substrate at all, and the package outline equals the die. That makes it the smallest and thinnest option, with very short connections that keep parasitic inductance low, which suits high-frequency and power-management parts.

Design trade-offs

Without a substrate in between, the silicon is soldered almost directly to the printed circuit board. Silicon and board materials expand differently with temperature, so thermal cycling strains the solder joints, and the strain grows with die size. Fan-in WLP is therefore most common for relatively small dies with modest pin counts, such as power-management chips, sensors and RF components in phones and wearables. Larger or higher-I/O dies move to fan-out WLP or to conventional flip-chip packages. The bare silicon edges also need care during handling and board assembly.

Answer: C. Redistribution and external terminals are formed while still in wafer form, and the wafer is then singulated

In WLP, redistribution layers and solder balls are formed across the whole wafer before dicing, then the wafer is singulated into chips. FAQ

What does WLP stand for in semiconductor packaging?

WLP stands for wafer-level package, a package whose redistribution wiring and solder terminals are formed on the whole wafer before it is diced into individual chips.

What is the difference between fan-in and fan-out WLP?

Fan-in WLP keeps all terminals within the die outline, so the package is exactly die-sized but I/O count is limited. Fan-out WLP embeds dies in mold compound so wiring and balls can extend beyond the die edge.

Is WLP the same as CSP?

WLP is one type of chip-scale package. CSP covers any package up to roughly 1.2 times the die area, while a fan-in WLP matches the die size exactly and has no separate substrate.
