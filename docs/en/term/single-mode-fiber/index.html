What is Single-mode fiber?

Optical fiber that carries only one propagation mode, with a core diameter of about 9 µm. With no pulse spreading from different modes traveling at different speeds (modal dispersion), it is used for long-distance and high-speed links.

Japanese name: シングルモードファイバ

When a silicon photonic circuit receives light from standard single-mode fiber, which is a correct reason polarization becomes an issue?

  1. AThe polarization in the fiber is not fixed, while silicon waveguides behave very differently depending on polarization
  2. BAfter passing through the fiber, light loses its polarization and can no longer propagate in a silicon waveguide
  3. CSilicon waveguides pass light the same way regardless of polarization, but the photodetector can detect only one polarization
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The polarization in the fiber is not fixed, while silicon waveguides behave very differently depending on polarization

Standard single-mode fiber does not maintain polarization, so fiber bending or temperature changes alter the polarization of the light arriving at the receiver. Meanwhile, in a flat silicon waveguide about 0.5 µm wide and about 0.22 µm thick, horizontally oscillating (TE) and vertically oscillating (TM) polarizations have very different effective indices and losses, and the characteristics of grating couplers and ring resonators also change with polarization. Solutions include devices that split light into two polarizations and rotate one of them, and two-dimensional grating couplers that handle both polarizations.

Why is active alignment—aligning while actually passing laser light through and measuring the output—often used when attaching single-mode fiber to a silicon photonic chip?

  1. AThe fiber core is thick, 50 µm or more in diameter, and aligning by eye using the outline makes the light so strong that it damages the photodetector
  2. BFixing the fiber while passing light through matches the refractive indices of the fiber and chip, so no loss arises even if the position shifts
  3. CThe mode is small, so even a misalignment of a few µm or less greatly increases loss, and the optimal position must be found by measurement
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The mode is small, so even a misalignment of a few µm or less greatly increases loss, and the optimal position must be found by measurement

Single-mode fiber has a mode diameter of about 10 µm, and even between modes of the same size, a lateral offset of about 2.5 µm adds about 1 dB of loss. At couplers where the chip-side mode is smaller, the allowable misalignment is submicron. Aligning by the outline alone (passive alignment) is often not accurate enough, so light is passed through, the position that maximizes received power is searched for, and the fiber is fixed with UV-curable resin or similar. However, this takes time for each fiber and is a major contributor to manufacturing cost, so passive alignment methods that align simply by seating the fiber in V-grooves or the like are also being researched and commercialized.
