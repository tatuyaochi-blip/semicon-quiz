What is Wi-Fi?

The common name for wireless LANs based on the IEEE 802.11 standards; the name shows that a device is certified by the industry group Wi-Fi Alliance. It mainly uses the 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz and 6 GHz bands and connects devices to a LAN through access points. Access to the air is coordinated with CSMA/CA, and speed and efficiency come from OFDM, MIMO and, from Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax), OFDMA. Traffic is encrypted with WPA2 or WPA3.

What is the main reason wireless LAN uses CSMA/CA rather than CSMA/CD, which has been used in wired Ethernet?

  1. AOn a wireless channel it is hard to detect a collision by receiving other stations' weak signals while transmitting, so stations wait before sending to avoid collisions as far as possible
  2. BWireless is faster than wired, so collisions have little effect on performance
  3. CIn wireless LAN a station and the access point always communicate one-to-one, so collisions cannot happen
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. On a wireless channel it is hard to detect a collision by receiving other stations' weak signals while transmitting, so stations wait before sending to avoid collisions as far as possible

CSMA/CD is used on shared wired media such as old hub-based Ethernet: a station that detects a collision while sending stops and resends after a random time. On a wireless channel, a station's own signal is so strong that it is hard to receive a distant station's weak signal at the same time, and collisions between stations that cannot hear each other (hidden stations) are invisible to the sender. So with CSMA/CA, a station confirms the channel is idle and then waits a fixed time plus a random backoff before sending. The receiver returns an ACK when it receives the frame correctly, and if no ACK arrives the sender assumes a collision or similar and retransmits. In today's wired Ethernet, connected full duplex to switches, collisions do not occur and CSMA/CD is effectively unused.

Which correctly describes a general difference between the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands of wireless LAN?

  1. AThe 2.4 GHz band reaches farther even with obstacles, but it is shared with microwave ovens, Bluetooth and others, so it gets congested easily and has few non-overlapping channels
  2. BThe 5 GHz band passes through walls and other obstacles more easily than the 2.4 GHz band and reaches farther
  3. CThe 2.4 GHz band is allocated exclusively to wireless LAN, so it suffers no interference from other devices
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The 2.4 GHz band reaches farther even with obstacles, but it is shared with microwave ovens, Bluetooth and others, so it gets congested easily and has few non-overlapping channels

In general, the higher the frequency, the more easily radio waves are weakened by walls and other obstacles and the shorter their range. The 2.4 GHz band reaches well, but as a licence-free ISM band it is used by many devices such as microwave ovens and Bluetooth, so it suffers interference, and only about three or four 20 MHz channels do not overlap one another. The 5 GHz band has more channels and makes it easy to bond them into wider channels for higher speed, but it is weaker against obstacles, and some channels require the DFS procedure to avoid interfering with weather radar and the like. In the 2020s, Wi-Fi 6E and Wi-Fi 7 also opened up the 6 GHz band (the usable range differs by country and region).
