What is FMEA (failure mode and effects analysis)?

A method that lists the possible failure modes of a product or process, evaluates their effects and severity, and plans countermeasures in advance.

Japanese name: FMEA(故障モード影響解析)

Field: Yield and quality control

How an FMEA is built

Failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA) is a structured, team-based review carried out before problems occur. For each component of a design or each step of a process, the team lists the ways it could fail (failure modes), the consequences of each failure (effects), the likely causes and the controls already in place. Each mode is then scored for severity, occurrence and detection, commonly on 1-to-10 scales, where a high detection score means the failure is hard to catch. Multiplying the three gives the risk priority number (RPN), which ranks where countermeasures should go first.

Design FMEA vs. process FMEA

A design FMEA examines a product's design, such as a chip, package or module, asking how each function could fail and what the user would experience. A process FMEA examines manufacturing steps. In a wafer fab, for example, a CMP step might fail by over-polishing, with the effect of thin copper lines and high resistance, caused by an incorrect endpoint, and detected by thickness metrology. Countermeasures can lower occurrence, for instance through automated run-to-run control, or improve detection through added inspection. After actions are taken, the scores are re-evaluated and the document is kept current.

Limits of the risk priority number

The RPN is convenient but crude. Very different risks can share the same product: a severe failure that is rare and easily detected may score the same as a trivial one that happens often. For this reason teams usually treat high-severity modes, especially those affecting safety, as requiring action regardless of RPN. Some newer guidelines, notably in the automotive industry, replace the RPN with an action-priority table that weights severity first. Scores are also subjective, so consistent rating criteria and cross-functional review matter as much as the arithmetic.

FMEA vs. other quality tools

FMEA is proactive and works bottom-up from individual failure modes to their effects. Fault tree analysis (FTA) works top-down, starting from one undesired event and tracing the combinations of causes that could produce it. Pareto charts and histograms analyze defects that have already occurred, statistical process control (SPC) watches running processes for abnormalities, advanced process control (APC) actively corrects tool settings, and gage R&R checks whether a measurement system is precise enough. In semiconductor ramp-up, FMEA results often decide where those other controls are applied.

Answer: A. A procedure for demonstrating to the customer, with documents and samples, that parts made with the same processes, equipment and conditions as mass production meet requirements, and obtaining approval

PPAP (Production Part Approval Process) is a procedure in which a supplier, before starting mass production, demonstrates that parts made with the same processes, equipment and conditions as mass production meet customer requirements and can be produced consistently, and obtains approval. Submissions include design records, process FMEA, control plan, dimensional and characteristic measurement results, process capability studies and samples. The approved process becomes the baseline for mass production, so changing the process later requires notifying the customer. It is separate from patent filing or field failure reporting.

Answer: A. Tabulating, for each part of a circuit, the failure modes and failure rates, their effects and the proportion detectable by safety mechanisms, and calculating SPFM, LFM and other metrics

FMEDA is FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis) with a diagnostic perspective added. For each part, such as memory, CPU, clock and power supply, failure rates and failure modes such as stuck-at faults and malfunctions are assigned, and the analysis summarizes whether each leads to violation of a safety goal and which safety mechanisms detect how much of it. From the results, SPFM, LFM and PMHF are calculated, and safety mechanisms are added if the targets are not met. Failure rate values are derived from failure rate handbooks, test data and so on.

FAQ

What does FMEA stand for?

FMEA stands for failure mode and effects analysis. It is a method for listing possible failures of a product or process, evaluating their effects and planning countermeasures before they happen.

How is the risk priority number calculated in FMEA?

The RPN is the product of the severity, occurrence and detection scores, each commonly rated from 1 to 10. Higher numbers indicate risks to address first, although high-severity items usually need action regardless of RPN.

What is the difference between FMEA and FTA?

FMEA starts from individual failure modes and works forward to their effects, covering the whole design or process. FTA starts from one specific undesired event and works backward through a logic tree of causes.
