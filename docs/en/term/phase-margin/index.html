What is Phase margin?

How far the phase lag is from 180° at the frequency where the feedback loop gain is 1 (0 dB). A small margin causes ringing, and zero or negative margin causes oscillation; 45-60° or more is a common target.

Japanese name: 位相余裕(フェーズマージン)

Which correctly describes a voltage follower using an op-amp?

  1. AIts voltage gain is 1, with high input impedance and low output impedance, so it is used as a buffer
  2. BIt is used as a comparator with infinite voltage gain
  3. CIt is used as a circuit that converts input current to voltage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Its voltage gain is 1, with high input impedance and low output impedance, so it is used as a buffer

A voltage follower is a non-inverting amplifier with the output tied directly to the inverting input (gain 1), so the output equals the input. Its very high input impedance and low output impedance make it a buffer that passes signals from high-impedance sensors or dividers to later stages or loads without altering them. As this is the deepest-feedback configuration, some op-amps lack phase margin and may oscillate, so check that the datasheet says "unity-gain stable."

What is the effect of applying negative feedback to an amplifier?

  1. AGain is reduced, but gain stability improves, bandwidth widens and distortion decreases
  2. BGain becomes infinite
  3. CIt always starts to oscillate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Gain is reduced, but gain stability improves, bandwidth widens and distortion decreases

Negative feedback subtracts part of the output from the input, reducing gain. In return, closed-loop gain becomes about 1/β (the inverse of the feedback fraction), largely independent of device variation and temperature; bandwidth widens; and distortion and output impedance shrink by a factor of (1 + loop gain). Op-amp circuits are built on this. At frequencies where the phase shifts enough to make feedback positive, however, the loop oscillates, so phase margin must be secured.
