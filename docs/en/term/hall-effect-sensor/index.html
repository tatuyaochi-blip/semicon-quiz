What is Hall-effect sensor?

A sensor that detects magnetic fields using the Hall effect: a magnetic field applied to a current-carrying semiconductor produces a voltage perpendicular to both. It is used, for example, to sense motor rotation.

Japanese name: ホール素子

Which compound semiconductor, with very high electron mobility, is used in magnetic sensors (Hall elements) and the like?

  1. ASiC
  2. BInSb (indium antimonide)
  3. CAlN
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. InSb (indium antimonide)

InSb has one of the highest room-temperature electron mobilities of any semiconductor, about 77,000 cm²/(V·s). Since Hall-sensor sensitivity is proportional to mobility, it yields large outputs from small fields; mass-produced by companies such as Asahi Kasei, InSb Hall sensors detect motor rotor position and serve in electronic compasses. Its small band gap (about 0.17 eV) also makes cooled InSb detectors useful in the 3–5 µm mid-infrared.

What does a sensor using the Hall effect (Hall element) mainly detect?

  1. ATemperature
  2. BMagnetic field
  3. CHumidity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Magnetic field

Applying a magnetic field perpendicular to current in a semiconductor deflects carriers by the Lorentz force, producing a Hall voltage perpendicular to both. Hall sensors use it to detect magnetic fields for brushless-motor rotor position, contactless current sensing and phone-cover detection. High-mobility InSb and GaAs give high sensitivity, and silicon Hall elements can be integrated with CMOS.
