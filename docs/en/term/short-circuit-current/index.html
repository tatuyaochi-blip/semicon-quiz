What is Short-circuit current?

Current that flows from supply to ground in a CMOS gate during the brief time while the input switches and both the pMOS and nMOS are on. Also called crowbar current, it adds to power consumption.

Japanese name: 貫通電流

Field: MOSFET

How short-circuit current flows in CMOS

In a CMOS inverter, only the pMOS is on when the input is 0 and only the nMOS is on when it is 1, so in steady state there is always an off transistor between supply and ground and almost no current flows. During an input transition, however, while the input voltage is above the nMOS threshold voltage and below the supply voltage minus the magnitude of the pMOS threshold voltage, both transistors are on at once and current flows directly from supply to ground. This is short-circuit current, also called crowbar current. In circuits whose supply voltage is below the sum of the two threshold voltages, the two transistors are not on simultaneously, so almost no short-circuit current flows.

Effect on power and how to reduce it

Short-circuit power grows as input rise and fall times get slower, because both transistors stay on longer. It is also proportional to the number of transitions, so it rises with operating frequency. If input and output transition times are similar, it typically stays at roughly 10% or less of total dynamic power. Logic design tools therefore set an upper limit on signal transition time (max transition) and insert buffers where long wires slow the transitions. Lowering the supply voltage narrows the voltage range in which both transistors conduct, which also reduces short-circuit current.

When inputs linger at intermediate voltages

If an input stays at an intermediate voltage for a long time, short-circuit current keeps flowing, raising supply current and heat, and the output may oscillate. Typical causes are floating inputs connected to nothing, slowly changing signals, undefined signals from powered-down blocks, and signals between blocks at different supply voltages. Countermeasures include tying unused inputs with pull-up or pull-down resistors, receiving slow signals with hysteretic Schmitt-trigger inputs, and inserting isolation cells at the outputs of blocks that can be powered down and level shifters where voltages differ. Datasheet limits on input transition time also exist to keep this current down.

Shoot-through in power circuits

In inverters and half-bridges, if the upper and lower switches are on at the same time, the supply is shorted through both switches and a very large current flows. This current is also called shoot-through current, or an arm short. Because it can destroy the devices, and because switches take time to turn off, a dead time with both switches off is inserted between turning one off and turning the other on. A fast voltage change from the other switch's transition can also couple through the gate-drain capacitance, raise the gate voltage and turn a switch on unintentionally, which is prevented with negative gate voltage or a Miller clamp.

Which is the correct definition of the fill factor (FF) of a solar cell?

  1. AShort-circuit current ÷ open-circuit voltage
  2. BOutput power ÷ incident light energy
  3. CMaximum power ÷ (open-circuit voltage × short-circuit current)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Maximum power ÷ (open-circuit voltage × short-circuit current)

The fill factor is the power at the maximum power point divided by the product of open-circuit voltage and short-circuit current, indicating how close the I-V curve is to a rectangle. Crystalline silicon cells typically reach about 0.75–0.85. It drops with high series resistance, low shunt resistance (leakage paths) or heavy recombination. Efficiency is η = Voc × Isc × FF ÷ incident light power, so FF is one of the three factors that determine it.

FAQ

What else is short-circuit current called?

In CMOS circuits it is called short-circuit current or crowbar current; the Japanese term is kantsu denryu. The current that flows when the upper and lower switches of an inverter or half-bridge are on at the same time is usually called shoot-through current.

How can short-circuit current be reduced?

In CMOS circuits the basics are to keep input transitions fast, inserting buffers to meet transition-time limits, to eliminate floating inputs, and to use Schmitt-trigger inputs for slow signals. In power circuits, set an appropriate dead time and design the gate drive so switches do not turn on by mistake.

How does short-circuit current differ from leakage current?

Short-circuit current is a switching-related current that flows only during the brief time when both the pMOS and nMOS are on as the input changes. Leakage current, such as subthreshold current and gate leakage, keeps flowing even when transistors are off, consuming power even when the circuit is idle.
