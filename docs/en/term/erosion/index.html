What is Erosion?

In CMP, excessive polishing of an entire region of dense lines, insulator included, so it ends up lower than its surroundings. Along with dishing of wide lines, it is a typical CMP defect.

Japanese name: エロージョン

Field: CMP and cleaning

Why dense arrays erode

In damascene copper chemical mechanical polishing (CMP), polishing continues past the point where the field is cleared so that no metal residue remains. In a region of closely spaced lines, the narrow dielectric strips between the lines cannot hold the pad up on their own, and the slurry removes barrier and dielectric along with the copper. The whole array therefore sinks below the surrounding open dielectric. Erosion is usually measured as the dielectric thickness lost in the array relative to the field, and it grows with metal pattern density and overpolish time.

Erosion vs. dishing

Dishing happens inside a single wide line or pad: the softer copper in the middle is polished below the adjacent dielectric, forming a dish whose depth increases with line width. Erosion is a regional effect across many narrow lines, metal and dielectric together, and depends on density rather than width. In a dense array both act at once, so the total copper thickness lost is roughly the erosion plus the dishing of each individual line. Together they are often reported as post-CMP topography.

Why it matters

Thinner copper means higher line resistance and wider resistance spread across the die, which shifts timing. The recessed array also passes its topography to the next level: the surface is no longer flat, consuming lithography depth of focus, and metal deposited for the next layer can remain in the low spots after its own CMP, leaving residues that short adjacent lines. Erosion therefore limits how much overpolish a process can afford.

How it is controlled

Layout rules keep metal density within a window over each area of the chip, and dummy fill is inserted into sparse regions so that polishing sees a more uniform pattern. On the process side, polishing is split into steps with different slurries, a bulk copper step followed by a barrier step whose selectivity is tuned to stop on the dielectric, and endpoint detection limits overpolish. Harder pads also spread pressure more evenly across arrays.

Answer: C. The center of wide wiring is over-polished relative to its surroundings, becoming dish-shaped

Dishing occurs in wide metal lines and pads, where the softer metal at the center is polished below the surrounding dielectric, forming a dish-shaped recess that deepens with width. Lines thin and their resistance rises, and topography carries into upper layers. Over-polishing of dielectric and metal together across dense arrays is erosion. Designs cap linewidth or slot wide lines to limit dishing.

FAQ

What is the difference between dishing and erosion in CMP?

Dishing is the recess of metal inside one wide feature and grows with width. Erosion is the loss of metal and dielectric together over a dense array and grows with pattern density.

How does dummy fill reduce CMP erosion?

Dummy metal shapes make pattern density more uniform, so the pad removes material at a more even rate everywhere. That reduces the height difference between dense arrays and their surroundings.

Does erosion mean anything else in semiconductor processing?

Yes. The word is also used for the race-track groove worn into a magnetron sputtering target, for the gradual loss of an etch mask at its edges, and for plasma wear of chamber walls and parts.
