What is DVFS (dynamic voltage and frequency scaling)?

Changing operating frequency and supply voltage dynamically according to load. Since dynamic power scales with the square of voltage, lowering voltage during light loads saves a lot of power.

Japanese name: DVFS(動的電圧・周波数制御)

How DVFS works

A CMOS chip's dynamic power is P = αCV²f, where α is switching activity, C the switched capacitance, V the supply voltage and f the clock frequency. A circuit only meets timing at a given frequency if the supply is high enough, so DVFS uses a table of valid voltage-frequency pairs, called operating points or P-states. A governor in the operating system or in on-chip power-management firmware watches load and temperature and picks a point. When speeding up, the voltage is raised first and the clock second; when slowing down, the clock drops first and the voltage follows, so the logic never runs too fast for its supply.

Why voltage is the big lever

Lowering frequency alone reduces power linearly but leaves the energy per operation, proportional to CV², unchanged, because the same work simply takes longer. Lowering voltage is what saves energy. If voltage can scale in proportion to frequency, halving both cuts dynamic power to one-eighth and energy for a fixed task to one-quarter. Even a modest 10% voltage reduction removes about 19% of dynamic power. In practice voltage cannot fall in proportion forever, so real savings are smaller than this ideal cubic picture.

Limits and race-to-idle

Two effects bound DVFS. First, there is a minimum workable voltage: as the supply approaches the transistor threshold, speed collapses and SRAM cells become unreliable, so the bottom of the voltage range is fixed well above zero. Second, leakage power flows for as long as the circuit is powered, so running slowly for longer accumulates leakage energy. When leakage is significant it can be better to race to idle: finish the work at a high operating point, then enter a deep sleep state (C-state) or power-gate the block entirely.

Where you will meet it

DVFS runs in essentially every smartphone SoC, laptop CPU, server processor and GPU. Each voltage domain, which may be a single core or a cluster of cores, needs its own adjustable supply, usually provided by a power management IC (PMIC) or on-chip regulators that change output on command from the SoC. The same mechanism implements thermal throttling, where operating points are lowered to keep junction temperature within limits, and short boost modes, where they are raised while thermal and current headroom allow.

Answer: A. Power falls to one-eighth and energy to one-quarter

Power is proportional to V²f, so halving both V and f gives (1/2)² × 1/2 = 1/8. The computation takes twice as long, so energy is 1/8 × 2 = 1/4. Equivalently, since energy per switching event is proportional to CV², energy scales with the square of voltage. In practice the voltage range is limited and static power exists, so the reduction is not this ideal.

FAQ

Does lowering CPU frequency alone save energy?

It lowers power but not dynamic energy per task, because each operation still costs about CV² and the work just takes longer; leakage energy can even rise. The energy savings come from the voltage reduction that a lower frequency makes possible.

What is the difference between P-states and C-states?

P-states are voltage-frequency operating points used while the processor is executing, which is what DVFS selects. C-states are idle states of increasing depth, from stopping the clock to removing power, used when there is no work.

What is the difference between DVFS and clock gating?

Clock gating stops the clock to idle logic so it stops switching, but the supply voltage and leakage remain. DVFS changes the voltage and frequency of a whole domain that is still doing work.
