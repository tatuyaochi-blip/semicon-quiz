What is GAA (gate-all-around)?

A transistor structure in which the gate surrounds the channel on all sides. It controls current better than FinFET and is used in the generations after FinFET.

Japanese name: GAA(ゲートオールアラウンド)

Field: Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA)

Why wrap the gate all the way around

Short-channel effects arise when the drain's electric field reaches into the channel and competes with the gate. The scale length λ estimates how far that influence extends; gate lengths of about 5–10 λ keep it small, and every additional gated surface shrinks λ. A planar MOSFET gates one face, a FinFET three, and a gate-all-around (GAA) transistor surrounds each channel completely. This strongest electrostatic control suppresses punch-through and leakage at shorter gate lengths, and like FinFETs, GAA devices use nearly undoped channels with threshold voltage set by the gate work function.

Nanosheet geometry

Production GAA devices use nanosheets: silicon plates about 5 nm thick and roughly 15–50 nm wide, typically three stacked vertically about 10 nm apart, each wrapped by gate dielectric and metal. A nanosheet resembles a FinFET fin laid on its side and stacked. A FinFET's width can only change in whole fins, but sheet width can be drawn continuously in layout, using wide sheets for high-performance cells and narrow ones for low power and SRAM. More sheets add current per footprint but complicate processing and add parasitic capacitance.

How nanosheets are built

Fabrication starts with an epitaxial stack of alternating Si and SiGe layers, the SiGe containing about 25–30% germanium. The stack is patterned into fins and a dummy gate is formed. The ends of the SiGe layers are recessed and filled with a dielectric inner spacer, which reduces gate-to-source/drain capacitance and prevents shorts. After source/drain epitaxy, the dummy gate is removed and the SiGe is etched away with high selectivity, leaving the silicon sheets suspended. ALD then fills the gaps with high-k dielectric and metal gate.

Industry adoption and what follows

Samsung announced volume production of GAA at its 3 nm node in 2022 under the name MBCFET; TSMC adopted nanosheets at its 2 nm node and Intel at 18A as RibbonFET, making GAA the successor to the FinFET. Researchers are pursuing further steps: the forksheet, which places nMOS and pMOS sheets on either side of a thin dielectric wall; the CFET, which stacks nMOS over pMOS; and two-dimensional channel materials such as MoS2 that remain semiconducting at a single layer about 0.65 nm thick.

A gate-all-around (GAA) transistor stacks several channels shaped as sheets a few nm thick (nanosheets) or wires, each fully surrounded by the gate. Control from all sides is even stronger than in a FinFET, suppressing short-channel effects down to shorter gate lengths. Samsung adopted it at 3 nm (2022), TSMC at 2 nm and Intel at 18A, making it the successor standard to the FinFET.

Which correctly describes the CFET (complementary FET), researched as the structure to follow GAA?

  1. AIt simplifies the structure by eliminating the gate electrode
  2. BIt stacks nMOS and pMOS vertically to reduce cell area
  3. CIt returns transistors to a planar structure

Answer: B. It stacks nMOS and pMOS vertically to reduce cell area

A CFET stacks the nMOS and pMOS nanosheets of a CMOS circuit vertically rather than placing them side by side, potentially building the same function in nearly half the area. Forming different materials and gate metals for n and p in the stack is complex, and routing connections to the upper and lower devices is challenging. Development targets practical use in the 2030s.

FAQ

What is the difference between GAA and FinFET?

A FinFET's gate covers three sides of a vertical fin, while a GAA transistor's gate surrounds stacked horizontal sheets or wires on all sides. GAA gives stronger gate control and allows the channel width to be set continuously instead of in whole fins.

What is a nanosheet transistor?

It is a GAA transistor whose channels are thin horizontal silicon sheets, about 5 nm thick and tens of nanometers wide, stacked about three high with the gate filling the spaces between and around them.

Why is SiGe used when making GAA transistors?

SiGe layers grown between the silicon channel layers act as sacrificial spacers. They can be etched away selectively later, opening gaps so the gate dielectric and metal can wrap around each silicon sheet.
