What is Selectivity?

In etching, the ratio of the etch rate of the target film to that of the mask or underlying film. Higher selectivity means less damage to the underlayer.

Japanese name: 選択比

Selectivity appears in 16 practice questions across 5 topics, including Etching, Process materials (gases and chemicals), CMP and cleaning.

Field: Etching

Try it: questions about Selectivity

Which correctly describes 'selectivity' in etching?

  1. AThe ratio of film thickness before and after etching
  2. BThe ratio of etch rates at the center and edge of the wafer
  3. CThe ratio of the etch rate of the target film to that of the mask or underlying film
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The ratio of the etch rate of the target film to that of the mask or underlying film

Selectivity is the ratio of the etch rate of the film being removed to that of a film that should remain (mask or underlying layer). Gate etching, for example, may need selectivity above 100 to the underlying gate dielectric only a few nm thick. Higher selectivity permits thinner masks and spares underlying layers. Variation of etch rate across the wafer is called uniformity.

If the oxide etch rate is 100 nm/min and the mask is consumed at 20 nm/min, what is the selectivity to the mask?

  1. A0.2
  2. B5
  3. C80
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 5

Selectivity is the etch rate of the target film divided by that of the mask (or underlayer): 100 ÷ 20 = 5. Etching 500 nm of oxide consumes 100 nm of mask, so allowing for overetch and edge erosion the mask should be at least about 150–200 nm thick. If selectivity is insufficient, a thicker mask or a hard mask is used.
