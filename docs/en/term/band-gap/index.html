What is Band gap?

The energy difference between the top of the valence band and the bottom of the conduction band. About 1.12 eV for silicon at room temperature. A larger band gap means better tolerance to high temperatures and voltages.

Japanese name: バンドギャップ

Field: Semiconductor physics

What the band gap controls

The band gap Eg sets which light a semiconductor absorbs or emits, via λ (nm) ≈ 1240 / Eg (eV): silicon at about 1.12 eV absorbs out to about 1.1 µm, germanium at about 0.66 eV to about 1.9 µm, and GaAs at 1.42 eV emits near 870 nm. It also sets the intrinsic carrier density, ni ∝ T^(3/2)·exp(−Eg/2kT), about 1×10^10 cm^-3 for silicon at room temperature, versus about 2×10^13 for germanium and 2×10^6 for GaAs. A small ni keeps junction leakage low and lets light doping control conductivity. The gap itself shrinks slightly with temperature, about 0.3 meV/K in silicon.

Direct vs. indirect band gap

In a direct-gap semiconductor such as GaAs or InGaN, the conduction-band minimum and valence-band maximum occur at the same crystal momentum, so an electron and hole can recombine by emitting a photon efficiently. Silicon has an indirect gap, so radiative recombination needs a phonon as well and is weak; recombination instead proceeds mainly through deep-level traps (Shockley-Read-Hall recombination). That is why LEDs and lasers use compound semiconductors: InGaN for blue and, with more indium, green; AlGaInP for red near 1.9–2.0 eV; AlGaN for deep ultraviolet.

Wide-bandgap materials for power

Wide-bandgap semiconductors, roughly 2–3 eV and above, tolerate high fields and high temperatures because few carriers are thermally excited. 4H-SiC (about 3.26 eV) and GaN (about 3.4 eV) have breakdown fields near ten times silicon's, so a SiC drift layer for the same voltage can be about one tenth as thick and about 100 times more heavily doped, sharply cutting on-resistance. Ultra-wide-bandgap Ga2O3 (about 4.5–4.9 eV) and diamond (about 5.5 eV) go further. Insulators sit higher still: SiO2 is about 9 eV and HfO2 about 5.8 eV.

Engineering the gap

Alloys tune Eg continuously: AlGaAs rises from 1.42 eV while staying lattice-matched to GaAs, In0.53Ga0.47As on InP gives about 0.75 eV for 1.55 µm photodetectors, and HgCdTe spans nearly 0 to 1.5 eV for infrared detectors. Heterojunctions such as the narrow-gap SiGe base of an HBT exploit gap differences. Very heavy doping narrows the apparent gap, while quantum confinement in fins or nanosheets a few nanometers thick widens it. The gap also sets the about 33% Shockley-Queisser limit for single-junction solar cells, and a bandgap reference circuit outputs about 1.2 V, close to silicon's gap extrapolated to 0 K.

Answer: B. About 1.12 eV

Silicon's band gap is about 1.12 eV at room temperature, so it absorbs light up to about 1.1 µm (1240 ÷ 1.12). About 0.66 eV is germanium, whose small gap makes leakage rise quickly at high temperature; about 3.4 eV is GaN, a wide-bandgap semiconductor suited to high voltage, high temperature and blue LEDs. Silicon sits in between, and together with its high-quality thermal oxide (SiO2) this made it the material of choice for integrated circuits.

  2. BJust above the top of the valence band
  3. CNear the middle of the band gap
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Just below the bottom of the conduction band (about 0.045 eV below)

The phosphorus donor level lies only about 0.045 eV below the conduction band, so even room-temperature thermal energy (about 0.026 eV) ionizes nearly all donors. Arsenic is about 0.054 eV, and the boron acceptor level is about 0.045 eV above the valence band. These shallow levels are weakly bound because the host's dielectric constant screens them, so they can be estimated with a hydrogen-atom model. Deep levels near midgap come from impurities such as gold and act as recombination centers.

FAQ

What is a band gap in simple terms?

It is the minimum energy needed to free an electron from a bond so it can conduct, equal to the energy difference between the top of the valence band and the bottom of the conduction band. Metals have no gap at the Fermi level, semiconductors have a moderate one and insulators a very large one.

How do you convert a band gap to a wavelength?

Use λ (nm) ≈ 1240 / Eg (eV). For silicon's 1.12 eV this gives about 1.1 µm, the longest wavelength silicon absorbs efficiently.

What is the difference between a direct and an indirect band gap?

In a direct gap the band edges align in crystal momentum, so electrons and holes recombine efficiently by emitting light, as in GaAs. In an indirect gap, such as silicon's, a phonon is also required, so light emission is weak.
