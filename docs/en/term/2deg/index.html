What is 2DEG (two-dimensional electron gas)?

A dense sheet of electrons confined at a heterojunction interface such as AlGaN/GaN. With little impurity scattering and high mobility, it serves as the channel of a HEMT.

Japanese name: 2次元電子ガス(2DEG)

  1. AA p-type inversion layer formed under the gate

Answer: C. A two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) formed by polarization at the AlGaN/GaN interface

In a GaN HEMT, the difference in spontaneous and piezoelectric polarization at the AlGaN/GaN interface creates a dense two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) of about 10¹³ cm⁻². Because no doping is needed to supply the electrons, scattering is low and mobility reaches about 2000 cm²/V·s, combining low on-resistance with fast switching. The 2DEG exists even at 0 V gate bias, so the basic device is normally on.

Answer: A. High-frequency, low-noise amplifiers in satellite TV receivers and mobile base stations

A HEMT uses the two-dimensional electron gas at a heterojunction as its channel; because the dopants supplying electrons are separated from the layer where electrons travel (modulation doping), impurity scattering is low, enabling low-noise, high-frequency amplification. Takashi Mimura of Fujitsu invented it in 1980. GaAs HEMTs serve satellite receivers and radio telescopes, and GaN HEMTs serve base-station and radar power amplifiers.
