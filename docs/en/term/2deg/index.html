What is 2DEG (two-dimensional electron gas)?

A dense sheet of electrons confined at a heterojunction interface such as AlGaN/GaN. With little impurity scattering and high mobility, it serves as the channel of a HEMT.

Japanese name: 2次元電子ガス(2DEG)

2DEG (two-dimensional electron gas) appears in 5 practice questions across 3 topics, including Compound semiconductors, Power semiconductors, Epitaxial growth.

Field: Compound semiconductors

Why the electrons are two-dimensional

At a heterojunction between a wide-bandgap barrier and a narrower-bandgap channel, the conduction-band offset and band bending create a roughly triangular potential well only a few nanometers wide on the channel side. Electrons trapped there have quantized energy levels for motion perpendicular to the interface but move freely along it, so they behave as a two-dimensional gas. Its density is therefore quoted per unit area, as a sheet carrier density in cm⁻², rather than per unit volume.

Modulation doping vs. polarization

In AlGaAs/GaAs structures, donors are placed in the AlGaAs barrier, often behind an undoped spacer layer, and their electrons fall into the GaAs well. This modulation doping separates the electrons from the ionized donors, reducing impurity scattering and giving very high mobility, especially at low temperature. AlGaN/GaN needs no intentional doping: GaN's wurtzite crystal is polar, and the difference in spontaneous and piezoelectric polarization at the interface induces a sheet density around 10¹³ cm⁻², roughly ten times typical GaAs values, with room-temperature mobility up to about 2000 cm²/V·s.

The 2DEG as a HEMT channel

In a high electron mobility transistor (HEMT), a gate on the barrier layer depletes or restores the 2DEG beneath it, switching the channel. High mobility gives low noise and high gain at microwave frequencies, which is why GaAs HEMTs serve satellite receivers and low-noise amplifiers. GaN's high density and breakdown field give high power density for base-station amplifiers, radar and power switching. Because the GaN 2DEG exists at zero gate bias, the basic device is normally on; normally-off parts use approaches such as a p-type GaN gate, a recessed gate or a cascode with a silicon MOSFET.

Keeping the 2DEG stable

A dense, mobile 2DEG gives low sheet resistance, typically a few hundred ohms per square in AlGaN/GaN. The layers around it must not conduct or trap charge. GaN buffers are made highly resistive with carbon or iron doping so the device can be turned off at high voltage, but traps in the buffer or at the surface can capture electrons under high-voltage stress and temporarily deplete the 2DEG. This current collapse, seen as a rise in dynamic on-resistance, is reduced with surface passivation, field plates and careful buffer design.

Answer: C. A two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) formed by polarization at the AlGaN/GaN interface

In a GaN HEMT, the difference in spontaneous and piezoelectric polarization at the AlGaN/GaN interface creates a dense two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) of about 10¹³ cm⁻². Because no doping is needed to supply the electrons, scattering is low and mobility reaches about 2000 cm²/V·s, combining low on-resistance with fast switching. The 2DEG exists even at 0 V gate bias, so the basic device is normally on.

Answer: A. High-frequency, low-noise amplifiers in satellite TV receivers and mobile base stations

A HEMT uses the two-dimensional electron gas at a heterojunction as its channel; because the dopants supplying electrons are separated from the layer where electrons travel (modulation doping), impurity scattering is low, enabling low-noise, high-frequency amplification. Takashi Mimura of Fujitsu invented it in 1980. GaAs HEMTs serve satellite receivers and radio telescopes, and GaN HEMTs serve base-station and radar power amplifiers.

FAQ

Why does a 2DEG have high electron mobility?

The electrons travel in an undoped region, separated from the ionized donors or polarization charges that supply them, so they suffer little impurity scattering. A smooth heterointerface also limits roughness scattering.

Why are GaN HEMTs normally on?

Polarization creates the 2DEG at the AlGaN/GaN interface without any gate voltage, so the channel conducts at 0 V. A negative gate voltage is needed to deplete it unless the structure is modified for normally-off operation.

What is the typical sheet density of a 2DEG in AlGaN/GaN?

About 10¹³ electrons per square centimeter, which together with mobility of around 1500 to 2000 cm²/V·s gives sheet resistance of a few hundred ohms per square.
