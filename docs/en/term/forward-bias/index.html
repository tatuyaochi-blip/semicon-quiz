What is Forward bias?

Applying a positive voltage to the p side and a negative voltage to the n side. The barrier drops and current flows readily. The opposite is reverse bias, under which almost no current flows.

Japanese name: 順方向バイアス

Forward bias appears in 6 practice questions across 4 topics, including PN junctions and diodes, Semiconductor physics, Bipolar transistors.

Field: PN junctions and diodes

What happens at the junction

At equilibrium, a PN junction has a built-in potential barrier, and the diffusion current of majority carriers trying to cross it exactly cancels the drift current pushed back by the junction field, so net current is zero. Forward bias, making the p side positive, opposes the built-in potential and lowers the barrier. Holes from the p side and electrons from the n side then diffuse across in large numbers, becoming injected minority carriers that recombine as they spread. Diffusion now outweighs drift, and current rises exponentially as I ≈ Is(exp(qV/nkT) − 1).

Typical numbers

At room temperature kT/q is about 26 mV, so an ideal diode (ideality factor n = 1) needs about 60 mV more forward voltage for each tenfold increase in current, or n × 60 mV in general; the slope of a semilog I-V plot gives n. Because the rise is exponential, a silicon diode seems to switch on at about 0.6–0.7 V at typical small-signal currents, though there is no sharp threshold. At constant current, the forward voltage of a silicon junction falls by roughly 2 mV per °C, a property used for temperature sensing.

Forward bias vs. reverse bias

Reverse bias raises the barrier and widens the depletion region, leaving only a tiny current of thermally generated minority carriers until breakdown. The dominant capacitance differs as well. Under reverse bias it is the depletion (junction) capacitance. Under forward bias, injected minority carriers stored near the junction create a diffusion capacitance, roughly Cd ≈ τI/(nkT/q), that grows with current and can reach several nanofarads. That stored charge must be removed before a diode or transistor can turn off, which limits switching speed.

Where you will meet it

A bipolar transistor in the active region has a forward-biased base-emitter junction; in saturation the base-collector junction is forward biased too, giving VCE of only about 0.1–0.2 V but slow turn-off because of stored charge. LEDs emit light when forward-injected carriers recombine radiatively. A forward-biased PIN diode floods its intrinsic layer with carriers, lowering its resistance (conductivity modulation), which makes it useful as an RF switch. Silicon optical modulators that inject carriers under forward bias are efficient but limited by nanosecond carrier lifetimes, so high-speed designs use reverse-biased depletion instead.

What causes a diffusion current to flow in a semiconductor?

  1. ACarrier movement due to an electric field
  2. BCarrier deflection due to a magnetic field
  3. CA gradient (difference) in carrier concentration
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. A gradient (difference) in carrier concentration

Carriers tend to spread from high to low concentration, and the current driven by this concentration gradient is the diffusion current (J = qD·dn/dx). Its cause differs from drift current, in which carriers are accelerated by an electric field. In a PN junction at equilibrium the two exactly cancel so the net current is zero; under forward bias diffusion wins and current flows. Current through the base of a bipolar transistor is also mainly diffusion current.

How is voltage applied to forward-bias a PN junction diode?

  1. AThe p side positive and the n side negative
  2. BThe p side negative and the n side positive
  3. CThe p side and n side at the same potential
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The p side positive and the n side negative

Making the p side (anode) positive and the n side (cathode) negative lets the applied voltage oppose the built-in potential, lowering the barrier so majority carriers are injected across the junction. This is forward bias, and current grows exponentially with voltage. Connecting it the other way raises the barrier, leaving only a tiny current of thermally generated minority carriers. At equal potential, diffusion and drift balance and the current is zero.

FAQ

How do you forward bias a diode?

Connect the p side (anode) to the more positive potential and the n side (cathode) to the more negative one, so the applied voltage lowers the junction barrier.

Why does current increase exponentially under forward bias?

The number of carriers with enough energy to cross the barrier follows a Boltzmann distribution, so lowering the barrier by V multiplies the injected current by about exp(qV/kT).

What is the difference between diffusion capacitance and junction capacitance?

Junction capacitance comes from the depletion region and dominates under reverse bias. Diffusion capacitance comes from minority carriers stored under forward bias and grows roughly in proportion to current.
