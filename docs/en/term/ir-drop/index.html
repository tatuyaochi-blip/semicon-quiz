What is IR drop?

The drop in supply voltage reaching a circuit because current (I) flows through the resistance (R) of the power wiring. It increases delay and can cause malfunctions.

Japanese name: IRドロップ

IR drop appears in 5 practice questions across 4 topics, including Interconnect processes, Packaging and assembly, IC design flow and EDA.

Field: Interconnect processes

Where the resistance comes from

Current reaches a transistor through a long chain: package balls or bumps, thick upper-level metal straps, stacks of vias, and finally the thin power rails that run along each row of standard cells. Every segment has resistance, and the lowest layers, being thinnest, contribute a disproportionate share. Ground has the same problem in reverse: current returning through VSS wiring raises the local ground. A cell therefore sees its supply squeezed from both sides, reduced VDD and elevated ground.

Static vs. dynamic IR drop

Static IR drop is computed from average current flowing through the grid resistance and shows regions that are chronically starved, such as the middle of a large block far from supply bumps. Dynamic IR drop captures short, local dips when many cells switch in the same clock edge and draw a burst of current. It is closely tied to inductive drop (L·di/dt) in the package and to how much decoupling capacitance sits nearby to supply charge during the burst.

Effect on timing and sign-off

Gate delay rises as supply voltage falls, so even a drop of about 5% (35 mV on a 0.7 V supply) can slow paths by several percent and break timing that passed at nominal voltage. For this reason sign-off before tape-out includes power-grid analysis of IR drop alongside electromigration checks, and the voltage maps may be fed back into timing analysis. Designers also keep voltage margin, which costs power, so a stronger grid lets that margin shrink.

How designers reduce it

The power grid is made wider and denser, with upper metal layers devoted largely to power and many parallel vias between layers. Packages assign a large fraction of balls or bumps to power and ground to spread current. Decoupling capacitor cells placed near busy logic supply charge during switching bursts, and placement can spread highly active cells apart. Power-gating switches add series resistance, so they are sized with IR drop in mind.

Try it: questions about IR drop

What is the phenomenon called in which current flowing through the resistance of power wiring lowers the supply voltage that reaches circuits?

  1. AIR drop
  2. BCrosstalk
  3. CDIBL
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. IR drop

Power wiring has resistance, so current I through it drops I × R, lowering the voltage reaching circuits—IR drop. Even a 5% drop (35 mV on a 0.7 V supply) can slow circuits by several percent, causing timing failures or malfunctions. Together with inductive drop (L·di/dt) from rapidly changing current, it is controlled with wide, meshed power grids and decoupling capacitance.

In manufacturing for backside power delivery, a wafer with transistors and wiring on its front is bonded to a carrier wafer, the original substrate is thinned from the back, and backside power wiring is then formed. What becomes especially difficult in this process?

  1. AThe number of front-side signal wiring layers increases, worsening routing congestion
  2. BBonding and thinning distort the wafer, making it hard to overlay backside patterns accurately on the front-side transistors
  3. CThe wiring path from the supply to the transistors becomes longer, increasing IR drop
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Bonding and thinning distort the wafer, making it hard to overlay backside patterns accurately on the front-side transistors

In backside power delivery, the wafer is flipped and bonded, thinned to between a few hundred nanometers and a few micrometers, and power is connected from the back right next to the transistors. Bonding distorts the wafer non-uniformly, so it becomes hard to align the backside exposure precisely to the front-side transistors and contacts. Simple errors such as overall expansion or rotation can be corrected by the exposure tool, but complex distortions that vary from place to place are hard to correct, and bonding methods and metrology are being improved. Moving power wiring to the back actually reduces front-side congestion and IR drop.

FAQ

What is an acceptable IR drop in chip design?

Budgets depend on the design and process, but drops of a few percent up to around 10% of the nominal supply are typical targets, with timing analyzed at the resulting lower voltage.

What is the difference between IR drop and electromigration?

IR drop is a voltage loss that slows circuits immediately, while electromigration is gradual metal damage from high current density that can open or short wires over the chip's lifetime. Both are checked on the power grid at sign-off.

What is ground bounce?

Ground bounce is the rise of the local ground voltage when current flows back through the resistance and inductance of the ground network, the counterpart of supply droop on VDD.
