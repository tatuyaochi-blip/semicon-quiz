What is RoHS Directive?

The EU regulation restricting the use of specific hazardous substances such as lead and mercury in electrical and electronic equipment.

Japanese name: RoHS指令

Try it: questions about RoHS Directive

Which composition is widely used as lead-free solder to meet environmental regulations?

  1. ASn-Pb eutectic
  2. BSn-Ag-Cu system
  3. CHg-Au system
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sn-Ag-Cu system

Because the EU RoHS directive and similar rules restricted lead, tin–silver–copper (SAC) solders became widespread. The common SAC305 alloy is Sn–3.0%Ag–0.5%Cu and melts at about 217–220 °C, higher than the 183 °C of eutectic Sn–Pb, so reflow temperatures also rose. Hg–Au is not a solder.

Which system shows chemical hazards in an internationally harmonized way, using pictograms such as a skull or flame and hazard statements?

  1. AISO 9001
  2. BRoHS Directive
  3. CGHS (Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. GHS (Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals)

GHS classifies chemical hazards by globally uniform rules and communicates them on labels and SDSs through nine pictograms, the signal words "Danger" and "Warning," and hazard statements. It harmonizes labeling that once differed by country, conveying the type of hazard even across language barriers. ISO 9001 is a quality management standard, and the RoHS directive restricts hazardous substances in electrical equipment.
