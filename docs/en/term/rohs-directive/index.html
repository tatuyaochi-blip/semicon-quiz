What is RoHS Directive?

The EU regulation restricting the use of specific hazardous substances such as lead and mercury in electrical and electronic equipment.

Japanese name: RoHS指令

Field: Environment, health and safety

What RoHS restricts

RoHS (Restriction of Hazardous Substances) applies to electrical and electronic equipment placed on the EU market and has applied since 2006. It originally restricted six substances: lead, mercury, cadmium, hexavalent chromium and two brominated flame retardants, PBB and PBDE. Four phthalate plasticizers were added later. The maximum concentration is 0.1 wt% for most substances and 0.01 wt% for cadmium. The 2011 recast, often called RoHS 2, tied compliance to CE marking and broadened the range of covered products.

The homogeneous-material rule

Limits apply to each homogeneous material, meaning a material that cannot be mechanically separated into different materials, not to the weight of the whole product. A lead's tin plating, a solder joint, the mold compound and the die-attach material are each judged separately. A tiny amount of lead concentrated in one solder joint can therefore violate the rule even if it is a negligible fraction of the device. Suppliers document compliance through material declarations and test reports, often using X-ray fluorescence screening.

What it changed in packaging

Banning lead pushed the industry from tin-lead solder to tin-silver-copper (SAC) alloys such as SAC305, which melt around 217 to 220 °C instead of 183 °C. Reflow peaks rose to about 260 °C, increasing package stress and moisture-induced cracking, so moisture sensitivity ratings and package materials had to be requalified. Pure tin lead finishes replaced tin-lead plating, which brought tin whisker growth back as a reliability concern to be managed.

Exemptions and related rules

Where no practical substitute exists, time-limited exemptions apply, for example high-lead solders used for die attach in some power packages and lead in certain glass and ceramic components. These exemptions are reviewed periodically. RoHS is often confused with REACH: REACH regulates chemicals themselves, requiring registration of substances made or imported at one tonne or more per year and disclosure of substances of very high concern above 0.1 wt% in articles, whereas RoHS bans specific substances in finished electrical equipment. Similar laws exist outside the EU.

Try it: questions about RoHS Directive

Which composition is widely used as lead-free solder to meet environmental regulations?

  1. ASn-Pb eutectic
  2. BSn-Ag-Cu system
  3. CHg-Au system
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Sn-Ag-Cu system

Because the EU RoHS directive and similar rules restricted lead, tin–silver–copper (SAC) solders became widespread. The common SAC305 alloy is Sn–3.0%Ag–0.5%Cu and melts at about 217–220 °C, higher than the 183 °C of eutectic Sn–Pb, so reflow temperatures also rose. Hg–Au is not a solder.

Which system shows chemical hazards in an internationally harmonized way, using pictograms such as a skull or flame and hazard statements?

  1. AISO 9001
  2. BRoHS Directive
  3. CGHS (Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. GHS (Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals)

GHS classifies chemical hazards by globally uniform rules and communicates them on labels and SDSs through nine pictograms, the signal words "Danger" and "Warning," and hazard statements. It harmonizes labeling that once differed by country, conveying the type of hazard even across language barriers. ISO 9001 is a quality management standard, and the RoHS directive restricts hazardous substances in electrical equipment.

FAQ

Which substances are restricted under RoHS?

Lead, mercury, cadmium, hexavalent chromium, PBB and PBDE flame retardants, and four phthalates. Most are limited to 0.1 wt% in any homogeneous material, and cadmium to 0.01 wt%.

Does RoHS ban all lead in electronics?

No. It limits lead to 0.1 wt% per homogeneous material, but listed exemptions still allow lead in some applications, such as certain high-lead die-attach solders and glass in components, where alternatives are not yet practical.

What is the difference between RoHS and REACH?

RoHS restricts a short list of hazardous substances inside electrical and electronic products. REACH is a broad EU chemicals regulation covering registration, evaluation, authorisation and restriction of substances across all industries.
