What is EDR (endpoint detection and response)?

A security product that continuously records activity on endpoints such as PCs and servers, including process launches, changes to files and settings and network connections, detects suspicious behavior and supports investigation and response (isolating the device, stopping processes and so on). Unlike traditional antivirus software, which recognizes known malware files, it assumes that intrusions cannot be prevented completely and aims to find activity after an intrusion.

What it records

An agent installed on the endpoint records process launches and their parent-child relationships, command-line arguments, changes to files and settings, network connections, logons and more, and sends them to a management server. This makes it possible to trace a sequence afterwards, such as "a script was launched from an email attachment and communicated with an external host".

Detection and response

Detection uses rules matching known attack techniques, behavioral analysis and matching against threat intelligence. After a detection, actions such as remotely isolating the device from the network, stopping a process or quarantining a file can be taken from the console, so SOC analysts can quickly contain the spread of damage.

XDR and MDR

Products that analyze not only endpoints but also email, network, cloud and identity data together are called XDR (extended detection and response). Services that outsource EDR monitoring and analysis to a specialist provider are called MDR (managed detection and response) and are used by organizations that cannot staff a 24-hour team themselves.

Answer: A. It continuously records behavior on endpoints, such as process launches and network connections, detects suspicious activity and supports responses such as isolating the device

Traditional antivirus software mainly compares files against the characteristics of known malware and aims to stop intrusion at the point of entry. EDR assumes that intrusion cannot be prevented completely and continuously records process launches, changes to files and settings, network connections and so on at the endpoint to detect suspicious behavior. Its features include going back through the records to investigate the intrusion path and the extent of the impact, remotely isolating the device from the network and stopping suspicious processes. Many products today provide antivirus functions and EDR in one.

FAQ

With EDR in place, is antivirus software unnecessary?

Their roles differ, so in many cases both are used. Antivirus software (EPP) stops known malware at the point of entry, and EDR finds and responds to attacks that get through. Many products today provide both functions in one.

Does installing EDR alone make us secure?

EDR is a tool that produces alerts and records, so people and procedures are needed to review them, decide and respond. Without a team that checks the alerts, response is delayed even when an attack is detected. If an in-house team is difficult, an external service such as MDR is used.
