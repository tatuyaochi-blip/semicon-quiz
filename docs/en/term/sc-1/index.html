What is SC-1 (APM)?

A mixture of ammonia, hydrogen peroxide and water used first in RCA cleaning. It removes particles and organics while lightly etching the surface.

Japanese name: SC-1(APM)

Field: CMP and cleaning

How SC-1 removes particles

SC-1, also called APM (ammonia-peroxide mixture), is NH4OH, H2O2 and water, classically about 1:1:5 at around 70 °C. Peroxide continuously oxidizes the silicon surface to a thin oxide about 1 nm thick, while ammonia slowly dissolves that oxide at roughly 0.1–1 nm per minute. This gentle simultaneous oxidation and etching undercuts particles and lifts them off. In the alkaline solution both the silicon surface and most particles carry negative zeta potential, so detached particles are repelled rather than redeposited. The oxidizing chemistry also removes light organic contamination.

SC-1 in the RCA clean

The RCA clean, published by Kern and colleagues in 1970, runs SC-1 first for particles and organics, then SC-2 (HCl, H2O2 and water) to dissolve metal contamination as chlorides, since metals tend to remain after the alkaline step. A dilute hydrofluoric acid (DHF) dip can be inserted to strip oxide, leaving a hydrogen-terminated, hydrophobic surface, and ultrapure-water rinses separate the steps. Heavier organics and resist residues are usually handled by sulfuric-peroxide mixture (SPM). Refined versions of this sequence remain the basis of front-end wafer cleaning.

Balancing peroxide, ammonia and roughness

The peroxide is what keeps SC-1 gentle. If its concentration is too low relative to ammonia, ammonia attacks bare silicon directly and roughens the surface, which can degrade a gate oxide grown later. Too aggressive a mix or too long a time removes more silicon or oxide than wanted. Ratios and temperature are therefore optimized, and modern fabs often use far more dilute SC-1 than the original recipe to limit material loss and roughening while still lifting particles.

SC-1 with megasonics

SC-1 is often combined with megasonic agitation at around 1 MHz. At tens of kilohertz, collapsing cavitation bubbles deliver shocks strong enough to break fine patterns; at megahertz frequencies, fast acoustic streaming and a thin boundary layer instead dislodge particles below 0.1 µm with far less damage. The chemistry loosens and electrostatically repels particles, and the acoustic energy carries them away, which is why the pairing is so effective. Batch tanks and single-wafer spray tools both use this approach.

Answer: B. Removing particles and organics

In the alkaline solution, particles and surface both charge negatively and repel, preventing redeposition, and organics are removed as well. Why are particles, once detached, unlikely to redeposit in alkaline cleaning solutions such as SC-1?

  2. BBecause the wafer surface and particles carry charges of the same sign and repel each other
  3. CBecause the particles dissolve in the solution and disappear

Answer: B. Because the wafer surface and particles carry charges of the same sign and repel each other

In alkaline solutions, silicon surfaces and most particles (silica, nitrides, etc.) both become negatively charged (negative zeta potential). Like charges repel, so particles lifted from the surface do not readily reattach. In acidic solutions zeta-potential signs vary among particle types, promoting redeposition. This is one reason SC-1 removes particles so effectively.

FAQ

What is SC-1 cleaning used for?

Removing particles and light organic contamination from silicon wafers. It is the first step of the RCA clean and is followed by SC-2 for metals.

What is the difference between SC-1 and SC-2?

SC-1 is alkaline (ammonia plus peroxide) and removes particles and organics; SC-2 is acidic (hydrochloric acid plus peroxide) and removes metal contamination.

Why does SC-1 contain hydrogen peroxide?

Peroxide keeps the silicon covered with about 1 nm of oxide that ammonia slowly etches, so the surface is removed gently and evenly. Without enough peroxide, ammonia etches silicon directly and roughens it.
