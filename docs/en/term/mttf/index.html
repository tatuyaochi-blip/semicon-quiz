What is MTTF (mean time to failure)?

The average time until a non-repairable product fails. It is used as an indicator of product lifetime.

Japanese name: MTTF(平均故障時間)

MTTF (mean time to failure) appears in 6 practice questions across 3 topics, including Reliability, Automotive Semiconductors and Functional Safety, Interconnect processes.

Field: Reliability

Definition and the exponential model

Mean time to failure (MTTF) is the expected time until a non-repairable item fails, mathematically the integral of the reliability function R(t). In the random-failure period the failure rate λ is roughly constant, time to failure follows an exponential distribution, R(t) = exp(−λt), and MTTF = 1/λ. A part with λ = 1×10⁻⁵ per hour therefore has an MTTF of 10⁵ hours, about 11 years. At t equal to MTTF only exp(−1), about 37%, of parts are still working, so the mean is far from a guaranteed life.

From FIT to MTTF

Semiconductor failure rates are usually quoted in FIT, one failure per 10⁹ device-hours. Converting, 100 FIT corresponds to an MTTF of 10⁷ hours, roughly 1,140 years for a single part. Such a number is meaningful only across populations: one million parts at 100 FIT running for 10,000 hours are expected to produce 10⁶ × 10⁴ × 100 / 10⁹ = 1,000 failures. When λt is small, λt itself is a good approximation of the probability that a given part fails within time t.

MTTF is not wear-out life

The failure rate over a product's life is often drawn as a bathtub curve: early infant-mortality failures, a long flat period of random failures, then rising wear-out failures. An MTTF computed from the flat-period rate says nothing about when wear-out begins and can exceed the actual useful life by orders of magnitude. For wear-out mechanisms, engineers instead fit distributions such as lognormal or Weibull to stress-test data and quote a median or percentile time to failure under stated conditions.

Black's equation for electromigration

Interconnect lifetime under electromigration is modelled by Black's equation, MTTF = A·J⁻ⁿ·exp(Ea/kT), where J is current density, n is about 2, Ea is an activation energy and T is absolute temperature. Higher current density and higher temperature both shorten life, the latter exponentially. Tests are therefore accelerated at elevated current and temperature, around 300 °C for example, and extrapolated to use conditions, and design rules cap current density per wire on the order of MA/cm². MTTF here describes component life, unlike the fault tolerant time interval in functional safety.

Answer: B. 1×10⁵ hours

With a constant failure rate, MTTF is the reciprocal of the failure rate: 1 ÷ (1×10⁻⁵) = 1×10⁵ hours, about 11 years. 1×10³ or 1×10⁷ hours result from exponent errors. However, this value derives from the random-failure rate and does not indicate when wear-out begins. MTTF can even exceed the actual wear-out life, so the two must not be confused.

FAQ

How do you calculate MTTF from a failure rate?

If the failure rate λ is constant, MTTF = 1/λ. For example, 1×10⁻⁵ failures per hour gives 10⁵ hours, and a rate given in FIT converts as MTTF = 10⁹ / FIT hours.

What percentage of parts have failed when they reach their MTTF?

Under a constant failure rate, about 63%, because reliability at t = MTTF is exp(−1) ≈ 37%. MTTF is an average, not a minimum life.

What is the difference between MTTF and MTBF?

MTTF applies to items that are discarded when they fail, while MTBF applies to repairable systems and measures time between successive failures. MTTR, mean time to repair, describes how long restoration takes.
