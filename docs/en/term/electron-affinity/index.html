What is Electron affinity?

The energy difference between the vacuum level and the bottom of the conduction band of a semiconductor. It is used to estimate band offsets at heterojunctions and barrier heights at metal contacts.

Japanese name: 電子親和力

Answer: A. Electron affinity

The electron affinity (χ) is the energy needed to remove an electron from the bottom of the conduction band into vacuum, about 4.05 eV for silicon. It is a material constant and does not change with doping. The work function, by contrast, is the energy from the Fermi level to the vacuum level and does depend on doping. In a heterojunction between different semiconductors, the difference in electron affinity sets the conduction-band offset (ΔEc) (Anderson's rule).
