What is SADP (self-aligned double patterning)?

Forming spacers on the sidewalls of a core pattern, removing the core and using the spacers as the mask to make a pitch half of what lithography alone allows. Doing it twice is SAQP.

Japanese name: SADP(自己整合ダブルパターニング)

Which technique, used to make the thin fins of FinFETs, uses sidewall spacers to create patterns finer than the lithography limit?

  1. ASADP/SAQP (self-aligned double/quadruple patterning)
  2. BOPC
  3. CCMP

Answer: A. SADP/SAQP (self-aligned double/quadruple patterning)

SADP coats a mandrel pattern with a conformal film (e.g., by ALD), forms sidewall spacers, then removes the mandrel, doubling line density. SAQP repeats this to quadruple it. Because line width is set by film thickness rather than exposure, variation is small, and fins and wires finer than ArF immersion's limit (about 80 nm pitch) become possible. Even with EUV, these techniques are combined for the tightest pitches.

  1. ATwo wafers are exposed at once
  2. BPatterns with a pitch too fine to resolve in one exposure are formed in multiple exposure and processing steps
  3. CCoating two layers of resist doubles the resolution
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Patterns with a pitch too fine to resolve in one exposure are formed in multiple exposure and processing steps

Double patterning forms pitches too fine for a single exposure either by splitting the layout into two interleaved patterns exposed and etched separately (LELE) or by doubling lines with sidewall spacers (SADP, SAQP). It pushed ArF immersion beyond its roughly 80 nm pitch limit into the 20 nm-plus range, but at a steep increase in steps, masks and cost, driving the adoption of EUV.
