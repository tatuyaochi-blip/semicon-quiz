What is GHS?

The internationally harmonized system that communicates chemical hazards with pictograms and precautionary statements.

Japanese name: GHS

GHS appears in 4 practice questions across 2 topics, including Environment, health and safety, Cleanrooms and equipment.

Field: Environment, health and safety

What GHS standardizes

GHS, the Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals, was developed under the United Nations to replace the patchwork of national labeling schemes. It defines common criteria for classifying physical hazards (such as flammability, oxidizing properties, gases under pressure and corrosion of metals), health hazards (such as acute toxicity, skin corrosion and carcinogenicity) and environmental hazards (such as aquatic toxicity), and it specifies how the result is communicated on labels and safety data sheets. GHS is not itself a law; countries and regions adopt it into their own chemical regulations.

Reading a GHS label

A label carries a product identifier, supplier details, one or more of nine pictograms, a signal word, hazard statements and precautionary statements. The pictograms are black symbols in red-bordered diamonds: flame, flame over circle (oxidizer), exploding bomb, gas cylinder, corrosion, skull and crossbones, exclamation mark, health hazard (a human silhouette) and environment. The signal word is Danger for the more severe categories and Warning for the less severe ones. Hazard statements describe the hazard in standard wording, while precautionary statements say how to store, handle and respond.

The safety data sheet

The safety data sheet (SDS) is the detailed companion to the label. Under GHS it has 16 sections in a fixed order, covering identification, hazards, composition, first aid, firefighting, accidental release, handling and storage, exposure controls and personal protection, physical and chemical properties, stability and reactivity, toxicology, ecology, disposal, transport, regulations and other information. The fixed layout means an engineer can find, for example, the required gloves or the first-aid measure for skin contact in the same place on any supplier's sheet.

GHS in a fab, and what it is not

Fab chemicals show the whole range of pictograms: hydrofluoric acid carries skull and corrosion symbols, concentrated hydrogen peroxide an oxidizer symbol, resist solvents a flame, and silane both flame and gas-cylinder symbols. GHS only classifies and communicates hazards; it does not restrict use. Restrictions on substances in products, such as lead in electronics under the EU RoHS directive, and reporting of chemical releases under pollutant release and transfer register (PRTR) schemes are separate regulations.

Answer: C. VC (Vibration Criteria) curves

VC curves set the allowable floor vibration for precision equipment as velocity limits per frequency band, in grades VC-A, VC-B and so on. VC-A is 50 µm/s, and each subsequent grade halves the limit, becoming stricter. Floors for scanners and electron microscopes require stringent grades, and the criteria guide building design and tool siting. ISO classes concern air, and GHS concerns chemical labeling.

  1. AISO 9001
  2. BRoHS Directive
  3. CGHS (Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals)

Answer: C. GHS (Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals)

GHS classifies chemical hazards by globally uniform rules and communicates them on labels and SDSs through nine pictograms, the signal words "Danger" and "Warning," and hazard statements. It harmonizes labeling that once differed by country, conveying the type of hazard even across language barriers. ISO 9001 is a quality management standard, and the RoHS directive restricts hazardous substances in electrical equipment.

FAQ

How many GHS pictograms are there?

There are nine GHS pictograms: flame, flame over circle, exploding bomb, gas cylinder, corrosion, skull and crossbones, exclamation mark, health hazard and environment. Some jurisdictions do not make the environment pictogram mandatory.

What is the difference between Danger and Warning on a GHS label?

Both are GHS signal words. Danger is used for the more severe hazard categories and Warning for the less severe ones, and a label shows only one of them.

What is the difference between GHS and RoHS?

GHS is a system for classifying chemical hazards and communicating them through labels and safety data sheets. RoHS is an EU directive that restricts hazardous substances such as lead and mercury in electrical and electronic equipment.
