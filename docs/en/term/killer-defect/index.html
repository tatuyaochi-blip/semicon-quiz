What is Killer defect?

A defect that makes a chip fail, such as particles or pattern anomalies of a size and location that cause shorts or opens in wiring.

Japanese name: キラー欠陥

Why are epitaxial layers important in the fabrication of SiC power devices?

  1. ATo form the voltage-blocking drift layer precisely, with low doping and the required thickness
  2. BTo make the wafer transparent
  3. CTo reduce wiring resistance
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. To form the voltage-blocking drift layer precisely, with low doping and the required thickness

Dopants barely diffuse thermally in SiC, so device layers are formed by epitaxy and ion implantation. For 1200 V devices the drift layer must be precisely controlled at about 10 µm and 10¹⁶ cm⁻³, directly setting breakdown voltage and on-resistance. Basal-plane dislocations and surface defects in the epi layer act as killer defects or cause bipolar degradation, so reducing them is key to yield and reliability.

Among defects on a wafer, what are those called that actually cause electrical failure of a chip?

  1. AKiller defects
  2. BFalse defects
  3. CNuisance defects
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Killer defects

Not every defect found by inspection causes a failure. Defects that do cause electrical failure, such as a large particle bridging adjacent wires, are called killer defects. Kill ratios estimated from defect size, location and layer are used to predict yield impact. Defects that cause no failure, and false detections by the inspection tool, are called nuisance defects.
