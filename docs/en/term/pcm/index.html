What is PCM (phase-change memory)?

Nonvolatile memory that stores information by the difference between the crystalline (low-resistance) and amorphous (high-resistance) states of a chalcogenide material.

Japanese name: PCM(相変化メモリ)

Field: Emerging memories

How set, reset and read work

A PCM cell is written with current pulses that Joule-heat a small volume of chalcogenide, typically GST (Ge2Sb2Te5). Reset uses a large, short pulse, tens to hundreds of nanoseconds, that melts the material above roughly 600 °C; quenching within nanoseconds freezes the disordered atoms into the high-resistance amorphous phase. Set uses a smaller, longer pulse that holds the material between its crystallization temperature (about 150 °C for GST) and the melting point until it crystallizes into the low-resistance phase. Reading applies a small bias that senses resistance without heating the cell enough to change it.

Multi-level cells and resistance drift

Because the crystalline and amorphous resistances differ by two to three orders of magnitude, PCM can store more than one bit per cell. Pulse amplitude and duration set the size of the amorphous region, producing intermediate resistance levels. The catch is resistance drift: the amorphous phase slowly relaxes, so its resistance rises roughly as a power of time (R ∝ t^ν, with ν around 0.1). Two-level storage tolerates this, but closely spaced levels can blur together, so multi-level PCM relies on write-and-verify programming, reference cells read alongside the data, and low-drift materials.

Scaling limits: reset current and thermal disturb

The reset current is PCM's main power cost, so cell designs shrink the volume that must melt. The mushroom cell, for example, forces current through a narrow heater electrode so only a small dome of GST is heated. Tight pitch brings a second problem, thermal disturb: when cells are only tens of nanometres apart, heat from a reset can partially crystallize an amorphous neighbour. Thermally insulating dielectrics around each cell help. Retention is also temperature-limited, since GST crystallizes near 150 °C, which is why Ge-rich compositions are used for automotive and other high-temperature applications.

PCM versus other nonvolatile memories

PCM sits between NAND flash and DRAM. Its endurance of about 10^6 to 10^9 cycles beats NAND's thousands to tens of thousands but falls short of MRAM (above 10^10) and DRAM, which is effectively unlimited. Unlike MRAM, which stores data in the magnetization of a magnetic tunnel junction, and ReRAM, which forms and breaks a conductive filament in an oxide, PCM changes the atomic structure of a whole volume. In dense cross-point arrays each PCM element is paired with an ovonic threshold switch (OTS) selector that blocks sneak currents through unselected cells.

Answer: B. Through differences in resistance between the crystalline and amorphous states of a chalcogenide material

PCM heats chalcogenide materials such as GeSbTe with Joule heating from current and quenches them, switching between a low-resistance crystalline state and a high-resistance amorphous state. The resistance ratio is large—two to three orders of magnitude—so intermediate levels can store multiple bits. The same materials, whose reflectivity also changes between phases, have been used in rewritable DVDs and Blu-ray discs. The large write current is a challenge.

  2. BBy heating it above the melting point with a large current pulse and then quenching it
  3. CBy holding it near the crystallization temperature for a long time

Answer: B. By heating it above the melting point with a large current pulse and then quenching it

Reset melts the material above its melting point (about 600 °C for GST) with a large current pulse of around tens of ns, then quenches it within nanoseconds so atoms have no time to order, leaving it amorphous. Set holds the material between its crystallization temperature and melting point long enough to crystallize. The large reset current is PCM's power challenge, reduced by structures that shrink the heated volume.

FAQ

What is the difference between PCM and ReRAM?

Both store data as a resistance state, but PCM switches a chalcogenide between crystalline and amorphous phases using heat, while ReRAM moves ions in a metal oxide to form or rupture a narrow conductive filament. PCM therefore needs relatively large write currents to melt the material.

Why is PCM set slower than reset?

Reset only needs to melt the material and quench it within nanoseconds, while set must hold the material below its melting point long enough for atoms to arrange into a crystal, typically tens to hundreds of nanoseconds. Crystallization time is therefore what limits PCM write speed.

What does GST stand for in phase-change memory?

GST refers to germanium-antimony-tellurium chalcogenide alloys, most commonly Ge2Sb2Te5. It crystallizes quickly yet keeps a stable amorphous phase at room temperature, and the same family of materials was used in rewritable optical discs.
