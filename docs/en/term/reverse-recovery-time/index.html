What is Reverse recovery time?

The time during which reverse current keeps flowing after a conducting diode is reverse-biased, until the stored minority carriers are removed. It causes switching loss and noise.

Japanese name: 逆回復時間

  1. ABecause the metal electrode resistance is large
  2. BBecause minority carriers stored during forward conduction do not disappear immediately

Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Because minority carriers stored during forward conduction do not disappear immediately

While a diode conducts forward, injected minority carriers accumulate on both sides of the junction. After switching to reverse, current keeps flowing backward until those carriers are swept out or recombine. This interval is the reverse recovery time (trr), and the reverse recovery current causes loss and noise. Remedies include fast-recovery diodes with lifetime control, and Schottky or SiC diodes that do not rely on minority carriers.
