What is FOUP?

A sealed container for transporting and storing 300 mm wafers.

Japanese name: FOUP

FOUP appears in 6 practice questions across 2 topics, including Cleanrooms and equipment, Silicon wafers.

Field: Cleanrooms and equipment

What a FOUP does

A FOUP (front opening unified pod) is a sealed plastic container holding up to 25 wafers of 300 mm diameter in slots, with a front door designed to be opened by equipment. The idea is a minienvironment: wafers are exposed only inside the FOUP and inside tools, so the extreme cleanliness is needed in those small volumes rather than across the whole fab. Dimensions, the door mechanism and mounting features are standardized, so a FOUP fits any vendor's tool. A related front-opening shipping box (FOSB) is used to ship wafers between companies.

Why transport is automated

A 300 mm wafer 775 µm thick weighs about 128 g (π × (15 cm)^2 × 0.0775 cm × 2.33 g/cm^3), against about 53 g for a 200 mm wafer. A loaded FOUP totals around 8 kg, too heavy and too valuable to carry by hand all day without drop and particle risk. Fabs therefore use an automated material handling system (AMHS) whose core is overhead hoist transport (OHT): vehicles running on ceiling rails lift FOUPs and lower them onto tool load ports, freeing floor space. Large fabs run hundreds of vehicles or more.

Docking at the tool

At each tool, the FOUP sits on a load port within the EFEM (equipment front end module). The load port unlatches and removes the door, and a robot moves wafers between FOUP and process chamber, all inside a space kept clean by a fan filter unit (FFU). Wafers therefore never meet room air between steps. When processing ends, wafers return to the FOUP, the door closes, and the OHT carries the pod to the next tool or to a stocker.

Nitrogen purge and the SMIF predecessor

Air sealed in a FOUP still contains oxygen and moisture, which can oxidize wafer surfaces and deposit molecular contamination while lots wait between steps. Purging FOUPs with nitrogen lowers both and also prevents gases outgassing from freshly etched wafers from building up and contaminating the others, a concern that grows in advanced processes. In the 200 mm era the SMIF (standard mechanical interface) pod served the same minienvironment purpose: a sealed pod around a separate cassette that opened downward into the tool. The FOUP merged cassette and pod into one front-opening box.

Answer: C. EFEM (equipment front-end module, including load ports and transfer robots)

An EFEM (equipment front end module) houses the load ports for FOUPs, the door-opening mechanism and the robot that transfers wafers into the tool, all in a space kept clean by an FFU. Wafers move between FOUP and tool without exposure to room air, making it the heart of the minienvironment. OHTs move FOUPs between tools, and ULPA filters clean the air.

FAQ

What does FOUP stand for?

Front opening unified pod: a sealed, front-opening container that carries and stores up to 25 wafers of 300 mm diameter between tools.

How heavy is a FOUP full of wafers?

About 8 kg with 25 wafers of 300 mm, each wafer being roughly 128 g, which is why fabs move FOUPs with overhead hoist transport.

What is the difference between a FOUP and a SMIF pod?

A SMIF pod, used mainly with 200 mm wafers, encloses a separate cassette and opens from the bottom. A FOUP integrates the cassette into the pod and opens from the front.
