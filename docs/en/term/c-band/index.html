What is C-band?

The optical fiber communication band around 1530 to 1565 nm. Silica fiber loss is lowest there (about 0.2 dB/km) and erbium-doped fiber amplifiers (EDFAs) can amplify it, so it is used for long-haul links and dense wavelength division multiplexing (DWDM).

Japanese name: Cバンド

Field: Silicon photonics

Why this wavelength range

Loss in silica fiber comes mainly from Rayleigh scattering, which falls steeply as wavelength increases (roughly as the inverse fourth power), and from infrared absorption by the glass, which rises at longer wavelengths. The minimum, about 0.2 dB/km in standard single-mode fiber, lies near 1550 nm, so 100 km of fiber costs only about 20 dB. Just as important, erbium-doped fiber amplifiers (EDFAs), pumped by lasers at 980 or 1480 nm, provide gain across roughly 1530 to 1565 nm, amplifying all channels at once in the optical domain without converting them to electrical signals.

C-band vs. the other telecom bands

The ITU (International Telecommunication Union) divides the low-loss region of fiber into bands: O (original, about 1260 to 1360 nm), E, S, C (conventional, 1530 to 1565 nm), L (long, about 1565 to 1625 nm) and U. In standard single-mode fiber, chromatic dispersion is close to zero around 1310 nm, in the O-band, which is why short-reach links such as those inside data centers often use it. The C-band has lower loss and EDFA gain but significant dispersion. The L-band extends capacity using amplifiers adapted to longer wavelengths.

Dense wavelength division multiplexing

Dense wavelength division multiplexing (DWDM) places many lasers on a frequency grid defined by ITU-T, anchored at 193.1 THz, with spacings such as 100 GHz (about 0.8 nm) or 50 GHz (about 0.4 nm). The C-band spans roughly 4.4 THz, so it holds about 40 channels at 100 GHz or about 80 at 50 GHz. Because the spacing is so narrow, laser wavelengths must be held stable, typically by temperature control, and filters, multiplexers and ring resonators must be tuned to the grid.

Dispersion and silicon photonics in the C-band

Standard single-mode fiber has a chromatic dispersion of around 17 ps/(nm·km) at 1550 nm, which broadens pulses over long distances. Older systems compensated it with special fiber; modern coherent receivers undo it with digital signal processing. For integrated photonics, the C-band photon energy of about 0.8 eV is below silicon's 1.12 eV band gap, so silicon waveguides are transparent, while germanium grown on silicon serves as the photodetector. Silicon's strong thermo-optic effect, however, shifts ring resonances by roughly 0.07 to 0.08 nm per °C.

In long-haul optical fiber communication, what is the main reason wavelengths around 1.55 µm (C-band) are widely used?

  1. AIt is the band where loss in silica optical fiber is lowest
  2. BIt is the band where silicon absorbs light most strongly, making photodetectors easy to build
  3. CIt is the band where chromatic dispersion is zero, so pulses do not spread at all
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It is the band where loss in silica optical fiber is lowest

Loss in silica (SiO2) fiber increases from Rayleigh scattering at short wavelengths and from infrared absorption at long wavelengths, so it is lowest in between, around 1.55 µm (about 0.2 dB/km). Even after 100 km the loss is only about 20 dB, and the ability to amplify the light directly in the optical domain with erbium-doped fiber amplifiers (EDFAs) is another reason the C-band is used for long-haul communication. In standard single-mode fiber, chromatic dispersion is nearly zero around 1.31 µm (O-band); dispersion remains at 1.55 µm. Silicon barely absorbs this wavelength.

For WDM in the C-band (around 1.55 µm), a frequency spacing of 100 GHz corresponds to roughly what wavelength spacing? (Δλ ≈ λ² × Δf / c, c = 3×10⁸ m/s)

  1. AAbout 8 nm
  2. BAbout 0.08 nm
  3. CAbout 0.8 nm

Answer: C. About 0.8 nm

Δλ ≈ (1.55×10⁻⁶ m)² × 100×10⁹ Hz ÷ (3×10⁸ m/s) ≈ 0.8×10⁻⁹ m, or about 0.8 nm. In DWDM (dense wavelength division multiplexing), used for long-haul, high-capacity communication, dozens of wavelengths are placed on the frequency grid defined by ITU-T at spacings such as 100 GHz (about 0.8 nm) or 50 GHz (about 0.4 nm). Because the spacing is narrow, laser wavelengths must be precisely stabilized by temperature control or similar means. Since optical frequency and wavelength are inversely proportional, when the spacing is set in frequency, the wavelength spacing becomes slightly narrower at shorter wavelengths.

FAQ

What wavelength range is the C-band?

The C-band, or conventional band, covers about 1530 to 1565 nm, corresponding to roughly 191.6 to 195.9 THz in frequency.

Why is 1550 nm used for long-distance fiber communication?

Silica fiber has its lowest loss there, around 0.2 dB/km, and erbium-doped fiber amplifiers can boost signals across the band without electrical conversion.

What is the difference between the O-band and the C-band?

The O-band around 1310 nm has near-zero chromatic dispersion in standard fiber and suits short links, while the C-band around 1550 nm has lower loss and optical amplification, suiting long-haul and DWDM systems.
