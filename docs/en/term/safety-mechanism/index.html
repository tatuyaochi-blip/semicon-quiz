What is Safety mechanism?

A means of detecting faults and moving to a safe state, or containing their effects, before they lead to a hazardous event. Examples include ECC, lockstep CPUs, watchdog timers, voltage and clock monitors, and BIST.

Japanese name: 安全機構

Field: Automotive Semiconductors and Functional Safety

What a safety mechanism must do

In functional safety standards such as ISO 26262 for road vehicles, a safety mechanism is a technical measure that detects a fault, or controls its effect, and brings the system to or keeps it in a safe state. Timing is part of the requirement. The fault tolerant time interval (FTTI) is the minimum time from a fault to a possible hazardous event, and fault detection plus reaction, such as shutting off outputs, resetting or switching to a degraded mode, must fit inside it. A diagnosis that runs too rarely fails this test even if it eventually catches the fault.

Common hardware mechanisms

Error-correcting codes (ECC) on memories correct single-bit errors and detect double-bit errors. Lockstep CPUs run two identical cores on the same instructions and compare outputs every cycle, often with a small time offset so a single disturbance does not hit both identically. Watchdog timers catch software that stops servicing them, and windowed watchdogs also catch servicing that comes too early. Clock monitors and voltage monitors check for stoppage, drift, undervoltage and overvoltage. Built-in self-test (BIST) checks logic and memories at start-up or periodically, and CRCs protect communication.

Diagnostic coverage and metrics

Diagnostic coverage (DC) is the fraction of faults a mechanism detects or controls. Failure rates are expressed in FIT, failures per 10⁹ device-hours, and a block with 200 FIT protected at 99% DC leaves 200 × (1 − 0.99) = 2 FIT undetected. ISO 26262 aggregates such numbers into the single-point fault metric (SPFM) and latent fault metric (LFM). For the highest integrity level, ASIL D, the targets are at least 99% for SPFM and 90% for LFM, and a residual hardware failure rate budget also applies.

Independence and latent faults

A mechanism must not fail together with what it monitors. A clock monitor needs a reference oscillator independent of the main clock, and a voltage monitor needs its own reference, otherwise a common fault moves both and goes unseen. The mechanism itself can also break silently: an ECC checker that has failed shows no symptom until a memory error arrives, which makes it a latent fault. Such mechanisms are therefore tested themselves, for example by injecting deliberate errors or running comparator self-tests at start-up.

A safety mechanism with 99% diagnostic coverage is added to a circuit with a failure rate of 200 FIT. What is the failure rate of the faults that remain undetected by the safety mechanism?

  1. A198 FIT
  2. B0.02 FIT
  3. C2 FIT
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 2 FIT

Diagnostic coverage (DC) is the proportion of faults that a safety mechanism can detect or control. The remaining failure rate is 200 × (1 − 0.99) = 200 × 0.01 = 2 FIT. 198 FIT is the failure rate of the faults the safety mechanism can detect, and 0.02 FIT is the result of misplacing the decimal point in the multiplication. Fault coverage in manufacturing test represents the proportion of defective parts found before shipment, whereas diagnostic coverage represents the proportion of faults occurring in use that are detected on the spot.

Which correctly describes how a lockstep CPU, used as a safety mechanism in automotive microcontrollers, works?

  1. ATwo CPU cores share different tasks between them, roughly doubling processing speed
  2. BTwo CPU cores execute the same instructions simultaneously, and their outputs are compared every clock cycle; a mismatch is reported as a fault
  3. CWhen a fault is detected, CPU operation is temporarily stopped (locked), switched to a spare circuit, and the faulty part is automatically repaired before resuming
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Two CPU cores execute the same instructions simultaneously, and their outputs are compared every clock cycle; a mismatch is reported as a fault

Lockstep is a scheme in which two identically configured CPU cores run the same program and a comparator checks their outputs against each other. If one result changes due to a bit flip from cosmic rays or a circuit fault, it is detected as a mismatch, giving high diagnostic coverage for CPU faults. Because the two cores do the same work, performance equals that of one core. Comparing two cores alone cannot tell which one is correct, so after detection the system performs a reset or a transition to a safe state.

FAQ

What is a safety mechanism in ISO 26262?

It is a measure implemented in hardware or software that detects faults or controls their effects and achieves or maintains a safe state. Examples include ECC, lockstep cores, watchdogs and voltage or clock monitors.

How is the residual failure rate calculated from diagnostic coverage?

Multiply the failure rate by one minus the coverage. For example, 200 FIT with 99% diagnostic coverage leaves 2 FIT of undetected faults.

Why can a lockstep CPU detect but not correct a fault?

With only two cores, a mismatch shows that one is wrong but not which one, so the system must reset or move to a safe state. Correcting by majority vote requires at least three redundant channels.
