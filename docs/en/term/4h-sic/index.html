What is 4H-SiC?

The SiC polytype mainly used for power devices. It has a wide band gap of about 3.3 eV and relatively high electron mobility.

Japanese name: 4H-SiC

4H-SiC appears in 2 practice questions across 1 topic, including Epitaxial growth.

Field: Epitaxial growth

What the name means

Silicon carbide is built from Si–C bilayers that can stack in many different sequences, giving more than 200 polytypes with identical chemistry but different crystal structures and properties. In Ramsdell notation the number is the count of bilayers in one repeat period and the letter gives the lattice symmetry. 4H-SiC repeats every four bilayers in a hexagonal lattice, 6H-SiC every six, and 3C-SiC every three in a cubic arrangement. The stacking sequence changes the band gap and electron mobility, so the polytype matters as much as the material.

Why power devices use 4H

4H-SiC combines a band gap of about 3.26 eV, an electron mobility of roughly 1000 cm²/V·s and only modest anisotropy. Its wide gap gives a critical electric field around ten times that of silicon, so a drift layer for a given blocking voltage can be about a tenth as thick and much more heavily doped, sharply reducing on-resistance. 6H-SiC has strongly direction-dependent mobility and 3C-SiC a smaller gap near 2.4 eV. Thermal conductivity several times higher than silicon's and mature substrate and epitaxy supply complete the case.

Step-flow epitaxy on off-axis substrates

On an exactly on-axis surface, new islands nucleate on flat terraces and can adopt the wrong stacking, often 3C-SiC. Substrates are therefore cut about 4° off the c-axis toward a ⟨11-20⟩ direction, which creates a dense staircase of atomic steps. Adatoms diffuse to step edges and attach there, so each step advances sideways and copies the substrate's stacking sequence. This step-flow growth, typically by CVD with silane and a hydrocarbon in hydrogen at temperatures above 1500 °C, reproduces the 4H polytype in the drift layer.

Known challenges

Crystal defects remain a concern. Basal-plane dislocations in the drift layer can expand into stacking faults under forward bipolar current, raising on-voltage over time, so epitaxial processes aim to convert them into less harmful threading edge dislocations. In MOSFETs, traps at the SiO2/SiC interface make the inversion-channel mobility far lower than bulk mobility, which is an active area of gate-oxide process development. Compared with GaN, which is mostly used in lateral transistors, 4H-SiC is favored for vertical devices blocking several hundred volts to kilovolts.

Answer: A. To grow laterally from atomic steps on the surface (step-flow growth) and inherit the same polytype as the substrate

SiC has many polytypes with different stacking sequences, and growth on a flat (on-axis) surface tends to nucleate unwanted ones such as 3C-SiC. Substrates cut about 4° off-axis present many atomic steps; adatoms incorporate at the steps and growth proceeds laterally (step-flow growth), so the crystal inherits the substrate's 4H-SiC stacking sequence.

Which polytype is mainly used for SiC power devices?

  1. A4H-SiC
  2. B3C-SiC
  3. C15R-SiC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 4H-SiC

SiC has over 200 polytypes, but power devices mainly use 4H-SiC because of its wide band gap (about 3.26 eV), high electron mobility (about 1000 cm²/V·s) and small anisotropy. 6H-SiC has strongly anisotropic mobility, and 3C-SiC a smaller gap of about 2.4 eV. Mature high-quality 4H substrates and epitaxy technology are another reason.

FAQ

What is the band gap of 4H-SiC?

About 3.26 eV at room temperature, roughly three times that of silicon (1.12 eV). This is often rounded to 3.2 or 3.3 eV.

Why is 4H-SiC preferred over 6H-SiC for power devices?

4H-SiC has a slightly wider band gap, higher electron mobility and much less mobility anisotropy than 6H-SiC, and high-quality 4H substrates and epitaxy are mature.

Why are SiC wafers cut off-axis for epitaxy?

An off-angle of about 4° creates many atomic steps so growth proceeds by step flow, which copies the substrate's 4H stacking instead of nucleating other polytypes such as 3C-SiC.
