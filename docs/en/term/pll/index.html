What is PLL (phase-locked loop)?

A circuit that detects the phase difference from a reference clock and controls an oscillator to produce a clock locked to the reference, for example at an integer multiple of its frequency. It is used to generate CPU clocks and synthesize radio frequencies.

Japanese name: PLL(位相同期回路)

A T flip-flop whose output toggles on every clock edge produces an output at what multiple of the clock frequency?

  1. A2 times
  2. B1/2
  3. C1/4
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1/2

A T flip-flop (T = 1) toggles on every clock edge, so its output completes one cycle every two clocks—half the clock frequency—with an exactly 50% duty cycle. Chaining them gives ÷4, ÷8 and so on, forming dividers and ripple counters. PLL feedback loops also use dividers, synthesizing multi-GHz frequencies from, say, a 100 MHz reference by changing the division ratio.

Which circuit generates a clock at a frequency such as an integer multiple of a reference clock while locking its phase to the reference?

  1. APLL (phase-locked loop)
  2. BRectifier
  3. CSample-and-hold circuit
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. PLL (phase-locked loop)

A PLL consists of a phase detector, loop filter, voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO) and divider, steering the VCO so that its divided output stays in phase with the reference clock. A divide ratio of N yields N times the reference frequency, so a 25 MHz crystal can produce a multi-GHz CPU clock. Fractional-N division synthesizes fine frequency steps, and PLLs also serve as radio local oscillators and for clock recovery in high-speed serial links.
