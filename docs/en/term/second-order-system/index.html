What is Second-order system?

A system whose transfer function is G(s) = Kωn²/(s² + 2ζωn s + ωn²), where ωn is the natural angular frequency and ζ the damping ratio. For 0 < ζ < 1 the step response approaches the final value while oscillating, for ζ = 1 it settles fastest without overshoot and for ζ > 1 it approaches slowly. Mechanical spring–mass–damper systems, RLC circuits and position control loops take this form. Higher-order systems are also often approximated by their two dominant poles.

Japanese name: 二次遅れ系

Damping ratio and the shape of the response

The overshoot is exp(−πζ/√(1 − ζ²)) and depends on ζ alone: about 16% at ζ = 0.5 and about 4.3% at ζ ≈ 0.707 (1/√2). The overshoot peaks at π/ωd, where ωd = ωn√(1 − ζ²) is the angular frequency of the decaying oscillation. The smaller ζ is, the faster the response rises, but the larger the overshoot and oscillation.

Relation to pole positions

For 0 < ζ < 1 the poles are at s = −ζωn ± jωd, at a distance ωn from the origin, and the angle θ they make with the negative real axis satisfies cos θ = ζ. The real part ζωn sets the rate of decay, and the ±2% settling time is estimated at about 4/(ζωn). This formula is an approximation derived from the envelope of the oscillation, and the true value is often a little shorter.

Seen in the frequency response

On a Bode plot, with ωn as the corner, the gain falls at −40 dB/dec at high frequencies and the phase changes from 0° to −180°. When ζ is below about 0.707, a resonant peak appears in the gain near ωn. In a feedback system, the smaller the phase margin, the smaller the closed-loop ζ; as a rule of thumb, ζ is about one hundredth of the phase margin in degrees.

In the series RLC circuit in the figure, L = 1 mH, C = 0.1 µF and R = 50 Ω. Which pair correctly gives the damping ratio ζ and the type of step response?

  1. Aζ = 0.25, damped oscillation
  2. Bζ = 0.50, damped oscillation
  3. Cζ = 4.0, overdamped
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. ζ = 0.25, damped oscillation

Method 1 (compare with the standard form): the characteristic equation is s² + (R/L)s + 1/(LC) = 0, and comparing it with the standard form s² + 2ζωn s + ωn² gives ωn = 1/√(LC) = 1×10⁵ rad/s and 2ζωn = R/L. From these, ζ = (R/2)√(C/L) = 25 × √(0.1×10⁻⁶/1×10⁻³) = 25 × 0.01 = 0.25. Method 2 (ratio to the critical resistance): the resistance for critical damping is 2√(L/C) = 200 Ω, so ζ = R/(2√(L/C)) = 50/200 = 0.25 as well. Since ζ < 1, the step response overshoots the final value and settles while oscillating: a damped oscillation.

Which of the following is the range of the damping ratio ζ for which the step response of the second-order system G(s) = ωn²/(s² + 2ζωn s + ωn²) overshoots the final value and approaches it while oscillating?

  1. Aζ > 1
  2. Bζ = 1
  3. C0 < ζ < 1
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 0 < ζ < 1

For 0 < ζ < 1 the poles are a complex-conjugate pair and the response approaches the final value while oscillating (underdamped). For ζ = 1 the poles are a repeated negative real root, and the response settles fastest among responses without overshoot (critically damped). For ζ > 1 the poles are two negative real numbers, and the response approaches slowly without overshoot (overdamped). For ζ = 0 the oscillation continues without decaying, and for ζ < 0 the system is unstable.

FAQ

What damping ratio should a design aim for?

For a fast response with a little overshoot, ζ = 0.5 to 0.8 is often chosen. ζ ≈ 0.707 gives an overshoot of about 4.3% and is also the boundary below which a resonant peak appears in the frequency response. Where overshoot is not allowed, the design aims for ζ ≥ 1.

Can the second-order formulas be used for third- and higher-order systems?

If the two poles closest to the imaginary axis (the dominant poles) are much slower than the others, the response can be estimated by approximating with those two poles. When other poles or zeros are close by, the error grows, so checking with a numerical calculation is the safe approach.
