What is Damascene process?

A method of forming interconnects by etching trenches in an insulating film, filling them with metal, and removing the excess metal by CMP. Used for copper wiring.

Japanese name: ダマシン法

Damascene process appears in 2 practice questions across 2 topics, including Interconnect processes, Deposition (CVD and PVD).

Field: Interconnect processes

Why copper needs a damascene flow

Aluminum interconnects were patterned subtractively: deposit a metal film, then dry-etch it through a resist mask. Copper does not form volatile halides at practical etch temperatures, so it cannot be plasma-etched the same way. The damascene process reverses the order. The pattern is etched into the insulating film first, copper fills the openings, and chemical mechanical polishing (CMP) removes the excess, leaving metal only where the trenches were. The name comes from the inlay craft associated with Damascus, where gold or silver was set into grooves cut in metal.

Step-by-step flow

A typical copper damascene sequence is: deposit the interlayer dielectric, often a low-k film; pattern and etch trenches, and for dual damascene also vias; sputter a barrier such as Ta/TaN to stop copper diffusing into the dielectric, followed by a copper seed layer a few to tens of nm thick; electroplate copper to fill the features; anneal to grow the copper grains; then polish away overburden copper and barrier by CMP. A dielectric cap is deposited on top to seal the copper before the next level is built.

Single vs. dual damascene

In single damascene, vias and lines are formed in separate cycles, each with its own fill and CMP. Dual damascene etches both the via holes and the line trenches into the dielectric and fills them in one plating and one CMP step, saving process steps and removing an interface between via and line. Bottom-up fill during electroplating is essential for both: the plating bath contains a suppressor, an accelerator and a leveler that make copper grow faster at the feature bottom so no voids or seams remain.

Practical challenges

CMP removes copper and dielectric at rates that depend on pattern density, so wide lines can dish and dense arrays can erode, thinning the wiring. Layout rules require minimum and maximum metal density, and dummy fill shapes are added to even it out. As line widths shrink, the barrier, which conducts poorly, occupies a larger share of the cross-section, and electron scattering at surfaces and grain boundaries raises copper resistivity, so narrow lines get disproportionately more resistive. Electromigration reliability also depends strongly on the quality of the cap interface.

FAQ

Why is copper wiring made with the damascene process instead of etching?

Copper does not form volatile etch products at practical temperatures, so it cannot be patterned by plasma etching like aluminum. Etching the dielectric, filling with copper and polishing off the excess avoids etching copper at all.

What is the difference between single and dual damascene?

Single damascene forms vias and lines in separate fill and CMP cycles, while dual damascene etches both into the dielectric and fills them with copper in a single plating and CMP step.

What is the barrier layer for in copper damascene?

A thin barrier such as Ta/TaN keeps copper from diffusing into the dielectric and silicon, where it would cause leakage and device failure, and it also helps adhesion.
