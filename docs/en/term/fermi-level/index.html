What is Fermi level?

The energy at which the probability of an electron occupying a state is exactly one half. It lies closer to the conduction band in n-type material and closer to the valence band in p-type.

Japanese name: フェルミ準位

Where is the Fermi level located in an n-type semiconductor?

  1. ACloser to the valence band than the intrinsic Fermi level
  2. BCloser to the conduction band than the intrinsic Fermi level
  3. CAlways inside the valence band
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Closer to the conduction band than the intrinsic Fermi level

In n-type material the conduction band holds many electrons, so the Fermi level moves from the intrinsic level near midgap toward the conduction band. Its position is given by Ec − EF ≈ kT·ln(Nc/ND), so it moves about 60 mV (at room temperature) closer to the conduction band for every tenfold increase in donor concentration. In p-type it moves toward the valence band. At very high doping it can even enter a band (a degenerate semiconductor).

What does the Fermi level mean?

  1. AThe energy at the top of the valence band
  2. BThe energy at the bottom of the conduction band
  3. CThe energy at which the probability of occupation by an electron is exactly 1/2
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The energy at which the probability of occupation by an electron is exactly 1/2

The Fermi level is the energy at which the Fermi-Dirac occupation probability is exactly 1/2, a kind of "water level" for electrons. It lies near midgap in an intrinsic semiconductor, toward the conduction band in n-type, and toward the valence band in p-type. In thermal equilibrium the Fermi levels of joined materials line up, which gives rise to the built-in potential of a PN junction and the flat-band voltage of a MOS structure. Applying a voltage V shifts the two sides' Fermi levels apart by qV.
