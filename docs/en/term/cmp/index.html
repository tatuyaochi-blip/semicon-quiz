What is CMP?

Chemical mechanical polishing. Planarizes the wafer surface by combining the chemical action of a slurry with mechanical polishing by a pad.

Japanese name: CMP

CMP appears in 55 practice questions across 13 topics, including CMP and cleaning, Interconnect processes, Silicon wafers.

Field: CMP and cleaning

How chemical mechanical polishing removes material

In chemical mechanical polishing (CMP), a head holds the wafer face-down against a porous polyurethane pad on a rotating platen roughly 70 to 80 cm across while slurry flows between them. The slurry chemistry converts the film surface into a softer layer, such as an oxidized metal or hydrated oxide, and abrasive particles tens to over a hundred nanometers in size, together with the pad, wipe it away. Removal follows Preston's equation: rate = k × pressure × relative velocity. Raised features carry more local pressure and polish faster, which is why the surface flattens.

Where CMP is used in a chip flow

Advanced logic uses dozens of CMP steps. Shallow trench isolation (STI) oxide is polished with ceria slurry down to a silicon nitride stop, with oxide-to-nitride selectivity of tens to over 100. Tungsten plugs need an oxidizer such as hydrogen peroxide to soften the metal. Copper wiring cannot be dry-etched because copper halides are not volatile, so it is made by damascene: copper fills trenches and CMP removes the excess in stages, bulk copper first, then the Ta/TaN barrier. Global flatness also keeps each level within a lithographic depth of focus of about 100 nm.

Dishing, erosion and pattern density

CMP results depend on layout. Dishing is the dish-shaped recess that forms in wide metal lines and pads, where the soft metal at the center is polished below the surrounding dielectric; it deepens with width and raises line resistance. Erosion is the thinning of both metal and dielectric across dense arrays of narrow lines. Because dense regions polish more slowly than sparse ones, designers cap line widths, slot wide lines and insert electrically inactive dummy fill so that pattern density, and therefore post-CMP thickness, stays uniform.

Keeping the process under control

Endpoint detection watches platen motor torque or the optical reflectance seen through a window in the platen, which change when the underlying film appears; eddy-current sensors track metal thickness. A diamond conditioner continuously dresses the pad to stop glazing, and multi-zone heads with about 5 to 10 pressure zones plus a retaining ring tune removal from center to edge. Scratches come mostly from agglomerated slurry particles, so slurry is filtered. Afterwards wafers move, still wet, to brush scrubbing and chemical cleaning so dried particles cannot bond to the surface.

Answer: B. Colloidal silica (fine silica particles)

Final wafer polishing uses colloidal silica—silica particles tens of nm across dispersed in an alkaline solution. The alkali chemically converts the silicon surface into a softer layer that the particles mechanically remove, and this combined chemical-mechanical action yields a damage-free mirror finish with roughness around 0.1 nm. The soft particles avoid deep scratches and metal contamination.

Answer: A. It planarizes the surface by combining the chemical action of a slurry with mechanical polishing by a pad

CMP presses a rotating wafer against a polishing pad while feeding slurry containing abrasive particles and chemicals. Chemistry converts the surface into a softer layer that particles and pad remove mechanically, achieving rates and flatness neither chemical nor mechanical action could alone. Developed at IBM in the 1980s, it keeps topography across the entire wafer to tens of nm or less and is indispensable for multilevel and copper interconnects.

FAQ

What does CMP stand for in semiconductor manufacturing?

CMP stands for chemical mechanical polishing, also called chemical mechanical planarization. It flattens the wafer surface using a slurry that chemically softens the film and a pad and abrasive particles that mechanically remove it.

What is the difference between dishing and erosion in CMP?

Dishing is the recess that forms at the center of a single wide metal line or pad because the metal polishes faster than the dielectric around it. Erosion is the overall loss of both metal and dielectric thickness across a dense array of fine lines.

Why is copper wiring made with CMP instead of etching?

Copper does not form volatile compounds with the halogen gases used in plasma etching, so it cannot be patterned cleanly by dry etching. Instead, trenches are etched in the dielectric, filled with copper, and CMP removes the copper outside the trenches.
