What is ReRAM (resistive RAM)?

Nonvolatile memory that applies voltage to form and break conductive paths in an oxide or similar material, storing information as differences in resistance.

Japanese name: ReRAM(抵抗変化メモリ)

Which correctly describes how ReRAM (resistive memory) stores data?

  1. AThrough differences in the magnetization direction of magnetic layers
  2. BBy applying voltage to a metal oxide or similar material to switch between high- and low-resistance states
  3. CThrough the presence or absence of charge stored in a capacitor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. By applying voltage to a metal oxide or similar material to switch between high- and low-resistance states

ReRAM is a simple stack of a thin film, such as a metal oxide, between electrodes. Applying voltage forms or ruptures a conductive filament of oxygen vacancies, changing resistance by tenfold or more. The small cells can be built in the interconnect layers, suiting embedding in logic and dense crosspoint arrays. It is in production as embedded memory in microcontrollers; large resistance variability is its main challenge.

In ReRAM, what is the initialization step called in which a relatively high voltage is first applied to form a conductive filament?

  1. ADoping
  2. BForming
  3. CAnnealing
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Forming

Most ReRAM cells start out highly resistive and need an initial voltage higher than normal operation to create a conductive path of oxygen vacancies or similar—forming. Afterward, only the thin tip of the filament is ruptured and reconnected, so lower voltages switch between high and low resistance. High forming voltages burden peripheral circuits, so forming-free materials and structures are being researched.
