What is ReRAM (resistive RAM)?

Nonvolatile memory that applies voltage to form and break conductive paths in an oxide or similar material, storing information as differences in resistance.

Japanese name: ReRAM(抵抗変化メモリ)

ReRAM (resistive RAM) appears in 9 practice questions across 2 topics, including Emerging memories, SRAM and flash memory.

Field: Emerging memories

How a filament switches

A resistive RAM (ReRAM) cell is a metal-insulator-metal stack, typically a few nanometers of hafnium, tantalum or titanium oxide between electrodes. Fresh cells are highly resistive, so a one-time forming step applies a higher voltage that moves oxygen ions and leaves a conductive filament of oxygen vacancies. After that, normal operation only ruptures and reconnects the thin tip of the filament: reset produces the high-resistance state and set restores the low-resistance state, typically differing by ten times or more. The state persists without power, so the memory is nonvolatile.

Bipolar, unipolar and CBRAM

Most practical ReRAM is bipolar: set and reset use opposite voltage polarities, with the field shuttling oxygen ions to connect or break the filament. Unipolar devices use one polarity and break the filament mainly by Joule heating, which is harder to control. Conductive-bridge RAM (CBRAM) is a related type in which silver or copper ions from an active electrode migrate through a solid electrolyte to build a metallic bridge, offering low voltage and low write energy. All of these store data as resistance, unlike flash, which stores trapped charge.

Strengths and weaknesses

ReRAM cells are small and built from materials already used in CMOS, and they can be placed in the interconnect layers above the transistors with only a few extra process steps. That makes them attractive as embedded nonvolatile memory in microcontrollers at nodes where embedded flash, with its floating gates, becomes hard to integrate. Endurance of roughly 10^6 to 10^9 cycles beats NAND flash but falls short of DRAM. The main weakness is variability: random filament shapes spread resistance from cell to cell and write to write, which narrows read margins and makes multilevel storage difficult.

Arrays and countermeasures

Cells are usually paired with an access transistor (1T1R), which also limits current during set so the filament does not grow too strong. Dense crosspoint arrays without transistors are possible, but current leaking through unselected cells (sneak paths) then requires a nonlinear selector device at each crossing. To tighten resistance distributions, controllers use write-verify, repeatedly pulsing and reading until the cell lands in its target window. Because resistance can be tuned in steps, ReRAM is also studied for analog in-memory computing of neural-network weights.

ReRAM is a simple stack of a thin film, such as a metal oxide, between electrodes. Applying voltage forms or ruptures a conductive filament of oxygen vacancies, changing resistance by tenfold or more. The small cells can be built in the interconnect layers, suiting embedding in logic and dense crosspoint arrays. It is in production as embedded memory in microcontrollers; large resistance variability is its main challenge.

In ReRAM, what is the initialization step called in which a relatively high voltage is first applied to form a conductive filament?

  1. ADoping
  2. BForming
  3. CAnnealing

Answer: B. Forming

Most ReRAM cells start out highly resistive and need an initial voltage higher than normal operation to create a conductive path of oxygen vacancies or similar—forming. Afterward, only the thin tip of the filament is ruptured and reconnected, so lower voltages switch between high and low resistance. High forming voltages burden peripheral circuits, so forming-free materials and structures are being researched.

FAQ

How does ReRAM store data?

By forming or breaking a nanoscale conductive filament, usually made of oxygen vacancies, in a thin oxide; the resulting low or high resistance represents the stored bit and remains without power.

What is the forming step in ReRAM?

A one-time application of higher-than-normal voltage that creates the initial conductive filament in a fresh, highly resistive cell, after which lower voltages switch it.

What is the difference between ReRAM and PCM?

Both store data as resistance, but ReRAM switches by moving ions to form or break a filament, while phase-change memory (PCM) switches a material between crystalline and amorphous phases by heating.
