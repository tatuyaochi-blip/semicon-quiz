What is Photodiode?

A diode that generates charge (current) when it receives light. Used as the light-receiving element in image sensor pixels.

Japanese name: フォトダイオード

How is a photodiode generally used to detect light?

  1. AA large forward current is passed to make it emit light
  2. BReverse bias is applied, and carriers generated by light in the depletion layer are collected as current
  3. CAC voltage is applied to make it resonate
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Reverse bias is applied, and carriers generated by light in the depletion layer are collected as current

Reverse-biasing a photodiode widens the depletion region, where light-generated electrons and holes are quickly separated by the field, producing a photocurrent proportional to light intensity. Reverse bias also reduces junction capacitance, speeding the response. Because the photocurrent tracks light level over many decades, photodiodes are used in light meters, optical-communication receivers and CD/DVD pickups. Zero-bias (photovoltaic) mode has lower dark current and suits precise measurement of weak light.

Which is a characteristic of an avalanche photodiode (APD)?

  1. AIt emits strong light when current flows
  2. BIt uses avalanche multiplication to amplify the photocurrent internally and can detect very weak light
  3. CIt converts AC to DC
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It uses avalanche multiplication to amplify the photocurrent internally and can detect very weak light

An APD is a photodiode biased near breakdown so that light-generated carriers are multiplied tens to hundreds of times by avalanche multiplication. This internal gain lets it detect weak light that would otherwise be buried in the following amplifier's noise. APDs are used in optical-communication receivers and LiDAR, and in Geiger mode (SPAD), biased above breakdown, even a single photon produces a large pulse, used in ranging and quantum optics.
