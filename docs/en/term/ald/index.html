What is ALD?

Atomic layer deposition. A deposition method that repeats self-limiting surface reactions of precursor gases to control film thickness one atomic layer at a time.

Field: Deposition (CVD and PVD)

ALD in the explanations

  • ALD repeats a cycle: dose a precursor gas that adsorbs only one layer on the surface, purge the excess, then supply a reactant gas to complete the reaction.

    Which is a characteristic of ALD (atomic layer deposition)?

  • ALD offers atomic-level thickness control and excellent conformality, so it deposits high-k hafnium oxide gate dielectrics, titanium nitride for metal gates and barriers, zirconium oxide in DRAM capacitors, films around 3D NAND charge-trap layers, and spacers for multiple patterning—wherever thin, uniform films are needed.

    Which is an example of a typical material deposited by ALD?

  • PEALD uses plasma during the reactant step to create highly reactive oxygen, nitrogen or hydrogen radicals, depositing at lower temperatures (room temperature to a few hundred °C) than thermal ALD and enabling chemistries that react poorly thermally, such as SiN and TiN.
