What is ALD?

Atomic layer deposition. A deposition method that repeats self-limiting surface reactions of precursor gases to control film thickness one atomic layer at a time.

Japanese name: ALD

Field: Deposition (CVD and PVD)

The ALD cycle

Atomic layer deposition (ALD) splits a CVD reaction into two half-reactions separated in time. A precursor such as trimethylaluminum (TMA) is dosed and chemisorbs on surface sites until they are filled; excess gas is purged; a co-reactant such as water then reacts with the adsorbed layer; and a second purge follows. Because each half-reaction stops once the surface is saturated, growth per cycle is fixed, typically about 0.1 nm or less, since bulky precursor molecules block neighboring sites. Film thickness is set simply by counting cycles.

The ALD window

Self-limiting growth only holds within a temperature range called the ALD window. Too cold, precursors condense or react incompletely; too hot, they decompose and grow CVD-like, or adsorbed species desorb. Within the window growth per cycle is nearly constant, about 150–300 °C for Al2O3 from TMA and water. Precursor choice shifts it: HfO2 can be grown from HfCl4, which is stable around 300 °C but may leave chlorine, or from organometallics such as TEMAHf, which react at lower temperature but may leave carbon. Such residues affect threshold voltage and reliability.

Conformality compared with CVD and PVD

Because growth is controlled by surface saturation rather than gas transport, ALD reaches nearly 100% step coverage, coating the sidewalls and bottoms of features with aspect ratios of several tens. Sputtering may leave under 10% of the top thickness at the bottom of deep holes, and CVD sits in between. This is why ALD deposits high-k HfO2 gate dielectrics, TiN for metal gates and barriers, ZrO2/Al2O3/ZrO2 stacks in DRAM capacitors, the gate stack wrapped around GAA nanosheets, and the spacers used in self-aligned double and quadruple patterning.

Throughput, PEALD and related techniques

The cost is speed: a cycle takes seconds to tens of seconds, so a few nanometers needs dozens of cycles. Batch reactors and spatial ALD, which moves wafers through separate gas zones, raise throughput. Plasma-enhanced ALD (PEALD) uses radicals in the reactant step to deposit at room temperature to a few hundred °C and to enable films such as SiN, at some cost in conformality inside deep features because radicals recombine. Atomic layer etching (ALE) applies the same self-limiting idea to removing material.

For transistors with channels made of 2D materials such as MoS2, what is the main reason it is hard to deposit a uniform high-k gate dielectric by atomic layer deposition (ALD)?

  1. A2D materials are metals and react with the dielectric
  2. BTheir surfaces have almost no dangling bonds, so there are few sites where ALD precursors can adsorb and start film growth
  3. CThe surfaces of 2D materials are very rough, so ALD precursors cannot reach deep areas
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Their surfaces have almost no dangling bonds, so there are few sites where ALD precursors can adsorb and start film growth

Because 2D materials have no dangling bonds on their layer surfaces, they keep their mobility even below 1 nm thickness. The same property means ALD precursors hardly adsorb chemically on the surface, so films nucleate as islands at defects and grain boundaries and do not form thin, uniform dielectrics. Plasma-based methods tend to damage the layer, so approaches such as first forming a thin nucleation layer are being studied.

Which is a characteristic of ALD (atomic layer deposition)?

  1. AIt can deposit films several µm thick quickly in a single step
  2. BIt repeats self-limiting surface reactions of precursors, controlling thickness at the atomic-layer level
  3. CIt is a printing technique that needs no vacuum
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. It repeats self-limiting surface reactions of precursors, controlling thickness at the atomic-layer level

ALD repeats a cycle: dose a precursor gas that adsorbs only one layer on the surface, purge the excess, then supply a reactant gas to complete the reaction. Because reaction stops once surface sites are filled (self-limiting), thickness is controlled precisely at about 0.1 nm per cycle, and films coat trenches with aspect ratios of several tens uniformly. The trade-off is a slow deposition rate.

FAQ

What is the difference between ALD and CVD?

In CVD all reactants are present together and the film grows continuously at a rate set by temperature and gas supply. In ALD the reactants are supplied separately with purges between them, so each step is self-limiting and thickness is set by the number of cycles.

How thick is the film deposited in one ALD cycle?

Typically about 0.1 nm, which is one atomic layer or less. A 2 nm film therefore needs on the order of 20 cycles.

Why is ALD slow?

Each cycle needs two precursor doses and two purges that must fully saturate and then fully clear the surface, taking seconds or longer, yet adds only a fraction of a nanometer.
