What is Wet etching?

Etching that dissolves films by immersing them in chemicals. Selectivity is high and damage low, but it generally proceeds isotropically and does not suit fine patterning.

Japanese name: ウェットエッチング

Wet etching appears in 7 practice questions across 1 topic, including Etching.

Field: Etching

Why wet etching is isotropic

A wet etchant attacks a film by chemical reaction wherever liquid touches it, and for amorphous or polycrystalline films the rate is the same in every direction. As the etch goes down, it also goes sideways under the mask edge. Clearing a 1 µm film undercuts the mask by about 1 µm, and overetch adds more. Because the dimensional change is of the order of the film thickness, wet etching cannot define features narrower than the film is thick. MEMS (micro-electro-mechanical systems) processing exploits the same behavior to hollow out material under suspended structures.

Common etchants

Silicon dioxide is etched in hydrofluoric acid; buffered HF (BHF), which adds ammonium fluoride, keeps the active HF2⁻ concentration and pH stable so thermal oxide etches steadily at tens to about 100 nm per minute without lifting resist. Silicon nitride is removed in concentrated phosphoric acid near 160 °C with selectivity of tens to over 100 against oxide, which depends on the dissolved silicon in the bath. Some materials resist wet chemistry altogether: GaN, for example, is etched with chlorine-based plasmas rather than liquids.

Anisotropic exception: KOH on silicon

Alkaline etchants such as potassium hydroxide (KOH) or TMAH (tetramethylammonium hydroxide) are crystallographic: the (111) planes of silicon etch roughly 100 times slower than (100) planes. On a (100) wafer, slow (111) planes emerge as sidewalls inclined at about 54.7°, forming V-grooves and pyramidal pits whose shape is fixed by the crystal rather than by time. This orientation-dependent etching produces MEMS pressure-sensor diaphragms, inkjet nozzles and the light-trapping texture on crystalline solar cells. It is anisotropic, but unlike plasma etching the direction is set by crystal planes, not by an ion beam.

Process control and where it is still used

Being thermally activated, wet etch rates often rise 1.5–2× per 10 °C, so baths are held to about ±0.5 °C and etch time is set from a known rate. Equipment is simple, batches of wafers can be processed together, selectivity is high and there is no plasma damage. Dry (plasma) etching owns fine patterning, but wet processes remain essential for blanket film removal, nitride mask stripping after LOCOS or STI, removing sacrificial nitride layers in 3D NAND stacks, native-oxide removal and wafer cleaning.

Answer: B. It generally proceeds isotropically and readily undercuts the mask

Wet etching dissolves films by chemical reaction in liquid, so it generally proceeds equally in all directions (isotropic) and undercuts the mask by about the film thickness. It needs simple equipment, processes many wafers at once and easily achieves high selectivity. Dry etching is used for fine patterning, but wet etching remains important for blanket film removal and cleaning.

Answer: B. Hot phosphoric acid

Silicon nitride is etched in concentrated phosphoric acid heated to about 160 °C with selectivity of tens to over 100 to oxide. It removes nitride masks after LOCOS or STI formation and, in 3D NAND, strips the nitride layers from alternating oxide/nitride stacks so they can be replaced with word-line metal. Selectivity depends on dissolved silicon in the bath, requiring tight control.

FAQ

What is the difference between wet etching and dry etching?

Wet etching dissolves material in liquid chemicals and is usually isotropic with high selectivity and no plasma damage. Dry etching uses plasma and directional ion bombardment to cut vertical profiles, which fine patterning requires.

How much undercut does isotropic wet etching cause?

Roughly equal to the etched depth: a 1 µm film etched to the underlayer is undercut about 1 µm from the mask edge, plus whatever overetch adds.

Can wet etching be anisotropic?

Yes, for single-crystal silicon in alkaline etchants such as KOH or TMAH, which etch (111) planes far more slowly than (100) planes and produce 54.7° sidewalls on (100) wafers.
