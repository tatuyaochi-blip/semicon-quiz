What is Degenerate semiconductor?

A semiconductor doped so heavily that the Fermi level lies inside the conduction or valence band. It behaves almost like a metal and is used in tunnel diodes and low-resistance contacts.

Japanese name: 縮退半導体

Where is the Fermi level located in an n-type semiconductor?

  1. ACloser to the valence band than the intrinsic Fermi level
  2. BCloser to the conduction band than the intrinsic Fermi level
  3. CAlways inside the valence band
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Closer to the conduction band than the intrinsic Fermi level

In n-type material the conduction band holds many electrons, so the Fermi level moves from the intrinsic level near midgap toward the conduction band. Its position is given by Ec − EF ≈ kT·ln(Nc/ND), so it moves about 60 mV (at room temperature) closer to the conduction band for every tenfold increase in donor concentration. In p-type it moves toward the valence band. At very high doping it can even enter a band (a degenerate semiconductor).

What is a semiconductor called whose impurity concentration is so high that the Fermi level lies inside the conduction or valence band?

  1. AIntrinsic semiconductor
  2. BInsulator
  3. CDegenerate semiconductor
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Degenerate semiconductor

When the doping is very high (roughly above 10¹⁸–10¹⁹ cm⁻³ in silicon), the Fermi level moves into the conduction or valence band and the material behaves almost like a metal. This is a degenerate semiconductor: the simple n × p = ni² relation no longer holds and the band gap appears smaller (bandgap narrowing). Examples include MOSFET sources and drains, regions for low-resistance metal contacts, and the heavily doped regions of tunnel diodes.
