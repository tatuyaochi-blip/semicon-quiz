What is Dicing?

Cutting a wafer into individual chips (dies). Besides sawing with a rotating blade, there are methods such as stealth dicing, which forms a modified layer inside the wafer with a laser.

Japanese name: ダイシング

Dicing appears in 14 practice questions across 7 topics, including Packaging and assembly, Silicon photonics, IC design flow and EDA.

Field: Packaging and assembly

Blade dicing

The wafer is first mounted on adhesive dicing tape stretched across a ring frame so the dies stay in place once cut. A diamond blade tens of micrometres thick, spinning at high speed, cuts along the scribe lines (streets) while water cools it and flushes away debris. The cut removes a kerf tens of micrometres wide, so streets must allow for kerf plus edge chipping, and test structures (TEGs) placed there are destroyed. With fragile low-k dielectric stacks, a laser usually grooves the surface first so the blade does not chip or delaminate them.

Stealth dicing

Stealth dicing focuses an infrared laser, at a wavelength silicon largely transmits, inside the wafer to create a modified layer along each street; expanding the tape then cleaves the wafer along it. The surface is not cut, so the process is dry, produces almost no debris and has nearly zero kerf. It suits contamination- and vibration-sensitive MEMS, thin wafers and very small dies, where narrower streets raise dies per wafer. Laser ablation grooving is different: it removes surface material, mainly to open the top layers before blade dicing.

Where dicing sits in the flow

Dicing follows wafer test and back grinding, which thins the wafer from about 750 µm to its target, down to tens of micrometres for stacked memory. Dies are then picked from the tape for die bonding, wire bonding or flip-chip attach, and encapsulation. In dicing before grinding (DBG), partial-depth grooves are cut first and grinding separates the dies, reducing breakage of very thin chips. In wafer-level packaging, redistribution layers and solder balls are formed before singulation. Slicing an ingot into wafers is a different step, done with a multi-wire saw.

Quality concerns

Dicing leaves damage at die edges: front-side and backside chipping, microcracks and a damaged sidewall layer. These flaws can grow under thermal cycling or handling and lower die strength, so cut quality is inspected and blade type, feed speed and cut depth are tuned to each wafer stack. Seal rings around each die help stop cracks and moisture from reaching active circuitry. In silicon photonics, edge couplers need a clean facet after singulation, while grating couplers allow optical testing on the wafer before dicing.

Answer: C. The step of cutting a wafer into individual chips

Dicing cuts a finished wafer into individual chips (dies) with a diamond blade tens of micrometers thick or a laser. The wafer is mounted on tape so the chips stay in place after cutting. When fragile low-k films are present, a laser first grooves the surface to prevent chipping. Encapsulation and electrical testing are separate steps.

What is the step called that grinds the back side of a wafer before dicing to thin it and make the package thinner?

  1. ABack grinding
  2. BCMP
  3. CEtch-back
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Back grinding

In backgrinding, the wafer face is protected with tape while a rotating grinding wheel thins the back from about 750 µm to the target thickness. For stacked memory it may go down to tens of micrometers. The damaged layer left by grinding can initiate cracks, so it is removed by polishing or etching. CMP planarizes the front surface, and etch-back removes a film uniformly.

FAQ

What is the difference between blade dicing and stealth dicing?

Blade dicing removes a kerf tens of micrometres wide with a spinning diamond blade and water, while stealth dicing forms a modified layer inside the wafer with an infrared laser and cleaves it by expanding the tape. Stealth dicing is dry, with almost no kerf or debris.

Is back grinding done before or after dicing?

Usually before: the wafer is thinned first and then diced. In dicing before grinding (DBG) the grooves are cut first and grinding completes the separation, which suits very thin chips.

What is kerf in wafer dicing?

Kerf is the width of material removed by the cut. With blade dicing it is tens of micrometres, which sets a minimum width for the scribe lines between dies.
