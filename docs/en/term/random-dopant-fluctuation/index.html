What is Random dopant fluctuation (RDF)?

Threshold-voltage variation in small transistors caused by statistical variation in the number and position of dopant atoms in the channel. FinFETs and FD-SOI, which allow lightly doped channels, reduce it.

Japanese name: ランダムドーパントゆらぎ(RDF)

Random dopant fluctuation (RDF) appears in 2 practice questions across 2 topics, including MOSFET, SRAM and flash memory.

Field: Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA)

Counting individual atoms

Doping concentrations look continuous on paper, but a small transistor's channel holds a countable number of dopant atoms. Their number follows roughly Poisson statistics, so the standard deviation is about the square root of the mean. For example, a 30 × 30 × 10 nm depletion volume doped at 10¹⁸ cm⁻³ contains on average only about 9 atoms, with a spread of about ±3, or roughly 33%. Their random positions also matter, since a dopant near the source end of the channel affects the barrier more than one deep in the substrate.

From dopants to threshold voltage

Threshold voltage depends on the depletion charge under the gate, so a different dopant count or arrangement shifts Vth from one transistor to the next even when they are drawn identically and sit side by side. The variation is random and local, so it cannot be corrected by tuning the process. Mismatch between paired devices is commonly described by Pelgrom's law: the standard deviation of the Vth difference scales as a coefficient divided by the square root of gate area, so halving width and length doubles the mismatch.

Why SRAM and analog suffer most

A six-transistor SRAM cell relies on a balance between near-minimum-size transistors, and a large array has hundreds of millions of cells, so the design must work at the far tails of the distribution, around six standard deviations. RDF-driven mismatch eats read and write margins and raises the minimum operating voltage, one reason SRAM cell area has scaled more slowly than logic. Analog circuits such as differential pairs and current mirrors face the same issue and use larger devices for matching.

How FinFETs and FD-SOI reduce it

The main cure is to remove most dopants from the channel. FinFET, gate-all-around and FD-SOI (fully depleted silicon on insulator) transistors control the channel electrostatically through thin silicon bodies, so they can use nearly undoped channels and set Vth with gate work-function metals instead of channel implants. This greatly reduces RDF, though variability remains from other sources, such as metal-gate grain orientation, line-edge roughness and fin or sheet thickness variation. FD-SOI can additionally tune Vth through back-gate bias.

Answer: A. It becomes higher

Higher substrate doping means more acceptor charge in the depletion region before the surface inverts, and a larger Fermi potential φF, so more gate voltage is needed and Vth rises. Channel implants adjust Vth this way. But heavy doping lowers mobility and increases variability from random dopant fluctuation, so FinFETs and GAA devices use nearly undoped channels and set Vth with the gate work function.

Answer: A. SRAM is sensitive to variations in transistor characteristics, making it hard to secure margins for stable reading and writing

An SRAM cell's stability depends on balancing the drive strengths of six near-minimum-size transistors. With scaling, variation from effects such as random dopant fluctuation grows relative to device size, making it hard to keep read and write margins even for the worst of hundreds of millions of cells. In recent nodes SRAM cell area has shrunk much less than logic, so cache area has become a growing share of the die.

FAQ

What causes random dopant fluctuation?

The finite, random number and positions of dopant atoms in a tiny channel, which change the depletion charge and therefore the threshold voltage from device to device.

How do FinFETs reduce threshold voltage variation from RDF?

Their thin, gate-wrapped bodies allow nearly undoped channels, with threshold voltage set by the gate work function instead of channel doping, so there are few dopants left to fluctuate.

What is Pelgrom's law?

It states that random mismatch between identical transistors, such as threshold-voltage difference, has a standard deviation inversely proportional to the square root of gate area.
