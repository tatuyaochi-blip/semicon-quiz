What is Moore's law?

The empirical rule that the number of transistors on an integrated circuit doubles about every two years. It has served as a target for semiconductor scaling.

Japanese name: ムーアの法則

Answer: C. The number of transistors on an integrated circuit doubles every fixed period (about two years)

Moore's law is the empirical observation that the number of transistors on an integrated circuit doubles at regular intervals. Gordon Moore proposed annual doubling in 1965 and revised it to about every two years in 1975. It is not a law of physics but has served as the industry's development target. It does not say prices halve every year or frequency rises tenfold in 18 months. The pace of doubling is said to have slowed recently.
