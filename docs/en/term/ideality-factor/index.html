What is Ideality factor?

The factor n in the diode equation I ∝ exp(qV/nkT). It is close to 1 when diffusion current dominates and close to 2 when recombination in the depletion layer dominates, so it indicates junction quality.

Japanese name: 理想係数

Field: PN junctions and diodes

Where n = 1 and n = 2 come from

In the ideal diffusion picture, forward bias raises the minority-carrier concentration at each edge of the depletion region in proportion to exp(qV/kT), and the current that diffuses away into the neutral regions follows the same law, giving n = 1. Recombination inside the depletion region behaves differently: it is strongest where electron and hole densities are equal, and there each equals ni·exp(qV/2kT). The resulting recombination current goes as exp(qV/2kT), so a diode dominated by it shows n ≈ 2.

Extracting n from an I-V curve

Plot forward current on a logarithmic axis against voltage. At room temperature an ideal diode rises one decade per about 60 mV, since kT/q × ln 10 ≈ 26 mV × 2.3; a diode with ideality factor n needs n × 60 mV per decade. Formally n = (q/kT)·dV/d(ln I). Use the straight middle portion of the curve: at low current, shunt leakage distorts it, and at high current series resistance bends it over and inflates the apparent n.

What the value tells you

Many silicon diodes show n close to 2 at low current, where depletion-region recombination is relatively important, and close to 1 at moderate current, where diffusion dominates. Under high-level injection, when injected carriers exceed the doping, n rises toward 2 again. Values above 2 point to other mechanisms such as tunneling or several junctions in series. Because recombination depends on defect density, n is used as a quick gauge of junction quality, for example in solar cells, which are often modeled with two diodes of n = 1 and n = 2.

Link to MOSFET subthreshold swing

The same thermal factor limits transistors. A MOSFET below threshold conducts by diffusion over a barrier, so its current also changes exponentially with voltage. Its subthreshold swing is (kT/q)·ln 10 × (1 + Cd/Cox), where Cd is depletion capacitance and Cox gate-oxide capacitance, giving at least about 60 mV per decade at room temperature. The bracketed factor plays the role of an ideality factor: gate voltage is only partly coupled to the channel potential.

  2. BAbout 60 mV

Answer: B. About 60 mV

Forward current is proportional to exp(qV/kT), so increasing it tenfold requires raising V by kT/q × ln 10 ≈ 26 mV × 2.3 ≈ 60 mV—hence the "about 60 mV per decade" rule. With an ideality factor n this becomes n × 60 mV, so n can be extracted from the slope of a semilog I-V plot. The theoretical limit of a MOSFET's subthreshold swing has the same value.

In the diode current expression I ∝ exp(qV / nkT), when is the ideality factor n close to 2?

  1. AWhen minority carrier diffusion current in the neutral regions dominates
  2. BWhen recombination current in the depletion layer dominates
  3. CWhen tunneling current dominates

Answer: B. When recombination current in the depletion layer dominates

When minority-carrier diffusion in the neutral regions dominates, n ≈ 1; when recombination through defect levels in the depletion region dominates, n approaches 2, because that current goes as exp(qV/2kT). In silicon diodes recombination current stands out at low currents. Extracting n from a semilog I-V curve gives a gauge of defect density and process quality and is also used to evaluate solar cells.

FAQ

What does an ideality factor of 2 mean?

It means the current is dominated by recombination through defect levels in the depletion region, or that the diode is in high-level injection, rather than by ideal minority-carrier diffusion.

How do you calculate the ideality factor from an I-V curve?

Take the slope of a semilog plot of forward current versus voltage in its straight region: n = (q/kT)·ΔV/Δ(ln I), or n equals the measured millivolts per decade divided by about 60 mV at room temperature.

Why does the measured ideality factor increase at high current?

Series resistance drops part of the applied voltage outside the junction and high-level injection changes the physics, both of which make the voltage per decade larger.
