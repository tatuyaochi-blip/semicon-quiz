What is Ideality factor?

The factor n in the diode equation I ∝ exp(qV/nkT). It is close to 1 when diffusion current dominates and close to 2 when recombination in the depletion layer dominates, so it indicates junction quality.

Japanese name: 理想係数

Try it: questions about Ideality factor

For an ideal diode (ideality factor n = 1) at room temperature, roughly how much must the forward voltage increase to raise the forward current tenfold?

  1. AAbout 6 mV
  2. BAbout 60 mV
  3. CAbout 600 mV
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. About 60 mV

Forward current is proportional to exp(qV/kT), so increasing it tenfold requires raising V by kT/q × ln 10 ≈ 26 mV × 2.3 ≈ 60 mV—hence the "about 60 mV per decade" rule. With an ideality factor n this becomes n × 60 mV, so n can be extracted from the slope of a semilog I-V plot. The theoretical limit of a MOSFET's subthreshold swing has the same value.

In the diode current expression I ∝ exp(qV / nkT), when is the ideality factor n close to 2?

  1. AWhen minority carrier diffusion current in the neutral regions dominates
  2. BWhen recombination current in the depletion layer dominates
  3. CWhen tunneling current dominates
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. When recombination current in the depletion layer dominates

When minority-carrier diffusion in the neutral regions dominates, n ≈ 1; when recombination through defect levels in the depletion region dominates, n approaches 2, because that current goes as exp(qV/2kT). In silicon diodes recombination current stands out at low currents. Extracting n from a semilog I-V curve gives a gauge of defect density and process quality and is also used to evaluate solar cells.
