What is Magnetic tunnel junction (MTJ)?

A structure with a thin insulating film between two magnetic layers. Resistance is low when the layers' magnetizations are parallel and high when antiparallel. It is the storage element of MRAM.

Japanese name: 磁気トンネル接合(MTJ)

Layers of an MTJ

A magnetic tunnel junction (MTJ) sandwiches an insulating tunnel barrier, typically MgO about 1 nm thick, between two ferromagnetic layers such as CoFeB. One layer, the reference or pinned layer, has its magnetization fixed by an antiferromagnet or a synthetic antiferromagnet so that write currents and stray fields cannot move it. The other, the free or storage layer, can be switched between two directions. Production MTJs are stacks of more than ten films, many of which exist only to stabilize the reference layer.

Why the resistance changes

Electrons cross the barrier by quantum tunneling, and in a ferromagnet majority-spin and minority-spin electrons have different numbers of available states. When the two layers are parallel, majority states on both sides match and tunneling is easy, giving low resistance. When antiparallel, majority electrons must enter minority states, so resistance is high. The tunnel magnetoresistance (TMR) ratio, (R_AP − R_P)/R_P, measures the contrast: 2 kΩ versus 5 kΩ gives 150%. Laboratory MgO junctions exceed 600% at room temperature, but production cells are around 100 to 200%, so the two states differ only two- to threefold.

Writing: STT versus SOT

In spin-transfer torque (STT) MRAM, write current flows through the junction; electrons spin-polarized by the reference layer transfer angular momentum to the free layer and flip it, with current direction selecting 0 or 1. Write current scales with cell area, which made STT the mainstream approach. Reads use the same path, so read current must stay well below the switching current to avoid read disturb. Spin-orbit torque (SOT) MRAM drives write current through a heavy-metal line such as tungsten or platinum under the free layer, separating read and write paths and allowing sub-nanosecond writes in a larger three-terminal cell.

Retention and integration

The energy barrier holding the free layer is roughly magnetic anisotropy times volume, so it shrinks as cells shrink. Its ratio to thermal energy kT, the thermal stability factor Δ, must be about 60 or more for ten-year retention; a larger Δ also demands more write current, which sets the scaling trade-off. Perpendicular magnetic anisotropy materials help. MTJs are built between interconnect layers in the back end of line (BEOL), so they must survive about 400 °C, and the magnetic films are patterned with ion-beam etching to avoid metal redeposition on the sidewalls.

In which process of a logic IC is the MRAM storage element (MTJ) generally built?

  2. BWhen the silicon wafer is manufactured
  3. CAfter assembly into a package

Answer: A. Between wiring layers in the interconnect (BEOL) process

MTJs are formed between interconnect layers in the back-end-of-line (BEOL), after the transistors are complete. They can be embedded without changing the transistor process, and the same MTJ technology can be reused across generations. MTJ materials must survive BEOL thermal steps (about 400 °C), and patterning the magnetic films requires special techniques such as ion-beam etching to prevent metal redeposition.

FAQ

What is the TMR ratio of a magnetic tunnel junction?

The tunnel magnetoresistance ratio is (R_antiparallel − R_parallel) divided by R_parallel. Production MRAM junctions typically show about 100 to 200%, meaning the high-resistance state is two to three times the low one.

What is the difference between an MTJ and a GMR spin valve?

Both use two magnetic layers whose relative alignment sets the resistance, but a giant magnetoresistance (GMR) spin valve uses a thin metallic spacer such as copper, while an MTJ uses an insulating tunnel barrier. The tunnel barrier gives a much larger resistance change, which is why MRAM uses MTJs.

Is MTJ-based memory volatile?

No. The bit is stored as the direction of the free layer's magnetization, which persists without power, so MRAM built from MTJs is non-volatile as long as the thermal stability factor is high enough.
