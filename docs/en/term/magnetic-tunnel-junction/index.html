What is Magnetic tunnel junction (MTJ)?

A structure with a thin insulating film between two magnetic layers. Resistance is low when the layers' magnetizations are parallel and high when antiparallel. It is the storage element of MRAM.

Japanese name: 磁気トンネル接合(MTJ)

In MRAM, what problem tends to arise if the storage element (MTJ) is made too small?

  1. AThermal fluctuations can easily reverse the magnetization, making data retention difficult
  2. BThe element emits light
  3. CThe element's resistance becomes zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Thermal fluctuations can easily reverse the magnetization, making data retention difficult

The energy barrier holding magnetization is magnetic anisotropy × volume, shrinking with cell size. Its ratio to thermal energy kT—the thermal stability factor Δ—must be roughly 60 or more for ten-year retention; below that, thermal fluctuations flip the magnetization spontaneously. Raising Δ also raises write current, so balancing retention against writability is the scaling challenge, addressed with perpendicular materials and cell shaping.

In which process of a logic IC is the MRAM storage element (MTJ) generally built?

  1. ABetween wiring layers in the interconnect (BEOL) process
  2. BWhen the silicon wafer is manufactured
  3. CAfter assembly into a package
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Between wiring layers in the interconnect (BEOL) process

MTJs are formed between interconnect layers in the back-end-of-line (BEOL), after the transistors are complete. They can be embedded without changing the transistor process, and the same MTJ technology can be reused across generations. MTJ materials must survive BEOL thermal steps (about 400 °C), and patterning the magnetic films requires special techniques such as ion-beam etching to prevent metal redeposition.
