What is Accelerometer?

A sensor that detects acceleration from the movement of a small spring-supported mass. It is used for detecting smartphone orientation, in airbags and elsewhere.

Japanese name: 加速度センサー

Field: MEMS and sensors

How a MEMS accelerometer senses motion

A MEMS accelerometer suspends a small proof mass on silicon springs. When the package accelerates, inertia makes the mass lag behind, deflecting the springs until spring force balances the inertial force. In capacitive designs, interdigitated comb electrodes on the mass and the frame change their gaps as it moves, and the capacitance change, often below a femtofarad for nanometer-scale displacement, is read by a sensitive charge amplifier. Differential electrodes, with one gap closing as the opposite one opens, double the signal and cancel common-mode drift. Capacitive sensing is low power and temperature-stable, which is why it dominates consumer parts.

Sensitivity versus bandwidth

The spring–mass system resonates at f = (1/2π)√(k/m), and below resonance the static deflection is roughly the acceleration divided by (2πf)². A stiffer spring or lighter mass raises the resonant frequency, extending usable bandwidth and shock tolerance but shrinking the deflection per g, which lowers sensitivity. Designers therefore pick a resonance, typically a few kHz, that balances these needs. Full-scale range is a separate digital trade-off: with a 16-bit output, ±2 g gives about 0.06 mg per least significant bit (LSB), while ±16 g gives about 0.5 mg per LSB.

Gravity is always in the reading

An accelerometer measures the force needed to accelerate its proof mass along with the package, so at rest on a table it reports about 1 g (9.8 m/s²) directed upward, and in free fall it reads nearly zero. That gravity component is useful: phones compute tilt and screen orientation from it, pedometers detect steps from its periodic variations, and laptops once used the free-fall signature to park hard-disk heads. When only motion matters, the gravity vector must be estimated and subtracted, often with help from a gyroscope.

Accelerometer vs. gyroscope and typical uses

An accelerometer senses linear acceleration plus gravity, whereas a MEMS gyroscope senses rotation rate, typically through the Coriolis force on a vibrating mass. Combined with a magnetometer that measures Earth's field for heading, the three form an inertial measurement unit for navigation and orientation. Airbag systems were an early mass market: the accelerometer detects a crash deceleration of tens of g, and the controller decides within a few tens of milliseconds whether to deploy. Wearables also use accelerometer data to cancel motion artifacts in optical heart-rate sensing.

Answer: B. Change in capacitance between electrodes caused by displacement of a movable mass

Under acceleration, a spring-suspended proof mass moves by inertia, changing its gap to comb-shaped fixed electrodes; the resulting capacitance change is measured to determine acceleration. Displacements are nanometers or less and capacitance changes below a femtofarad, read with differential electrodes and sensitive circuits. Simple, low-power and temperature-stable, capacitive accelerometers are ubiquitous in smartphones.

  2. BBecause the sensor is broken
  3. CBecause temperature alone produces an output

Answer: A. Because it detects gravitational acceleration

An accelerometer measures proof-mass displacement from inertial forces, so even at rest it reports gravity as about 1 g (9.8 m/s²), pointing upward. Phones use this to sense tilt, screen orientation and steps. In free fall the output drops to nearly zero, a fact used for fall detection to protect laptop hard drives. To isolate motion, the gravity component must be separated.

FAQ

Why does an accelerometer show 1 g when the phone is lying still?

The sensor cannot distinguish gravity from acceleration, and resisting gravity looks to the proof mass exactly like accelerating upward at 9.8 m/s². In free fall the reading drops to about zero.

Should I choose a ±2 g or ±16 g accelerometer range?

Pick the narrowest range that will not saturate: ±2 g gives finer resolution for tilt and step counting, while ±16 g is needed for impacts, drops and sports motion. Most digital parts let you change the range in a register.

What is the difference between an accelerometer and a gyroscope?

An accelerometer measures linear acceleration including gravity, while a gyroscope measures angular velocity. Orientation tracking usually fuses both because each covers the other's weaknesses.
