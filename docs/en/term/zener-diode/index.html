What is Zener diode?

A diode that provides a constant voltage by using the nearly fixed reverse breakdown voltage.

Read the beginner explainer: What Is a Diode? How It Lets Current Flow Only One Way

Japanese name: ツェナーダイオード

Field: PN junctions and diodes

How it is used: reverse bias in breakdown

A Zener diode is connected with its cathode more positive than its anode and operated beyond its reverse breakdown voltage. In that region the current can change greatly while the voltage changes very little, meaning the dynamic resistance is low, so the diode acts as a voltage reference. The datasheet Zener voltage is specified at a stated test current, and the diode regulates best near that current. Below breakdown only a tiny leakage current flows. In the forward direction it behaves like an ordinary silicon diode and conducts at about 0.6 to 0.7 V.

Zener effect and avalanche effect

The breakdown voltage is set by doping. Heavily doped junctions have very thin depletion layers and very strong fields, so electrons tunnel directly from the valence band on the p side to the conduction band on the n side; this true Zener breakdown dominates below about 5 V and has a negative temperature coefficient. More lightly doped junctions break down by avalanche multiplication, dominant above roughly 5 V, with a positive temperature coefficient. Parts near 5 to 6 V mix both mechanisms, whose coefficients partly cancel, so they tend to show the smallest drift with temperature.

Designing a simple shunt regulator

The basic regulator places a resistor between the input and the Zener, with the load in parallel with the diode. The resistor limits current and absorbs input variation as its own voltage drop. Choose R = (minimum input − Vz) ÷ (maximum load current + minimum Zener current). For a 9 to 12 V input, a 5.1 V Zener, a 10 mA load and 5 mA minimum Zener current, R ≈ 260 Ω. Then check the worst case: at 12 V with no load, about 26 mA flows through the diode, dissipating about 0.14 W. Because current flows continuously, this circuit is inefficient and suits only small loads.

Clamping and protection

Zener diodes also clamp voltages. Placed across a sensitive input, one conducts only when the voltage exceeds its rating, protecting against overvoltage. In a relay driver, a plain flyback diode across the coil clamps the turn-off spike to about one diode drop but makes the relay release slowly; adding a Zener in series lets the voltage rise to a controlled higher level, dissipating the coil's energy faster for quicker release. Transient voltage suppressor (TVS) diodes are related avalanche devices optimized for absorbing large surge energy rather than for precise regulation.

Answer: A. To limit the current through the diode and take up the difference between input and output voltage

A Zener diode holds a nearly constant voltage in breakdown, but without current limiting it would be destroyed by excessive current and heat. The series resistor limits the current and absorbs variations in input voltage as its own voltage drop, keeping the output nearly constant. Its value is chosen as (minimum input voltage − Zener voltage) ÷ (maximum load current + minimum Zener current). Because it is inefficient, this circuit suits low-current voltage references.

FAQ

Can a Zener diode be used in the forward direction?

It conducts forward like an ordinary diode at about 0.6 to 0.7 V, but that is not its intended use. For voltage regulation it must be reverse biased beyond its breakdown voltage.

How do I choose the series resistor for a Zener regulator?

Use R = (minimum input voltage − Zener voltage) ÷ (maximum load current + minimum Zener current), then check that the Zener's power rating is not exceeded at maximum input with the lightest load.

What is the difference between a Zener diode and a TVS diode?

Both operate in reverse breakdown, but a Zener diode is designed for a precise, steady reference voltage at small currents. A TVS diode is built to absorb brief high-energy surges and clamp them, with less emphasis on voltage accuracy.
