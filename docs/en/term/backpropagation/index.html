What is Backpropagation?

The training computation that propagates the error between output and target backward from output to input to obtain each weight's gradient.

Japanese name: 逆伝播

Try it: questions about Backpropagation

When training neural networks in FP16 (half precision: 1 sign, 5 exponent, 10 significand bits), which problem tends to arise and is countered by scaling up the loss (loss scaling)?

  1. AThe significand is too long, increasing memory usage
  2. BThe range is narrow, so small gradients become zero
  3. CValues are treated as integers, so fractions cannot be represented
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. The range is narrow, so small gradients become zero

With only 5 exponent bits, FP16 represents positive values from about 6 × 10⁻⁸ (including subnormals) to 65504, a narrow range. Many gradients computed during training are very small and underflow to zero in FP16, stalling learning. Loss scaling multiplies the loss by, say, 1024 before backpropagation, lifting gradients into the representable range, and undoes the scaling before updating weights. The significand is 10 bits, shorter than FP32's, and fractions are representable.

What is the difference between training and inference for a neural network?

  1. ATraining repeats forward passes, backpropagation and weight updates to determine weights; inference only runs forward passes with fixed weights to produce answers
  2. BBoth training and inference perform only forward passes
  3. CInference updates weights, and training uses no weights
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Training repeats forward passes, backpropagation and weight updates to determine weights; inference only runs forward passes with fixed weights to produce answers

Training computes outputs from inputs (the forward pass), propagates the error against the correct answer backward from output to input to obtain each weight's gradient (backpropagation), and adjusts the weights slightly according to the gradients, repeating this over large amounts of data. Inference uses the learned weights and runs only the forward pass. Backpropagation needs about twice the computation of the forward pass and intermediate results must be stored, so training is far heavier than inference.
