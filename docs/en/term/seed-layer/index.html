What is Seed layer?

A thin copper film deposited by sputtering or similar before copper electroplating. It provides the conductive path for the plating current.

Japanese name: シード層

Seed layer appears in 1 practice question across 1 topic, including Deposition (CVD and PVD).

Field: Interconnect processes

Why a seed layer is needed

Electroplating that fills copper trenches and vias makes the wafer the cathode and passes current so that copper ions in the plating bath are reduced and deposited on the surface. That requires an unbroken conductive layer over the whole wafer, including the inside of every trench. The barrier layer (such as Ta/TaN) that stops copper diffusion has high resistance and readily forms surface oxides, so plating directly on it does not start uniformly. A thin copper film is therefore sputtered on top to carry the plating current and give copper a surface on which to start depositing. This is the seed layer, named because it seeds the growth.

Deposition and scaling challenges

The seed layer is typically deposited a few nanometers to a few tens of nanometers thick, often by ionized sputtering (iPVD), which ionizes atoms ejected from the target and draws them vertically onto the wafer. If it is too thick, film overhangs at the trench opening and narrows it, so the opening closes before plating fills the trench and a void remains. If it is too thin, the film breaks up low on the sidewalls, copper does not deposit there and voids form. The narrower the lines, the harder this balance becomes. Remedies include ruthenium or cobalt liners, which copper wets readily, underneath the seed, and seed enhancement by CVD or similar methods.

The seed layer in the plating sequence

In the damascene process, trenches and vias are formed in the dielectric, the barrier and seed layers are deposited, copper is filled by electroplating, and after annealing, CMP removes the excess copper and barrier. The seed layer merges with the plated copper and remains inside the trenches as part of the wiring. If the seed is thin, voltage drops as current flows from contacts at the wafer edge toward the center, so the edge tends to plate thicker (the terminal effect); plating tools adjust the current distribution to compensate. Because a long wait between seed deposition and plating lets the surface oxidize and causes plating defects, that time is controlled.

Seed layers in packaging and TSVs

Seed layers are also used to form package redistribution layers (RDL) and bumps. There, an adhesion layer of Ti and a copper seed are first sputtered over the whole wafer, resist is patterned in the shape of the wiring or bumps, and copper is plated thick only in the openings (semi-additive process). After the resist is removed, the seed left between the lines must be wet-etched away or it will short them, so removing only the seed without thinning the lines is a challenge. In TSVs, getting a continuous seed all the way to the bottom of deep holes is critical.

Answer: B. Electroplating

In the damascene process, a barrier (e.g., Ta/TaN) and a Cu seed layer a few to tens of nm thick are sputtered into the trenches, and copper is then electroplated to fill them. Three additives in the bath—suppressor, accelerator and leveler—promote bottom-up fill, growing copper preferentially from the trench bottom so no voids remain. Plating deposits thick films quickly and cheaply at room temperature and atmospheric pressure, making it the mainstay for copper wiring.

FAQ

What is a seed layer?

It is a thin metal film deposited on the underlying surface before growing copper or other metals by electroplating. It is both the conductive layer that carries the plating current and the base on which the metal begins to deposit. For copper interconnects, a thin copper film is usually sputtered on top of the barrier layer.

Why not plate directly on the barrier layer?

Barrier layers such as Ta and TaN have high resistance and oxidize easily, so current does not flow uniformly and copper deposition does not start uniformly. A seed layer in between lets plating start evenly across the wafer. Plating directly on liners such as ruthenium is also being researched.

What happens to the seed layer after plating?

In the damascene process the seed merges with the plated copper and becomes part of the wiring, and the portion outside the trenches is removed by CMP along with the excess copper. In semi-additive processes such as package redistribution, the seed in the areas that were not plated is etched away.
