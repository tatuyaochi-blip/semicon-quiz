What is Solid solubility?

The maximum concentration of an impurity that can dissolve in a crystal at a given temperature. It sets the upper limit of electrically active dopant concentration.

Japanese name: 固溶度

Try it: questions about Solid solubility

Under the same annealing conditions, how does the sheet resistance of the implanted layer generally change when the implant dose is increased?

  1. AIt decreases
  2. BIt increases
  3. CIt does not change
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It decreases

Higher dose means more dopant and carriers in the implanted layer, so sheet resistance falls—roughly 1/(q × active dose × average mobility), around 100 Ω/□ for 10¹⁵ cm⁻². Beyond the solid solubility, however, much of the extra dopant stays inactive and mobility declines, so further dose increases yield diminishing reductions in sheet resistance.

When dopants are activated by annealing, which property sets the upper limit of the concentration that can be electrically activated?

  1. ASolid solubility
  2. BMobility
  3. CPermittivity
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Solid solubility

Solid solubility is the maximum dopant concentration a crystal can dissolve in equilibrium—about 2×10²⁰ cm⁻³ for boron in silicon near 1000 °C and around 10²¹ cm⁻³ for arsenic and phosphorus. Excess dopant beyond it precipitates or clusters and stays electrically inactive. Millisecond anneals and solid-phase epitaxial regrowth quench before equilibrium is reached, achieving metastable activation above solubility.
