What is STI (shallow trench isolation)?

Isolating devices by etching shallow trenches in silicon, filling them with insulator and planarizing by CMP. It has no bird's beak like LOCOS and is the standard isolation for fine-pitch ICs.

Japanese name: STI(シャロートレンチ分離)

Field: Oxidation and diffusion

The STI process flow

A thin pad oxide and a silicon nitride layer are deposited, then lithography and etching open trenches about 200 to 300 nm deep into the silicon. A thin liner oxide is grown, often by thermal or ISSG oxidation, to repair etch damage and round the trench corners. The trench is filled with CVD oxide and annealed to densify it. Chemical mechanical polishing (CMP) removes the excess oxide and stops on the nitride, often using ceria slurries with oxide-to-nitride selectivity of tens to over 100. Finally the nitride is stripped in hot phosphoric acid at about 160 °C.

Why STI replaced LOCOS

In local oxidation of silicon (LOCOS), nitride masks the active areas while exposed silicon is thermally oxidized, but oxidant diffuses sideways under the nitride edge and grows a bird's beak that can encroach about half the oxide thickness on each side. That eats into active area, limits how close devices can be placed and leaves surface steps. STI isolates with an etched and filled trench, so the surface stays flat and isolation width is set by lithography. It replaced LOCOS around the 0.25 µm generation and is used today to isolate FinFET fins as well.

Gap fill and corner engineering

Ordinary CVD tends to pinch off at the trench opening and trap a void. From about the 0.25 µm to 65 nm generations, high-density plasma CVD filled STI by sputtering the corners while depositing; narrower trenches, down to around 10 nm, use flowable CVD or spin-on dielectrics that fill from the bottom and are then converted to oxide. Corners matter as well: a sharp top corner concentrates the gate field and creates a parasitic edge transistor, and an oxide divot from later wet cleans makes this worse, so corner rounding and recess control are tuned carefully.

Stress and layout effects

The oxide in STI trenches stresses the neighboring silicon, and the amount depends on how far a transistor sits from the trench edge, so drive current can vary with active-area size and layout. Circuit models and design rules account for this. Trench etch must also avoid microtrenching at the bottom corners, which can create later filling or field concentration problems. In FinFET flows, the STI oxide is recessed after CMP to expose the upper part of each fin, and the recess depth sets the active fin height.

Answer: A. Ceria (cerium oxide)

Ceria (cerium oxide) bonds chemically with oxide and removes it efficiently, while additives such as amino-acid surfactants adsorb on nitride and suppress its polishing, giving oxide-to-nitride selectivity of tens to over 100. It is used in STI CMP, where nitride serves as the stop, to combine planarity with a reliable stop. Ceria also polishes quickly at low particle concentrations.

FAQ

What is the difference between STI and LOCOS?

LOCOS grows thick thermal oxide in unmasked regions and suffers bird's-beak encroachment and surface steps. STI etches trenches, fills them with oxide and planarizes by CMP, giving flat surfaces and much narrower isolation.

Why is it called shallow trench isolation?

Its trenches are only a few hundred nanometers deep, shallow compared with the deep trench isolation, several micrometers deep, used in some bipolar, power, image sensor and older DRAM technologies.

Why is ceria slurry used in STI CMP?

Ceria removes silicon oxide efficiently, and with suitable additives it barely polishes silicon nitride. The high oxide-to-nitride selectivity lets polishing stop reliably on the nitride layer.
