What is Tail current?

Current that keeps flowing like a tail as an IGBT turns off, because stored minority carriers prevent it from dropping to zero immediately. It causes turn-off loss.

Japanese name: テール電流

Tail current appears in 5 practice questions across 3 topics, including Power semiconductors, Automotive Semiconductors and Functional Safety, Ion implantation.

Field: Power semiconductors

Why an IGBT current trails off

An insulated-gate bipolar transistor (IGBT) has a p+ collector layer that injects holes into the lightly doped n-drift region while the device conducts. These minority carriers flood the drift region and lower its resistance, an effect called conductivity modulation, which gives the IGBT its low on-state voltage. At turn-off the gate removes the MOS channel and electron supply stops almost at once, but the stored holes cannot vanish instantly. They must recombine or be swept out by the growing electric field, and the collector current decays with them, producing the tail.

Tail current and switching loss

The tail flows after the collector-emitter voltage has already risen to the bus voltage, so voltage times current is large during it and it adds directly to the turn-off energy (Eoff). Because this energy is paid every switching cycle, it limits silicon IGBTs to switching frequencies of roughly tens of kHz or less. The tail also grows at higher temperature, since carrier lifetime in silicon increases with temperature. Designers face a trade-off: more stored charge lowers the on-state voltage but lengthens the tail, so conduction loss and switching loss pull in opposite directions.

How it is shortened

Lifetime control introduces recombination centers so stored carriers disappear faster; electron irradiation and hydrogen or helium implants can place these defects at chosen depths. Field-stop designs add an n-type buffer layer that lets the drift region be thinner, so less charge is stored, and a lightly injecting collector reduces how many holes enter in the first place. Unipolar devices avoid the problem entirely: silicon MOSFETs and SiC MOSFETs conduct with majority carriers only, so they show no tail current and can switch much faster.

The other meaning: differential pair tail current

In analog circuits, tail current means something unrelated: the bias current of the current source tied to the common emitters or sources of a differential pair. The input voltage difference steers this fixed current between the two transistors, so it sets the pair's transconductance, its maximum output current and the slew rate of an amplifier built on it. The output impedance of the tail current source determines how well the pair rejects common-mode signals, which shows up as the common-mode rejection ratio (CMRR).

  2. BA device driven by a MOS gate that achieves a low on-state voltage through conductivity modulation from bipolar operation
  3. CA thyristor turned on by light

Answer: B. A device driven by a MOS gate that achieves a low on-state voltage through conductivity modulation from bipolar operation

An IGBT combines a MOSFET input with a bipolar output, adding a p+ layer on the drain side of a MOSFET-like structure. It is voltage-driven, simplifying gate drive, and when on, holes injected from the p+ layer sharply reduce drift-layer resistance (conductivity modulation), keeping on-voltage low even at 600 V to 6.5 kV ratings. It is the workhorse of EV, rail and industrial inverters, though tail current makes it unsuitable for high frequencies.

At IGBT turn-off, what is the phenomenon called in which the current does not drop to zero immediately but trails off because of stored minority carriers?

  1. AInrush current
  2. BTail current
  3. CDark current
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Tail current

An IGBT lowers drift resistance by injecting minority carriers (holes) when on, so at turn-off current continues until they recombine or are swept out—the tail current. Because it flows while the voltage is already high, it is a major source of turn-off loss and limits IGBT switching frequency to tens of kHz or less. Lifetime control and field-stop structures shorten it. MOSFETs have no tail current.

FAQ

Why do MOSFETs have no tail current?

A MOSFET conducts only with majority carriers through its channel, so there is no stored minority-carrier charge to remove when the gate turns it off. An IGBT relies on injected minority carriers for conductivity modulation, and removing them is what creates the tail.

Does IGBT tail current increase with temperature?

Yes. Carrier lifetime in silicon rises with temperature, so stored charge decays more slowly and turn-off energy is noticeably higher at elevated junction temperatures.

What is tail current in a differential amplifier?

It is the bias current supplied by the current source at the common node of the differential pair. The input difference splits this current between the two transistors, and its value sets gain, slew rate and output swing limits.
