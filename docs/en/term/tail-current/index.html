What is Tail current?

Current that keeps flowing like a tail as an IGBT turns off, because stored minority carriers prevent it from dropping to zero immediately. It causes turn-off loss.

Japanese name: テール電流

What does a differential pair, with the emitters of two transistors connected to a common current source, do?

  1. AAmplifies the sum of the two input voltages
  2. BAmplifies the difference between the two input voltages
  3. CRectifies the input voltage
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Amplifies the difference between the two input voltages

In a differential pair, the tail current source's current is split between the two transistors according to the difference between the input voltages. If both inputs move together (common mode) the split does not change, so only the difference is amplified while noise or drift common to both cancels. The ratio of differential to common-mode gain is the CMRR (common-mode rejection ratio). Differential pairs form op-amp input stages and receivers for high-speed differential links such as LVDS.

Which correctly describes an IGBT?

  1. AA two-terminal device with only two PN junctions
  2. BA device driven by a MOS gate that achieves a low on-state voltage through conductivity modulation from bipolar operation
  3. CA thyristor turned on by light
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A device driven by a MOS gate that achieves a low on-state voltage through conductivity modulation from bipolar operation

An IGBT combines a MOSFET input with a bipolar output, adding a p+ layer on the drain side of a MOSFET-like structure. It is voltage-driven, simplifying gate drive, and when on, holes injected from the p+ layer sharply reduce drift-layer resistance (conductivity modulation), keeping on-voltage low even at 600 V to 6.5 kV ratings. It is the workhorse of EV, rail and industrial inverters, though tail current makes it unsuitable for high frequencies.
