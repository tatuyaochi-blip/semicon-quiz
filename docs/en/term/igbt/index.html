What is IGBT?

Insulated-gate bipolar transistor. Driven by a MOS gate, it achieves a low on-state voltage even at high voltage ratings through conductivity modulation.

Japanese name: IGBT

IGBT appears in 5 practice questions across 3 topics, including Power semiconductors, Bipolar transistors, Ion implantation.

Field: Power semiconductors

Try it: questions about IGBT

Which correctly describes an IGBT?

  1. AA two-terminal device with only two PN junctions
  2. BA device driven by a MOS gate that achieves a low on-state voltage through conductivity modulation from bipolar operation
  3. CA thyristor turned on by light
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A device driven by a MOS gate that achieves a low on-state voltage through conductivity modulation from bipolar operation

An IGBT combines a MOSFET input with a bipolar output, adding a p+ layer on the drain side of a MOSFET-like structure. It is voltage-driven, simplifying gate drive, and when on, holes injected from the p+ layer sharply reduce drift-layer resistance (conductivity modulation), keeping on-voltage low even at 600 V to 6.5 kV ratings. It is the workhorse of EV, rail and industrial inverters, though tail current makes it unsuitable for high frequencies.

At IGBT turn-off, what is the phenomenon called in which the current does not drop to zero immediately but trails off because of stored minority carriers?

  1. AInrush current
  2. BTail current
  3. CDark current
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Tail current

An IGBT lowers drift resistance by injecting minority carriers (holes) when on, so at turn-off current continues until they recombine or are swept out—the tail current. Because it flows while the voltage is already high, it is a major source of turn-off loss and limits IGBT switching frequency to tens of kHz or less. Lifetime control and field-stop structures shorten it. MOSFETs have no tail current.
