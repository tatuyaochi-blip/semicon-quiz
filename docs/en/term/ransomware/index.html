What is Ransomware?

Malware that makes files on a computer unusable, for example by encrypting them, and demands a ransom in exchange for restoring them. Some attackers also threaten to publish stolen data. Backups kept disconnected from the network are an important safeguard.

Japanese name: ランサムウェア

  2. BMalware that makes files on a computer unusable, for example by encrypting them without permission, and demands money (a ransom) in exchange for restoring them
  3. CAn attack that sends huge numbers of packets to make a server unable to respond
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Malware that makes files on a computer unusable, for example by encrypting them without permission, and demands money (a ransom) in exchange for restoring them

Ransomware is malware that makes files on infected computers or networks unusable, for example by encrypting them, and demands a ransom in exchange for the key to restore them. As of the mid-2020s, many reported cases involve intruding into an organization's network and encrypting many servers at once, or threatening to publish data stolen before the encryption (double extortion). Paying gives no guarantee of recovery and funds criminals, so not paying is recommended. The core defenses are applying fixes for vulnerabilities that attackers use to get in, multi-factor authentication for remote access, minimizing privileges, and backups kept somewhere disconnected from the network.

Regarding backups as a defense against ransomware, which method best follows the "3-2-1 rule"?

  1. ACreate three folders on the same file server and copy the data to them once every two hours
  2. BPrepare three backup disks that are always connected to the network, placing two in the same server and one in the same room
  3. CKeep three copies including the original data, store them on two different types of media, and keep one of them in a separate location (such as one disconnected from the network)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Keep three copies including the original data, store them on two different types of media, and keep one of them in a separate location (such as one disconnected from the network)

The 3-2-1 rule is a guideline for backups: keep at least three copies of the data including the original, store them on at least two different types of media (disk, tape, cloud and so on), and keep at least one of them in a separate location. The idea is that a failure of one device, a defect common to one type of media, or damage at one site such as a fire or disaster should not affect all copies at once. Ransomware may encrypt or delete backups that are connected to the network and writable, so it is important to keep at least one copy offline (disconnected from the network) or set so that it cannot be modified for a certain period (immutable). It is also necessary to confirm regularly through restore tests that the data can actually be recovered when needed.
