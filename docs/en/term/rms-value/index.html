What is RMS value?

The size of an AC voltage or current expressed as the DC value that would dissipate the same average power in the same resistance. It is the square root of the mean of the square (root mean square); for a sine wave it is 1/√2 ≈ 0.707 times the peak value. Household AC of 100 V is an RMS value, with a peak of about 141 V. AC power calculations and phasor magnitudes normally use RMS values.

Try it: questions about RMS value

Which is closest to the peak value (amplitude) of a sinusoidal AC voltage with an RMS value of 100 V?

  1. AAbout 141 V
  2. B100 V
  3. CAbout 71 V
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. About 141 V

For a sine wave the RMS value is 1/√2 times the peak, so the peak is √2 times the RMS value: 100 V × √2 ≈ 141 V (141.4 V). The RMS value is the DC voltage that would dissipate the same power in the same resistor; AC voltmeter readings and nominal mains voltages (such as 100 V) are RMS values. The peak-to-peak value is about 283 V, so component voltage ratings must be judged against the peak, not the RMS value.

Which of the following expresses the sinusoidal AC voltage v(t) = 100√2 sin(ωt + π/6) [V] as a phasor (polar form) whose magnitude is the RMS value?

  1. A100∠60° V
  2. B100∠30° V
  3. C141∠30° V
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 100∠30° V

Method 1 (from the definition): the magnitude of the phasor is the RMS value, the peak value 100√2 V divided by √2, which is 100 V. The angle is the initial phase π/6 rad = 30°, so the phasor is 100∠30° V. Method 2 (check with complex numbers): 100∠30° = 100(cos 30° + j sin 30°) = 86.6 + j50 V; multiplying by √2 and e^(jωt) and taking the imaginary part gives back the original v(t). In circuit calculations it is usual to use the RMS value, not the peak value, as the magnitude of a phasor.
