What is Problem management?

The process whose goal is to identify the root cause (the problem) behind one or more incidents and prevent recurrence with a permanent fix. It includes reactive work, investigating causes from incidents that have occurred, and proactive work, analyzing incident trends to prevent failures that have not happened yet. A problem whose root cause or workaround is known but whose fix is not yet complete is recorded as a known error and shared with the service desk and others. When a fix changes a system, it goes through change management.

Problems and known errors

In problem management, the not-yet-understood root cause behind incidents is called a problem. A problem is registered and investigated when incidents with the same symptoms keep recurring or when an incident with a large impact occurs. A problem whose root cause has been identified, or for which a workaround has been found, but whose permanent fix is not yet complete is called a known error and is recorded together with the workaround. The service desk uses these records to respond quickly to the same incident.

How causes are investigated

To find the root cause, incident records, logs and configuration management information (what changed just before) are compared, and events are put in time order. Methods include repeatedly asking "why" to trace the cause, listing candidate causes with a cause-and-effect diagram, and narrowing the targets with a Pareto chart that orders items by count. Once the cause is known, a permanent fix is considered, and if the system must be changed, a change request is submitted to change management.

Reactive and proactive work

Investigating causes in response to incidents that have already happened is reactive problem management. Proactive problem management, by contrast, analyzes trends in incident records and monitoring data to find signs before they become major failures and acts first. For example, noticing that disk replacements for one model are increasing and replacing them in a planned way is proactive work.

A file server stops responding with the same error several times a week, and each time an operations staff member restarts it to restore service. From the standpoint of problem management, which of the following is an appropriate action?

  1. ASince a restart restores service, do not treat it as a problem and keep restarting the server each time
  2. BWithout knowing the cause, keep changing the settings of the production server one after another at the staff member's discretion to see what happens
  3. CRegister the series of incidents as a problem, investigate the root cause, and carry out the permanent fix through change management
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Register the series of incidents as a problem, investigate the root cause, and carry out the permanent fix through change management

When the same incident keeps recurring, a root cause probably remains, so it is registered as a problem and investigated. Until the root cause is found, the restart is recorded as a workaround and shared as a known error. When a permanent fix changes the server's settings or programs, its impact and risk are assessed in change management and it is approved before being carried out.

FAQ

If a workaround keeps the business running, is problem management unnecessary?

No. A workaround is a temporary measure; as long as the root cause remains, the same incident keeps recurring, each time costing effort and affecting the business. The workaround is recorded as a known error, and the cost and benefit of a permanent fix are compared to decide whether to carry it out.

Can a fix found in problem management be applied to production right away?

Changes to the production environment go through change management, where their impact and risk are assessed and they are approved before being carried out. Even a fix for a root cause can cause new failures if it is applied without assessment.
