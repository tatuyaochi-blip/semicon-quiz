What is Low-k material?

An interlayer dielectric with a low dielectric constant. It lowers the capacitance between wires, reducing signal delay and power consumption.

Japanese name: Low-k材料

What problem must be watched for when etching or ashing low-k films?

  1. AThe film becomes far too hard
  2. BPlasma damages the film, for example by removing carbon, raising its dielectric constant
  3. CThe film emits light
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Plasma damages the film, for example by removing carbon, raising its dielectric constant

SiOC low-k films lower permittivity with Si-CH3 (methyl) groups, but exposure to oxygen plasma strips them into Si-OH, making the film absorb moisture and raising its permittivity from about 3.0 to above 4. In porous films damage penetrates through the pores. Low-damage ashing (e.g., hydrogen-based), silylation treatments that restore damaged surfaces and post-process dehydration are therefore required.

In chips using mechanically weak low-k films, what is the problem called in which stress during packaging cracks or delaminates the films?

  1. AQuantization error
  2. BChip-package interaction (CPI) problems
  3. CShort-channel effects
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Chip-package interaction (CPI) problems

Low-k films, often porous, have elastic moduli several times lower than oxide, so stresses from solder-bump joining, molding and thermal-expansion mismatch between die and package substrate can crack or delaminate interconnect layers—the chip-package interaction (CPI) problem. It is most likely at die corners and beneath bumps and is addressed with reinforcing structures, low-stress underfills and careful package material selection.
