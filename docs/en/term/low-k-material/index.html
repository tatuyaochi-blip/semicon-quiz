What is Low-k material?

An interlayer dielectric with a low dielectric constant. It lowers the capacitance between wires, reducing signal delay and power consumption.

Why low-k materials became necessary

A low-k material is an interlayer dielectric whose relative permittivity (k) is below that of SiO2, about 3.9. Capacitance between wires is proportional to the dielectric's permittivity and inversely proportional to wire spacing, so it rises as scaling packs wires closer together. Higher capacitance increases RC delay (set by its product with wire resistance), the power lost charging and discharging wires, and crosstalk from neighboring lines. In parallel with the switch to copper, which lowered resistance, the industry worked to lower the permittivity of the insulator, and the combination has kept interconnect performance in check.

Typical materials and their permittivity

Permittivity is lowered either by using bonds that polarize less or by lowering film density. Fluorine-doped SiO2 (FSG, about 3.5–3.7) came first, followed by SiOC films that incorporate carbon or methyl groups into silicon oxide (about 2.7–3.0), which became widespread. Most are deposited by plasma CVD. Porous films containing tiny voids reach about 2.2–2.5. Because air has a relative permittivity of nearly 1, air gaps, which leave voids between wires, are also used, but only in the layers where cutting capacitance matters most.

The price of lower permittivity

Lower-permittivity films are generally softer and mechanically weaker. In films whose Young's modulus is only a fraction of that of oxide, stresses from CMP polishing, solder-bump bonding and molding readily cause cracking and delamination (a chip-package interaction problem). SiOC films also lose methyl groups when exposed to oxygen plasma and the like, absorb moisture, and can see their permittivity rise from 3.0 to above 4. In porous films the damage spreads inward through the pores, so low-damage ashing and treatments that repair the damage are needed.

Effective permittivity and how low-k differs from high-k

The space between wires holds more than the low-k film: higher-permittivity layers such as copper-diffusion caps (SiCN and similar) and etch stops are stacked in as well. Circuit performance therefore depends on the effective permittivity of the whole stack, which is higher than the low-k material's own value. Despite the similar name, high-k materials are the opposite: high-permittivity gate dielectrics (about 20–25 for HfO2). A gate wants large capacitance to control the channel strongly, while interconnects want to shed unneeded capacitance, so the goals are exactly reversed.

Answer: B. Plasma damages the film, for example by removing carbon, raising its dielectric constant

SiOC low-k films lower permittivity with Si-CH3 (methyl) groups, but exposure to oxygen plasma strips them into Si-OH, making the film absorb moisture and raising its permittivity from about 3.0 to above 4. In porous films damage penetrates through the pores.   2. BChip-package interaction (CPI) problems

Answer: B. Chip-package interaction (CPI) problems

Low-k films, often porous, have elastic moduli several times lower than oxide, so stresses from solder-bump joining, molding and thermal-expansion mismatch between die and package substrate can crack or delaminate interconnect layers—the chip-package interaction (CPI) problem. It is most likely at die corners and beneath bumps and is addressed with reinforcing structures, low-stress underfills and careful package material selection.

FAQ

What is the difference between low-k and high-k materials?

Both names classify insulators by relative permittivity (k), with SiO2 at about 3.9 as the reference. Low-k materials fall below it and are used as interlayer dielectrics to cut capacitance between wires. High-k materials lie above it (about 20–25 for HfO2) and are used as gate dielectrics to get large gate capacitance while suppressing gate leakage.

How much do low-k materials reduce wire-to-wire capacitance?

Capacitance is proportional to permittivity, so changing all of the dielectric between wires from 3.9 to 2.7 cuts capacitance by about 31% in a simple calculation. In practice higher-permittivity cap layers are also present and fields above, below and at the edges of wires contribute, so the real benefit is smaller.

What are the challenges of porous low-k films?

More pores lower the permittivity but also the mechanical strength, so the films crack and delaminate more easily under CMP and packaging stress. Plasma, chemicals and moisture can also penetrate deep into the film through the pores, raising permittivity and degrading reliability, so processing conditions are adjusted and pore-sealing treatments are used.
