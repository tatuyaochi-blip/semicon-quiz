What is Synchronous rectification?

A technique, used in DC-DC converters and elsewhere, that replaces a rectifying or freewheeling diode with a MOSFET turned on at the moments the diode would conduct. The loss changes from the diode's forward voltage × current to the MOSFET's current² × on-resistance; at 10 A, for example, a diode with a 0.5 V forward voltage dissipates 5 W, while a MOSFET with 5 mΩ on-resistance dissipates 0.5 W. The lower the output voltage, the greater the benefit. A dead time is needed so that the two switches are never on at the same time, and during it the body diode carries the current.

Japanese name: 同期整流

  1. ATo make the output voltage higher than the input voltage
  2. BTo keep the switching frequency constant automatically even when the load current changes
  3. CTo reduce the conduction loss caused by the diode forward voltage and raise efficiency
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. To reduce the conduction loss caused by the diode forward voltage and raise efficiency

A diode has a forward voltage of about 0.3 to 0.5 V even if it is a Schottky barrier diode, producing a loss of current × forward voltage. Turning on a MOSFET with a low on-resistance in place of the diode changes the loss to current² × on-resistance, which can reduce it greatly. The lower the output voltage, such as around 1 V, the larger the diode drop relative to the output, so synchronous rectification helps more. In exchange, the switches must be driven with a dead time so that the two are never on at the same time.
