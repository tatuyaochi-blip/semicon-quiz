What is Machine epsilon?

The difference between 1 and the next larger representable floating-point number, a measure of relative precision: 2⁻²³ (about 1.19 × 10⁻⁷) in single precision and 2⁻⁵² (about 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁶) in double.

Japanese name: マシンイプシロン

What is the machine epsilon (the gap between 1 and the next larger representable number) in IEEE 754 single precision?

  1. A2⁻²³ (about 1.19 × 10⁻⁷)
  2. B2⁻⁸ (about 3.9 × 10⁻³)
  3. C2⁻⁵² (about 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁶)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 2⁻²³ (about 1.19 × 10⁻⁷)

1.0 is represented as 1.000…0 × 2⁰ with a 23-bit significand, so incrementing the last significand bit gives 1 + 2⁻²³. That gap, 2⁻²³ ≈ 1.19 × 10⁻⁷, is machine epsilon, indicating single precision's relative precision of about seven digits. 2⁻⁵² is the machine epsilon of double precision with its 52-bit significand. 2⁻⁸ confuses it with the exponent width. The value also guides tolerances for comparing floating-point numbers.
