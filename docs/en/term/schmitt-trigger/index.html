What is Schmitt trigger?

A circuit whose switching threshold differs for rising and falling inputs (hysteresis). Its output does not chatter even with slowly changing or noisy signals.

Japanese name: シュミットトリガ

Schmitt trigger appears in 2 practice questions across 2 topics, including Analog and electronic circuits, Embedded Systems & Firmware.

Try it: questions about Schmitt trigger

Which circuit has different thresholds for rising and falling inputs and prevents the output from chattering due to noise?

  1. APLL
  2. BSample-and-hold circuit
  3. CSchmitt trigger
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Schmitt trigger

A Schmitt trigger has a higher threshold for rising inputs and a lower one for falling inputs; the difference is hysteresis. It prevents the output from toggling repeatedly (chattering) when a slowly varying or noisy signal hovers near the threshold. It is made by adding positive feedback to a comparator and is also available as logic ICs such as the 74HC14. Uses include cleaning up switch inputs and building RC relaxation oscillators.

What is the phenomenon called in which a mechanical switch's contacts bounce, turning on and off many times in a short period when pressed?

  1. ALatch-up
  2. BCrosstalk
  3. CContact bounce (chattering)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Contact bounce (chattering)

When a mechanical switch closes, the metal contacts bounce and turn on and off for a few milliseconds. A microcontroller reads its pins very fast, so counting naively would register one press as several. The bounce is removed in software by waiting and rereading, or in hardware with a capacitor or Schmitt trigger to smooth the waveform. This processing is called debouncing.
