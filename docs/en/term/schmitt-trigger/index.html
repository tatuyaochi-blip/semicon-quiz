What is Schmitt trigger?

A circuit whose switching threshold differs for rising and falling inputs (hysteresis). Its output does not chatter even with slowly changing or noisy signals.

Japanese name: シュミットトリガ

Schmitt trigger appears in 2 practice questions across 2 topics, including Analog and electronic circuits, Embedded Systems & Firmware.

Field: Analog and electronic circuits

How hysteresis is created

A Schmitt trigger is a comparator with positive feedback. In the common inverting form, the input drives the inverting input while a divider from the output feeds a fraction of the output voltage back to the non-inverting input. When the output is high, that fraction sets a higher threshold; when it is low, the threshold moves down. With output swings of ±Vsat and a divider of R2 from output to the non-inverting node and R1 from that node to ground, the thresholds are ±Vsat·R1/(R1+R2) and the hysteresis width is twice that.

Why it stops chattering

A comparator with a single threshold toggles every time noise carries a slowly moving signal back and forth across that threshold, producing a burst of output edges known as chattering. In a Schmitt trigger, the moment the output switches, the threshold jumps away from the input, so noise smaller than the hysteresis width cannot switch it back. The design rule is to make the hysteresis larger than the expected peak-to-peak noise, while keeping it small enough that the shifted thresholds do not cost too much sensitivity or timing accuracy.

Schmitt inputs vs. ordinary logic inputs

A standard CMOS logic input has one switching point near half the supply. If the input edge is slow, it lingers in the region where both the pull-up and pull-down transistors conduct, drawing extra current and possibly oscillating. Gates with Schmitt-trigger inputs, such as the 74HC14 hex inverter, have separate rising and falling thresholds and tolerate slow or noisy edges. Many microcontroller input pins include a Schmitt trigger for the same reason, which helps with long cables, RC-filtered signals and reset lines.

Where you will meet it

Mechanical switch contacts bounce for a few milliseconds when closed. A resistor-capacitor filter slows the bouncing waveform, and a Schmitt trigger turns it back into one clean edge; software debouncing that waits and rereads is the alternative. A single Schmitt inverter with a resistor from output to input and a capacitor from input to ground forms an RC relaxation oscillator: the capacitor charges and discharges between the two thresholds, so the frequency depends on RC and on the hysteresis. Schmitt triggers also square up sine waves and sensor signals.

Answer: C. Schmitt trigger

A Schmitt trigger has a higher threshold for rising inputs and a lower one for falling inputs; the difference is hysteresis. It prevents the output from toggling repeatedly (chattering) when a slowly varying or noisy signal hovers near the threshold. It is made by adding positive feedback to a comparator and is also available as logic ICs such as the 74HC14. Uses include cleaning up switch inputs and building RC relaxation oscillators.

What is the phenomenon called in which a mechanical switch's contacts bounce, turning on and off many times in a short period when pressed?

  1. ALatch-up
  2. BCrosstalk
  3. CContact bounce (chattering)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Contact bounce (chattering)

When a mechanical switch closes, the metal contacts bounce and turn on and off for a few milliseconds. A microcontroller reads its pins very fast, so counting naively would register one press as several. The bounce is removed in software by waiting and rereading, or in hardware with a capacitor or Schmitt trigger to smooth the waveform. This processing is called debouncing.

FAQ

What is hysteresis in a Schmitt trigger?

It is the difference between the rising-input threshold and the falling-input threshold. The output only changes state when the input crosses the far threshold, which gives immunity to noise smaller than that gap.

How do you debounce a button with a Schmitt trigger?

Place an RC low-pass filter between the switch and a Schmitt-trigger input. The filter smooths the bounces into a slow ramp, and the hysteresis converts that ramp into a single clean transition.

What is the difference between a comparator and a Schmitt trigger?

A plain comparator switches at one threshold and can chatter on noisy inputs. A Schmitt trigger is a comparator with positive feedback, giving two thresholds and a clean single transition.
