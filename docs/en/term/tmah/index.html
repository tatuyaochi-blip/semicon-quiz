What is TMAH?

Tetramethylammonium hydroxide, a metal-ion-free organic alkali used as a photoresist developer. Care is needed because it can cause poisoning through skin absorption.

Japanese name: TMAH

What TMAH is and why fabs use it

TMAH (tetramethylammonium hydroxide, (CH3)4N+ OH−) is a quaternary ammonium hydroxide, a strong base that contains no metal atoms. Sodium or potassium hydroxide would also dissolve exposed photoresist, but alkali metal ions that reach the gate oxide become mobile ions that drift under electric fields and shift threshold voltages, the problem that made early MOS devices unstable. A metal-free organic alkali avoids that contamination path, which is why TMAH became the standard developer for positive photoresists in semiconductor manufacturing.

How TMAH development works

The industry-standard developer is 2.38 wt% aqueous TMAH, about 0.26 N. In a positive resist, exposure makes the irradiated regions alkali-soluble: in novolak resists for i-line the photoactive compound converts into a carboxylic acid, and in chemically amplified resists for KrF and ArF, acid-catalyzed deprotection exposes acidic groups on the polymer. These regions dissolve in TMAH while unexposed regions remain. Using one common concentration across fabs keeps dissolution rates reproducible. Negative-tone development is different: an organic solvent dissolves the unexposed areas instead.

Toxicity and handling

TMAH is hazardous beyond being a caustic alkali. Tetramethylammonium ions absorbed through the skin interfere with signal transmission from nerves to muscles and can cause respiratory arrest, and fatal accidents after skin contact have been reported. Concentrated stock solutions, around 25%, are far more dangerous than the 2.38% developer, because the absorbed dose rises with concentration and contact area. Handling requires chemical-resistant gloves, face protection and aprons, and any contact calls for immediate flushing with water and prompt medical care even if the skin looks unharmed.

Beyond lithography

TMAH also etches silicon anisotropically, like KOH: (100) planes etch much faster than (111) planes, producing V-grooves and cavities bounded by sloped facets. Because it is metal-free and compatible with CMOS processing, TMAH solutions are widely used in MEMS fabrication to release structures or form membranes. The etch rate and surface smoothness depend on concentration and temperature, and the etch strongly slows on heavily boron-doped silicon, which can serve as an etch stop.

What is the main reason organic alkalis such as TMAH, rather than sodium hydroxide, are used as semiconductor developers?

  1. ABecause TMAH has a stronger color
  2. BBecause TMAH is always cheaper
  3. CTo avoid contamination by metal ions such as sodium

Answer: C. To avoid contamination by metal ions such as sodium

Alkali metal ions such as sodium and potassium become mobile ions that drift through oxide under an electric field, shifting transistor threshold voltages. Sodium contamination was the main cause of instability in early MOS devices. TMAH is a metal-free organic alkali, so it poses no such risk. Color and price are not the reason; TMAH is not necessarily cheaper.

Why is skin contact with concentrated TMAH (tetramethylammonium hydroxide), used in developers, considered especially dangerous?

  1. ABecause it emits strong radiation
  2. BBecause it is absorbed through the skin and can interfere with nerve and muscle function, causing systemic poisoning such as respiratory arrest
  3. CBecause it instantly freezes and causes frostbite

Answer: B. Because it is absorbed through the skin and can interfere with nerve and muscle function, causing systemic poisoning such as respiratory arrest

TMAH not only burns skin as a strong alkali; tetramethylammonium ions absorbed through the skin block nerve-to-muscle signal transmission and can cause respiratory arrest. Concentrated solutions around 25% are far more dangerous than the 2.38% developer, and fatal accidents from skin contact have been reported in Taiwan and elsewhere. Protective equipment, immediate rinsing after contact and prompt medical care are essential.

FAQ

What concentration of TMAH is used as a photoresist developer?

The standard is 2.38 wt% TMAH in water, about 0.26 N, which gives reproducible development rates and is used by most fabs.

Why is TMAH used instead of NaOH as a developer?

NaOH leaves sodium ions that can migrate into gate oxide as mobile charge and shift threshold voltages. TMAH is a metal-free organic base, so it develops resist without that contamination.

Why is TMAH so dangerous on skin?

Besides causing alkaline burns, TMAH is absorbed through the skin and its tetramethylammonium ions block nerve-to-muscle signaling, which can cause respiratory failure, especially with concentrated solutions.
