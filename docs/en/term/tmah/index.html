What is TMAH?

Tetramethylammonium hydroxide, a metal-ion-free organic alkali used as a photoresist developer. Care is needed because it can cause poisoning through skin absorption.

Japanese name: TMAH

Try it: questions about TMAH

What is the main reason organic alkalis such as TMAH, rather than sodium hydroxide, are used as semiconductor developers?

  1. ABecause TMAH has a stronger color
  2. BBecause TMAH is always cheaper
  3. CTo avoid contamination by metal ions such as sodium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. To avoid contamination by metal ions such as sodium

Alkali metal ions such as sodium and potassium become mobile ions that drift through oxide under an electric field, shifting transistor threshold voltages. Sodium contamination was the main cause of instability in early MOS devices. TMAH is a metal-free organic alkali, so it poses no such risk. Color and price are not the reason; TMAH is not necessarily cheaper.

Why is skin contact with concentrated TMAH (tetramethylammonium hydroxide), used in developers, considered especially dangerous?

  1. ABecause it emits strong radiation
  2. BBecause it is absorbed through the skin and can interfere with nerve and muscle function, causing systemic poisoning such as respiratory arrest
  3. CBecause it instantly freezes and causes frostbite
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Because it is absorbed through the skin and can interfere with nerve and muscle function, causing systemic poisoning such as respiratory arrest

TMAH not only burns skin as a strong alkali; tetramethylammonium ions absorbed through the skin block nerve-to-muscle signal transmission and can cause respiratory arrest. Concentrated solutions around 25% are far more dangerous than the 2.38% developer, and fatal accidents from skin contact have been reported in Taiwan and elsewhere. Protective equipment, immediate rinsing after contact and prompt medical care are essential.
