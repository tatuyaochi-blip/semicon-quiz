What is Absolute maximum ratings?

Limits on voltage, current, temperature and so on that may damage or degrade the device if exceeded even momentarily. Staying within them does not guarantee correct operation; normal use is within the recommended operating conditions.

Japanese name: 絶対最大定格

What the table covers

Absolute maximum ratings are stress limits: supply voltage, voltage on input and output pins, input and output current, power dissipation, junction and storage temperature, and often ESD tolerance. Exceeding any of them, even momentarily, may cause permanent damage or gradual degradation. Staying inside them guarantees only survival, not correct function. For a 3.3 V part a typical supply entry might read −0.3 V to 4.6 V; pin limits a few tenths of a volt beyond the rails reflect internal protection diodes that begin to conduct there.

Absolute maximum vs. recommended operating conditions

Recommended operating conditions define the range in which the electrical characteristics table applies, for example 3.0 V to 3.6 V for that 3.3 V part. Between the recommended limit and the absolute maximum lies a zone where the device should not be damaged but timing, thresholds, accuracy and lifetime are not guaranteed. Designs should sit inside the recommended conditions with margin, and the absolute maximum ratings must hold even after adding tolerances, noise, ringing and surges.

Momentary overstress

Datasheets typically warn that stresses beyond the listed ratings may cause permanent damage, and this applies regardless of duration. A brief surge above the maximum can partially damage gate oxide, junctions or protection structures without immediate failure, appearing later as higher leakage current or shortened lifetime. A part that has been overstressed should be treated as outside its reliability guarantee and replaced, and the cause suppressed, for example with TVS diodes, filters or series resistors, before re-evaluation.

Easily overlooked traps

Many violations come from conditions that are easy to miss. Driving an input high while the chip itself is unpowered forces current through the input protection diode into its supply rail. Inductive kickback, hot-plugged cables and poor sequencing between multiple supply rails create brief overshoots. Separately, a signal can be within the absolute maximum ratings yet outside another specified range, such as an op-amp's common-mode input range; the part survives, but its output may distort or stick because correct operation is only promised inside that range.

Answer: B. Absolute maximum ratings are limits beyond which damage may occur; characteristics are guaranteed only within the recommended operating conditions

Absolute maximum ratings are limit values that, if exceeded even momentarily, may cause damage or degradation; staying within them only means the device will not be damaged—it does not guarantee correct operation. The values in the electrical characteristics table are guaranteed only within the recommended operating conditions. For example, a 3.3 V device might specify an absolute maximum supply voltage of −0.3 to 4.6 V and recommended operating conditions of 3.0 to 3.6 V. In design, use the device within the recommended operating conditions, and confirm that the absolute maximum ratings are not exceeded even including noise and surges.

FAQ

Can I operate a chip at its absolute maximum rating?

No. The absolute maximum is a damage threshold, not an operating point, and performance is not guaranteed there. Operate within the recommended operating conditions with margin.

What happens if an absolute maximum rating is exceeded for a short time?

The device may fail immediately, or it may keep working with hidden damage that later shows up as leakage or early failure. Treat such a part as unreliable, replace it and fix the cause of the overstress.
