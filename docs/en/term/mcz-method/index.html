What is MCZ method (magnetic-field-applied CZ)?

A CZ variant that applies a magnetic field to the crucible to suppress melt convection. It makes the oxygen concentration in the crystal easier to control and is used for large-diameter crystals.

Japanese name: MCZ法(磁場印加CZ法)

Which impurity is incorporated in relatively large amounts from the crucible into silicon crystals grown by the CZ method?

  1. AGold
  2. BOxygen
  3. CHelium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Oxygen

CZ silicon is melted in a quartz (SiO2) crucible, and oxygen dissolving from it enters the crystal at about 10¹⁸ cm⁻³. Oxygen pins dislocations, strengthening the wafer, and its precipitates act as gettering sites, but excess oxygen causes defects and thermal donors. Concentration is therefore controlled, for example with magnetic CZ (MCZ) that suppresses melt convection, and specified per application.

Which method applies a magnetic field to the crucible in the CZ method to suppress melt convection and control oxygen concentration and other properties?

  1. AFZ method
  2. BMCZ method (magnetic-field-applied CZ)
  3. CBridgman method
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. MCZ method (magnetic-field-applied CZ)

Molten silicon conducts electricity, so an applied magnetic field exerts a Lorentz force opposing melt motion and damps convection. Magnetic CZ (MCZ) thereby reduces oxygen transport from the crucible into the crystal and suppresses temperature fluctuations and growth striations, improving uniformity. With melts of hundreds of kg for 300 mm crystals, convection is vigorous, so transverse-field MCZ is standard.
