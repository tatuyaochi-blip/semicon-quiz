What is Power MOSFET?

A MOSFET built to handle large currents and voltages. It switches quickly and is widely used in power supply circuits and the like.

Japanese name: パワーMOSFET

Power MOSFET appears in 6 practice questions across 2 topics, including Power semiconductors, How to read datasheets.

Field: Power semiconductors

How it differs from a logic MOSFET

Most discrete power MOSFETs are vertical devices: source and gate are on top, the drain is the back of the chip, and current flows through the channel and then down through a lightly doped n-type drift region. That drift region holds the blocking voltage, and a higher voltage rating needs a thicker, more lightly doped drift layer, which raises on-resistance. Many small cells are connected in parallel to carry large current. The p-type body, tied to the source, forms a PN junction with the drift region, creating a built-in body diode that conducts from source to drain.

On-resistance and conduction loss

When fully on, the device behaves as a resistor, RDS(on), so conduction loss is I²R: 10 A through 10 mΩ dissipates 1 W, and doubling the current quadruples it. In silicon, RDS(on) rises with temperature as carrier mobility falls, typically to about 1.5–2 times the 25 °C value at 125 °C, so loss must be estimated from the datasheet's normalized RDS(on)-versus-temperature curve. This positive temperature coefficient helps paralleled devices share current, since a hotter device conducts less.

Switching and the Miller plateau

Turning the device on means charging its gate capacitances. During the transition, the drain voltage swings and the gate drive current is consumed charging the gate-drain (Miller) capacitance Cgd, so the gate voltage stalls on the Miller plateau. Most switching loss occurs in this interval, whose length is roughly the gate-drain charge Qgd divided by gate current, so low-Qgd devices and strong gate drivers switch faster. The gate oxide is thin, so gate-source voltage is typically rated around ±20 V.

Body diode, avalanche and safe operating area

In bridges the body diode can carry freewheeling current, but in silicon its slow reverse recovery causes loss, so designs add fast external diodes or use synchronous rectification. Interrupting an inductive load releases energy ½LI², driving the drain into avalanche breakdown; avalanche ruggedness, tested by unclamped inductive switching (UIS), matters for relay, solenoid and automotive drivers without clamps. The safe operating area (SOA) chart bounds allowed voltage-current combinations; most are drawn for a 25 °C case and single pulses, so they must be derated, especially for linear-mode uses such as inrush limiting.

It can serve as the freewheeling path in inverters, but in silicon MOSFETs its slow reverse recovery causes losses, so fast applications add external diodes or rely on synchronous rectification.

What is the conduction loss of a power MOSFET with an on-resistance of 10 mΩ carrying 10 A?

  1. A0.1 W
  2. B1 W
  3. C100 W
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1 W

Conduction loss is P = I²R: (10 A)² × 0.01 Ω = 1 W. Because it scales with current squared, doubling the current quadruples the loss, so at high currents small differences in on-resistance matter a lot. On-resistance also rises with temperature (about 1.5× at 100 °C), so real heating exceeds room-temperature estimates. Devices are often paralleled to reduce effective resistance.

FAQ

How do you calculate power MOSFET conduction loss?

Use P = I²·RDS(on), with RDS(on) taken at the expected junction temperature rather than the 25 °C datasheet headline value, since it can be 1.5–2 times higher when hot.

What is the body diode in a power MOSFET?

It is the parasitic PN junction between the source-connected p body and the n drift region, which conducts from source to drain. It can act as a freewheeling diode but has slow reverse recovery in silicon devices.

What is the Miller plateau?

The flat portion of the gate-voltage waveform during switching, when gate current goes into charging the gate-drain capacitance while the drain voltage transitions.
