What is XRD (X-ray diffraction)?

An analysis method that shines X-rays on a crystal and uses the angles and intensities of the diffracted beams to determine crystal structure, lattice constant, film strain and crystal quality.

Japanese name: XRD(X線回折)

Field: Metrology and analysis

How diffraction reveals lattice spacing

X-rays scattered by parallel planes of atoms reinforce one another only when the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths, which is Bragg's law, 2d·sinθ = nλ. Laboratory instruments commonly use copper Kα radiation with a wavelength of about 0.154 nm, comparable to atomic spacings. In a θ-2θ scan, peak positions give plane spacings d, which identify the crystal phase and lattice constant. Peak broadening reflects small crystallite size and non-uniform strain, and relative peak intensities reveal preferred orientation.

High-resolution XRD of epitaxial layers

High-resolution XRD (HRXRD) uses a conditioned, nearly monochromatic beam to resolve tiny angle differences. In an epitaxial stack such as SiGe on Si, the separation between the layer peak and the substrate peak gives the lattice mismatch, from which strain and composition follow. Interference fringes around the layer peak give its thickness. A rocking curve, scanning the sample angle with the detector fixed, measures crystal perfection: near-perfect silicon gives a width of a few arcseconds, while heavily dislocated GaN on sapphire gives hundreds.

Thin films and related methods

For polycrystalline thin films, grazing-incidence XRD keeps the beam near the surface to increase signal from the film rather than the substrate, and pole-figure measurements map texture, such as the preferred orientation of copper interconnect grains. Reciprocal space maps separate composition from strain relaxation in epitaxial layers. X-ray reflectivity (XRR), measured on the same instruments at very low angles, gives thickness, density and interface roughness even for amorphous films, since it relies on electron density contrast rather than crystal planes.

Strengths and limits

XRD is non-destructive, needs no vacuum or sample preparation in most cases, and averages over a spot typically millimetres across, giving good statistics but no nanometre-scale detail; transmission electron microscopy (TEM) is used for local structure. It requires crystalline order, so amorphous films show only broad humps. It is also distinct from X-ray fluorescence (XRF), which detects characteristic X-rays emitted by elements to measure composition rather than crystal structure.

Answer: A. That there is little tilt, mosaicity or defects, so the crystal quality is good

A rocking curve measures the intensity of a chosen diffraction peak while the sample angle is scanned with the detector fixed. Orientation spread (mosaicity) and dislocations broaden the peak, so a narrower full width at half maximum (FWHM) means better crystal quality—a few arcseconds for near-perfect silicon versus hundreds of arcseconds for GaN on sapphire. It is also used to estimate dislocation density.

  2. BSheet resistance
  3. CCrystal structure, lattice constants and film strain

Answer: C. Crystal structure, lattice constants and film strain

XRD determines crystal structure and lattice spacing from diffraction at atomic planes according to Bragg's law, 2d·sinθ = nλ. High-resolution XRD evaluates the composition and strain of epitaxial films such as SiGe from peak positions and crystal perfection from peak widths. For polycrystalline films it also reveals preferred orientation. It cannot measure organic contamination or sheet resistance.

FAQ

What is Bragg's law in X-ray diffraction?

Bragg's law, 2d·sinθ = nλ, states that X-rays reflected from atomic planes spaced d apart interfere constructively at angle θ when the path difference equals a whole number of wavelengths. Measuring the diffraction angle therefore gives the plane spacing.

What is the difference between XRD and XRF?

XRD measures diffraction angles to determine crystal structure, lattice constants and strain. XRF measures fluorescent X-rays emitted by atoms to identify which elements are present and how much.

What does a rocking curve FWHM tell you?

It measures the spread of crystal plane orientations in a layer. A narrow full width at half maximum indicates few dislocations and little mosaicity, while a broad one indicates a defective crystal.
