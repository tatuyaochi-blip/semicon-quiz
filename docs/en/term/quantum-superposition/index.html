What is Quantum superposition?

The ability of a quantum system such as a qubit to be in a state formed by adding several states, such as |0⟩ and |1⟩, with complex amplitudes. A measurement gives one of the outcomes at random, and differences in the phases of the amplitudes affect the results through interference.

Japanese name: 量子重ね合わせ

Try it: questions about Quantum superposition

A qubit is rotated with microwave pulses tuned to its resonance frequency, and the Rabi frequency (the frequency of one round trip between |0⟩ and |1⟩) is 10 MHz. What is the length of the π/2 pulse that takes |0⟩ to a superposition in which the amplitudes of |0⟩ and |1⟩ have equal magnitude?

  1. A25 ns
  2. B50 ns
  3. C100 ns
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 25 ns

Under continuous microwave driving, the probability of being in |1⟩ oscillates as sin²(Ωt/2) with Ω = 2π × 10 MHz, and the period of one round trip is 1 ÷ 10 MHz = 100 ns. The rotation angle Ωt reaches π/2 at t = 25 ns, when the probability of being in |1⟩ is 50%. A rotation angle of π (50 ns) is an X gate (π pulse) that swaps |0⟩ and |1⟩, and 2π (100 ns) returns the qubit to |0⟩. The rotation angle is set by the pulse amplitude and length, so errors in the microwave amplitude or timing translate directly into gate errors.
