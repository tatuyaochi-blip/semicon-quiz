What is Flip chip?

A mounting method in which the chip is placed face down and connected directly to the substrate with bumps. Short connections make it suitable for high pin counts.

Japanese name: フリップチップ

Field: Packaging and assembly

Flip-chip structure

In flip-chip mounting, small bumps are formed on the electrode pads of the chip's circuit side, and the chip is turned over (flipped) and connected directly to pads on a package substrate or another chip. An under-bump metallization (UBM) layer between pad and bump provides adhesion and stops solder from diffusing into the pad. Bumps can be solder balls, copper pillars capped with a little solder, or gold stud bumps. The gap between chip and substrate is filled with underfill resin, and a heat sink or heat spreader is attached to the back of the chip, which now faces up.

Flip chip versus wire bonding

Wire bonding runs fine metal wires one by one from pads arranged around the chip's edge, so the number of terminals is limited by the chip's perimeter, and the wire length adds inductance and resistance. Flip chip can place bumps in a grid across the whole chip area, allowing thousands of terminals with short connections. That favors high-speed signaling and high-current power delivery through many bumps. On the other hand, it needs wafer-level bumping and a substrate with fine wiring, so it costs more, and wire bonding is still used for more units overall. Flip chip traces back to the C4 process IBM developed in the 1960s.

The assembly process and flip-chip bonders

First, UBM deposition, bump plating and reflow are done while the chips are still on the wafer (wafer bumping), and the wafer is then diced into chips. A flip-chip bonder picks up each chip, flips it, aligns chip and substrate with cameras and places it. For solder bumps, the usual approach is mass reflow: chips are placed with flux and then heated together in a reflow oven, where the surface tension of molten solder also pulls each chip into position (self-alignment). For fine-pitch copper pillars and microbumps, thermocompression bonding (TCB) bonders heat and press each chip individually. Finally, underfill is dispensed and cured.

Applications and reliability

Large chips with many terminals, such as CPUs, GPUs, FPGAs and networking chips, are mostly packaged as FC-BGAs, flip-chip mounted on build-up substrates. Smaller FC-CSPs are used for smartphone processors and similar chips, and flip chip with microbumps also connects chips to interposers and to each other in stacks. The main reliability concerns are thermal fatigue, where the thermal expansion mismatch between silicon (about 3 ppm/K) and organic substrates (about 15–17 ppm/K) strains the bumps repeatedly, and electromigration in bumps carrying high current. Underfill and copper pillars, which spread current more evenly, address these.

Answer: B. A method that places the chip circuit side down and connects it directly to the substrate with bumps

In flip-chip packaging, the chip is turned face down and connected to the substrate through solder or copper-pillar bumps formed on its circuit side. The short connections have low inductance, benefiting high-speed signals and power delivery, and bumps can cover the whole die, enabling thousands of terminals. It originated with IBM's C4 technology in the 1960s. It has nothing to do with shipping unencapsulated chips or mounting them vertically.

  3. CTo block heat dissipation from the chip

Answer: A. To keep stress from thermal expansion mismatch between chip and substrate from concentrating on the bumps, improving connection reliability

Silicon expands at about 3 ppm/K while organic substrates expand at about 15–17 ppm/K, so each temperature cycle repeatedly strains the small bumps, risking fatigue cracks. Filling the chip–substrate gap with underfill bonds them together, spreading stress across all bumps and greatly extending joint life. Underfill is usually dispensed at the edge and drawn in by capillary action.

FAQ

What is the difference between flip chip and wire bonding?

Wire bonding places the chip face up and connects pads around its edge to the substrate with fine metal wires. Flip chip turns the chip over and connects it directly to the substrate through bumps spread across the circuit side. Flip chip allows more terminals and shorter connections, suiting fast, high-pin-count chips, but it costs more.

What is a flip-chip bonder?

It is a machine that picks up a diced chip, flips it, aligns it to the substrate pads with cameras and places it. Either the placed chips are soldered together afterward in a reflow oven, or the bonder head heats and presses each chip to join it one at a time (thermocompression bonding, TCB); the latter is used for fine-pitch connections.

What is a flip-chip package (FC-BGA)?

It is a BGA package in which the chip is flip-chip mounted on the package substrate and solder balls are arrayed in a grid on the substrate's underside. A build-up substrate with fine wiring layers fans out the connections from the chip's thousands of bumps to the wider pitch of a printed circuit board. FC-BGAs are widely used for processors and other high-performance chips.
