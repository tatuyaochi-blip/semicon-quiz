What is 128b/130b encoding?

Line coding that sends 128 data bits with a 2-bit header, about 98.5% efficient, relying on scrambling for transitions. Used from PCI Express 3.0 onward.

Japanese name: 128b/130b符号化

Try it: questions about 128b/130b encoding

Which is closest to the efficiency of the 128b/130b encoding adopted from PCI Express 3.0 onward?

  1. AAbout 80%
  2. BAbout 50%
  3. CAbout 98.5%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. About 98.5%

128b/130b encoding sends 128 data bits with a 2-bit header as 130 bits, so efficiency is 128/130 ≈ 98.5%, a big reduction in waste compared with 8b/10b's 80%. Transitions are ensured by scrambling the data (XOR with a fixed pseudo-random sequence) to mix 0s and 1s. This is why PCI Express 3.0 nearly doubled effective throughput while raising the signaling rate only from 5 to 8 GT/s.

What is closest to the effective one-direction bandwidth of a PCI Express 3.0 x16 link (8 GT/s per lane, 128b/130b encoding)?

  1. AAbout 128 GB/s
  2. BAbout 1 GB/s
  3. CAbout 16 GB/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. About 16 GB/s

The effective rate of one lane is 8 Gb/s × 128/130 ≈ 7.88 Gb/s, or about 0.985 GB/s after dividing by 8. Sixteen lanes give about 15.8 GB/s, roughly 16 GB/s in one direction, or about 32 GB/s for both directions together. PCI Express bandwidth is the product of the generation's per-lane rate, the coding efficiency and the lane count. Packet headers and other overhead make the real figure somewhat lower.
