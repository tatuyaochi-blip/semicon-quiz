What is Arithmetic shift?

A right shift of a signed integer that copies the sign bit into the vacated high bits. Shifting a negative number right by one divides by 2, rounding toward negative infinity. Filling with zeros is a logical shift.

Japanese name: 算術シフト

  1. A−7 / 2 is −3, and −7 >> 1 is −4
  2. BBoth are −4
  3. C−7 / 2 is −4, and −7 >> 1 is −3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. −7 / 2 is −3, and −7 >> 1 is −4

C (since C99) defines integer division as truncating toward zero, so −3.5 becomes −3. An arithmetic right shift instead rounds toward negative infinity: shifting −7 (1111 1001) right by one gives 1111 1100 = −4. For negative numbers, shifting and dividing therefore disagree, so when compilers replace division by 2 with a shift they add correction instructions for negatives. For positive numbers the two agree.
