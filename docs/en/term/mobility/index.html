What is Mobility?

How easily carriers move when an electric field is applied. In silicon, electron mobility is higher than hole mobility.

Japanese name: 移動度

Mobility appears in 67 practice questions across 16 topics, including Semiconductor physics, Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA), MOSFET.

Field: Semiconductor physics

How mobility is defined

At low electric fields a carrier's average drift velocity is proportional to the field, v = μE, and the constant μ is the mobility. Its unit follows directly: (cm/s) divided by (V/cm) gives cm²/(V·s); the SI unit m²/(V·s) equals 10⁴ cm²/(V·s). Physically, mobility is set by two things: how heavy the carrier appears inside the crystal (its effective mass) and how long it travels between scattering events. Lighter carriers and fewer collisions mean higher mobility. Mobility then enters conductivity as σ = q(nμn + pμp), so carrier density and mobility together decide resistivity.

What limits mobility

Two scattering mechanisms dominate in bulk silicon. Lattice vibrations (phonons) grow stronger with temperature, so in lightly doped silicon mobility falls roughly as T^(−1.5 to −2.5), which is the main reason MOSFET drive current drops when a chip runs hot. Ionized dopants scatter carriers by Coulomb attraction or repulsion, so mobility falls as doping rises; in compensated material every ionized donor and acceptor counts, not just the net doping. In a MOSFET channel, surface roughness and interface charge add further scattering and can cut mobility to half the bulk value or less.

Typical numbers

In lightly doped silicon at room temperature, electron mobility is about 1,400 cm²/(V·s) and hole mobility about 450, a ratio of roughly three caused mainly by the heavier hole effective mass. Electron mobility drops to around 100 cm²/(V·s) near 10¹⁹ cm⁻³ doping. GaAs electrons reach about 8,500 and InSb about 77,000 cm²/(V·s). The linear v = μE picture fails at high fields: for silicon electrons, velocity growth slows above roughly 10⁴ V/cm and saturates near 10⁷ cm/s, a regime that short MOSFET channels operate in.

Where mobility shows up in practice

Because holes are slower, a CMOS pMOS is usually drawn two to three times wider than its nMOS partner to balance rise and fall times, and advanced processes add compressive strain from SiGe to speed up pMOS holes. Through the Einstein relation D = μkT/q (kT/q ≈ 26 mV at room temperature), mobility also fixes the diffusion coefficient, about 36 cm²/s for electrons in silicon. Mobility is commonly measured by combining a Hall measurement, which gives carrier density, with resistivity, using μ = 1/(qnρ).

Which correctly compares electron and hole mobility in silicon?

  1. AElectron mobility is higher
  2. BHole mobility is higher
  3. CThey are always equal
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Electron mobility is higher

In silicon at room temperature and low doping, electron mobility is about 1400 cm²/V·s and hole mobility about 450 cm²/V·s, so electrons are about three times faster, mainly because holes have a larger effective mass. An nMOS of the same size therefore carries more current, and in CMOS the pMOS is typically made 2–3 times wider than the nMOS to balance drive strength. Advanced processes boost pMOS mobility with compressive strain from SiGe.

What is the drift velocity of electrons with a mobility of 1000 cm²/V·s in an electric field of 100 V/cm?

  1. A1×10³ cm/s
  2. B1×10⁵ cm/s
  3. C1×10⁷ cm/s
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 1×10⁵ cm/s

Drift velocity is v = μE, so 1000 cm²/V·s × 100 V/cm = 1×10⁵ cm/s. The units work out as cm²/V·s × V/cm = cm/s, which is a handy check. The proportionality holds only at low fields; for electrons in silicon the increase slows above roughly 10⁴ V/cm and the velocity saturates at about 1×10⁷ cm/s (velocity saturation). The channels of scaled MOSFETs operate in this high-field regime.

FAQ

Why is electron mobility higher than hole mobility in silicon?

Holes in silicon behave as if they had a larger effective mass than electrons, so the same field accelerates them less between collisions. At room temperature and low doping this gives roughly 1,400 versus 450 cm²/(V·s).

What is the difference between mobility and drift velocity?

Drift velocity is the actual average speed of carriers in a field, while mobility is the proportionality constant linking the two (v = μE). The relation holds only at low fields; at high fields the velocity saturates even as the field keeps rising.

Does mobility increase or decrease with temperature?

In lightly doped silicon near room temperature it decreases, because phonon scattering grows. In heavily doped material or at low temperature, ionized-impurity scattering dominates and weakens as temperature rises, so mobility can increase over that range.
