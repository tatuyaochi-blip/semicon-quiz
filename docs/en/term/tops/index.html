What is TOPS?

Trillions of operations per second, a performance figure for AI accelerators, usually a theoretical peak counted at low precision.

How a TOPS figure is calculated

TOPS means tera, or trillion, operations per second. For an accelerator it is normally the peak value: number of multiply-accumulate (MAC) units, times two operations per MAC (one multiply and one add), times clock frequency. An array of 4,096 INT8 MAC units at 1 GHz therefore gives about 8.2 TOPS. The figure assumes every unit does useful work on every cycle, which a real workload never fully achieves. It is a statement of hardware capacity, not of how fast any particular model will run.

What a TOPS number hides

Always check the conditions behind the number. It is usually quoted at INT8 or lower; the same hardware may claim twice the TOPS at INT4 and far fewer at FP16. Some figures assume structured sparsity, where skipping zero weights is counted as doing the operations. The number says nothing about memory bandwidth, on-chip buffer size, which operators the hardware supports, or how much work falls back to a CPU. For energy efficiency, TOPS per watt is quoted, but it depends heavily on voltage, clock and which parts of the chip are included in the power.

Peak versus achieved: the roofline view

Achievable performance is the smaller of peak compute and memory bandwidth multiplied by arithmetic intensity, the number of operations performed per byte moved. An accelerator rated at 100 TOPS with 100 GB/s of memory bandwidth needs about 1,000 operations per byte fetched to reach its peak. Layers with little data reuse, such as small-batch fully connected layers or token-by-token generation in language models, fall far short and are bandwidth-bound. Convolutions with high reuse come closer. This is why measured throughput on the target model is the only fair comparison.

TOPS versus FLOPS

FLOPS counts floating-point operations per second, while TOPS is mostly used for integer or mixed low-precision operations, though vendors sometimes quote TOPS for low-precision floating point as well. The two are not interchangeable even when the numbers match, because an FP16 operation does more work and costs more area than an INT8 one. When comparing devices, convert both to the same precision, note whether sparsity is assumed, and look for benchmark results measured under identical conditions, such as the same model, batch size and accuracy target.

FAQ

What does TOPS stand for?

Tera operations per second, meaning trillions of operations per second. For AI accelerators it is usually a theoretical peak counting each multiply-accumulate as two operations at a low precision such as INT8.

Does a higher TOPS rating mean faster AI performance?

Not necessarily. Real speed also depends on memory bandwidth, utilization of the compute units, supported operators and software, so a chip with fewer TOPS can outperform one with more on a given model.

How is TOPS different from TFLOPS?

TFLOPS counts floating-point operations, while TOPS usually counts integer or low-precision operations. Because the operation types differ, the numbers cannot be compared directly without stating the precision.
