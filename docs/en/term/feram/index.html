What is FeRAM (ferroelectric RAM)?

Nonvolatile memory that stores information in the polarization direction of a ferroelectric. Writes are fast and low-power, and it is used in IC cards and the like.

Japanese name: FeRAM(強誘電体メモリ)

Field: Emerging memories

How the cell stores a bit

A ferroelectric has a spontaneous electric polarization that can be flipped by an applied field and stays in place when the field is removed. Plotting polarization against field gives a hysteresis loop; the polarization left at zero field is the remanent polarization (Pr), and the field needed to flip it is the coercive field (Ec). FeRAM stores a 1 or 0 in the sign of that polarization, usually in a one-transistor, one-capacitor (1T1C) cell resembling DRAM but with a ferroelectric capacitor. The "Fe" stands for ferroelectric, not iron.

Reading is destructive

In capacitor-type FeRAM, a read pulses the cell toward a known polarization and senses the charge released onto the bit line. A cell whose polarization flips delivers noticeably more charge than one that does not, and a sense amplifier distinguishes the two. Because the read flips cells storing the opposite state, the data must be written back afterward, much as in DRAM. Every read therefore counts as a switching cycle. Unlike DRAM, though, the information lives in polarization rather than stored charge, so no refresh is needed and data survives power loss.

Where it fits

Writing simply flips polarization at a few volts, so FeRAM writes in tens of nanoseconds with little energy and endures about 10^12 cycles or more, far beyond flash. That suits contactless smart cards powered by harvested radio energy, meters and medical devices that log data frequently and must survive sudden power loss, and radiation-exposed equipment, since polarization is not easily upset by ionizing radiation. Its weakness is density: ferroelectric capacitors in traditional lead zirconate titanate (PZT) scale poorly, keeping capacities in the few-megabit range.

HfO2 ferroelectrics, FeFETs and FTJs

Around 2011, doped hafnium oxide was reported to become ferroelectric in a specific orthorhombic phase at about 10 nm thickness. Because HfO2 is already a standard high-k dielectric deposited by atomic layer deposition (ALD), this revived interest in embedding ferroelectric memory with advanced logic. The same films enable the ferroelectric FET (FeFET), where polarization shifts the transistor threshold voltage, and the ferroelectric tunnel junction (FTJ), where polarization changes tunneling resistance. Both read without flipping the polarization, avoiding the destructive read of capacitor FeRAM.

Answer: C. The direction of spontaneous (remanent) polarization of a ferroelectric

FeRAM uses the remanent polarization of ferroelectrics—polarization that persists after the voltage is removed—storing 0 or 1 in its direction. Writing simply flips the polarization, so no flash-style high voltage is needed; it is fast (tens of ns), low-power and endures 10¹² cycles or more. PZT (lead zirconate titanate) has been the traditional material. Conductive filaments are ReRAM, and magnetoresistance is MRAM.

Answer: A. Hafnium oxide (HfO2)-based materials

Hafnium oxide had long been used in CMOS as a high-k gate dielectric, but around 2011 it was reported that doping it with Si or Zr to form a specific (orthorhombic) phase makes it ferroelectric even at about 10 nm thickness. Traditional PZT contains lead and degrades when thinned, complicating CMOS integration, whereas HfO2-based films can be deposited thin and uniform by ALD, promising FeRAM and FeFETs embedded with advanced logic.

FAQ

Does FeRAM contain iron?

No. Fe stands for ferroelectric, a name borrowed from ferromagnetism because both show hysteresis; common materials are PZT, SBT and doped hafnium oxide.

Why is FeRAM read destructive?

Reading works by driving the cell toward one polarization and detecting whether it switches, which erases the original state of switched cells, so the data must be rewritten.

What is the difference between FeRAM and MRAM?

FeRAM stores bits as the electric polarization direction of a ferroelectric capacitor, while MRAM stores them as the magnetization direction of a magnetic tunnel junction and reads the resulting resistance.
