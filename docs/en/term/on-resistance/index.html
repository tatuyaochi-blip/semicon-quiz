What is On-resistance?

The resistance of the current path when a power device is on. The smaller it is, the lower the conduction loss.

Japanese name: オン抵抗

On-resistance appears in 17 practice questions across 9 topics, including Power semiconductors, MOSFET, PN junctions and diodes.

Field: Power semiconductors

What makes up RDS(on)

A power MOSFET's on-resistance, RDS(on), is a series chain: the inversion channel, the JFET region between cells in planar designs, the lightly doped drift layer that holds off voltage, the substrate, and finally contacts, bond wires and package. Low-voltage devices are dominated by channel, substrate and package; devices rated at hundreds of volts by the drift layer. The channel part falls as gate voltage rises, so datasheets quote RDS(on) at a stated gate drive and temperature. Technologies are compared by specific on-resistance, resistance times active area.

Conduction loss and temperature

While on, the switch dissipates P = I²R: 10 A through 10 mΩ gives 1 W, and doubling the current quadruples the loss. RDS(on) rises with temperature because carrier mobility falls; roughly 1.5 times the room-temperature value at 100 °C is a common rule of thumb, so thermal estimates should use hot values. This positive coefficient makes paralleling easy, since a hotter device carries less current. Near threshold, at low gate drive, the coefficient turns negative, so linear-mode operation or slow switching can still cause current crowding.

The breakdown-voltage trade-off

Blocking more voltage needs a thicker, more lightly doped drift layer, which is more resistive. For silicon unipolar devices the ideal specific on-resistance scales with about the 2.5th power of breakdown voltage, so doubling the rating raises it about 5.7 times: the silicon limit. Trench gates pack more channel width per area and remove the JFET resistance. Superjunction MOSFETs use alternating p and n pillars that deplete sideways, bringing 600 V-class devices well below the limit. SiC and GaN tolerate a much higher critical field, allowing thinner, more heavily doped drift regions.

On-resistance vs. on-state voltage

A conducting MOSFET operates in its linear region, where current is nearly proportional to VDS, so it behaves like a resistor. Bipolar transistors and IGBTs instead show an on-state voltage: a saturated BJT drops about 0.1–0.3 V, and an IGBT has a junction-related knee before its resistive slope. At low voltage, a small RDS(on) usually beats a fixed knee, so MOSFETs dominate. At several hundred volts and high current, the IGBT's conductivity modulation keeps the drop low where a silicon MOSFET's drift resistance would soar.

Answer: C. The source-drain resistance when the device is on; the smaller it is, the lower the loss

On-resistance RDS(on) is the drain-source resistance when the MOSFET conducts, and conduction loss is I²R—10 A through 10 mΩ dissipates 1 W. It decreases as gate voltage rises (more channel carriers) and increases with temperature (about 1.5× at 100 °C as a rule of thumb). Answer: C. On-resistance rises with temperature, so a device that heats up naturally carries less current and the current spreads out

A MOSFET's on-resistance increases with temperature because mobility drops (a positive temperature coefficient). If one of several paralleled devices heats up, its resistance rises and its current falls, shifting current to the others, so thermal runaway is unlikely and paralleling is easy. Near threshold, at low gate voltage, the coefficient turns negative, however, so linear-mode operation or slow switching can still cause current crowding.

FAQ

Why does MOSFET on-resistance increase with temperature?

Higher temperature increases lattice scattering, which lowers carrier mobility in both the channel and the drift layer. As a rough guide, RDS(on) at 100 °C is about 1.5 times the room-temperature value.

How do you calculate MOSFET conduction loss from RDS(on)?

Use P = I²·RDS(on) with the RMS current through the device and the on-resistance at the expected junction temperature. In a switching converter, the RMS current already accounts for the duty cycle.

Why do high-voltage MOSFETs have higher on-resistance?

A higher rating needs a thicker, more lightly doped drift layer, and for silicon the ideal on-resistance grows with about the 2.5th power of breakdown voltage. Superjunction structures and SiC or GaN devices are used to get around this.
