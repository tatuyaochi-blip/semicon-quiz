What is On-resistance?

The resistance of the current path when a power device is on. The smaller it is, the lower the conduction loss.

Japanese name: オン抵抗

Try it: questions about On-resistance

When a MOSFET is used as a switch, which statement correctly describes its on-resistance?

  1. AThe source-drain resistance when the device is off
  2. BThe resistance of the gate electrode itself
  3. CThe source-drain resistance when the device is on; the smaller it is, the lower the loss
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The source-drain resistance when the device is on; the smaller it is, the lower the loss

On-resistance RDS(on) is the drain-source resistance when the MOSFET conducts, and conduction loss is I²R—10 A through 10 mΩ dissipates 1 W. It decreases as gate voltage rises (more channel carriers) and increases with temperature (about 1.5× at 100 °C as a rule of thumb). In power MOSFETs, on-resistance rises steeply with voltage rating, so superjunction structures and SiC are used to break that trade-off.

Power MOSFETs are easy to use in parallel. Which statement about the temperature dependence of on-resistance explains why?

  1. AOn-resistance is independent of temperature
  2. BOn-resistance falls as temperature rises, so current concentrates further in the hottest device
  3. COn-resistance rises with temperature, so a device that heats up naturally carries less current and the current spreads out
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. On-resistance rises with temperature, so a device that heats up naturally carries less current and the current spreads out

A MOSFET's on-resistance increases with temperature because mobility drops (a positive temperature coefficient). If one of several paralleled devices heats up, its resistance rises and its current falls, shifting current to the others, so thermal runaway is unlikely and paralleling is easy. Near threshold, at low gate voltage, the coefficient turns negative, however, so linear-mode operation or slow switching can still cause current crowding.
