What is Air gap?

Leaving part of the insulator between wires as empty space. Since air has a relative permittivity of about 1, it greatly reduces capacitance between wires.

Japanese name: エアギャップ

Air gap appears in 1 practice question across 1 topic, including Interconnect processes.

Field: Interconnect processes

Why air between wires lowers capacitance

The capacitance between neighboring wires is proportional to the relative permittivity (k) of the material between them. Silicon dioxide has k of about 3.9, carbon-doped low-k films reach roughly 2.5 to 3, and air or vacuum sits at about 1, lower than any solid. Replacing part of the inter-wire dielectric with an air gap therefore cuts inter-wire capacitance by about 20 to 30% in practical structures, which reduces RC delay, dynamic switching power and crosstalk between adjacent signals.

How air gaps are formed

One approach finishes the copper lines first, removes the dielectric between them by etching, and then deposits a cap film with poor step coverage so that it pinches off at the top and seals a void below. Another deliberately deposits a dielectric that cannot fill narrow spaces conformally, leaving voids between closely spaced lines. Either way, the gap forms naturally only where lines are tight, which matches where capacitance matters most. Air-gap ideas are also applied to the spacers beside transistor gates to cut gate-to-contact capacitance.

Trade-offs that limit their use

A cavity weakens the wiring stack mechanically, which matters during CMP, wafer dicing and packaging stress. Air conducts heat poorly, so wires surrounded by gaps run hotter. A via landing from the level above must not punch into a gap, so layouts keep gaps away from via landing areas, sometimes with an extra mask. Moisture and process chemicals must not get trapped inside. For these reasons air gaps are typically used only on the dense, tight-pitch levels where capacitance reduction pays off most, while other levels keep solid low-k dielectric.

Intended air gaps vs. unwanted ones

Outside interconnect design, an air gap is usually a defect. In packages, delamination between mold compound and die or lead frame leaves a thin air layer that weakens the joint and can lead to cracking. Scanning acoustic microscopy (SAT) detects it non-destructively: solids and air differ so strongly in acoustic impedance that an air gap reflects nearly all of the ultrasound, making delaminations, cracks and voids stand out clearly. Unintended voids in metal or dielectric fill are similarly treated as defects.

Answer: B. To reduce capacitance between wires (air has a relative permittivity of about 1)

Air (vacuum) has a relative permittivity of about 1, lower than any dielectric, so air gaps between wires cut inter-wire capacitance by about 20–30%. They are formed by removing dielectric between finished lines and pinching off the top, or by deliberately leaving voids during deposition. FAQ

Why do air gaps reduce interconnect capacitance?

Capacitance scales with the permittivity of the material between conductors, and air has a relative permittivity of about 1, lower than any solid dielectric. Replacing part of the dielectric with air lowers inter-wire capacitance, typically by about 20 to 30%.

What is the difference between an air gap and a low-k dielectric?

A low-k dielectric is a solid film with reduced permittivity, typically around 2.5 to 3, that fills the space between wires. An air gap leaves part of that space empty, reaching lower effective capacitance but with weaker mechanical strength and heat conduction.

What are the disadvantages of air gaps?

They reduce mechanical strength, worsen heat dissipation, and create a risk that vias land in the cavity. These issues limit air gaps to the densest wiring levels.
