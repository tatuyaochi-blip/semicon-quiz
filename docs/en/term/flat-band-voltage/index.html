What is Flat-band voltage?

The gate voltage at which the bands at the semiconductor surface of a MOS structure are flat. It is set by the work-function difference between gate and substrate and by charges in the oxide, and is one component of the threshold voltage.

Japanese name: フラットバンド電圧

Field: MOSFET

Why the bands bend at zero bias

In a MOS structure the gate and the semiconductor usually have different work functions. In equilibrium their Fermi levels must line up, so the work-function difference φms appears as a potential split between the oxide and the semiconductor surface, bending the bands even with the gate grounded. Charge in the dielectric adds to this: fixed charge near the interface, mobile ions such as sodium, trapped charge and charged interface states all induce opposite charge in the semiconductor. The flat-band voltage VFB is the gate voltage that exactly cancels these effects, leaving the surface neither accumulated nor depleted.

The formula and its role in threshold voltage

For charge Qox located at the oxide-silicon interface, VFB = φms − Qox/Cox; charge distributed inside the oxide counts less the closer it sits to the gate. Threshold voltage builds on it: for an nMOS device, Vth = VFB + 2φF + Qdep/Cox, where φF is the Fermi potential and Qdep the depletion charge at strong inversion. Any shift in VFB therefore moves Vth one for one. For an n+ polysilicon gate on p-type silicon of moderate doping, φms is roughly −0.9 V, and positive oxide charge pushes VFB further negative.

Setting it with the gate metal

In high-k/metal-gate transistors, choosing the gate work function is the main lever on VFB and thus on Vth. nMOS devices need a work function near the silicon conduction band, around 4.1 eV, and pMOS devices one near the valence band, around 5.1 eV. Stacks of thin metal layers of different composition and thickness give several threshold flavors on one chip for high-speed and low-leakage circuits.

Measuring it with C-V

A capacitance-voltage (C-V) sweep of a MOS capacitor moves through accumulation, depletion and inversion. Accumulation capacitance gives Cox and hence oxide thickness, and VFB is read where the curve reaches the flat-band capacitance, the series combination of Cox and the semiconductor's capacitance at flat band. Comparing measured VFB with the expected φms gives the effective oxide charge. A VFB shift after bias-temperature stress reveals mobile ion contamination, while stretch-out of the curve indicates interface traps, which makes VFB a routine process-control check.

Answer: C. The gate voltage at which the bands at the semiconductor surface are not bent (are flat)

The flat-band voltage VFB is the gate voltage at which the semiconductor's bands at the surface are flat, given by VFB = φms − Qox/Cox (work-function difference and charge in the dielectric and at the interface). Threshold voltage is VFB plus 2φF and the voltage from depletion charge. Shifts in VFB seen in C-V measurements reveal oxide charge and contamination such as Na ions, making it an important process-control metric.

FAQ

What determines the flat-band voltage of a MOS capacitor?

The work-function difference between gate and semiconductor and the charges in the oxide and at the interface. For interface charge it is VFB = φms − Qox/Cox.

Is flat-band voltage the same as threshold voltage?

No. Flat band is the gate voltage where the surface bands are flat, while threshold is where strong inversion begins. Threshold equals VFB plus the voltage needed to bend the bands by 2φF and support the depletion charge.

How is flat-band voltage measured?

From a C-V measurement on a MOS capacitor: the gate voltage at which the capacitance equals the calculated flat-band capacitance is taken as VFB. Shifts in VFB reveal changes in oxide charge.
