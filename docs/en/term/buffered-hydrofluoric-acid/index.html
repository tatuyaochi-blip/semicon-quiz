What is Buffered hydrofluoric acid (BHF)?

Hydrofluoric acid buffered with ammonium fluoride. It is used for wet etching of SiO2 with a stable etch rate and little damage to resist.

Japanese name: バッファードフッ酸(BHF)

Buffered hydrofluoric acid (BHF) appears in 1 practice question across 1 topic, including Etching.

Field: Etching

Composition and buffering

Buffered hydrofluoric acid (BHF) is hydrofluoric acid (HF) buffered with ammonium fluoride (NH4F), and it is also called BOE (buffered oxide etch). Common mixtures combine roughly 40% NH4F solution and roughly 49% HF in volume ratios such as 6:1 or 10:1. Because NH4F replenishes fluoride ions, the balance between HF and HF2⁻ in the solution is maintained, and the concentration and pH change little even as the etch consumes acid. The main species that dissolves SiO2 is HF2⁻, and keeping its concentration stable is what keeps the etch rate stable.

Etch rate and selectivity

SiO2 reacts with hydrofluoric acid and ultimately dissolves as water-soluble hexafluorosilicic acid (H2SiF6). The etch rate of thermal oxide in BHF varies with composition and temperature, roughly tens of nanometers to about 100 nm per minute. Oxides deposited by CVD and glasses containing phosphorus or boron dissolve faster than thermal oxide. Silicon, by contrast, hardly dissolves, and silicon nitride dissolves much more slowly than oxide, so the oxide can be removed selectively. Being a wet etch, it proceeds isotropically, so care is needed with lateral etching under the mask (undercut).

Versus dilute HF (DHF)

Dilute HF (DHF) is hydrofluoric acid simply diluted with water; as the etch consumes acid, the concentration drops and the rate tends to change. BHF is buffered and has a somewhat higher pH, so its rate is stable and photoresist is less likely to lift off, which is why it has been used to pattern oxide with a resist mask. On the other hand, dilute HF is often used to strip very thin oxide in a short time, as in pre-gate-oxidation cleaning and native-oxide removal. After either treatment, the silicon surface is hydrogen-terminated and repels water.

Uses and handling

Besides patterning oxide, BHF is used in MEMS to dissolve sacrificial oxide layers and release structures, and to evaluate the quality of deposited oxide films. The ratio of a CVD film's etch rate to that of thermal oxide serves as an indicator of film density. In handling, like hydrofluoric acid, it can penetrate the skin and bind calcium in the body, with pain that may appear only later, so chemical-resistant gloves and protective equipment are always worn. In case of contact, rinse immediately with plenty of water, apply treatment such as calcium gluconate gel, and see a doctor.

Answer: C. Buffered hydrofluoric acid (BHF)

Buffered hydrofluoric acid (BHF) adds ammonium fluoride to HF to stabilize pH, so the concentration of the main etching species (HF2⁻) changes little as the bath is consumed, keeping etch rates steady and resist intact. It etches thermal oxide at tens to about 100 nm per minute and has been used for oxide patterning, native-oxide removal and film-quality evaluation.

FAQ

What is buffered hydrofluoric acid?

It is hydrofluoric acid buffered with ammonium fluoride, also called BOE. It is used for wet etching of SiO2, and its etch rate changes little as the solution is consumed, while resist is less likely to lift off.

What is the etch rate of BHF?

It depends on composition and temperature, but tens of nanometers to about 100 nm per minute for thermal oxide is a rough guide. CVD-deposited oxide etches faster, and silicon hardly dissolves. Confirm exact values by measuring with the actual solution and film.

What is the difference between BHF and dilute HF (DHF)?

DHF is hydrofluoric acid simply diluted with water; its rate tends to change as it is consumed, and it is often used to remove very thin native oxide. BHF is buffered with ammonium fluoride, so its rate is stable, making it well suited to patterning oxide with a resist mask.
