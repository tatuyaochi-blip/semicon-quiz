What is FD-SOI (fully depleted SOI)?

Building transistors in a very thin silicon layer on buried oxide so the channel is fully depleted. The channel can be lightly doped, and body bias can tune the threshold voltage easily.

Japanese name: FD-SOI(完全空乏型SOI)

Field: Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA)

What fully depleted means

In silicon on insulator (SOI), transistors sit in a silicon film above a buried oxide (BOX). If the film is thicker than the depletion region the gate creates, a neutral, electrically floating body remains underneath; this is partially depleted SOI (PD-SOI), which suffers floating-body effects such as the kink in its output curves. In FD-SOI the film is only about 5–7 nm thick, so the whole film is depleted. There is no floating neutral region, and no current path exists far from the gate.

Undoped channels and lower variability

Because the thin film itself suppresses short-channel effects, FD-SOI does not need heavy channel doping to keep the drain from controlling the channel. An undoped or lightly doped channel avoids random dopant fluctuation, the statistical spread in the number and position of dopant atoms that scatters threshold voltage by tens of millivolts in small bulk transistors. Threshold voltage is instead set mainly by the gate work function. Less impurity scattering also helps carrier mobility, and matching between neighboring transistors improves, which benefits SRAM and analog circuits.

Body biasing through a thin BOX

The BOX in FD-SOI is thin, around 20 nm, so the doped substrate region below it acts as a second, back gate. Applying a voltage there shifts the threshold voltage: forward body bias lowers it for speed, and reverse body bias raises it to cut leakage. Because the BOX insulates the channel from the well, the bias range is wider than in bulk CMOS, where forward bias is limited by junction turn-on. Designers can change bias at run time, trading speed and leakage between active and standby modes.

FD-SOI vs. FinFET

Both are fully depleted devices. A FinFET wraps the gate around a tall vertical fin on three sides, giving more drive current per footprint and better scaling to the most advanced nodes. FD-SOI stays planar, with a simpler process and fewer masks, and offers body biasing, which FinFETs largely lack. The trade-offs are more expensive SOI wafers, typically made by Smart Cut bonding and splitting, and the need for very uniform film thickness. FD-SOI suits low-power IoT, automotive and RF chips rather than top-performance processors.

Answer: C. The channel can be lightly doped, threshold voltage variation is reduced, and the threshold is easy to adjust with body bias

In FD-SOI, an extremely thin silicon layer of about 5–7 nm is fully depleted, so short-channel effects are controlled without channel doping and random-dopant variability is small. Applying voltage from the substrate through a thin buried oxide (about 20 nm) shifts the threshold dynamically, making FD-SOI attractive for low-voltage, low-power IoT, automotive and RF applications.

FAQ

What is the difference between FD-SOI and PD-SOI?

In FD-SOI the silicon film is so thin that it is completely depleted, so there is no floating neutral body. PD-SOI uses a thicker film that leaves a neutral region under the channel, causing floating-body effects such as the kink effect.

What is body biasing in FD-SOI?

It is applying a voltage to the substrate under the thin buried oxide so it acts like a back gate, lowering the threshold voltage for speed (forward bias) or raising it to reduce leakage (reverse bias).

Is FD-SOI better than FinFET?

Neither is better overall. FinFETs give higher performance and density at advanced nodes, while FD-SOI offers a simpler planar process, wide body-bias tuning and good RF and low-power behavior.
