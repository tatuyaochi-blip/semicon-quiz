What is Lead frame?

A thin metal sheet combining the pad that holds the chip with the external terminals (leads). It is used in packages such as QFP.

Japanese name: リードフレーム

Lead frame appears in 5 practice questions across 2 topics, including Packaging and assembly, Metrology and analysis.

Field: Packaging and assembly

Anatomy of a lead frame

A lead frame is stamped or etched from a thin metal sheet, roughly 0.1 to 0.25 mm thick, as a strip holding many units. Each unit has a central die pad (paddle) held by tie bars, inner leads that fan in toward the die, outer leads that become the package pins, and dam bars that keep the leads in place and stop mold resin from escaping. Rails along the strip carry indexing holes so assembly machines can position each unit precisely.

From strip to package

Assembly starts with die bonding, which fixes the chip to the die pad with silver paste, solder or die-attach film. Wire bonding then connects each chip pad to an inner lead with fine gold or copper wire. The strip is encapsulated in epoxy molding compound, after which trim and form cuts away the dam bars and rails and bends the outer leads into gull-wing or J shapes. In leadless packages such as QFN (quad flat no-lead), the leads stay flat on the bottom and units are separated by sawing.

Copper alloy vs. Alloy 42

Copper alloys conduct electricity and heat far better and are the usual choice, especially for power and thermally demanding parts, but their thermal expansion (about 17 ppm/K) is much larger than silicon's (about 2.6 ppm/K). Alloy 42, an iron-nickel alloy with about 42% nickel, expands only around 4 to 5 ppm/K, close to silicon, which reduces stress on large dies, but it is a poor heat conductor. Frames are plated to aid bonding and soldering, for example silver on bond areas and tin or nickel-palladium-gold on the leads.

Lead frames vs. substrates

Lead-frame packages such as SOP, QFP, QFN and TO-type power packages are cheap and robust, and a QFN's exposed die pad soldered to the board makes an efficient heat path. Their limitation is that all leads sit on the perimeter, so pin count and pitch are bounded. High-pin-count chips instead use ball grid arrays (BGAs) on organic laminate substrates with area-array connections. Reliability concerns for lead frames include mold-compound delamination from the pad or leads, typically inspected with a scanning acoustic microscope after moisture-soak reflow testing.

Answer: C. Die bonding

Die bonding fixes a singulated chip to a lead frame or substrate using adhesives such as silver paste, solder or die-attach film. Power devices with high current and heat use solder or sintered silver for thermal and electrical conductivity, while stacked memory uses film for uniform thickness. Wire bonding then makes the electrical connections. Dicing is the step that separates the chips.

FAQ

What is a lead frame made of?

Usually a copper alloy, chosen for its high electrical and thermal conductivity, or Alloy 42 (iron with about 42% nickel), chosen because its thermal expansion is close to silicon's. The frame is plated to make wire bonding and soldering reliable.

What is the difference between a lead frame and a package substrate?

A lead frame is a single patterned metal sheet whose leads form the external terminals around the package edge. A substrate is a multilayer wiring board that routes signals to an area array of solder balls, supporting far more connections.

Why do QFN packages have an exposed pad on the bottom?

The exposed pad is the lead frame's die pad. Soldering it to a copper area on the circuit board gives a short, low-resistance path for heat and ground current.
