What is TMR ratio?

In an MTJ, the difference between the resistances in the parallel and antiparallel states divided by the parallel-state resistance. The larger it is, the easier it is to tell 0 from 1; MgO barriers give large values.

Japanese name: TMR比

How is data read from MRAM?

  1. AA small current is passed through the MTJ and its high or low resistance is detected
  2. BA strong magnetic field is applied and the response observed
  3. CLight is shone on it and the reflection measured
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. A small current is passed through the MTJ and its high or low resistance is detected

MRAM is read by passing a current smaller than the write current through the MTJ and comparing against a reference to detect parallel (low resistance) or antiparallel (high resistance) states as a current or voltage difference. Even with a TMR ratio of 100–200%, the resistances differ by only 2–3×, giving smaller read margins than DRAM or flash. Reading does not flip the magnetization, so no write-back is needed.

Which correctly describes the tunnel magnetoresistance (TMR) ratio of an MTJ?

  1. AThe difference between the antiparallel and parallel resistances divided by the parallel resistance
  2. BThe ratio of insulator thickness to magnetic layer thickness
  3. CThe maximum current that can flow through the MTJ
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. The difference between the antiparallel and parallel resistances divided by the parallel resistance

The TMR ratio is (R_antiparallel − R_parallel)/R_parallel, expressing how different the two resistance states are; with R_parallel = 2 kΩ and R_antiparallel = 5 kΩ it is 150%. A higher ratio makes 0 and 1 easier to distinguish and reads faster. Values above 600% at room temperature have been reported with MgO barriers, but production MRAM runs around 100–200% given small cell size and thermal-budget constraints.
