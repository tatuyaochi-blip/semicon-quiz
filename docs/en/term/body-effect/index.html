What is Body effect?

An increase in threshold voltage when reverse bias is applied between source and substrate (body), because the depletion charge grows. Also called the substrate bias effect; it is sometimes used to tune the threshold.

Japanese name: 基板バイアス効果

Try it: questions about Body effect

In an n-channel MOSFET, what happens to the threshold voltage when a negative voltage is applied to the substrate (body) relative to the source (body bias)?

  1. AIt rises
  2. BIt falls
  3. CIt does not change
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It rises

Reverse-biasing the source-to-body junction widens the depletion region under the gate and increases its charge, so a larger gate voltage is needed to compensate—the threshold voltage rises (the body effect). In stacked transistors the upper devices see raised source potentials, so their Vth increases and they slow down. Conversely, adjusting body voltage to tune Vth is used to cut leakage.

Which is a characteristic of a MOSFET source follower (common-drain) circuit?

  1. AIts voltage gain is slightly less than 1 and its output impedance is low
  2. BIts voltage gain is very large and its output is inverted
  3. CIts current gain is zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Its voltage gain is slightly less than 1 and its output impedance is low

In a source follower the source tracks the gate minus VGS, so the voltage gain is slightly below 1 (gmRs/(1 + gmRs)) and non-inverting; the body effect lowers it further. The input impedance is essentially infinite (it is a gate), and the output impedance is low, about 1/gm, so it serves as a buffer. It is the counterpart of the emitter follower, but its level shift (VGS) is larger than VBE and varies more.
