What is Miss penalty?

The extra time needed to fetch data from the next level when a cache miss occurs, determined by the lower level's access time and the line transfer time.

Japanese name: ミスペナルティ

Try it: questions about Miss penalty

With a cache hit time of 2 cycles, a miss rate of 4% and a miss penalty of 150 cycles, what is the average memory access time in cycles?

  1. A8 cycles
  2. B6 cycles
  3. C152 cycles
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 8 cycles

Average memory access time = hit time + miss rate × miss penalty = 2 + 0.04 × 150 = 2 + 6 = 8 cycles. The hit time applies to every access, and the miss penalty is added only on misses. 6 cycles omits the hit time, counting only the misses. 152 cycles is the time for a single access that misses, not the average. Even a 4% miss rate quadruples the average.

A processor has an average CPI of 1.0 with a perfect cache. Each instruction makes 1.3 memory accesses on average, with a 2% miss rate and a 100-cycle miss penalty. What is the actual average CPI?

  1. A3.0
  2. B3.6
  3. C1.3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 3.6

Memory stall cycles per instruction are 1.3 accesses × 0.02 × 100 cycles = 2.6 cycles. Adding them to the ideal CPI gives 1.0 + 2.6 = 3.6, so performance falls to 1/3.6 of ideal. 3.0 wrongly assumes one access per instruction (1.0 + 1 × 0.02 × 100). 1.3 is the number of accesses per instruction, not the CPI. Even a small miss rate badly hurts performance when the miss penalty is large.
