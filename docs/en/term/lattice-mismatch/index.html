What is Lattice mismatch?

A difference in the atomic spacing (lattice constant) between a grown film and its substrate. A large mismatch causes strain and defects such as dislocations.

Japanese name: 格子不整合

Lattice mismatch appears in 3 practice questions across 3 topics, including Epitaxial growth, Compound semiconductors, Advanced transistors (FinFET and GAA).

Field: Epitaxial growth

What problem tends to occur when there is a large difference in lattice constant (lattice mismatch) between the substrate and the grown film?

  1. ACrystal defects such as misfit dislocations form
  2. BThe film always becomes amorphous
  3. CThe growth rate becomes zero
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Crystal defects such as misfit dislocations form

Growing a material with a different lattice constant forces the film to compress or stretch to match the substrate, storing strain; beyond the critical thickness, misfit dislocations form at the interface to relieve it. Some thread up through the film, causing leakage, reduced light output and shorter lifetime. Remedies include graded buffer layers, superlattices that bend dislocations and selective-area growth.
