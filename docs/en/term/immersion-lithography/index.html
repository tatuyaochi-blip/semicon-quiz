What is Immersion lithography?

Exposure with the space between the lens and the wafer filled with water. It allows a numerical aperture above 1, so ArF excimer lasers can form finer patterns.

Japanese name: 液浸露光

Immersion lithography appears in 3 practice questions across 2 topics, including Lithography, Process materials (gases and chemicals).

Field: Lithography

Why water raises the numerical aperture

The numerical aperture (NA) of a projection lens is NA = n·sinθ, where n is the refractive index of the medium between the final lens element and the wafer and θ is the largest ray angle. In air n is about 1, so NA cannot exceed 1, and practical dry ArF (193 nm argon fluoride laser) scanners stopped a little above 0.9. Filling the gap of roughly 1 mm with ultrapure water, whose index is about 1.44 at 193 nm, lets steeper rays reach the resist, and NA up to about 1.35 becomes possible.

What resolution it buys

Resolution follows the Rayleigh-type relation half-pitch = k1·λ/NA. With λ = 193 nm and NA = 1.35, and k1 in its practical single-exposure range of about 0.27 to 0.3 (the theoretical floor is 0.25), the smallest half-pitch is roughly 38 to 43 nm. Below that pitch, immersion ArF is combined with multiple-patterning schemes such as self-aligned double patterning. EUV (extreme ultraviolet, 13.5 nm) lithography reaches finer pitches by changing the wavelength instead, and since it runs in vacuum with mirrors, it cannot use a liquid at all.

Engineering the water layer

The water must stay under the lens while the wafer stage scans at high speed, so a nozzle assembly (often called an immersion hood) continuously supplies and recovers it over only a local area. The water is degassed and tightly temperature-controlled because its refractive index changes with temperature, and bubbles or droplets left behind cause defects. Water touching the resist can leach out components such as the photoacid generator, contaminating the lens, or soak in and distort the pattern. A hydrophobic topcoat over the resist, or a resist formulated to be water-repellent without a topcoat, prevents both and lets the meniscus slide cleanly.

Polarization at high NA

At NA 1.35, rays in the water travel at about 70° from the normal. When two such beams interfere, the electric-field components perpendicular to the plane of incidence (TE polarization) still interfere fully, but components in the plane of incidence (TM polarization) point in increasingly different directions and partly cancel. TM light therefore lowers image contrast. High-NA immersion scanners use polarized illumination arranged to be TE relative to the line orientation being printed, and mask and resist models must include polarization effects that could be ignored at low NA.

Which correctly describes ArF immersion lithography?

  1. AThe whole wafer is submerged in water for development
  2. BThe resist is diluted with water before coating
  3. CThe space between the projection lens and the wafer is filled with water to increase the numerical aperture (NA)

Answer: C. The space between the projection lens and the wafer is filled with water to increase the numerical aperture (NA)

Immersion lithography fills the roughly 1 mm gap between the final lens and wafer with ultrapure water (refractive index about 1.44 at 193 nm) instead of air (about 1). Since NA = n·sinθ, NA can exceed 1 (up to 1.35), resolving finer patterns at the same 193 nm wavelength. Introduced to production around 2006, it required nozzles that hold water in place under fast wafer motion and resist topcoats that resist water penetration.

In immersion lithography with very high numerical aperture (NA), why does light polarization need to be considered?

  1. ABecause polarization changes the wavelength of light
  2. BBecause polarization changes resist thickness
  3. CBecause light arrives at large angles, the strength of interference (image contrast) depends on the polarization direction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Because light arrives at large angles, the strength of interference (image contrast) depends on the polarization direction

High NA means steep incidence angles—about 70° in water at NA 1.35. When the electric fields of interfering beams are perpendicular to the plane of incidence (TE polarization) they interfere strongly, but when parallel (TM polarization) larger angles increasingly cancel, reducing image contrast. Immersion lithography therefore uses polarized illumination controlled to be TE relative to the line orientation.

FAQ

How can numerical aperture be greater than 1 in immersion lithography?

NA equals the refractive index of the medium times the sine of the ray angle. With water (n about 1.44) instead of air between lens and wafer, the product can exceed 1, reaching about 1.35 in practice.

Why is EUV lithography not done with immersion?

EUV light is absorbed strongly by almost all materials, including water, so EUV tools operate in vacuum with reflective mirror optics. They improve resolution by using a 13.5 nm wavelength rather than a higher-index medium.

What is a topcoat in immersion lithography?

A thin protective film coated over the photoresist that stops resist components from leaching into the water and water from soaking into the resist, while keeping the surface hydrophobic so no droplets are left during fast scanning.
