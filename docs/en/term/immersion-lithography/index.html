What is Immersion lithography?

Exposure with the space between the lens and the wafer filled with water. It allows a numerical aperture above 1, so ArF excimer lasers can form finer patterns.

Japanese name: 液浸露光

Try it: questions about Immersion lithography

Which correctly describes ArF immersion lithography?

  1. AThe whole wafer is submerged in water for development
  2. BThe resist is diluted with water before coating
  3. CThe space between the projection lens and the wafer is filled with water to increase the numerical aperture (NA)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The space between the projection lens and the wafer is filled with water to increase the numerical aperture (NA)

Immersion lithography fills the roughly 1 mm gap between the final lens and wafer with ultrapure water (refractive index about 1.44 at 193 nm) instead of air (about 1). Since NA = n·sinθ, NA can exceed 1 (up to 1.35), resolving finer patterns at the same 193 nm wavelength. Introduced to production around 2006, it required nozzles that hold water in place under fast wafer motion and resist topcoats that resist water penetration.

In immersion lithography with very high numerical aperture (NA), why does light polarization need to be considered?

  1. ABecause polarization changes the wavelength of light
  2. BBecause polarization changes resist thickness
  3. CBecause light arrives at large angles, the strength of interference (image contrast) depends on the polarization direction
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Because light arrives at large angles, the strength of interference (image contrast) depends on the polarization direction

High NA means steep incidence angles—about 70° in water at NA 1.35. When the electric fields of interfering beams are perpendicular to the plane of incidence (TE polarization) they interfere strongly, but when parallel (TM polarization) larger angles increasingly cancel, reducing image contrast. Immersion lithography therefore uses polarized illumination controlled to be TE relative to the line orientation.
