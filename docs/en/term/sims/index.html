What is SIMS (secondary ion mass spectrometry)?

A method that sputters a sample with primary ions and mass-analyzes the secondary ions to measure impurity depth profiles with high sensitivity.

Japanese name: SIMS(二次イオン質量分析法)

SIMS (secondary ion mass spectrometry) appears in 7 practice questions across 1 topic, including Metrology and analysis.

Field: Metrology and analysis

How SIMS measures a depth profile

Secondary ion mass spectrometry (SIMS) bombards the sample with a focused primary ion beam, typically cesium or oxygen, which sputters away atoms layer by layer. A small fraction of the sputtered atoms leave as ions, and these secondary ions are separated by mass in a magnetic-sector, quadrupole or time-of-flight analyzer. Recording selected masses while the crater deepens gives intensity versus sputter time. Measuring the final crater depth, for example with a stylus profiler, converts time to depth, and reference samples with known implanted doses convert intensity to concentration.

Sensitivity, the matrix effect and depth resolution

SIMS is among the most sensitive routine techniques, reaching around parts per billion for favorable elements, far beyond EDX at about 0.1 wt%. The choice of primary beam matters: oxygen enhances positive-ion yields of electropositive elements such as boron, while cesium enhances negative-ion yields of electronegative ones. Ion yields depend strongly on the surrounding material, the matrix effect, so quantification requires standards in the same matrix and profiles near interfaces need care. Depth resolution is limited by ion-beam mixing and crater roughness, so ultra-shallow junctions use low-energy primary beams.

SIMS vs. spreading resistance and other methods

SIMS counts all atoms of an element, whether or not they sit on lattice sites and contribute carriers. Spreading resistance profiling (SRP) bevels the sample at a shallow angle and steps probes along the bevel to measure resistance, giving the electrically active carrier concentration versus depth. Comparing the two profiles reveals how much of an implant was activated by annealing. SIMS is destructive because it consumes the measured area, in contrast to non-destructive methods such as X-ray reflectometry for film thickness or scatterometry for dimensions.

Where you will meet it

SIMS is the standard check for ion-implantation profiles and doses, dopant diffusion after anneals, junction depth, and dopant segregation at interfaces. It also detects contaminants and light elements such as hydrogen, carbon, oxygen and nitrogen in films and at interfaces, which most other techniques cannot quantify at low levels. The depth-profiling mode described here is often called dynamic SIMS; a low-dose variant, often with time-of-flight analyzers, probes only the outermost monolayers for surface contamination and organic residues.

Answer: C. The sample is sputtered with primary ions while the emitted secondary ions are mass-analyzed to determine the depth distribution of impurities

SIMS bombards the surface with primary ions such as cesium or oxygen, gradually sputtering it, and mass-analyzes the ejected secondary ions. Continuing as it erodes yields depth profiles of dopant concentration, with sensitivity down to around ppb for some elements. It is widely used to evaluate implantation and diffusion, but it is destructive since it consumes the sample. Identifying elements by characteristic X-rays is EDX, and measuring thickness from reflectance is an optical method.

Whereas SIMS measures the atomic concentration of impurities, which method measures the depth distribution of electrically active carrier concentration?

  1. AEDX
  2. BAtomic force microscopy (AFM)
  3. CElectrical measurements such as spreading resistance profiling (SRP)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Electrical measurements such as spreading resistance profiling (SRP)

Spreading resistance profiling bevels the sample at a shallow angle to stretch out the depth axis, then steps two probes along the bevel measuring resistance, yielding the depth profile of carrier concentration. SIMS measures total atomic concentration including inactive dopants, so comparing the two reveals the activated fraction. EDX identifies elements and AFM measures shape.

FAQ

Is SIMS a destructive technique?

Yes. The primary ion beam sputters away the material being analyzed, leaving a crater, so the measured area cannot be used as product.

What is the difference between SIMS and SRP?

SIMS measures the total atomic concentration of a dopant regardless of whether it is electrically active. SRP measures the concentration of active carriers, so comparing the two shows the activation fraction.

Why does SIMS use cesium or oxygen primary beams?

Oxygen raises the yield of positive secondary ions and cesium raises the yield of negative ones, so the beam is chosen to maximize sensitivity for the element of interest, for example oxygen for boron.
