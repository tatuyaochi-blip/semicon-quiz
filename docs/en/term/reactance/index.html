What is Reactance?

The imaginary part of impedance: how strongly a coil or capacitor opposes an AC current [Ω]. A coil has XL = ωL = 2πfL, which grows in proportion to frequency, and a capacitor has XC = 1/(ωC) = 1/(2πfC), which shrinks in inverse proportion to frequency. For example, a 1 µF capacitor has about 159 Ω at 1 kHz. Unlike resistance it consumes no power, and it shifts the phase between voltage and current by 90°.

Try it: questions about Reactance

Which is closest to the reactance of a 1 µF capacitor at a frequency of 1 kHz?

  1. AAbout 6.3 mΩ
  2. BAbout 159 Ω
  3. C1 kΩ
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. About 159 Ω

Capacitive reactance is Xc = 1/(2πfC). Since 2π × 1×10³ × 1×10⁻⁶ ≈ 6.28×10⁻³, Xc ≈ 1/(6.28×10⁻³) ≈ 159 Ω. At ten times the frequency, 10 kHz, it drops to about 15.9 Ω—inversely proportional to frequency. An inductor's reactance, XL = 2πfL, is instead proportional to frequency.

What is the magnitude of the impedance of a circuit with a resistor R = 30 Ω in series with a coil of reactance XL = 40 Ω? (Ignore the coil's resistance.)

  1. A70 Ω
  2. B10 Ω
  3. C50 Ω
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 50 Ω

The voltages across a resistor and an inductor are 90° out of phase, so they are combined as complex numbers (vectors), not added directly. Z = R + jXL = 30 + j40 Ω, with magnitude √(30² + 40²) = √2500 = 50 Ω. The current lags the voltage by φ = tan⁻¹(40/30) ≈ 53°, and the power factor is cos φ = 30/50 = 0.6.
