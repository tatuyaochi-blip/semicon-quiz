What is Propagation loss?

The fraction of optical power lost to scattering and absorption as light travels through a waveguide or fiber, expressed in dB/cm or dB/km. In silicon waveguides the main cause is scattering from sidewall roughness, with typical values around 1 to 3 dB/cm, far higher than fiber's roughly 0.2 dB/km.

Japanese name: 伝搬損失

Try it: questions about Propagation loss

When light travels 5 mm through a silicon waveguide with a propagation loss of 2 dB/cm, which is closest to the fraction of optical power remaining at the output?

  1. AAbout 79%
  2. BAbout 90%
  3. CAbout 50%
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. About 79%

5 mm is 0.5 cm, so the loss is 2 dB/cm × 0.5 cm = 1 dB. The loss L in dB and the transmittance T are related by T = 10^(−L/10); 10^(−0.1) ≈ 0.79, so about 79% remains. It is handy to remember that 3 dB is roughly half and 10 dB is one-tenth. About 90% corresponds to a loss of 0.5 dB, and about 50% to a loss of 3 dB. Using dB turns the multiplication of each component's transmittance into addition, making it easy to total the losses of multiple components.

Which is considered one of the main causes of propagation loss (dB/cm) in silicon waveguides?

  1. ALight scattering from fine roughness on sidewalls created by etching
  2. BBand-to-band absorption in which telecom-wavelength photons excite silicon electrons from the valence band to the conduction band
  3. CSignal distortion caused by excessive amplification of light in the oxide surrounding the waveguide
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Light scattering from fine roughness on sidewalls created by etching

In silicon waveguides, roughness of a few nm formed on the sidewalls by lithography and etching scatters light out of the waveguide. Because the index contrast between silicon and oxide is large and the waveguide is thin, the light intensity at the sidewalls is also high, so scattering has a strong effect. Propagation loss of a typical strip waveguide is roughly 1–3 dB/cm depending on conditions (below 1 dB/cm in low-loss processes), and it is reduced by using rib waveguides, widening the waveguide, or smoothing the sidewalls by oxidation or annealing in hydrogen. The photon energy at telecom wavelengths is smaller than silicon's band gap, so band-to-band absorption does not occur.
